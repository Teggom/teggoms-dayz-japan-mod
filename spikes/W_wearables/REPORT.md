# Spike W: new rigged clothing on the DayZ player skeleton (wearables)

Agent W, spike wave 1, 2026-09-26/27. I started and stopped nothing and ran no git commands.

(The agent returned this as text because subagents can't write report files; the lead saved it on 2026-09-27.)

## The answer on one screen

- **It works offline, end to end.** Four garments, each with male and female worn models and a ground model, are
  generated as MLOD, bound to the vanilla skeleton, binarized cleanly and packed into
  `@Japan\addons\jp_characters.pbo`.
- **Reading the binarized files back** (`verify_odol.py`) shows:
  - the vanilla skeleton, bone for bone
  - every vertex with the weights we wrote (at most 4 bones)
  - the correct winding
- **Renders from the shipped files, posed with real vanilla animation clips:**
  - The hat, the socks and sandals, and the short kimono follow the body in every pose tried.
  - The floor-length robe works standing, but breaks when walking at full stride, sprinting, crouching, sitting and
    prone.
- **Not yet seen in the game.** The checklist below is the proof.
- **Settled facts:**
  - The skeleton is **`DayzTemporarySkeleton`**. `OFP2_ManSkeleton` isn't used.
  - Garments are modelled in the **xob bind pose**: facing -Z, the character's left at +X, the pose of
    `player_testing.xob`.
  - **Male and female share one identical skeleton.** Only the body meshes differ.
- **The biggest win:** the vanilla naked body parts (`torso3`, `legs3`, `feet3` and `hands3`, male and female) can be
  read out of the binarized game data, including their skin weights and UVs. So the fit is measured against the real
  body, and weights are transferred from it. This is the community Blender workflow, done in code.
- **Surprise 1: the vanilla "naked" male torso is an undershirt.** Its trunk uses `underwear_m_beige_co`, so the
  per-character skin atlas has no chest area.
  - The kimono's V chest skin is mapped to a plain arm-skin area of that atlas. It is tagged `personality`, so it
    still takes each character's skin tone.
  - The forearm skin inside the sleeves uses the vanilla arm's exact UVs.
- **Surprise 2: binarize reverses every face's vertex order.** I proved it with a test cube, and it explains why
  vanilla ODOL winding looks backwards.

- **Hidden selections:** `camoGround`, `camoMale` and `camoFemale`.
  - The kimonos add `personality`, with 3 textures listed, the same pattern as vanilla `WoolCoat`.
  - Vanilla `Clothing_Base.SwitchItemSelectionTextureEx` puts the wearer's skin texture and material on `personality`

  - The parent's `hitpoints` and `GlobalArmor` are copied verbatim: shirt 70 plus its Melee and Infected armour,

    slot.

### The pieces

  - It is 100 % on `head`, and `_m` and `_f` are the same mesh, as vanilla `CowboyHat` does.
  - I fitted it by script (`kasa.fit_apex`) against **all 31 vanilla heads, hair included**. The underside clears
    every head, including f_judy's bun, and every head fits inside the ring. So no hair hiding is needed; the
    inherited `Clipping_CowboyHat` is harmless.
  - Triangles per LOD: 2334 / 702 / 240.
- **Short kimono (kosode):** the hem is at 0.72 m, mid-thigh. The vanilla raincoat ends at 0.81 m.
  - The V collar crosses left over right. The collar is black with a white juban edge.
  - The sleeves are flat and wide, with a pouch and a partly closed cuff.
  - It has an obi with a knot, a back crest and a same-cloth lining.
  - The `personality` skin covers the V, a ring around the neck under the collar, and the forearm inside each sleeve.
  - Triangles per LOD: 8636 / 4444 / 2144.
- **Long kimono:** the same garment with the hem at 0.085 m. Triangles per LOD: 11772 / 5788 / 2608.
- **Tabi and waraji, one item:**
  - The sock is the vanilla foot with the toes merged, a split-toe groove, and the cuff raised to 18.5 cm. Brass
    kohaze clasps are painted on.
  - The waraji is a straw sole plus cords routed over the sock: the front loop, the side loops, the heel loop and the
    ankle wrap.
  - Triangles per LOD: 3860 / 2932 / 2452.
- **Ground models:**
  - The hat rests on its rim.
  - Each kimono is **folded flat, the traditional way**: a 42 × 30 cm bundle showing the crest, the collar and the obi.
  - The tabi pair stands side by side.

### LODs match vanilla (read from vanilla ODOL headers)

- **Vanilla worn models:**
  - Resolution LODs 1 to 5, each with `lodnoshadow=1`
  - a Geometry LOD with `Component01` and `autocenter=0`
  - **no** Memory, View Geometry or Fire Geometry. Some items also have a 1100 first-person LOD, or a 2e15 LOD.
- **Vanilla ground models:** Resolution, Geometry, Memory (`invview`, `ce_center`, `ce_radius`, `boundingbox_min`,
  `boundingbox_max`), View Geometry, and Fire Geometry with `penetration\fabric_thin`.
- **Ours:** exactly these sets, with 3 resolution LODs. So the brief's "Resolution, Geometry, Fire Geometry, Memory"
  is the **ground** model's set.

### The pipeline (`spikes/W_wearables/tools/`)

- **Reading vanilla binarized files:** `lzo.py`, `odol_probe.py`, `odol_mesh.py`, `odol_sections.py` and
  `pstrings.py`. Together they are an LZO1X decompressor plus probes that pull positions, faces, UVs, weights and
  sections. They are not a full ODOL parser.
- **Posing:** `anm_pose.py` reads the xob bind pose and `.anm` clips, then runs the forward kinematics and the
  skinning.
- **Generators:** `kasa.py`, `tabi.py`, `kimono.py` with `kimono_parts.py`, and `ground.py`.
- **Helpers:**
  - `garment.py`: weights, smoothing, lining, triangulation and orientation
  - `geom.py`: closest-point transfer, slicing, hulls and normals
  - `textures.py`: procedural textures, converted with ImageToPAA
- **Build:** `build_p3d.py` writes the MLODs, rvmats, `model.cfg` (from `modelcfg.py`) and `config.cpp` (from
  `configgen.py`). It then runs the CfgConvert check, binarize (cwd `P:\`) and the PBO pack.
- **Checks:** `verify_odol.py`, and `test_cube_binarize.py` for the skeleton and winding proof.
- **Renders:** `render_scene.py`, `scene.py` and `final_renders.py`.
- **Rebuild everything:** run `build_p3d.py`, then `verify_odol.py`. It takes about 40 s.

**The method for loose tops (it scales to dozens of pieces):**
1. Slice the vanilla torso without its arm faces, plus the legs, every centimetre.
2. Take the convex hull of each slice and read its radius in 72 directions.
3. Add ease and apply the garment's drape rules.
4. Lay a row and column grid over the result, then add the collar, obi and sleeve parts.
5. Transfer weights by closest point from the body and smooth them.
6. Below the crotch, use the vanilla coat rule, read from raincoat, labcoat, woolcoat and nursedress: each side follows
   its own thigh, the centre is split between both thighs, and the pelvis share falls with depth.
7. Sleeves are weighted along the arm chain.
8. Triangulate before making the lining.

**For socks and fitted pieces:** offset a smoothed vanilla part.

## Verified offline

1. **Skeleton.** Every vanilla worn `_m` and `_f` model I scanned embeds `DayzTemporarySkeleton`, and the ground
   models embed none. The same 159 (bone, parent) pairs appear in raincoat_m/f, cowboyhat, athleticshoes_m, torso3_m
   and baseballcap_m. `OFP2_ManSkeleton` appears in none of them.
2. **Frame.** In the extracted vanilla body vertices, each bone's vertices centre on its xob joint. The toes and the
   baseball cap's peak point to -Z. The male and female xob files match to 1e-6 m.
3. **Binarize and config.** Binarize is clean on all 12 models, with only the generic environment warnings and "UV
   mapping too varied" notes. CfgConvert passes.
4. **`verify_odol.py`: ALL PASS.**
   - The skeleton equals vanilla.
   - `camomale`, `camofemale`, `camoground` and `personality` are sectional, as in vanilla.
   - Every LOD 0 vertex matches, and the weights agree within 0.4 % (1/255 quantisation).
   - No vertex has more than 4 bones.
   - Every checked face's ODOL order is the MLOD order reversed, as with the cube and vanilla.
5. **Renders of the shipped LOD 0** (`renders/final/*/..._sheet.png`). Each garment is skinned by its own weights over
   the vanilla body, on these clips:
   - `p_erc_idle`
   - `p_erc_walkf` and `p_erc_sprintf`, at the widest stride
   - `p_cro_idle` and `p_cro_walkf`
   - `p_cro_sit_loop`
   - `p_pne_idle`
   - `p_sur_erc_idle` (arms up)
   - `p_rfl_erc_idle_ras` (rifle raised)

   The clips drive the vanilla corrective "Extra" bones themselves. Back faces render magenta in QA passes and are
   culled in the game-like passes.

   Results by set:
   - **Kasa:** rigid on the head in every pose, with no hair showing through.
   - **Tabi:** the sock follows the foot and toes, the cuff meets the leg with no gap, and the sole bends at the toes.
   - **Short kimono** (both sexes, 10 poses × 4 views):
     - It follows the body in every pose.
     - The sleeves hang behind the arms when relaxed, and under the forearms with a rifle raised.
     - The skirt follows the thighs. In a full sprint the thigh shows at the hem, like vanilla coats.
     - Prone, the skirt fans out.
     - Arms up: the sleeves stick out sideways. This is the expected limit of linear-blend skinning.
   - **Seams:** the V skin reaches the head's neck, the back collar is closed, the hand comes out of the cuff with skin
     behind it, and the tabi cuff meets the leg.
   - **LODs 0, 1 and 2** keep the silhouette.
   - **The full outfit** reads as a Japanese traveller.

## The long robe (the R&D question)

The evidence is in `renders/final/long/long_sheet.png` (mode B, shipped). The drafts `long_modeA_draft_sheet.png` and
`long_modeB_draft_sheet.png` compare the two modes.

- **Fine:** the bind pose, idle, and arms up, for both sexes. It looks right.
- **Walk (widest stride):** the forward knee and shin show through the front, and the back splits between the legs,
  showing the dark inside.
- **Sprint:** large holes at the front, and the back panel fans out.
- **Crouch and crouch-walk:** the knees and shins come through the front.
- **Sit:**
  - Mode A (thighs only) makes a rigid "tent" out over the knees.
  - Mode B (below the knee, up to 45 % follows the shins) wraps the legs better, but the knees still show.
- **Prone:** a hole at the back of the thigh.
- **The rifle stance:** the slightly bent knee already pokes through.
- **Cause:** one tube around two legs that move apart can't be skinned to two thighs and two shins. The skeleton has
  no cloth bones, and more ease only makes a bell.

Mode B is shipped, so Stephen can judge it in game.

## Failed / unknown

- **The in-game result is unproven.** This is especially true of the engine's inverse-bind (our geometry is in
  vanilla's pose and frame) and of the `personality` hookup (copied from WoolCoat).
- **The V chest skin is sampled from an upper-arm area of the atlas.** The tone is right, but it has no chest detail.
- **LODs are 3, not 5, and are only lightly reduced.**
  - Tabi LOD 2 is still 2.4k triangles.
  - Kimono LOD 0 is 8.6k triangles (short) and 11.8k (long). The lining doubles the count. For comparison, vanilla
    woolcoat and gulagjacket are the same size or bigger.
- **The sleeve pouch is a skinning compromise.** It points behind the arm, which is good arms-down and aiming. It
  sticks out when the arms are raised, and in the A-pose (inventory and character screen) it looks like panels behind
  the arms.
- **The kasa's rim in first person** is untested.
- **Older vanilla heads are stored 0.907 m low in their p3d.** That covers Adam, Boris, Guo, Eva and others, but not
  Keiko or Naomi. The offline fit compensates for it; the engine handles it.
- **Our bone selections carry points only, while vanilla's also carry faces.** The skin weights live in the per-vertex
  bone references, which I verified, so I don't expect this to matter.

## Decisions I made

1. **Weights come from the real vanilla body, not a proxy.** It is readable from the game data. The derived files are
   reference only, stay in git-ignored `data/W/`, and are never shipped.
2. **Parents:**
   - **CowboyHat_ColorBase** for the kasa, as the wide-brimmed hat.
   - **Shirt_ColorBase for both kimonos:** cotton, 0.4 insulation. Raincoat is waterproof, WoolCoat (0.9) is too warm,
     and LabCoat (0.2) is colder than the short kimono should be.
   - **Sneakers_ColorBase** for the tabi: light, 0.2 insulation, and no skin showing. Ballerinas would show skin.
3. **The collar crosses left over right,** with a white juban edge. The V chest skin is still modelled as
   `personality`, as the brief asked.
4. **No hair hiding** (see the kasa fit). **The kasa `_m` and `_f` share one mesh,** as vanilla does.
5. **The obi is part of the kimono.** No vanilla slot fits it as a separate item.
6. **Long robe:** skirt mode B, chosen from the renders. The short kimono uses the vanilla coat rule (mode A). Both
   modes stay in `kimono_parts.skirt_weights`.
7. **Textures are procedural,** so there are no downloads or licences. The kimono is 2048²; the hat and tabi are 1024².
   The long kimono has its own texture so the crest isn't stretched.
8. **Everything is triangulated before the lining is made.** Quads split on different diagonals let the lining cross
   the outer shell.
9. **I moved all render frames and drafts to `data/W/`.** About 140 MB was too much for git. Only the contact sheets
   stay in the spike folder.

## What this means for samurai armour, hakama and floor-length robes

- **Rigid armour is easy.** That covers the kabuto, menpo, sode, do, kote and suneate. Each is plates on one or two
  bones: the kasa's path with a fit against the vanilla body. Lamellar generates well from code. Expect **about one
  piece per session once there is a template.**
- **Kusazuri tassets** should be separate rigid plates, weighted like the short skirt (pelvis plus the plate's own
  thigh). They overlap instead of stretching, so knee-length kusazuri are fine.
- **Hakama are the answer to long garments.** They are divided trousers, so each leg is its own tube, which is how
  vanilla trousers are weighted. Build them with the same profile method per leg, pleats as geometry, weights
  transferred from `legs3`, in the Legs slot. Expect some clipping between the wide legs at a sprint. **Make this the
  next wearable.**
- **Knee-length robes are fine:** haori, the short kimono, jinbaori, or a kimono over hakama. The longest vanilla Body
  garment ends at 0.66 m.
- **Floor-length robes on the stock skeleton: NO for players.**
  - They are acceptable for NPCs that only stand and walk: priests, merchants, court ladies.
  - Cloth bones would mean shipping our own player xob and animation graph. Only one mod per server can own that, and
    riding needs it too.
  - If players must look long-robed, use a kimono over hakama.

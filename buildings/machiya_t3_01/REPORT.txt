4. PERIOD JUSTIFICATION (per element)
------------------------------------
- Gable roof, eave to the street, sangawara, stacked ridge with end tiles, small round eave tiles: the Ioka house
  (Nara, late 17th-early 18th c.) [E03, x01, x03]; sangawara from 1674 [T07]; tile on T3 townhouses (§2.1).
  Plain round eave tiles, as §6.1 describes sangawara (the mon-boss tomoe tile is the hongawara form).
- 3-ken main span + rear lean-to: commoner beam span <= 3 ken, extra depth from geya [T48, §2.2]. 4-ken frontage
  follows Ioka (7.9 m = 4 kyo-ken) on our 1.82 grid.
- Low upper storey, 1.30 m street wall, sealed: rule 2, §4, D3 [T43, T05]; G0-4 default sealed. No full storey
  (banned in 1730, §1).
- Small oval mushiko: the 1730 form, rectangles are Meiji [E05, T09].
- Koshi lattice + de-goshi shop front: the 18th c. Kyoto standard front [E10]; dark timber, no bengara (restricted,
  and the Ioka front is dark).
- Street pent and side pent: Ioka has pents front and side [E03].
- Udatsu: T3 Kamigata [T08]. On this lone test house they mark the party-wall lines of a future street.
- Dodai on individual dressed stones: rule 3, §6.4.
- Walls: earth shinkabe (earth_wall_aged) on the ground floor, shikkui only on the upper storey and gables (G1 d1,
  build list T3 text); dark vertical boards on the toriniwa gable (Ioka lower walls [x01]); koshiita wainscots
  (§6.2); exposed posts; no plane > 2 x 2 m without a frame line.
- Doors: period ~0.9 m leaves, two per 1-ken bay (§4 [T39], §6.3), heads raised to 2.00 (D2).
- Plan: 1-ken earth toriniwa through the house, shop at the front, rooms raised about 0.45 [T04, T17, §2.1];
  tatami in the living rooms, shugi layout (§3); board storage; kitchen doma with a kamado (T3 interiors).
- Zashiki kept plain: no tokonoma, nageshi or shoin, the 1668 commoner rules [T48, §2.2].
- Weathering: W1 grime bands on wall bases and posts, wear _w1 throughout (rule 4).

6. DEVIATIONS, CONFLICTS AND DECISIONS
--------------------------------------
Playbook conflicts:
 a. The library has no tatami, floor-board or doma material (the §9 families floor/ground were never built).
    Stand-ins, all jp_common: straw_mushiro for the mat facing (each mat is modelled: 0.055 thick, weave turned per
    mat), wood_sooted for the heri edges, wood_weathered for floor boards, wall_arakabe for the doma (as the parts
    test assembly did). Ask the materials agent for jp_m_floor_tatami / floor_boards / ground_doma; swapping them is
    one path change and a rebuild.
 b. Budget class: the brief says "LOD budgets"; the whole house cannot meet the 6,000-face standard-house budget
    with a 3,000-face kawara roof (rule 1). I used the large/landmark class (12,000 / 4,600 / 1,600), since §11
    lists "the main machiya archetype" as a hero landmark. To get under it: Resolution 3 is the exterior shell only
    (interior walls, floors, loft, kamado drop out), koshiita and board walls are one slab from Resolution 2 on,
    the geya has one modelled eave row (the library pent recipe's setting) instead of two, hidden bottom faces of
    mats and boards are left out, and there is no ranma.
Decisions (not engine-tested):
 c. Sealed loft (G0-4). No reason found to open it: the house holds no loft loot and there's no stair.
 d. Twin doors (section 1) use the vanilla DoorsTwin convention. This is the first time this project uses it:
    in-game item 2.
 e. Walkable roofs: tile slopes, both pents and the lean-to have Roadway, and a ridge strip lets you walk the
    ridge. The street pent sits under the main eave (under 1.2 m of clearance), so there you crawl, not stand.
    Rooftop access with no stair: climb the rear lean-to eave (tile edge about 2.53 m; vanilla climbs 1.7-2.75 m,
    dayzplayerimplementjumpclimb.c), then vault about 0.5 m onto the main roof's back eave. Unknown: whether the
    climb test accepts an overhanging eave. Fallback: a woodpile or garden wall at the site stage.
 f. Stone courses sit 0.10 outwards, so the inside face is flush with the wall. Centred courses made a kerb in the
    doma and put stones under two doorways.
 g. At the two room corners where a door line meets a partition, the partition's head rail stops 0.12 m short. The
    far leaf's top ran 3 cm into it.
 h. Udatsu are built with the _sode recipe but sized 3.05-4.12 m. The library size (to 4.55) cuts into the main
    eave.
 i. The kamado and its sooted boards are modelled as a built-in fixture. The pots are left to the decorator.
 j. Mushiko openings are closed in Geometry, as the part is. The loft can be seen through them, not entered.
 k. Stephen dropped the old-vs-new comparison, so the sheet shows only the new house.

# Named landmarks: Edo and the Tōkaidō to Ōtsu (LM-E)

Research agent LM-E, 2026-09-29. Text only. Anchor year **1730** (Kyōhō 15: Yoshimune is shogun, Ōoka Tadasuke is South
Edo magistrate, Tokugawa Muneharu has just become lord of Owari).

**Scope.** Edo, Shinagawa, the Tōkaidō and its 53 stations, Hakone, Fuji, the Izu edge, the Suruga and Tōtōmi coast,
Mikawa, Owari, the Ise coast and Suzuka pass, and Ōmi as far as Ōtsu. LM-W owns Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, Lake Biwa's
shores and the Nakasendō, so this file stops at Ōtsu. Ise (off the main map) is a side trip at the end.

**Order.** East to west, the way a traveller leaving Nihonbashi walks.

**Entry format.**
- **1730:** what stood, the layout, the scale and the "tell".
- **Era:**
  - `IN`: stood in 1730.
  - `REBUILT`: what you see today is later; the entry says what stood in 1730.
  - `[not yet in 1730: date]`
  - `[gone by 1730: date]`
- **Fame:** national / regional / local, with the evidence.
- **Play:** why a player goes there, and the set size (`single` / `compound` / `district` / `natural site`).
- **Src:** a short source note.

**How fame was judged (honest note).** I did not have the text of the *Tōkaidō meisho-ki* (c.1658–61) or the
*Tōkaidō bunken ezu* (1690) open. Fame is graded on:
- what the site was: a shogunal institution, a checkpoint, a station everyone had to pass through, an ichinomiya
- documented period fame: Bashō's verse, the kabuki stage, the 47 rōnin, Kaempfer's route (1691–92), the *Tōkaidō
  meisho zue* (1797, a later guide but it records long-standing fame)
- Hiroshige, used for FORM only (1830s)

"(verify)" marks a date or detail I recall but did not re-check this session. Everything else was either checked
against a web source this session (named in Src) or is standard textbook history.

---

== Edo: the castle and the centre ==

### E01. Edo Castle, the keepless citadel (Edo-jō) — centre of Edo
- **1730:** The largest castle in Japan: concentric moats and stone walls about 2 km across. The honmaru palace
  (goten) sits on the top level, and the corner turrets (sumi-yagura) are whitewashed. Great box gates guard it:
  Ōte-mon, Sakurada-mon, Hirakawa-mon. On the honmaru stands a huge dressed-granite keep base, rebuilt in 1659, with
  NO keep on it. The three-storey Fujimi-yagura does duty as the keep.
- **Era:** Castle IN. Keep `[gone by 1730: burned in the Meireki fire, 1657; never rebuilt]`. The empty base
  (tenshudai) is IN; today's base is that 1659 one.
- **Fame:** national. It is the shogun's seat.
- **Play:** The map's top-tier fortress. The empty keep base is the tell: a flat granite platform with a view over
  the whole city, a perfect sniper nest and the capstone of any Edo raid. Walls and gates make it a layered
  chokepoint. Set: district (a condensed version: 2 baileys, 3 gates, 4 turrets, the keep base, the palace roofline).
- **Src:** Edo Castle histories; the keep was abandoned after 1657 by Hoshina Masayuki's decision.

### E02. Nihonbashi bridge (Nihonbashi) — heart of the merchant city
- **1730:** A wooden arched trestle bridge about 50 m long over the Nihonbashi river, with railings capped by giboshi
  (onion-knob) finials. It is the zero point of the Five Routes (Gokaidō): all distances are counted from here. The
  south end carries the shogunate's official notice boards (takafuda-ba) and a display ground where condemned
  criminals were exposed (sarashi-ba) (verify: which bank). Fuji and the castle are both visible from the crown.
- **Era:** REBUILT. The wooden bridge was rebuilt many times after fires; the stone bridge is
  `[not yet in 1730: 1911]`. A wooden bridge of the same form stood in 1730.
- **Fame:** national. It is the start of the Tōkaidō and the opening print of every Tōkaidō series.
- **Play:** The map's origin point and spawn-worthy hub. It is the one crossing over the central canal, a crowded
  chokepoint, and the notice board is a place for server messages and bounties. Set: single bridge plus dressing, set
  in the merchant district.
- **Src:** Nihonbashi (Wikipedia); Hiroshige, *Nihonbashi* (form).

### E03. Nihonbashi fish market (Nihonbashi uogashi) — north bank, Nihonbashi to Edobashi
- **1730:** A strip of fish wholesalers' open-fronted shops and landing steps. It runs along the north bank from
  Nihonbashi to Edobashi (Honfune-chō, Anjin-chō, Odawara-chō) and is packed with baskets, tubs and plank counters from
  dawn. The salt-fish dealers are across the river at Yokkaichi-chō.
- **Era:** IN since the early 1600s; moved to Tsukiji `[not yet in 1730: 1923/1935]`.
- **Fame:** national. It was proverbially one of Edo's "thousand-ryō a day" places.
- **Play:** Food-loot district and a canal landing: boats, fish, knives, ice-less spoilage. Set: district strip (6–10
  shop fronts plus steps).
- **Src:** Nihonbashi (Wikipedia); MFA *Nihonbashi uo-ichi* prints.

### E04. Echigoya dry-goods store (Echigoya, Mitsui) — Suruga-chō, Nihonbashi
- **1730:** A huge double-frontage kimono store with indigo noren curtains and a raised tatami sales floor staffed by
  dozens of clerks. It sold for cash at fixed prices ("genkin kakene nashi"). Down Suruga-chō street, Fuji lines up
  over the roofs.
- **Era:** IN. Mitsui moved here in 1683; the store burned and was rebuilt more than once.
- **Fame:** national. The house was an Edo byword for wealth and the future Mitsui bank.
- **Play:** A high-tier civilian loot building (cloth, coin, ledgers, the money exchange next door). The
  Suruga-chō street-with-Fuji view is a cheap postcard. Set: single large building plus a kura.
- **Src:** Mitsui history (standard); Hiroshige *Suruga-chō* (form).

### E05. Kodenmachō jail (Kodenmachō rōyashiki) — Kodenmachō, north of Nihonbashi
- **1730:** The shogunate's main prison, in use since the early 1600s: a walled compound of about 2,600 tsubo. It held
  plank cell blocks graded by status (commoners' ōrō, samurai agari-ya, a women's block), with an execution yard for
  beheadings, a moat-like ditch and a single guarded gate.
- **Era:** IN (in use c.1613–1875).
- **Fame:** national, as a dread name.
- **Play:** THE Chernarus-prison analogue. It is a walled compound with one gate, cell blocks for close-quarters
  combat, and loot in the guards' office. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Criminal punishment in Edo-period Japan (Wikipedia); size (assumed from memory, verify).

### E06. South and North Magistrates' offices (Minami / Kita machi-bugyōsho) — Sukiyabashi and Tokiwabashi gates
- **1730:** Two walled official compounds just inside the outer moat gates. Each has a white-sand court (shirasu)
  where suspects knelt, an audience hall, and nagaya barracks for yoriki and dōshin. In 1730 the South office is
  Ōoka Echizen-no-kami Tadasuke's (in office 1717–1736).
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national. Ōoka's judgements were already stories in his lifetime.
- **Play:** Law-and-order set: a court, records, confiscated weapons (a mid-high military tier). Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard; Ōoka's tenure dates are well documented.

### E07. Daimyo mansion row (Daimyō-kōji / Maru-no-uchi) — between the inner and outer moats
- **1730:** Block after block of daimyo main residences (kami-yashiki), each walled with long nagaya-mon gatehouses.
  The frontage is blank white walls with barred windows and the domain crest over the gate. Each block is 100–200 m.
- **Era:** IN. (Kaga's famous red gate at Hongō is `[not yet in 1730: 1827]`.)
- **Fame:** national. Every daimyo lived here on alternate attendance.
- **Play:** The Edo high-tier samurai district: long walls and few doors, so it plays as a maze of corridors between
  compounds. Set: district (reuse one mansion 4–6 times with different crests).
- **Src:** Standard.

== Edo: north and east (Kanda, Ueno, Asakusa, the Sumida) ==

### E08. Kanda Myōjin (Kanda Myōjin) — Yushima heights above the Kanda river
- **1730:** Edo's "general guardian" shrine, on the bluff since 1616. It is a compound of gate, worship hall and
  sub-shrines; Taira no Masakado is one of its gods. Its festival parade was one of the two allowed into the castle.
- **Era:** IN (the buildings of that date were later lost in 1923; the present hall is 1934 concrete).
- **Fame:** national (the Kanda matsuri).
- **Play:** Hilltop shrine with a view over the Kanda river and the city: mid loot plus a view. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E09. Yushima Seidō, the Confucian academy (Yushima Seidō) — Shōhei-zaka, beside Kanda Myōjin
- **1730:** The shogunate's Confucian temple and school, moved here in 1690 under Tsunayoshi. The Taisei-den hall is
  black-lacquered Chinese style and sits in a walled court entered through the Kyōkaku-mon and Nyūtoku-mon gates.
- **Era:** IN (the 1690 site; today's hall is a 1935 concrete rebuild of the later 1799 form).
- **Fame:** national among samurai.
- **Play:** Rare "Chinese-style black hall" silhouette; books and scholarly loot. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard (verify the 1935 detail).

### E10. Kan'ei-ji and Ueno hill (Tōeizan Kan'ei-ji, Ueno no yama) — Ueno, the north-east of Edo
- **1730:** A vast Tendai temple-city covering the whole Ueno hill. It includes:
  - the Konpon Chūdō main hall (1698)
  - the Kiyomizu Kannon-dō on its stilted terrace (1631; moved 1694)
  - a five-storey pagoda (1639)
  - Ueno Tōshōgū (1651)
  - the mausolea of shoguns Ietsuna and Tsunayoshi
  - dozens of sub-temples
  - Shinobazu pond below, with its Benten island hall
  Yoshimune planted cherries here (1720) for commoner hanami.
- **Era:** IN in 1730. Most of it was `[destroyed 1868, Battle of Ueno]`; the pagoda, the Tōshōgū and the Kiyomizu hall
  survive from 1730.
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** A hill fortress-temple: high loot in the mausoleum precinct, water at Shinobazu, and the pagoda as a
  sightline landmark. The 1868 last stand makes it a natural "final fight" set. Set: district.
- **Src:** Kan'ei-ji (Wikipedia); Kiyomizu Kannon-dō dates.

### E11. Nezu Gongen shrine (Nezu jinja) — Nezu, below Ueno
- **1730:** A lacquered gongen-style shrine that Tsunayoshi built in 1706: a vermilion tower gate, a karamon, and a
  worship hall joined to the main hall by a sunken room (ishi-no-ma), with a sweep of azaleas on the slope.
- **Era:** IN, and the 1706 buildings survive today.
- **Fame:** regional (Edo).
- **Play:** A compact, colourful "mini-Tōshōgū" set that reuses the Tōshōgū kit. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard (verify).

### E12. Sensō-ji and Asakusa (Kinryūzan Sensō-ji) — Asakusa, on the Sumida
- **1730:** Edo's oldest and busiest temple. The approach runs:
  1. the Kaminarimon, with its giant red lantern and the wind and thunder gods (the 1649 gate)
  2. the approach street lined with stalls
  3. the two-storey Niōmon
  4. the huge main Kannon hall, rebuilt by Iemitsu in 1649, with a five-storey pagoda (1648) beside it
  Around it are Asakusa Shrine (1649), the Denbō-in abbot's garden and a fairground of show booths, archery galleries
  and tea houses.
- **Era:** IN (1649 complex). The Kaminarimon `[burned 1757, again 1865]`; the present one is 1960. The main hall and
  pagoda burned in 1945 and were rebuilt in concrete. The Nitenmon (1618) and Asakusa Shrine (1649) survive.
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** The capital's crowd hub: the fairground and stalls give mid-tier civilian loot, and the pagoda is a
  landmark. Set: district.
- **Src:** Kaminarimon, Sensō-ji (Wikipedia).

### E13. Asakusa rice granaries (Asakusa o-kura) — west bank of the Sumida, south of Sensō-ji
- **1730:** The shogunate's rice storehouses, in use from 1620: some 50+ long storehouse rows on a walled riverside
  site, with eight comb-like docks (hori) cut in from the Sumida for barges. The Kubi-o-matsu pine marks the bank.
  The rice brokers (fudasashi) have their district opposite in Kuramae.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national. It paid the shogun's retainers.
- **Play:** Food-loot jackpot and a waterfront fight with barges. Set: compound (reuse one kura 10+ times).
- **Src:** Standard (the count of docks is assumed, verify).

### E14. New Yoshiwara pleasure quarter (Shin-Yoshiwara) — rice fields north of Asakusa
- **1730:** A walled, moated rectangle about 270 × 360 m set alone in paddies, reached by the Nihon-zutsumi dike. At
  the Mikaeri-yanagi ("looking-back willow") the path bends down to the single Ōmon gate, which has a guard post. Five
  streets of latticed brothels and guide tea houses lie inside, circled by the black "tooth-blackening" moat
  (ohaguro-dobu).
- **Era:** IN (moved here in 1657 after the Meireki fire).
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** A self-contained walled district with ONE entrance: a classic trap and loot box (coin, fine cloth,
  tobacco, sake). The lone dike approach across the paddies is a sniper's dream. Set: district.
- **Src:** Standard; the Mikaeri-yanagi and ohaguro-dobu are standard descriptions.

### E15. Kozukappara execution ground (Kozukappara keijō) — by Senju Ōhashi, the northern exit of Edo
- **1730:** A roadside field by the Ōshū/Nikkō road. It had crucifixion posts, a pit, a stone-lined washing area and a
  temple (Ekō-in branch) for the dead. Heads were displayed to travellers entering Edo. Senju Ōhashi (1594) spans the
  Sumida beside it.
- **Era:** IN (1651–1873). The large stone Kubikiri Jizō (1741) is `[not yet in 1730: 1741]`.
- **Fame:** regional (dread).
- **Play:** A grim roadside set at a bridgehead chokepoint: bones and wanderer loot. Pair it with the bridge.
  Set: natural site plus 2 small buildings.
- **Src:** Kozukappara execution grounds (Wikipedia); Jizō date (verify).

### E16. Ryōgoku bridge and its firebreak plaza (Ryōgoku-bashi, Ryōgoku hirokōji) — over the Sumida, Edo/Honjo
- **1730:** A long wooden trestle bridge (c. 170–200 m) built 1659–61 after the Meireki fire, linking Musashi and
  Shimōsa. At the Edo end is a wide firebreak plaza, which by day filled with show booths, tea stalls and storytellers
  and was cleared at night.
- **Era:** Bridge IN. The famous fireworks `[not yet in 1730: began 1733]`. The Ekō-in regular sumo `[not yet in 1730:
  from 1768; permanent 1833]`.
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** The longest bridge in Edo: a huge exposed chokepoint over the river, with a fairground plaza for loot and
  crowds. Set: bridge plus plaza.
- **Src:** NDL Image Bank, "Fireworks and Sumida River Opening"; standard.

### E17. Ekō-in, the temple of the nameless dead (Ekō-in) — Honjo, east end of Ryōgoku bridge
- **1730:** Founded 1657 for the 100,000 dead of the Meireki fire, it grew into the memorial ground for all unclaimed
  dead (drowned, executed, animals). It has a mound-grave (muen-zuka) and fund-raising temple displays (kaichō).
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** A graveyard set at the bridgehead: eerie, low loot. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E18. Tomioka Hachimangū and the Fukagawa archery hall (Tomioka Hachimangū, Fukagawa Sanjūsangendō) — Fukagawa
- **1730:**
  - Tomioka Hachiman (1627) is a big shrine on reclaimed land. It was the cradle of Edo kanjin-zumō: fund-raising sumo
    was held there from 1684.
  - The Sanjūsangendō is a copy of Kyoto's long archery hall, moved to Fukagawa in 1701. It is about 120 m long, and
    archers shot along its veranda.
  - Fukagawa's unlicensed geisha tea houses surround both.
- **Era:** IN. The archery hall was `[gone: dismantled 1872]`.
- **Fame:** regional to national.
- **Play:** The 120 m veranda is a ready-made long-range corridor. Set: compound (the shrine plus one long hall).
- **Src:** Standard (verify the 1701 relocation).

### E19. Kameido Tenjin (Kameido Tenmangū) — Kameido, east of the Sumida
- **1730:** A Tenjin shrine (1662) modelled on Dazaifu. It has a heart-shaped pond crossed by two steep drum bridges
  (taiko-bashi) and wisteria trellises along the water.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** regional (the wisteria).
- **Play:** A striking water-garden shrine; the drum bridges are a signature silhouette. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard; Hiroshige (form).

### E20. Kabuki theatre district (Sakai-chō / Fukiya-chō / Kobiki-chō shibai-machi) — near Nihonbashi
- **1730:** The licensed theatres (Nakamura-za, Ichimura-za, Morita-za), each marked by a drum-tower (yagura) over the
  entrance and hung with banner-boards. Theatre tea houses and puppet theatres fill the streets around. The fourth
  house, the Yamamura-za, was closed in 1714 (the Ejima-Ikushima scandal).
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national. Danjūrō II is the star of 1730.
- **Play:** A big-interior public building (galleries, stage, trapdoors) makes a great indoor fight map. Set: district
  (1 theatre, 3–4 tea houses).
- **Src:** Standard.

### E21. Koishikawa herb garden and free clinic (Koishikawa Yakuen, Koishikawa Yōjōsho) — Koishikawa
- **1730:** The shogunal medicinal-plant garden (from 1684), with rows of herb beds. Inside it is the free paupers'
  hospital set up in 1722 after a petition dropped in Yoshimune's new complaint box (meyasubako, 1721).
- **Era:** IN, and new in 1730.
- **Fame:** regional. It was a talking point of the Kyōhō reforms.
- **Play:** THE medical loot site: bandages, herbs, remedies. Set: compound (garden plus a long ward building).
- **Src:** Standard.

== Edo south: Shiba, Takanawa, Shinagawa ==

### E22. Zōjō-ji (Sanenzan Zōjō-ji) — Shiba, on the Tōkaidō's way out of Edo
- **1730:** The Tokugawa family's Jōdo temple. The approach is the red two-storey Sangedatsu-mon (1622), which leads to
  a great main hall. Walled, gilded mausolea stand for Hidetada (Taitoku-in, 1632), Ienobu (1712) and Ietsugu (1716),
  among a forest of sub-temples. The Tōkaidō runs right past its gate.
- **Era:** IN in 1730. The Sangedatsu-mon survives; the mausolea `[destroyed 1945]`.
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** The south-side counterpart to Ueno: a gilded-mausoleum high-tier loot site right on the main road.
  Set: district.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E23. Sengaku-ji and the graves of the 47 rōnin (Sengaku-ji) — Takanawa
- **1730:** A Sōtō Zen temple (1612, moved 1641) with the Asano family graves. Since 1703 it also holds the 47 rōnin's
  graves in a row behind the hall, plus the well where they washed Kira's head. Only 27 years on, pilgrims were
  already burning incense there.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national. The incident of 1701–03 was already staged in disguise by 1706; *Kanadehon Chūshingura*
  (1748) came later.
- **Play:** A strong-story shrine: the "47 graves" row is a memorable set piece and a good spot for a quest
  flag. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E24. Takanawa Great Gate (Takanawa Ōkido) — the Tōkaidō entrance to Edo
- **1730:** Built in 1710: stone-faced earth ramparts about 9 m long × 7 m thick × 3 m high on both sides of the
  road. A black wooden gate once hung between them. There is a notice board, and a guard checked weapons in and women
  out. The sea is on the left.
- **Era:** IN (1710; the gate itself was later removed, and the stone walls partly survive).
- **Fame:** regional. Every Edo arrival passed it.
- **Play:** The map's Edo "city gate" chokepoint: seaside road, rampart cover. Set: single (two ramparts plus a
  guardhouse).
- **Src:** Takanawa Great Wooden Gate (Wikipedia); Tokyo Metropolitan Library *Edo meisho zue*.

### E25. Shinagawa-juku (Shinagawa-shuku) — station 1
- **1730:** The first station and Edo's unofficial pleasure town, strung along the shore for about 2 km. It had a
  honjin, 90+ inns with meshimori serving-women, and tea houses on piles over the bay for moon-viewing. Tōkai-ji
  (1638) stands behind the town. The Meguro river is crossed by a small bridge.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national. It was the leaving-party town for Edo.
- **Play:** The map's first post town: mid loot and a seaside strip. Set: district.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E26. Gotenyama cherry hill (Gotenyama) — above Shinagawa
- **1730:** A bluff over Shinagawa bay, once the site of a shogunal rest lodge. Yoshimune had cherries planted here
  in 1720 for public hanami, so in 1730 they are young trees. There is a view over the bay and the Shinagawa
  offshore anchorage.
- **Era:** IN, but the trees are young. (The coastal gun batteries, Odaiba, are `[not yet in 1730: 1853]`.)
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** A viewpoint hill over the road and the harbour (sniper/overwatch). Set: natural site.
- **Src:** NDL *Kaleidoscope of Books*, cherry blossom spots.

### E27. Suzugamori execution ground (Suzugamori keijō) — the Tōkaidō beside the sea, south of Shinagawa
- **1730:** A roadside plot by the shore, with a stone for the burning post and a round stone for the crucifixion
  post. Heads were displayed facing the road into Edo. It was a warning to rōnin; Kaempfer, entering Edo in 1691,
  describes the executed bodies at this approach (verify: exact wording and place).
- **Era:** IN (1651–1871).
- **Fame:** regional to national (dread). The kabuki bandit Shirai Gonpachi was said to have died here (1679).
- **Play:** A grim shoreline set on the main road, for ambient horror and wanderer loot. Set: natural site plus posts.
- **Src:** Suzugamori execution grounds (Wikipedia); Atlas Obscura.

== Shinagawa to Odawara: Musashi and the Sagami coast ==

### E28. Rokugō ferry over the Tama river (Rokugō no watashi) — between Shinagawa and Kawasaki
- **1730:** A broad river crossed by flat ferry boats. Kawasaki village took the ferry rights in 1709 (verify). The
  bridge was given up after its seventh wash-out in 1688.
- **Era:** IN, with no bridge. `[bridge gone by 1730: 1688]`
- **Fame:** regional (Kawasaki's print).
- **Play:** The first ferry chokepoint out of Edo; the "no bridge here" river divides the map. Set: ferry landings
  on both banks plus a ferry hut.
- **Src:** Rokugō ferry history (Ōta tourism; Hiroshige Kawasaki print notes).

### E29. Kawasaki Daishi (Heiken-ji) — near Kawasaki station
- **1730:** A Shingon temple (1128) of Kōbō Daishi, with gate, main hall and an approach street. Visits were popular
  on "yakudoshi" (unlucky years).
- **Era:** IN (buildings 1945 and later today).
- **Fame:** regional in 1730; it became a mass pilgrimage site later, with shogun Ienari's visits c.1790s (verify).
- **Play:** A mid-tier temple one short hop off the road. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E30. Yugyō-ji (Tōtaku-san Yugyō-ji) — Fujisawa, station 6
- **1730:** The head temple of the Ji sect of wandering nembutsu monks (founded 1325). Its big hall is on a rise above
  the Fujisawa station, with a gingko and a long approach.
- **Era:** IN (hall rebuilt later).
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** The Fujisawa station anchor and the fork for the Enoshima/Kamakura side roads. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E31. Enoshima Benzaiten (Enoshima) — tidal island south of Fujisawa (side trip, OFF the Tōkaidō)
- **1730:** A wooded rock island joined to the shore at low tide by a sand spit. Shrines to Benzaiten climb the
  slope, with sea caves (Iwaya) at the far end. Edo pleasure-pilgrims came in crowds.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national (Edo tourist favourite).
- **Play:** A tide-gated island (a timed access puzzle) with sea caves. Set: natural site plus 3 small shrines.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E32. Kamakura: Tsurugaoka Hachimangū, the Great Buddha, Tōkei-ji (Kamakura) — side trip south of Totsuka/Fujisawa
- **1730:** The ruined shogun city of 1185–1333, now a farming town dotted with old temples. It had:
  - Tsurugaoka Hachimangū on its hill, with the long Dankazura causeway to the sea
  - the bronze Great Buddha, sitting in the open air (its hall had been gone since 1498) and restored by Yūten from
    1712
  - Tōkei-ji, the "divorce temple", where a wife who fled there could win her divorce
  - Kenchō-ji and Engaku-ji Zen temples
- **Era:** IN. The Buddha is in the open air. (Hachimangū's current main hall is 1828; the 1626 buildings stood in 1730.)
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** A "ruined ancient capital" zone: medieval walls, cliff tombs (yagura caves), and a giant open-air Buddha as
  a landmark you can see from a distance. Set: district, off the main map (or a compressed Buddha only).
- **Src:** Kōtoku-in histories; Tōkei-ji (standard).

### E33. Ōyama Afuri shrine (Ōyama Afuri jinja) — Mount Ōyama, inland from Hiratsuka/Fujisawa (side trip)
- **1730:** A mountain shrine-temple (Ōyama-dera). Edo craftsmen's clubs climbed it every summer carrying huge wooden
  swords. Guide-lodgings (shukubō) line the approach.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** regional to national (Ōyama-mairi was an Edo craze).
- **Play:** A mountain pilgrimage lodge village and a peak shrine: good for a side route. Set: district plus a summit
  shrine.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E34. Shigitatsu-an hermitage (Shigitatsu-an) — Ōiso, station 8
- **1730:** A small thatched poets' hermitage on a creek by the beach, founded in 1664 on the spot of Saigyō's snipe
  poem. Nearby is the Tora-ga-ishi, a stone linked to Tora, the lover of one of the Soga brothers.

- **Fame:** regional (literary).
- **Play:** A quiet coastal set piece; low loot and water. Set: single.

### E35. Sakawa river crossing (Sakawa-gawa no watari) — just east of Odawara
- **1730:** A fast gravel river. In summer porters carried travellers across on their backs or on platforms; in

- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** A seasonal ford chokepoint (a "river is up" server event is possible). Set: two banks plus a porters' hut.

### E36. Odawara castle (Odawara-jō) — station 9
- **1730:** The Ōkubo domain castle guarding the eastern foot of Hakone. It has a three-tier keep, rebuilt in 1706

- **Fame:** national. It was the Hōjō capital, besieged by Hideyoshi in 1590.
- **Play:** A mid-high castle at the Hakone gate. The old Hōjō outer banks are overgrown earthworks, good for a

### E37. Uirō apothecary (Uirō-ya) — Odawara, on the Tōkaidō
- **1730:** The Uirō (Chin) family's medicine shop, in business since Hōjō Sōun invited them in the early 1500s. It
  looks like a small castle: a white-plastered, many-gabled (yatsumune) building on the main street. It sold tōchinkō
  lozenges. The *Uirō-uri* tongue-twister speech, which Danjūrō II made famous in 1718, was a hit in 1730.

== Hakone: the pass, the lake and the checkpoint ==

### E38. Hakone Yumoto and Sōun-ji (Yumoto, Sōun-ji) — the foot of the pass
- **1730:** The first of the Hakone hot springs, with inns along the Hayakawa gorge. Sōun-ji, the Hōjō family temple
  (1521), holds the Hōjō graves; it burned in 1590 and was rebuilt in 1627. The Tōkaidō leaves the river here and
  climbs.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** regional to national (the hot springs).
- **Play:** A hot-spring village (warmth, healing) and the start of the climb. Set: district (small).
- **Src:** Standard.

### E39. The Hakone old road: stone paving and cedar avenue (Hakone hachiri, ishidatami, sugi-namiki) — Yumoto to Hakone and on to Mishima
- **1730:** The "eight ri of Hakone" climb, paved with rough volcanic stones since 1680 to end the knee-deep mud. The
  cedar avenue between Moto-Hakone and Hakone-juku was planted in the 1610s (verify). There are tea stops at Hatajuku
  and at the Amazake-chaya (sweet-sake tea house).
- **Era:** IN. The paving is 1680; the Amazake-chaya existed in the Edo period, but the current building is a modern
  rebuild. Yosegi marquetry, now Hatajuku's craft, is `[not yet in 1730: c.1800s]`.
- **Fame:** national. There was a proverb: "Hakone's eight ri, even a horse can cross; the Ōi can't be crossed"
  (Hakone hachiri wa uma demo kosu ga...).
- **Play:** THE pass road: a stone-paved switchback through dark cedar, with fog, ambush and a tea-house rest stop.
  Set: natural site (a road kit) plus 1–2 tea houses.
- **Src:** Hakone Japan / Hakone navi on the 1680 paving.

### E40. Hakone checkpoint (Hakone sekisho) — south shore of Lake Ashi, between Moto-Hakone and Hakone-juku
- **1730:** Built in 1619. The road is squeezed between Yashiroyama hill and the lake and runs through two gates,
  the Edo-guchi and the Kyō-guchi. Inside are:
  - the main guardhouse (ō-bansho), with officials' seats and weapons racks
  - a foot-soldiers' guardhouse
  - a lookout (tōmi-bansho) on the hill
  - a jail
  Palisades run up the slope and into the lake so nobody can bypass it. Women going out and guns coming in were
  checked hardest.
- **Era:** IN, the 1619 site. It was `[dismantled 1869]`; the present buildings are a 2007 reconstruction from
  records.
- **Fame:** national. It was THE barrier of Japan, and Kaempfer describes passing it (verify: his exact details).
- **Play:** The map's signature chokepoint: lake on one side, hill on the other. It carries high military loot
  (guns, spears, bows) and a jail. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard; the 2007 reconstruction.

### E41. Lake Ashi and Hakone Gongen (Ashi-no-ko, Hakone Gongen) — Moto-Hakone
- **1730:** A crater lake with Fuji rising over its far end. On the wooded west shore is the Hakone Gongen
  shrine-temple (with the bettō temple Kongō-ō-in): cedar-lined stone steps climb to lacquered halls. It was a
  Minamoto/Soga/Tokugawa patron shrine.
- **Era:** IN, as a Shinto-Buddhist complex (separated in 1868). The famous torii standing IN the lake is `[not yet in
  1730: 1952]`.
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** A lake as a water source and boat route; the shrine is a mid-high loot hill. Use the classic "Fuji over
  the lake" skyline composition. Set: compound plus lake.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E42. Moto-Hakone stone Buddhas and the Soga brothers' grave (Moto-Hakone sekibutsu-gun, Soga kyōdai no haka) — the saddle above Lake Ashi
- **1730:** On the bleak volcanic saddle the old road crossed, rock-cut and free-standing Jizō stand by the Shōjin
  pond:
  - the 3.5 m seated Rokudō Jizō (1299)
  - the Hitaki Jizō (1311)
  - 25 bodhisattvas
  - stone pagodas
  Travellers had long likened the place to hell. The big gorintō stupas are the "grave of the Soga brothers", heroes
  of the 1193 revenge story and a kabuki staple.
- **Era:** IN. It was already 400 years old in 1730.
- **Fame:** regional to national (the Soga story).
- **Play:** A haunting landmark on the pass: a giant rock Jizō, fog and a pond, good for stash spots and eerie
  atmosphere. Set: natural site plus carved rocks.
- **Src:** Moto-Hakone Stone Buddhas (Wikipedia).

### E43. Hakone-juku (Hakone-shuku) — station 10, lakeside west of the checkpoint
- **1730:** A post station created in 1618 on bare ground at the lake. It had six honjin (for the daimyo processions
  held here) and a harbour for lake boats.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national (every traveller stopped).
- **Play:** The loot town next to the checkpoint; its honjin are high-mid tier. Set: district (small).
- **Src:** Standard.

### E44. Ōjigoku, "Great Hell" (Ōjigoku; renamed Ōwakudani in 1873) — the crater north of the lake, off the road
- **1730:** A barren crater-side of yellow sulphur, roaring fumaroles and boiling mud above the tree line, with Jizō
  statues and a path. It was avoided by ordinary travellers; ascetics and sulphur-gatherers used it.
- **Era:** IN, under this name. The black eggs (kuro-tamago) are a modern custom (verify).
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** A toxic-gas hazard zone (a DayZ contaminated area in period dress), with sulphur for gunpowder crafting.
  It is the volcano's signature. Set: natural site.
- **Src:** Standard; renaming date is standard.

### E45. The Seven Hot Springs of Hakone (Hakone nanayu: Yumoto, Tōnosawa, Miyanoshita, Dōgashima, Sokokura, Kiga, Ashinoyu)
- **1730:** Hot-spring hamlets strung along the Hayakawa and Ashinoyu. They had cure-inns for multi-week stays
  (tōji), bathing halls over the springs, and a local wood-turning craft (Hakone-zaiku).
- **Era:** IN. (The famous Western hotel, Fujiya at Miyanoshita, is `[not yet in 1730: 1878]`.)
- **Fame:** national (tōji destination).
- **Play:** Healing and warmth sites: a string of small onsen villages off the main road. Set: 2–3 small clusters.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E46. Hakone aqueduct tunnel (Hakone yōsui / Fukara yōsui) — through the crater rim at Kojiri, the lake's west side
- **1730:** A 1.3 km hand-dug tunnel (1666–1670) that draws Lake Ashi water through the crater wall to irrigate the
  Susono plain in Suruga. It has a stone-lined intake and a small shrine.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** local.
- **Play:** A secret tunnel through the mountain: a hidden route or stash under the rim. Set: natural site plus

### E47. Yamanaka castle ruins (Yamanaka-jō ato) — on the Tōkaidō, west side of the Hakone pass
- **1730:** The overgrown earthworks of the Hōjō mountain castle that fell to Hideyoshi's army in a single day in
  1590. It shows grass-covered baileys, and the famous "waffle" moats (shōji-bori): ridged dry moats like a grid of

### E48. Mishima Taisha (Mishima Taisha) — station 11, Mishima

### E51. Shuzen-ji hot spring (Shuzen-ji) — central Izu (off the main road)

### E52. Nirayama intendant's office (Nirayama daikansho, Egawa-tei) — Nirayama, Izu

### E53. Senbon Matsubara pine beach (Senbon Matsubara) — Numazu to Hara
- **1730:** About 10 km of black pines along the Suruga bay shore, with Fuji behind. The pines were replanted in the

### E54. Shōin-ji and Hakuin (Shōin-ji) — Hara, station 13
- **1730:** A poor little Rinzai temple in Hara post town where the Zen master Hakuin Ekaku (1686–1769) had lived

### E55. Ukishima-ga-hara marsh and the "left-hand Fuji" (Ukishima-ga-hara, hidari Fuji) — Hara to Yoshiwara
- **1730:** Low reed marsh behind the dunes, with Fuji towering and reflected in the water. Yoshiwara station (twice

### E56. Mount Fuji: summit crater, pilgrim huts and the Hōei crater (Fuji-san, Hōei-zan) — the skyline
- **1730:** The volcano dominates the whole road from Hakone to Satta.
  - Pilgrims climb in summer by the Murayama (south), Subashiri (east) and Yoshida (north) routes, through stations

  - East of it the Subashiri side is still under black scoria.

### E57. Fujisan Hongū Sengen Taisha and Wakutama pond (Fujisan Hongū Sengen Taisha) — Ōmiya (Fujinomiya), inland from Yoshiwara

### E58. Murayama shugen base (Murayama Sengen, Murayama Kōbō) — on Fuji's south slope above Ōmiya
- **1730:** The mountain-ascetic (yamabushi) headquarters for the southern Murayama route: guide houses, a Dainichi

### E59. Fuji river ferry (Fujikawa no watashi) — between Yoshiwara and Kanbara (Iwabuchi)

== The Ōi river to Arai: Tōtōmi ==

### E69. Ōi river crossing office and porters (Ōigawa kawa-kaisho, kawagoshi) — Shimada (station 23) to Kanaya (station 24)
- **1730:** The widest unbridged, unferried river on the road: about 1 km of gravel channels. There is no ferry, by
  shogunal policy. Travellers bought tickets at the crossing office (from 1696) and were carried by numbered porters,

  (kawadome), and thousands were stuck in Shimada and Kanaya for days. The porters' barracks rows stand behind the

- **Era:** IN (office from 1696).
- **Fame:** national. The proverb says "...kosu ni kosarenu Ōigawa", "the Ōi you can't cross".
- **Play:** The map's great river barrier: fording is slow and exposed; "kawadome" makes the river impassable in a
  storm event. Set: compound (office plus porter rows) plus a wide gravel riverbed.

### E75. Hamamatsu castle and the Mikatagahara battlefield (Hamamatsu-jō, Mikatagahara kosenjō) — station 29
- **1730:** Ieyasu's castle of 1570–86: stone walls of rough "nozura" masonry, with a two-storey turret in the
  second bailey doing duty as a keep. North of town is the Mikatagahara plateau, where Takeda Shingen crushed Ieyasu
  in 1573.
- **Era:** Castle IN with no true keep (records suggest none was built). The concrete keep is 1958.
- **Fame:** regional (the battle is national).
- **Play:** A modest castle plus an open battlefield plateau with grave mounds. Set: compound plus natural site.
- **Src:** Hamamatsu Castle (Wikipedia).

### E76. Imagiri ferry across Hamana (Imagiri no watashi) — Maisaka (station 30) to Arai (station 31)
- **1730:** A one-ri boat crossing over the mouth of Lake Hamana, a gap torn open by the 1498 earthquake and tsunami.
  It lands directly at the Arai checkpoint.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national (the only lake/sea ferry before Miya).
- **Play:** A boat-only approach to a checkpoint: a water chokepoint. Set: landings.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E77. Arai checkpoint (Arai sekisho / Imagiri sekisho) — Arai, station 31
- **1730:** A checkpoint on the lake shore with its own boat landing: every boat from Maisaka docks inside the
  barrier. It has an inspection hall with raised seats, guardrooms and a women-inspecting room. It was notorious for
  inspecting women (the searchers were women). The site moved in 1701 after storm and tsunami, and again in 1708
  after the Hōei tsunami.
- **Era:** IN (the 1708 buildings). The survivor is the 1855–58 rebuild, the only original sekisho building left in
  Japan, so the form is authentic.
- **Fame:** national. Kaempfer notes it (verify).
- **Play:** The second big checkpoint, water-fronted. It pairs with the "Himekaidō" bypass (E78). Set: compound plus
  landing.
- **Src:** Arai Barrier (Wikipedia); Guidoor.

### E78. The Princess Road bypass (Hime-kaidō / Honsaka-dōri) — north around Lake Hamana
- **1730:** A slower inland loop over the Honsaka pass that avoided both the Imagiri ferry and Arai. Women travellers
  used it to dodge the Arai search, hence the name.
- **Era:** IN (the name is later, verify).
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** The smugglers' route: a designed alternate path around a chokepoint. Set: natural site (road).
- **Src:** Standard.

== Mikawa: Shirasuka to Chiryū ==

### E79. Shiomi-zaka (Shiomi-zaka) — Shirasuka, station 32
- **1730:** A slope where the road climbs a bluff and suddenly shows the open Pacific ("tide-seeing slope"). The
  station itself was moved uphill after the 1707 tsunami.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** regional (a famous sea view).
- **Play:** A coastal viewpoint; a tsunami-lore location. Set: natural site.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E80. Yoshida castle and Yoshida bridge (Yoshida-jō, Yoshida-ōhashi / Toyobashi) — Yoshida (Toyohashi), station 34
- **1730:** A river-bend castle with no keep but three three-storey turrets over the Toyo river. Below it is one of
  the Tōkaidō's great wooden bridges, about 220 m (verify). Toyokawa Inari is a side trip.
- **Era:** IN. No keep; the 1954 concrete turret is modern.
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** A castle and long-bridge chokepoint over a big river. Set: compound plus bridge.
- **Src:** Yoshida Castle (Wikipedia).

### E81. Goyu pine avenue and Akasaka's Ōhashiya inn (Goyu no matsu-namiki, Ōhashiya) — Goyu (35) and Akasaka (36)
- **1730:**
  - About 1.5 km of big black pines line the road between two stations only 1.7 km apart; the avenue was planted
    from 1604.
  - Akasaka is lined with inns whose serving-women dragged guests in off the street.
  - The Ōhashiya inn opened in 1649 and its building DATES FROM 1716.
- **Era:** IN. The Ōhashiya building of 1716 stood until the inn closed in 2015.
- **Fame:** regional (notorious touts).
- **Play:** A real, datable 1716 inn floor plan to copy. Set: road plus single inn.
- **Src:** Akasaka-juku (Wikipedia).

### E82. Okazaki castle and the 27 bends (Okazaki-jō, Nijūnana-magari) — Okazaki, station 38
- **1730:** Ieyasu's birthplace castle (born 1543), with a three-storey keep (1617) above the Sugō and Yahagi rivers.
  The Tōkaidō zigzags through the castle town in 27 deliberate right-angle turns for defence. Hacchō miso makers
  are on the west edge (Hacchō village).
- **Era:** Keep IN (the 1617 keep). It was `[demolished 1873]`; the present keep is a 1959 concrete copy.
- **Fame:** national (Ieyasu's birth).
- **Play:** A castle plus a maze-like town road, good for urban ambush. Set: compound plus district.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E83. Yahagi bridge (Yahagi-bashi) — west of Okazaki
- **1730:** The longest bridge on the Tōkaidō, about 208 ken (roughly 370 m) of wooden trestle over the Yahagi. The
  legend says the young Hideyoshi met the bandit Hachisuka Koroku here.
- **Era:** IN (repeatedly rebuilt; this is the Edo-period form).
- **Fame:** national (the longest bridge).
- **Play:** A great exposed river-crossing chokepoint. Set: single long bridge.
- **Src:** Standard; the length is from later guides (verify).

### E84. Daiju-ji (Daiju-ji) — north of Okazaki
- **1730:** The Matsudaira/Tokugawa family temple. It holds life-size memorial tablets of the shoguns, cut to each
  shogun's height (verify). A sightline was kept open from the gate to Okazaki castle.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** A high-tier family temple with a castle sightline. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E85. Chiryū horse fair and Yatsuhashi (Chiryū uma-ichi, Yatsuhashi) — Chiryū, station 39
- **1730:** Each spring the big horse fair filled the fields with hundreds of horses. Nearby is Yatsuhashi, the
  zig-zag plank bridges and irises of *Tales of Ise*, by then marshy fields with a memorial.
- **Era:** IN. (Muryōju-ji's iris garden is `[not yet in 1730: 1812]`, verify.)
- **Fame:** regional (the horse fair); national as a poetic name (Yatsuhashi).
- **Play:** A seasonal horse source; the zig-zag bridge over marsh is a cute set. Set: natural site.
- **Src:** Standard.

== Owari: Narumi, Miya, Nagoya and the sea crossing ==

### E86. Arimatsu tie-dye street (Arimatsu shibori) — between Chiryū and Narumi
- **1730:** A merchant village of big dye houses selling shibori tie-dyed cotton, founded in 1608. It was a fixed
  souvenir stop on the road.
- **Era:** IN. (Today's street is largely rebuilt after an 1784 fire, verify.)
- **Fame:** national (the souvenir).
- **Play:** A cloth-loot street. Set: small district.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E87. Okehazama battlefield (Okehazama kosenjō) — near Arimatsu/Narumi
- **1730:** The low wooded hollow where Oda Nobunaga surprised and killed Imagawa Yoshimoto in 1560, with a few
  grave mounds and markers.
- **Era:** IN, as a site (the monuments are mostly later, verify).
- **Fame:** national (the battle).
- **Play:** A battlefield zone: mounds, rusted gear, rare old-weapon loot. Set: natural site.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E88. Atsuta shrine (Atsuta jingū) — Miya, station 41
- **1730:** One of the greatest shrines in Japan, keeper of the Kusanagi sword (one of the three imperial regalia).
  Its halls were in the local owari-zukuri style in a vast camphor grove. It keeps the Nobunaga-bei, a thick
  tile-and-earth wall Nobunaga gave after Okehazama, once about 400 m long.
- **Era:** REBUILT. What stood in 1730 was the owari-zukuri complex; it was rebuilt in the Ise (shinmei) style in
  1893. The Nobunaga-bei survives in part.
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** A top-tier shrine in a forest, with a signature wall. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Atsuta Shrine (Wikipedia).

### E89. Miya ferry landing and the Seven-Ri Crossing (Miya no watashi, Shichiri no watashi) — Miya to Kuwana
- **1730:** The largest post town on the Tōkaidō by inn count, built around a harbour. There is a stone lantern-tower
  (jōyatō) on the quay and a time-bell tower. Big passenger boats sailed about 7 ri (28 km) across Ise bay to Kuwana:
  half a day, tide and weather permitting, with wrecks not unknown. The inland alternative is the Saya road with a
  3-ri river boat.
- **Era:** IN. (The lantern and bell tower on site today are reconstructions, verify.)
- **Fame:** national. It was the Tōkaidō's only sea leg, and Kaempfer took it (verify).
- **Play:** The map's sea-gate: a port hub where boats can "fast-travel" across the bay, or the long way round.
  Set: harbour district.
- **Src:** Standard.

### E90. Nagoya castle (Nagoya-jō) — Nagoya, north of Miya (just off the Tōkaidō)
- **1730:** The Owari Tokugawa's giant castle (1610–12). A five-storey keep is crowned by golden shachi (dolphin-tiger
  finials), with the Honmaru palace below. In 1730 the new lord Tokugawa Muneharu is openly defying Yoshimune's
  austerity, building theatres and pleasure quarters: Nagoya is a boom town.
- **Era:** Keep IN (the 1612 keep). It `[burned 1945]`; the present keep is 1959 concrete.
- **Fame:** national. The saying goes "Owari Nagoya is held up by its castle" (Owari Nagoya wa shiro de motsu).
- **Play:** The map's most spectacular standing keep, with the golden shachi as the ultimate loot trophy. Set:
  compound, or district with the boom town.
- **Src:** Tokugawa Muneharu (Wikipedia: tenure 1730–39).

== Ise: Kuwana to the Suzuka pass ==

### E91. Kuwana castle and landing (Kuwana-jō, Kuwana no watashi-guchi) — Kuwana, station 42
- **1730:** A water castle at the mouth of the Ibi and Nagara rivers. The boats from Miya land right under its walls.
  Kuwana is famous for grilled clams (yaki-hamaguri).
- **Era:** Castle IN, but the keep is `[gone by 1730: burned 1701, rebuilding refused]`. The big "first torii of Ise"
  at the landing is `[not yet in 1730: probably 1797, verify]`.
- **Fame:** national (the clams, the landing).
- **Play:** The western sea-gate with a keepless water castle; food at the clam stalls. Set: compound plus landing.
- **Src:** Kuwana Castle (Wikipedia).

### E92. Hinaga fork to Ise (Hinaga no oiwake) — south of Yokkaichi, station 43
- **1730:** The fork where the Ise pilgrims' road leaves the Tōkaidō, marked by a signpost stone. During the 1705
  mass pilgrimage (okage-mairi), millions passed this way.
- **Era:** IN. The torii over the fork is `[not yet in 1730: 1774, verify]`.
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** A junction and pilgrim traffic, with a route to the Ise side zone. Set: single (signpost, tea house).
- **Src:** Standard.

### E93. Kameyama castle (Kameyama-jō) — Kameyama, station 46
- **1730:** A hill castle whose keep was demolished BY MISTAKE in 1632: the shogunate had ordered work on the other
  Kameyama (in Tanba), and the builder took the wrong one. Rebuilding was refused. A long tamon-yagura (1636) stands
  on the keep base instead.
- **Era:** Castle IN; keep `[gone by 1730: 1632]`. The tamon-yagura survives.
- **Fame:** regional (the blunder is a good story).
- **Play:** A castle with a comic history. Set: compound.
- **Src:** Kameyama Castle (Mie) (Wikipedia).

### E94. Seki-juku and Seki Jizō-in (Seki-juku, Seki Jizō-in) — Seki, station 47
- **1730:** A long, intact post town at the foot of the Suzuka pass, where the roads to Ise and Iga branch off. Its
  centre is Jizō-in: a Jizō temple claimed to date from 741, with a big main hall (c.1700, verify), a bell tower and
  an Aizen hall. A folk song mentions the Jizō in a festive long-sleeved robe.
- **Era:** IN. Seki is the best-preserved Tōkaidō townscape today, a model for the kit.
- **Fame:** regional to national.
- **Play:** A full post-town template: honjin, inns and a temple. Set: district.
- **Src:** Japan Travel (Seki-juku); standard.

### E95. Suzuka pass and the Mirror Rock (Suzuka-tōge, Kagami-iwa) — between Sakashita (station 48) and Tsuchiyama (station 49)
- **1730:**
  - A steep, forested pass on the Ise/Ōmi border, notorious since antiquity for bandits.
  - The Kagami-iwa is a polished rock face in which, legend says, bandits watched travellers coming.
  - There is a shrine to the demon-slaying general Sakanoue no Tamuramaro.
  - Sakashita below had honjin and dozens of inns.
  - A song goes: "Sakashita is sunny, Suzuka is cloudy, and in Tsuchiyama in between, it rains."
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national (bandits, the song).
- **Play:** The canonical bandit pass: an ambush chokepoint with a lookout rock. Set: natural site plus a pass shrine.
- **Src:** Standard.

== Ōmi: Tsuchiyama to Ōtsu (boundary with LM-W) ==

### E96. Tamura shrine (Tamura jinja) — Tsuchiyama, station 49
- **1730:** A shrine to Sakanoue no Tamuramaro in a riverside grove, where he is said to have subdued the Suzuka
  bandits/demon.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** regional.
- **Play:** A mid shrine on the rainy side of the pass. Set: compound (small).
- **Src:** Standard.

### E97. Minakuchi castle and town (Minakuchi-jō) — Minakuchi, station 50
- **1730:** A small square-moated castle, built in 1634 as a shogun's lodging on the road to Kyoto and a domain seat
  from 1682 (verify). The town is laid out as three parallel streets and sells dried gourd strips (kanpyō).
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** local.
- **Play:** A small low-tier "castle lodge". Set: compound.
- **Src:** Standard (verify).

### E98. Kusatsu honjin and the Tōkaidō/Nakasendō junction (Kusatsu-juku honjin, Kusatsu oiwake) — Kusatsu, station 52
- **1730:** The two great roads meet here and share the last stretch to Kyoto. The Tanaka family's honjin is the
  largest surviving honjin, with daimyo guest registers (verify: the rebuild date after the 1718 fire). There are
  Uba-ga-mochi rice-cake tea houses.
- **Era:** IN. The present stone signpost "right Tōkaidō, left Nakasendō" is `[not yet in 1730: 1816]`.
- **Fame:** national (the junction).
- **Play:** THE route-merge point: whoever holds Kusatsu holds both roads. The honjin is a high-tier lodging.
  Set: compound plus junction. (Shared with LM-W's Nakasendō end.)
- **Src:** Standard.

### E99. Seta long bridge (Seta no Karahashi) — over the Seta river, between Kusatsu and Ōtsu
- **1730:** The only bridge out of Lake Biwa, in two spans (the long bridge, roughly 170 m, and a short bridge of
  about 50 m) linked by a mid-river island. It is one of the Eight Views of Ōmi. The saying goes: "who controls
  Seta controls the realm".
- **Era:** IN (repeatedly rebuilt wooden bridge).
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** The final great chokepoint before Ōtsu and Kyoto. Set: bridge plus island. (It is on the lake edge, so
  confirm it with LM-W.)
- **Src:** Standard (the span lengths are approximate, verify).

### E100. Ōtsu, the end of this slice (Ōtsu-juku) — station 53
- **1730:** The biggest Tōkaidō station and a lake port: shogunal rice warehouses, the boat landings, the Ōtsu-e
  folk-painting shops at Oiwake.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** A hand-off point. LM-W owns the town, Mii-dera, Ishiyama-dera and the Ōsaka barrier to Kyoto. Listed
  here only as the endpoint.
- **Src:** Standard.

== Side trip: Ise (OFF the main map) ==

### E101. Ise Grand Shrines (Ise Jingū: Naikū and Gekū) — Ise, 2–3 days south of the Hinaga fork
- **1730:** Two great forest shrines, the Outer Shrine at Yamada and the Inner Shrine at Uji, joined by an
  inns-and-pleasure road over the Ai-no-yama hill (Furuichi). Plain thatched cypress halls stand inside rings of
  fences. The halls were REBUILT IN 1729 (the 20-year renewal), so in 1730 they are a year old, gleaming raw wood,
  with the old site bare beside them. The Uji bridge leads to the Inner Shrine.
- **Era:** IN (the 1729 renewal).
- **Fame:** national, the supreme pilgrimage. 1705's okage-mairi drew millions.
- **Play:** If it is ever built: the pilgrimage end-point, a spiritual zone, low loot, sacred. Set: district.
- **Src:** The 20-year cycle counted back from 2013 (62nd): 1729 is a renewal year.

### E102. Futami's Wedded Rocks (Futami, Meoto-iwa) — the coast near Ise
- **1730:** Two sea rocks tied with a thick straw rope, with the sun rising between them at the solstice. Pilgrims
  purified themselves in the sea here before Ise.
- **Era:** IN.
- **Fame:** national.
- **Play:** A coastal natural landmark. Set: natural site.
- **Src:** Standard.

# Top 15 for a condensed map

1. **Hakone checkpoint (E40)**: The signature PvP chokepoint, squeezed between the lake and the hill, with high
   military loot. It is the reason the map has a pass at all.
2. **Edo Castle's empty keep base (E01)**: A capital fortress whose centrepiece is a granite platform with no keep.
   It is period-exact, memorable, and a natural sniper throne.
3. **Mount Fuji with the Hōei crater and pilgrim huts (E56)**: The skyline itself. The 1707 crater and ash fields give
   a fresh-eruption wasteland only 23 years old.
4. **Ōi river crossing (E69)**: A 1 km unbridged, ferry-less river crossed by porters, with "river closed" events.
   A unique map-splitting barrier.
5. **Kodenmachō jail (E05)**: The prison analogue: a walled compound with one gate, cell blocks and a guard office.
6. **Nihonbashi with the notice boards and fish market (E02, E03)**: The zero point of the map and a crowded hub.
   Bundle them as one site.
7. **Kunōzan Tōshōgū (E63)**: A gilded clifftop shrine with one 1,159-step stair: the best defensive loot set on the
   coast.
8. **Arai checkpoint plus the Hime-kaidō bypass (E77, E78)**: A second checkpoint that works differently (a
   water-fronted boat landing), plus a designed smuggler route.
9. **Ōjigoku (E44)**: A sulphur and toxic-gas hazard zone at the volcano, with a gunpowder resource.
10. **Yoshiwara (E14)**: A walled, moated district with one gate at the end of a lone dike: a loot box and a trap.
11. **Nagoya castle (E90)**: The one great standing keep with golden shachi on the road side of the map, where Edo's
    and Sunpu's keeps are gone.
12. **Satta pass (E60)**: The most famous view on the Tōkaidō, on a cliff chokepoint with a risky tide-path below.
13. **Miya harbour and the Seven-Ri Crossing (E89)**: The sea-gate; boats as fast travel versus the long Saya road.
    Pair it with the Atsuta grove (E88).
14. **Suzuka pass and the Mirror Rock (E95)**: The canonical bandit pass, with a built-in lookout legend.
15. **Moto-Hakone stone Buddhas and the Soga grave (E42)**: A giant 1299 rock-cut Jizō in a fog-bound volcanic saddle.
    Cheap to build and unforgettable. (Runner-up: Yamanaka's waffle-moat ruins, E47.)

Next tier, if there is room: Sengaku-ji's 47 graves (E23), Takanawa Ōkido (E24), Kan'ei-ji / Ueno (E10), Odawara
castle (E36), Yahagi bridge (E83), Kusatsu junction (E98), Mariko's Chōjiya (E67).

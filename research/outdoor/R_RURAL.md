# Outdoor list, rural man-made: everything in and around villages and farms, outside the buildings

Research agent OD-R, 2026-09-29. Text only. Companion to `research/buildings/BUILDING_LIST.md` (BL). Anchor year 1730;
1680–1750 fully in; wider Edo items are listed but tagged. Deliberately over-detailed: Stephen collapses it later.

**Entry format.** `- **Name (romaji)**: what it is. Setting, how common, size. Hook: ... Src: ...`
- **Setting:** yard / field / commons (village commons, roadside, temple graveyard) / shrine / shore.
- **How common:** common / occasional / rare, in a 1730 village of the kind where it belongs (a salt-field item is
  "common" in a salt village, not on the whole map).
- **Size:** small prop / medium / large / `[terrain]` (landform or ground treatment, stage T).
- **Tags** as in BL: `[outside 1680-1750: date]`, `[off-map: region]`, `(assumed)`, `(verify)`. `BL:` points at the
  building list section that already has a related entry. Pointer lines in italics are not counted.
- **Loot rule (BL, binding):** nothing here is a loot container. Mats, racks and bales are surfaces loot can lie on.

**Sources used (short names).** NGZ = Miyazaki Yasusada, *Nōgyō Zensho* (1697). WKS = *Wakan Sansai Zue* (1712).
Aizu Nōsho (1684). Kubota "Tanbo" rice history pages. ja.wikipedia (千歯扱き, 千石どおし, 賽銭, 火の見櫓, 屋敷林, しし垣,

Settsu city leaflet (千石通し in WKS 1712). Anything not in these is marked `(assumed)` or `(verify)`.

## == 1. Rice paddies and paddy landforms ==

- **Flat paddy (heichi no ta / hira-ta)**: flooded plots on the plain, irregular in shape and size in 1730; the neat
  rectangular grid is a Meiji land-replotting product. Field, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: water (muddy, not drinkable),
  slow wading, wide open sightlines. Src: grid = Kōchi Seiri law 1899 (verify date).
- **Stone-walled terraced paddy (tanada, ishizumi)**: small paddies stepped up a hillside behind dry-stone walls,
  built by families over generations; mountain villages and valley sides. Field, common in hills, `[terrain]` + large
  walls. Hook: climbing route, cover behind walls, landmark views.
- **Earth-bunded terraced paddy (dozumi tanada)**: cheaper terraces with grassed earth risers instead of stone.
  Field, common in hills, `[terrain]`. Hook: grassy slopes are fast but exposed.
- **Wet deep-mud paddy (shitsuden / fukada / numata)**: undrainable valley-bottom and delta paddies with knee- to
  waist-deep mud, worked with paddy boats and mud boards; one crop a year only. Field, occasional, `[terrain]`. Hook:
  very slow movement, stamina drain, a real trap in a chase.
- **Paddy bund (aze / kuro)**: the raised earth path, 30–60 cm wide, that divides and holds each paddy; re-plastered
  with mud every spring (aze-nuri). Field, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: the only fast path across paddy country.
- **Bund-top soybeans (aze-mame)**: soybeans planted in a line along the bund tops to use every foot of land. Field,
  common, small prop (plants). Hook: food (forage in autumn). (assumed; common practice in Edo farm manuals, verify)
- **Seedling bed (nawashiro)**: a small, carefully levelled plot near the house or the water inlet where rice is
  sown thick in spring before transplanting. Field, common (spring), `[terrain]` small. Hook: season marker; food
  nothing. See minakuchi offering (§14).
- **Double-cropped paddy with winter barley ridges (nimōsaku-den)**: in Kinai the drained paddy grows barley or
  wheat on ridges over winter, flooded again in early summer. Field, common in Kinai, `[terrain]` (seasonal variant).
  Hook: food (barley), a second paddy look for winter. Src: NGZ (Kinai double cropping) (verify).
- **Harvested stubble paddy (inakabu no ta)**: autumn-winter paddy of cut stubble rows, dry or half-dry. Field,
  common (seasonal), `[terrain]`. Hook: walkable in autumn, the season swap for paddy ground.
- **Raised strip fields with creeks (horiage-ta / kuriku / hori-ta)**: in low deltas, soil was dug out to raise
  strips for rice or crops, leaving narrow creeks between them used by small boats. Field, occasional (Osaka lowland,
  Kiso delta, Kanto river lowlands), `[terrain]`. Hook: boat routes, water everywhere, maze-like. (verify for Osaka)
- **Ring dyke (wajū tei)**: an embankment enclosing a village and its fields against floods, Kiso–Nagara–Ibi delta
  (Mino/Owari). Field, rare (regional), `[terrain]` large. Hook: landmark, high dry route. BL: flood farmhouse.
- **Sea dyke of reclaimed land (shinden tsutsumi / shio-dome)**: earth-and-stone bank holding back the sea or a
  lagoon for new paddy; the Kyōhō reforms (1720s) pushed exactly this kind of reclamation. Shore/field, occasional,
  `[terrain]` large. Hook: long straight high route along the coast. Src: Kyōhō shinden kaihatsu (1722 notice) (verify).
- **River levee with planted bamboo (tsutsumi / dote, suibō-rin)**: earth levees along rivers, often planted with
  bamboo whose roots bind the soil in floods. Field, common along rivers, `[terrain]` + bamboo. Hook: high ground,
  cover, bamboo. (assumed practice, verify period)
- **Open levee with gaps (kasumi-tei)**: overlapping, broken levees that let floodwater spread back out slowly; the
  Takeda (Kōshū) method, still used in the Edo period. Field, occasional (Kōshū, Kanto), `[terrain]`. Hook: odd
  levee geometry, landmark. (verify)
- **Lotus-root field (hasu-da / renkon-ta)**: flooded plots of lotus grown for the root, flowers in summer. Field,
  occasional (Kinai, Owari lowlands), `[terrain]` + plants. Hook: food. (verify period)
- **Taro wet field (ta-imo, mizu-imo)**: taro grown in wet plots in some regions. Field, occasional, `[terrain]`.
  Hook: food. (assumed)
- **Rush field for tatami (igusa-da)**: flooded fields of soft rush. Field, rare, `[terrain]`. Hook: crafting
  (mats). `[off-map: Bingo/Bitchū; some Ōmi]` (verify)

## == 2. Irrigation and water control ==

- **Main irrigation channel (yōsui / segi / ide)**: an earth or stone-lined channel from a weir, threading through the
  village and past the houses; it also served for washing. Field/yard, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: water (boil first),
  the spine of village layout. BL: §2.1.
- **Field ditch and drain (mizo / hori / haisui)**: small earth ditches along plots. Field, common, `[terrain]`. Hook:
  shallow water, cover when crouched.
- **Stone-lined village channel (ishi-gumi mizo)**: a channel walled with river stones where it passes the houses,
  with a step down to the water at each house. Yard/commons, common, `[terrain]` + stone. Hook: water, washing.
- **Wooden sluice gate (hi / hi-guchi / suimon)**: sluice boards slid up and down in a timber or stone frame at a
  channel intake. Field, common, medium. Hook: interactable (open/close water), landmark. BL: Civic, water gate.
- **Paddy water notch (mizu-kuchi / mizu-jiri)**: a cut in the bund with a board, sod or straw plug to let water in
  or out of one paddy. Field, common, small prop. Hook: detail only.
- **Water divider (bunsui)**: a board or stone set in a channel with notches of fixed widths so each village or
  field gets its share; the object of fierce water disputes. Field, occasional, small–medium. Hook: lore (water
  quarrels), landmark. (assumed form)
- **Incense water-clock (senkō-mizu)**: each farmer's turn at the shared water was timed by burning incense sticks;
  a prop at a sluice or water-guard hut. Field, rare, small prop. Hook: lore. Src: Sanuki custom (verify date).
  BL: water guard hut.
- **Irrigation pond (tame-ike)**: an earth dam across a small valley holding water for dry-season paddies; many in
  Kinai and Sanuki; Mannō-ike was rebuilt in 1631. Field, common in Kinai, `[terrain]` large. Hook: water, fish;
  the autumn drain-down (ike-hoshi) when villagers catch the carp and crucian is a good event. BL: Civic, reservoir.
- **Pond outlet with plug tower (soko-hi / tate-hi)**: a wooden or stone outlet tower in the pond, opened by pulling
  plugs one by one. Field, occasional, medium. Hook: interactable, landmark. (assumed form; verify name)
- **Pond spillway (yosui-bake)**: a cut in the dam crest for floodwater, lined with stone. Field, occasional,
  `[terrain]`. (assumed; name verify)
- **River weir, stone and timber (seki / iseki)**: a weir across the river turning water into the channel. Field,
  common, large. Hook: crossing point, fish, noise. BL: Civic, weir.
- **Brushwood-and-stake weir (shiba-zeki / kui-zeki)**: a cheap weir of stakes and bundled brush, rebuilt after each
  flood. Field, common, medium. Hook: crossing, crafting (stakes). (assumed)
- **Bamboo gabions (jakago)**: long cylindrical bamboo baskets filled with stones, laid along banks and around
  weirs. Field, common along rivers, medium. Hook: cover, stones and bamboo.
- **Timber river-crib spurs (seigyū / waku)**: tripod or box frames of timber weighted with stones, set in rivers to
  turn the current. Field, occasional, medium. Hook: cover in water, landmark. (verify names and period)
- **Bamboo pipe (kakehi / kakei)**: split bamboo on forked sticks carrying spring water to a house or trough.
  Yard/field, common in hills, small–medium. Hook: clean water source. BL: §2.3 mountain village.
- **Wooden flume on trestles (toi / kakehi)**: a board trough carried across a gully or road on posts. Field,
  occasional, medium. Hook: landmark, climbable. BL: Civic, aqueduct bridge (the city version).
- **Irrigation tunnel (manbo / mabu / zuidō)**: hand-dug tunnel bringing water through a ridge; the Hakone Yōsui
  (finished 1670) takes Lake Ashi water under the caldera rim to the Susono plain. Field, rare, `[terrain]` large +
  portal. Hook: landmark, hidden route. Src: Hakone Yōsui 1666–70 (verify exact dates). Relevant to the Hakone test DEM.
- **Treadwheel (fumi-guruma)**: small paddle wheel trodden by a man to lift water from a ditch into the paddy; in
  use from the 1660s (Osaka). Field, common in Kinai lowlands, medium. Hook: interactable. BL: Civic, treadwheel.
- **River lift wheel (agemizu-guruma / mizu-guruma)**: a current-driven wheel with buckets or tubes lifting water
  into a high channel; the Yodo castle wheel on the Yodo river was a famous sight. Field, occasional, large. Hook:
  landmark, sound. (Yodo wheel date: verify)
- **Dragon-backbone chain pump (ryūkotsu-sha)**: Chinese-style chain of paddles in a trough; used in early Edo but
  replaced by the treadwheel by the late 1600s. Field, rare, medium. `[outside 1680-1750: mostly before 1680]` (verify)
- **Field sweep (hane-tsurube, field version)**: the counterweighted lever well used to lift water from a ditch or
  pit into a higher field. Field, common, medium. Hook: water.
- **Two-person swing bucket (furi-oke; name verify)**: a bucket on two ropes swung by two people to throw ditch water
  up into a paddy. Field, common, small prop. Hook: lore. (assumed name)
- **Spring and spring pool (izumi / shimizu)**: a village spring with a stone surround and a dipper, often with a
  tiny Suijin stone. Commons, common, small–medium. Hook: clean water.
- **Stepping stones and slab bridge over a channel (tobi-ishi / ishi-bashi)**: stones or a single slab across a
  ditch. Field/yard, common, small. Hook: crossing. BL: Civic, bridges (for plank and log bridges).
- **Paddy boat (ta-bune)**: a small flat punt for moving sheaves, seedlings and manure through deep paddies and
  delta creeks. Field, occasional (deltas), medium. Hook: vehicle in creek country, wood.
- *Also relevant, not counted: public wash place (arai-ba), Ōmi kabata, water guard hut, flood boat under the eaves
  (age-bune): all in BL.*

## == 3. Dry fields, kitchen gardens, orchards and cash crops ==

- **Dry field with ridges (hata / hatake, une)**: upland fields ridged for barley, millet, beans, buckwheat and
  vegetables. Field, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: food (seasonal forage).
- **Kitchen garden plot (saien / kado-batake / senzai-batake)**: the family plot beside or behind the house: daikon,
  eggplant, cucumber, negi, burdock, taro, gourds, greens. Yard, common, `[terrain]` small. Hook: food.
- **Eggplant and cucumber stakes (nasu-bō, kyūri-dana)**: stakes and a low cross-pole frame for climbing vines.
  Yard, common, small prop. Hook: food.
- **Gourd trellis (yūgao-dana / hyōtan-dana)**: a flat overhead trellis of bamboo by the house with bottle gourds
  or moonflower gourds hanging; kanpyō strips come from the moonflower gourd (brought to Mibu, Shimotsuke, 1712).
  Yard, common, medium. Hook: shade, food, a gourd water flask to craft. Src: Mibu kanpyō 1712 (verify).
- **Loofah trellis (hechima-dana)**: loofah vines on a trellis, for scrubbers and "loofah water". Yard, occasional,
  medium. Hook: crafting. (verify date for Edo commoners)
- **Bean poles (sasage / ingen supports)**: tall poles or tepees for runner beans. Yard/field, common, small–medium.
  Hook: food. (Ingen bean brought 1654 by the monk Ingen: legend, verify)
- **Taro patch (sato-imo)**: big-leaved taro in dry-field rows. Yard/field, common, `[terrain]` + plants. Hook: food.
- **Daikon field (daikon-batake)**: long white radishes in rows; Nerima daikon near Edo (Tsunayoshi legend). Field,
  common, `[terrain]`. Hook: food.
- **Greens and leaf mustard (na / komatsuna)**: winter greens near towns. Field, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: food.
  (Komatsuna naming legend: Yoshimune, 1719; verify)
- **Onion rows (negi)**: Senju and Kujō negi near Edo and Kyoto. Field, common near towns, `[terrain]`. Hook: food.
- **Burdock rows (gobō)**: deep-rooted burdock, a Japanese-only crop. Field, occasional, `[terrain]`. Hook: food.
  (assumed)
- **Buckwheat field (soba-batake)**: on poor upland, white-flowered in late summer. Field, common in hills,
  `[terrain]`. Hook: food, landmark colour.
- **Millet fields (awa / hie / kibi)**: foxtail, barnyard and proso millet, staples of the poor and of hills. Field,
  common, `[terrain]`. Hook: food.
- **Wheat and barley field (mugi-batake)**: winter crop, harvested early summer. Field, common, `[terrain]`. Hook:
  food, straw.
- **Soybean and adzuki field (daizu / azuki)**: Field, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: food.
- **Sesame field (goma-batake)**: Field, occasional, `[terrain]`. Hook: food, oil.
- **Rapeseed field (natane-batake)**: yellow spring fields grown for lamp oil, very large in Settsu and Kawachi.
  Field, common in Kinai, `[terrain]`. Hook: oil (lamp fuel crafting), landmark colour in spring. BL: oil press.
- **Cotton field (wata-batake)**: Kawachi, Settsu, Owari and Mikawa cotton, a boom crop of the 17th–18th c. Field,
  common in Kinai, `[terrain]`. Hook: cloth and bandage crafting.
- **Indigo field (ai-batake)**: indigo plants for dye. Field, occasional (Musashi, Settsu; main area Awa
  `[off-map: Shikoku]`), `[terrain]`. Hook: dye. BL: indigo barn.
- **Hemp field (asa-batake)**: tall hemp for fibre, cloth and rope. Field, occasional (mountain villages),
  `[terrain]` tall. Hook: rope and cloth, tall cover.
- **Tobacco field (tabako-batake)**: legal and widely grown by 1730; Hadano (Sagami, near Hakone) was a known
  tobacco area. Field, occasional, `[terrain]` + tall plants. Hook: trade good. BL: tobacco drying shed. (verify
  Hadano's Edo start)
- **Konnyaku field (konnyaku-batake)**: devil's-tongue corms on shaded slopes, Kōzuke and hills. Field, occasional,
  `[terrain]`. Hook: food.
- **Sweet potato field (satsuma-imo)**: `[outside 1680-1750 for our map: Satsuma c.1705, Inland Sea c.1711, Kanto
  trial plots 1735 (Aoki Konyō)]`. Field, rare, `[terrain]`. Hook: famine food. Stephen's call.
- **Corn patch (tōmorokoshi / nanban-kibi)**: maize in mountain villages, cobs hung under eaves. Field, rare,
  `[terrain]`. Hook: food. (verify spread by 1730)
- **Ginseng bed with shade roof (ninjin-hata)**: Korean ginseng grown under low thatch shades; Yoshimune had seeds
  planted at Nikkō around 1728 and distributed them from 1738. Field, rare (official gardens only in 1730), medium.
  Hook: medicine, rare find. (verify dates)
- **Wasabi terrace (wasabi-da)**: gravel beds stepped down a cold mountain stream; Utogi (Suruga) since the early
  1600s. Field (mountain stream), rare, `[terrain]`. Hook: food, clean water. (verify)
- **Tea bushes on field edges (aze-cha / kuro-cha)**: in 1730 tea grew as scattered bushes along bunds, field
  boundaries and hillside plots, not in rows. Field, common, small–medium. Hook: tea leaves (drink), low cover.
  Src: Makinohara city: before Meiji, tea bushes stood scattered on field edges; rows began with Makinohara, 1869.
- **Uji shaded tea garden (ōishita-en)**: tea bushes under canopies of reed screens and straw on pole frames,
  for top-grade matcha leaf; Uji only. Field, rare, large. Hook: landmark, shade and cover. (Gyokuro, 1835, is later)
- **Tea row plantation (chabatake, clipped rows)**: `[outside 1680-1750: Meiji]`. Listed so it is not built by
  mistake. Field, —, `[terrain]`.
- **Mulberry field and mulberry hedge (kuwa-batake)**: pollarded mulberry for silkworms, in 1730 mostly on field
  edges, river flats and small plots; the big mulberry landscapes are later. Field, occasional (common in Kōzuke,
  Shinano, Musashi), medium bushes. Hook: berries, cover. (assumed; verify)
- **Paper-mulberry and mitsumata patches (kōzo / mitsumata)**: shrubs cut for washi bark, on field edges in
  mountain villages. Field, occasional, medium bushes. Hook: paper crafting.
- **Lacquer trees along bunds (urushi)**: trunks scored in rows of horizontal cuts for sap. Field, occasional,
  large (tree). Hook: lacquer; touching the sap gives a rash (fun hazard).
- **Wax trees (haze / hazenoki)**: for vegetable wax. Field, rare on our map, large (tree). `[off-map: mostly
  Kyushu, Shikoku]`. BL: wax works.
- **Yard persimmon tree (kaki)**: almost every farmyard had one; astringent kinds gave dried persimmons and tannin
  (kakishibu). Yard, common, large (tree). Hook: food (autumn), tannin crafting, climbable.
- **Plum grove (ume-bayashi)**: plums for umeboshi; Soga near Odawara, next to Hakone. Field/yard, common,
  large (grove). Hook: food. BL: umeboshi works.
- **Chestnut trees (kuri)**: yard and field-edge chestnuts; Tanba chestnuts famous. Yard/field, common, large.
  Hook: food (autumn).
- **Citrus terraces (mikan-batake)**: Kishū (Arida) mandarins on stone terraces, shipped to Edo. Field, rare on our
  map, large `[terrain]` + trees. Hook: food, landmark. `[off-map: Kii, edge of map]` (verify)
- **Yard citrus and other fruit trees (yuzu, biwa, nashi, momo)**: single trees by the house. Yard, occasional,
  large (tree). Hook: food.
- **Pear orchard on overhead trellis (nashi-dana)**: pears trained on a flat head-high trellis. Field, rare,
  large. Hook: food, cover under the trellis. (verify that trellis pears predate 1750)
- **Bamboo grove behind the house (takeyabu)**: planted and cut, the farm's source of poles, baskets and shoots;
  often the north windbreak. Yard, common, large. Hook: bamboo crafting, shoots (food), cover. Also nature flavour.
- **Grass-cutting slope (kusakari-ba / magusa-ba)**: commons where grass and leaves were cut to trample into paddies
  as green manure (karishiki) and for fodder. Field/commons, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: fodder, open ground.
- **Thatch meadow (kaya-ba)**: commons of pampas grass cut for roofing. Commons, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: thatch,
  tall cover. BL: Rural industry, thatch meadow.
- **Hotbed frames with oiled paper (onsho / abura-shōji)**: forcing beds warmed with fermenting manure under
  oiled-paper covers, for early vegetables; Sunamura on the Edo outskirts. Field, rare, medium. Hook: warmth.
  (verify; the bakufu restricted early-season vegetables, 1686)
- **Compost heap (tsumi-goe / taihi)**: straw, leaves and stable muck piled to rot. Yard, common, medium. Hook:
  fertiliser crafting, smell.
- **Stable manure heap (umaya-goe)**: muck from the stable heaped by its door. Yard, common, medium. Hook: as above.
- **Field manure jar (koe-game; name verify)**: a big jar sunk at a field corner holding night soil to ripen before

- **Night-soil buckets and pole (koe-oke / tago)**: two lidded buckets on a shoulder pole. Yard/field, common,
  small prop. BL: manure shed.
- **Field boundary stake (sakai-kui / bōji-kui)**: wooden stake or stone marking plot or village limits. Field,
  common, small. Hook: map detail.
- **Burnt clearing fields (yakihata)**: slash-and-burn plots on mountain slopes. Field, occasional (mountain
  villages), `[terrain]`. Hook: open ground. Also wilderness flavour.

## == 4. Harvest, drying and processing outdoors ==

- **Rice-drying rack, pole type (hasa / hazakake / inakake)**: horizontal poles or bamboo lashed to uprights, sheaves
  hung head-down in autumn; one to three tiers in Kinai and Kanto. Field, common (autumn), medium–large. Hook: cover
  in autumn, straw. Src: Kubota; NGZ 1697; rack drying is recorded from 841.
- **Tall multi-tier rack (hasa, 5–10 rungs)**: the ladder-like racks of wet snow country. Field, rare on our map,
  large. `[off-map: Echigo, Hokuriku]`.
- **Living-tree rack row (hasa-gi / inaki)**: alder or ash planted in lines on bunds so poles can be lashed between
  them. Field, rare on our map, large. `[off-map: mostly Echigo, Hokuriku]` (verify for Kanto)
- **Ground-standing sheaves (ji-boshi / ta-boshi)**: sheaves stood in small stooks or cones on the dry paddy. Field,
  common, small. Hook: low cover.
- **Rice-sheaf stack (ine-zuka / nio)**: a round stack of sheaves waiting for threshing. Field/yard, common,
  medium. Hook: cover, straw.
- **Straw stack (wara-nio / wara-bocchi / suzumi)**: conical or cylindrical stack of threshed straw round a centre
  pole with a straw cap; many regional names. Yard/field, common, medium. Hook: cover, hide inside, straw crafting,
  burns.
- **Straw bundles in stooks (wara-taba)**: bundles leaning together to dry. Yard/field, common, small. Hook: straw.
- **Threshing yard (kado / niwa)**: the swept, hard-packed earth yard in front of the farmhouse, the farm's
  working floor at harvest, spread with mats. Yard, common, `[terrain]`. Hook: open ground, loot on mats.
- **Threshing comb (senba-koki)**: iron-toothed comb on a stand; invented in the Genroku years (1688–1704) at
  Takaishi, Izumi, and spreading fast by 1730; nicknamed "widow-toppler" (goke-daoshi) because it put widow
  threshers out of work. Yard, common, small–medium. Hook: crafting source of iron. Src: ja.wikipedia 千歯扱き.
- **Chopstick thresher (koki-bashi)**: two sticks pinched over the stalks; the older method, still used by poor
  households. Yard, occasional, small prop.
- **Flail (karazao / kururi-bō)**: swinging-bar flail for beans, barley and wheat. Yard, common, small prop. Hook:
  improvised weapon.
- **Winnowing machine (tōmi)**: box fan turned by a crank; first noted in the *Aizu Nōsho* (1684) as still rare,
  spreading from the late 1600s. Yard, occasional, medium. Hook: grain processing station. Src: Aizu Nōsho 1684.
- **Winnowing basket (mi)**: wide shovel-shaped basket for tossing grain. Yard, common, small prop.
- **Sloped grain sieve (sengoku-dōshi / mangoku-dōshi)**: a sloping wire or bamboo screen that sorts hulled rice
  from husks; shown in the WKS (1712) as a new box type. Yard/doma, occasional, medium. Src: Settsu city leaflet.
- **Hulling mill (suri-usu / tsuchi-usu)**: clay-and-wood grinding mill for husking rice; used indoors or on the
  yard on fine days. Yard, occasional, medium.

- **Night watch fires (kagari)**: small fires or torches kept at field edges against boar. Field, occasional,

## == 14. Festivals and seasonal temporary structures ==

- **Village festival float (danjiri / dashi / yatai)**: a wheeled or carried float; Kishiwada's danjiri date from

- **Bon-dance tower (bon-odori yagura)**: a temporary scaffold with a drum and singer in the middle of the dance

- **Festival lanterns (chōchin on lines, mandō frames)**: paper lanterns on ropes or wooden frames. Shrine, common
  (seasonal), small–medium. Hook: light.
- **New Year bonfire pyre (sagichō / dondo-yaki)**: a cone of bamboo, straw and New Year decorations burned in mid
  January in a field or riverbed. Field/commons, common (seasonal), large. Hook: warmth, event.
- **Insect-sending procession (mushi-okuri)**: summer torches, drums and a straw figure (often "Sanemori") carried

- **Seedbed inlet offering (minakuchi-matsuri)**: branches, flowers and a shrine charm stuck at the seedbed water
  inlet in spring. Field, common (seasonal), small. Hook: lore.
- **Harvest offering sheaves (kariage)**: first or last sheaves offered to the field god. Field/yard, common
  (seasonal), small.
- **Rice-planting festival gear (ta-ue matsuri / hana-taue)**: decorated ox saddles and drums of the flower

- **Tug-of-war rope (tsuna-hiki)**: a huge straw rope for inter-village tugs at New Year or Bon. Commons, rare,
  large prop. `[mostly off-map: Kyushu, Okinawa]` (verify Kinai examples)
- **Carp streamers (koinobori)**: born among Edo townsmen around the mid-Edo period; in 1730 boys' festival houses
  flew warrior-picture banners (musha-e nobori) and plain streamers (fukinagashi). Yard, rare, medium. Koinobori
  `[outside 1680-1750: mid-to-late 18th c.]` (verify); warrior banners are in. Src: ja.wikipedia こいのぼり.
- **Warrior-picture banner (musha-e nobori / sekku nobori)**: tall painted banner for the Boys' Festival (5th of

- **Rain-prayer props (amagoi)**: drums, banners and fires on the hill; see §13 for the fire site. Commons,

- **Doll-floating boats (hina-nagashi)**: straw-and-paper dolls floated away in spring. Shore/field, rare, small.

- **Temporary kagura or performance stage (kari-butai)**: planks on trestles for travelling players or kagura.

- **Sumo straw-bale ring (temporary)**: BL. Not counted.

## Obvious collapse candidates (advice only: Stephen decides)

1. **Paddy ground: 4 terrain textures do it all.** Flat paddy (flooded / stubble / winter-barley variants), terraced
   paddy (stone or earth riser as two wall parts), deep-mud paddy (same texture, slower movement), creek-strip
   fields. Lotus, taro and rush fields are one "wet crop" texture with a different plant proxy.
2. **Dry fields: one ridged-field texture plus ~8 crop proxies.** Daikon, greens, negi, taro, millet, barley, soy,
   buckwheat all become "ridges + plant proxy". Cash crops (rapeseed, cotton, indigo, hemp, tobacco) are the same
   field with a tall or colourful proxy; rapeseed in spring is worth keeping for its colour.
3. **Channels and water control: 1 channel kit.** Earth channel, stone-lined channel, ditch = one spline with two
   bank materials. Sluice, notch, divider and pond outlet = one "boards in a frame" part at three sizes.
4. **Water lifting: 3 props.** Lever well (also the field sweep), treadwheel, river lift wheel. Drop the chain pump
   and the swing bucket (or keep the bucket as a hand prop).
5. **Drying racks: 1 rack kit.** Hasa poles with swappable loads: rice sheaves, daikon, persimmons, noodles,
   kanpyō, squid, fish, seaweed, nori mats, nets, laundry. That single kit covers ~15 entries across §4, §6, §15.
6. **Stacks: 1 stack kit.** Straw stack, sheaf stack, thatch bundles, firewood, brushwood, fuel piles, rice bales,
   salt bales: one "stack" part family with material swaps.
7. **Mats on the ground: 1 prop.** Grain, beans, cotton, sardines, radish strips, umeboshi, agar: a straw mat with a
   texture overlay.
8. **Threshing and grain tools: one "harvest set"** of ~6 props (comb, winnower, sieve, mortar, quern, flail), placed
   together in the threshing yard at harvest.
9. **Scarers: 3 props.** Scarecrow (with the Sanemori doll as a variant), clapper line (with the rope lines), knocking
   bamboo. Smell scarer and charms become small stick props on the same part.
10. **Boar defences: 2 kits.** Stone boar wall (can reuse the terrace-wall part) and stake-and-brush fence (reuses the
    shiba-gaki). Earth bank is a terrain edit.
11. **Fences: 4 kits.** Open bamboo grid, closed panel (kenninji / brush / reed / board by material), live hedge,
    stone wall (terrace / plot / cobble by material). Snow and Izumo pine fences drop (off-map).
12. **Gates: 3.** Post gate, kabuki-mon, wicket. The roofed ridge gate is a temple / samurai piece (BL).
13. **Roadside stones: 1 "stele" part family, ~6 shapes.** Standing figure (Jizō, Kannon, Batō Kannon), relief
    panel (Kōshin, paired dōsojin), character stele (dōsojin, myōgō, daimoku, pilgrim, boundary), round stone,
    phallic stone, small pagoda (gorintō / hōkyōintō). The inscription or relief is the variant. About 30 entries
    in §11–§12 collapse into this.
14. **Graveyard: reuse the stele family** plus sotoba tablets, earth mound, bamboo dog-guard and the bucket rack.
15. **Shrine outdoor set: ~8 props.** Wooden torii, stone torii, lantern pair, basin, komainu pair, bell rope, ema
    rack, strength stone. Hundred-times stone, banner stones and offering box are optional adds.
16. **Festival dressing: 1 seasonal layer.** Banners, lanterns, mikoshi, pyre, Bon lanterns, kadomatsu,
    shimenawa as one event kit toggled by date; float and bon tower as two larger optional pieces.
17. **Shore: 3 kits.** Beach boat + skids + capstan; net rack (from the drying-rack kit); shore clutter (floats,
    pots, traps, anchors, driftwood).
18. **Salt field: 1 terrain + 1 filter-pit prop + a tool rack.** The tidal and spread fields look nearly the same
    in game; keep the tidal (Kinai-side) one unless Kanto coast needs the spread one.
19. **Likely outright cuts:** everything in §17; all `[off-map]` windbreaks and snow gear; ginseng beds, hotbeds,
    agar fields and wasabi terraces unless Stephen wants rare landmarks; the tsunami stone; pear trellis; the
    incense water clock (keep as a lore line).

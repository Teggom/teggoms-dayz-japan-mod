# Town waterways, 1730: types, parts and every settlement mapped

Research agent WW, 2026-09-29. Research only: nothing is modelled. Data: `waterways.json` (types, parts, settlement -> types). Generated by `tools/gen_waterways.py`: edit `tools/ww_types.py` / `tools/ww_settlements.py` and rerun. Images: `data/research_ww/refs/` (licences in `CREDITS.md`).

**Binding rules applied:** the era test (existed or still standing in 1730); a dead world 0-2 years abandoned; autumn; loot tiers ignored. **Dating caution (PLAYBOOK 1):** Hiroshige (1830s-50s), Morse (1886) and the surviving 'old canals' (Kurashiki, Kyoto Shirakawa, Kanazawa) are used for form and mood only, never to date a feature. The prints dated before 1730 are scarce: the Toyoharu (1764-72), Harunobu (c.1767) and Eiri (1796-1801) prints are the closest.

## Decisions for Stephen (one line each, with my recommendation)

1. **Seven types, not six:** add g, shore landing (harbour, beach, lake shore): 28 settlements (ports, fishing and salt villages, lake towns) fit none of a-f. Recommend YES.
2. **Split c:** c1 levee river / c2 braided, unbridged gravel river / c3 town river. The Oi, Abe, Sakawa and Fuji crossings have no bridge by shogunal policy and are a Tokaido signature. Recommend YES.
3. **b stays a Kyoto hero:** the Takase gets its own look (8 m, 30 cm of water, funairi basins), but it is built from a's wall, step and bridge parts at low heights; Fushimi and Ogaki reuse it. Recommend YES.
4. **Where the water comes from:** coastal canal towns (Edo, Osaka, Sakai, Nagoya if on the bay) sit at 1-3 m and their canals are cut below sea level, so real sea water fills them; everything inland uses pond models. Recommend YES, and a map rule: every a-type city touches the coast.
5. **Abandoned water levels:** sea-level canals full, with exposed silt at the wall foot; the Takase and d2 street channels near-dry (no one keeps the intakes); moats full but green and lotus-choked; springs (d3) keep running. Recommend YES.
6. **Street channels:** d1 gutters everywhere by default; a d2 mid-street channel only where sourced (Nakatsugawa, Narai, Tsumago, Magome, Suhara, Kamiyoshida's plot streams, Sakamoto). Recommend YES: a channel in every post town would be a modern Gujo / Tsuwano look.
7. **Planting:** willow at canal corners and bridge ends; pines on levees and moat banks; wild cherries only on the Sumida levee (1717) and at temples; no cherry rows along canals (Kiyamachi and Ogaki rows are modern Somei-yoshino). Recommend YES.
8. **Numazu:** had NO castle in 1730 (built 1777). Drop it from CASTLE_TOWNS and build a post town + river port. Recommend YES.
9. **Two missing small castles:** Kano (Nakasendo, 1601) and Minakuchi (Tokaido, domain seat from 1682) were castle towns in 1730 but are not in CASTLE_TOWNS. Recommend: give each a small moat (f1) and gate, no keep. Low priority.
10. **Engine test before any river:** a stepped pond-model river and a pond moat need one small test (swim depth, the joins at each step, drinking) on the test island before the c/b/f build lists. Recommend YES, one short agent session.

### What changed from the starting set
- **(g) added:** ports, fishing and salt villages and lake towns had no home in a-f.
- **(c) split in three:** the levee river, the unbridged braided river and the small town river look and play differently (long bridge / porters / one plank bridge).
- **(d) widened:** it now covers spring streams (Mishima, Samegai, Tarui), hot-water gutters (spa towns) and Kamiyoshida's plot streams, which are all 'water that runs with the street'.
- **(f) gained dry and round moats:** the five hill and spur castles have dry moats; Tanaka's four round moats are unique.
- **(b) kept but small:** it is really one place (Kyoto), plus Fushimi and Ogaki.

## The types in detail

### (a) City canal (horikawa)

Straight dug canal between stone or timber walls; the streets of Osaka and eastern Edo, Nagoya's Horikawa, Sakai's moat ring. Kura and open quays (kashi / hama) line it; many plank bridges.

- a1 main canal: 20-36 m water, dressed-stone walls, trestle or long plank bridges, kura-yashiki boat basins (funairi)
- a2 back canal: 8-15 m water, rough stone or pile-and-board banks, short plank bridges, house backs with steps
- a3 river kashi: a natural river used as a canal (Ise Kawasaki on the Seta, Fushimi port): kura straight onto the water

**Cross-section**

| Dimension | Value | Source or reason |
|---|---|---|
| water width, main | 20-55 m | Sanjukkenbori was 30 ken (~55 m) from 1612, narrowed to 19 ken in 1828 [S-30K]; Nagoya Horikawa 12-48 ken (22-87 m) [S-NGY]; Nagahori ~40 m, Dotonbori 30-50 m (today's widths) [S-OSK]. Game: 20 m and 36 m |
| water width, back canal | 8-15 m (assumed) | between the Takase (7-8 m, a hauled canal) and the named main canals; Hacchobori / Kaede-gawa scale. Game: 10 m |
| water depth | 1.8-2.5 m (assumed) | carried loaded flat lighters (hirata) at low tide; must be deep enough to swim in DayZ |
| wall top above water | 2.0-3.0 m | Hiroshima domain kura-yashiki revetment, Osaka: stone walls 3 m high [S-KURA]; the castle-side wall of Edo's outer moat along the Nihonbashi River is ~6 m [S-EDOM] (castle scale, not a canal) |
| quay strip (kashi / hama) | 4-10 m (assumed) | open unloading strip between the wall and the kura or house row, with sheds (naya), stacked goods and landing steps [l02, l07] |

- **Edges and revetment:** Dressed stone (uchikomi-hagi / kirikomi-hagi) for main canals, kura-yashiki frontages and quays; rough stone (nozura) on back canals; pile-and-board revetment (kui-dome: posts ~0.9 m apart with boards behind) for cheap banks, clearly shown along the Onagi-gawa [l08].
- **Steps and landings:** Gangi quay steps at every quay and at each kura-yashiki (the Hiroshima domain yard had stone walls + gangi + a 51 x 47 m boat basin cut into the plot [S-KURA]); plank landing stages on piles; short private step flights at house backs.
- **Mooring:** Timber mooring posts standing in the water at the wall foot; holed mooring stones set in the wall top.
- **Outfalls:** Street-drain mouths in the wall every block (gesui-guchi); Osaka's back-to-back sewers end here [OL U streets].
- **Bridges:** KEEP_CIVIC plank bridge (most; Osaka had ~200 townsmen's bridges), trestle bridge (the big public bridges: Nihonbashi, Tenma-bashi, Naniwa-bashi), occasional arched bridge. No stone slab bridges (spans too long).
- **Planting:** Weeping willow at bridge ends and canal corners [l07]; a single old pine at a quay corner (Onagi-gawa's 'five pines' [l08]); no cherry rows (Kiyamachi-style cherry rows are Meiji or later).
- **How buildings meet the water:** Kura backs straight onto the wall with water doors (Nagoya Horikawa, Ise Kawasaki [S-NGY, S-ISE]); Osaka hama frontage: kura-yashiki and merchant houses each with their own steps; Edo kashi: an open public strip with sheds and kura set back. Laundry is not done in city canals (tidal, dirty).
- **Boats:** chokibune, yanebune, hiratabune (flat cargo lighter), tenmasen; yakatabune as a rare hero (OUTDOOR_LIST U boats, section 7.14).
- **Abandoned state (0-2 years, autumn):** Water brown-green; a mud-and-silt shelf exposed along the wall foot; a hirata sunk with only its gunwale showing; a chokibune swamped against the gangi; a flotsam raft of planks, straw bales and leaves jammed against a bridge pier; one landing stage with half its planks gone; weeds in every wall joint and a green algae band at the waterline; one pile-and-board section slumped into the water.
- **[terrain] work:** Channel cut (bed below sea level where the town is coastal), the quay strip as flat hard ground, bridge-end plazas (hashi-zume).
- **Placeable models:** Wall modules, gangi, landing stages, mooring posts and stones, outfalls, bridges, boats, debris.
- **Water surface:** SEA: coastal canal towns sit at 1-3 m above sea level and their canals are cut below 0 m, so the ocean fills them for free (FEASIBILITY 2.3). Inland a3 (Ise Kawasaki, Fushimi) needs pond models.
- **Refs:** `l01`, `l02`, `l07`, `l08`, `l14`, `l10` (see References)

### (b) Shallow town canal (Takase type)

A narrow, very shallow cargo canal with flat boats hauled back upstream by men walking the bank, and side basins (funairi) cut into one bank where boats turned and unloaded. Kyoto's Takase (1611, Nijo to Fushimi, ~10 km) is the model.

- b1 Takase: stone-walled, 7-8 m, funairi basins
- b2 moat-canal: a castle-town canal of the same scale doubling as a moat (Ogaki's Suimon-gawa to the Funamachi port)

**Cross-section**

| Dimension | Value | Source or reason |
|---|---|---|
| channel width | 7-8 m | Takase channel ~8 m, water ~7 m wide [S-TAK] |
| water depth | ~0.3 m normal | 'a few tens of cm'; normal depth about 30 cm [S-TAK] |
| bank height | 1.0-1.5 m (assumed) | house backs and a towpath just above water; boats are flat and low |
| towpath | 1.5-2.0 m (assumed) | haulers (hikiko, ~700 at the peak) walked the bank pulling on a rope [S-TAK] |
| funairi basin | ~100 x 11 m (55 x 6 ken), stone-walled | Ichi-no-funairi; another record gives 134 x 16 m; 9 basins between Nijo and Shijo, about every 100 m, on the west bank [S-TAK] |
| boat | ~13 x 2 m, flat-bottomed, max 15 koku (~2.25 t) | takasebune; ~200 boats at the peak [S-TAK] |

- **Edges and revetment:** Stone-walled both sides (the funairi are recorded as stone-walled [S-TAK]); rough stone where it runs out of town.
- **Steps and landings:** Short gangi and plank landings inside each funairi; a few steps at the house backs.
- **Mooring:** Posts inside the funairi; boats tied bow to stern along the towpath side.
- **Outfalls:** Small drain mouths from the houses.
- **Bridges:** Many short, low plank bridges (one per street crossing); stone slab bridges where it is narrow.
- **Planting:** Willows along the towpath (assumed); NO cherry rows (the Kiyamachi and Ogaki cherry rows are modern Somei-yoshino).
- **How buildings meet the water:** House backs and inns straight onto the water on the east bank; timber, charcoal and rice merchants' yards at each funairi.
- **Boats:** takasebune (flat, 13 x 2 m); the local boat type OUTDOOR_LIST 7.14 lost, back in as a length variant of the flat cargo boat.
- **Abandoned state (0-2 years, autumn):** With no one keeping the intake weir, the 0.3 m of water drops to a trickle between gravel and silt bars; takasebune lie grounded and tilted; the funairi basins are silted, reed-choked and hold standing water; a hauling rope still tied to a post; leaves pile against every low bridge.
- **[terrain] work:** Shallow channel cut, the funairi cuts, the towpath strip.
- **Placeable models:** Wall modules (low heights), funairi corner pieces (= wall modules), gangi, landing, plank and slab bridges, boats.
- **Water surface:** POND: Kyoto is inland and uphill. The trickle state is cheap: a thin stepped pond strip, or even a dry gravel bed with puddles.

### (c) Big river through or beside a town

The river a town grew beside: gravel flats (kawara), earth levees (tsutsumi) with planting, a long bridge or a ferry, and river works.

- c1 levee-banked lowland river: Kamo, Sumida, Yodo, Yahagi, Tama, Sagami, Kiso: long bridge or ferry, levees with roads on top
- c2 braided gravel river, unbridged: Oi, Abe, Sakawa, Fuji, Tenryu, Kamanashi: a wide stony bed with shifting channels, crossed by porters or ferry
- c3 town river: a 20-60 m river through a post or castle town, one trestle or plank bridge, gravel bars (Totsuka's Kashio, Fujieda's Seto)

| c1 low-water channel | 30-60 m (assumed) | autumn low water leaves wide gravel and sand flats (kawara) [OL U water] |
| c1 levee | 3-5 m high, crest 4-8 m (assumed) | grass and planted crest; a road on top where the levee is the road (Bunroku levee, Yodo; mem) |
| c1 bridges | Sanjo Ohashi 61 x 3 ken (~109 x 5.5 m, 1780 record), stone piers; Yahagi-bashi 208 ken (~378 m), the Tokaido's longest | [S-KAMO] [S-YAH] |

- **Edges and revetment:** Earth levees with turf; stone-faced reaches in cities (Kamo, 1670); stone-filled bamboo gabions (jakago) along banks and round weirs; timber crib spurs (seigyu) and groynes pushing the current off the bank; open staggered levees (kasumi-tei) on c2 rivers; Shingen-zutsumi at Nirasaki is an old survivor [OL W 12].
- **Steps and landings:** Earth steps with log risers down the levee [l09]; ferry landings (earth or stone ramp, bell post, waiting bench); porter platforms (rendai) and the river-crossing office on c2; a boat landing at river ports (Kuragano, Fushimi, Hirakata).
- **Mooring:** Stakes in the gravel; boats hauled up on the flats.
- **Outfalls:** Town drains and irrigation outlets through the levee (small sluices).
- **Bridges:** KEEP_CIVIC trestle bridge at hero length (Sanjo, Yahagi, Ryogoku, Seta, Uji); plank bridges on c3; boat bridge as a rare hero; NONE on c2 (ferry or porters). Stone abutments left standing where a flood took the deck [OL U bridges parts].
- **Planting:** Levee crests: black or red pine rows, bamboo groves as flood protection, and on the Sumida levee the cherries planted in 1717 under Yoshimune [S-SUMI] (wild types: yamazakura / edohigan, never Somei-yoshino); willows and medake thickets at the water; reeds (yoshi) on the flats; bank willow.
- **How buildings meet the water:** Towns stand back behind the levee; tea stalls on the bridge-end plaza; riverbed shows and stalls on the kawara; houses on stilts over the water at Ryogoku [l14]; outcast hamlets on the flats (neutral).
- **Boats:** watashi-bune ferry; sanjikkoku-bune (17 x 2.5 m) on the Yodo [S-FSM]; kurawanka food boats at Hirakata; river cargo boats (takasebune) at Kuragano and Kajikazawa; timber rafts (ikada) on the Tenryu, Oi and Tama.
- **Abandoned state (0-2 years, autumn):** Autumn low water; a ferry boat stranded high on the gravel, its rope still across the river; one or two bays of a long trestle bridge washed out, leaving pile stumps (a real chokepoint); flotsam piled against the piers; levee grass long and seeded; summer riverbed platforms (Kamo) left as stacked frames on the flats, never set out [S-YUKA]; a porter's platform tipped over on c2.
- **[terrain] work:** Levees, kawara gravel flats, braided channels, the bridge-end plaza. This is mostly [terrain].
- **Placeable models:** Bridges, ferry landing, river-works kit (gabions, cribs, groynes), levee steps, boats, water-level post, debris, riverbed platform remains.
- **Water surface:** POND for every inland river (stepped pond segments; FEASIBILITY 2.3 says unproven). Rivers at their mouths (Sumida, Yodo at Osaka, Tama at Kawasaki) can be sea-level.
- **Refs:** `l04`, `l05`, `l06`, `l09`, `l14`, `l01` (see References)

### (d) Street-side channel

Water that runs WITH the street: the stone gutter along house fronts in every town, a wider stone-lined street channel in some post towns, spring-fed streams through a town, hot-water gutters in spa towns.

- d1 house-front gutter (omote-dobu): the default in every town; = the jp_s_gutter kit
- d2 street channel (yosui): a wider stone channel down the middle or one side of the street, with a slab bridge to each door (Nakatsugawa, Narai troughs, Tsumago, Magome)
- d3 spring stream: clear, cold, weedy spring water through town (Mishima, Samegai, Tarui)
- d4 hot-water gutter: steaming gutters and troughs in spa towns
- d5 plot stream: a small stream crossing each long plot behind the street frontage (Kamiyoshida's Yana-gawa, where pilgrims purified before entering the oshi house)

- **Mooring:** None.
- **Outfalls:** d1 ends in an outfall into a canal, river or ditch (jp_s_gutter _outfall).
- **Bridges:** Stone slab footbridge (1-3 slabs; Morse 268-270) at every door; a plank bridge where a cart crosses d2/d3; gutter slabs on d1.
- **Planting:** Mostly none; a willow at a corner; baikamo and watercress in d3.
- **How buildings meet the water:** House fronts face the channel; troughs at the inn doors; on d5 the channel crosses each plot in front of the house.
- **Boats:** None.
- **Abandoned state (0-2 years, autumn):** Silted to half depth with leaves and mud; grass in the joints; a slab tipped into the channel; d2 channels dry or trickling because no one clears the intake (puddles where the silt dams it); a trough overflowing into the street and a wet patch; d3 keeps flowing (springs don't stop) and grows a thick green weed; d4 still steams, with sulphur crust.

- **Placeable models:** Gutter pieces (existing kit), wider channel pieces, slab bridges, steps, troughs, spring basins.
- **Water surface:** Mostly NO water surface needed: d1/d2 are dry or a decal film. d3 needs thin pond strips (clear-water material). d4: steam particles over a wet decal.

### (e) Village stream / irrigation channel

The water every farm village lives on: the main irrigation channel (yosui) threading past the houses from a weir, field ditches, and in the hills a boulder stream with bamboo pipes and log bridges.

- e1 lowland: earth-banked irrigation channel, river-stone lined where it passes the houses, sluices, a weir upstream, wash steps
- e2 mountain: boulder stream in a narrow valley, dry-stone terraces, bamboo pipes (kakehi) to each house, log and slab bridges

- **Edges and revetment:** Earth banks with grass; river-stone or field-stone walls near houses; stake-and-wattle (shigarami) on bends; gabions (jakago) at the weir.
- **Steps and landings:** Wash steps (arai-ba) at each house or group; a wooden board-on-posts platform variant; stepping stones.

- **Outfalls:** Paddy water notches (mizu-kuchi) and field sluices.

- **Boats:** Paddy punt (ta-bune) in delta and wajuu villages only.

- **Water surface:** POND strips for the main channel only; field ditches are wet-mud texture. Mountain streams: stepped pond segments or dry boulder beds with pools.
- **Refs:** `m268`, `m269`, `m052` (see References)

### (f) Castle moat

The wet or dry moat round a castle, the one waterway every castle town shares.

- f1 wet moat: stone wall on the castle side, earth bank or low stone on the town side
- f2 dry moat: hill castles and spurs (Komoro, Iida, Takato, Inuyama; Nagoya's honmaru)
- f3 concentric round moats: Tanaka only (four rings, ~600 m across) [S-TNK]

| f1 width | 20 m typical; 35-50 m big; 70-100 m+ Osaka outer | Himeji: average 20 m, max 34.5 m [S-HMJ]; Matsumoto up to ~50 m [S-MTM]; Osaka's outer moat passed 100 m at its widest [S-HMJ page] |

| f2 dry moat | 10-20 m wide x 5-10 m deep (assumed) | hill castles |

- **Edges and revetment:** Ishigaki (KEEP_CIVIC stone rampart; nozura / uchikomi-hagi / kirikomi-hagi as 3 materials, OL 7.17); 'belt' walls (koshimaki / hachimaki: an earth rampart with a stone band at the foot or the top, Edo and Hikone, mem); earth ramparts with turf and pines.

- **Mooring:** One or two posts (assumed).

- **How buildings meet the water:** Castle walls, turrets and tamon on top; samurai houses behind board walls on the town side; a guard hut at each gate.
- **Boats:** A small patrol or maintenance punt (assumed; one per moat).
- **Abandoned state (0-2 years, autumn):** Algae-green still water; dead lotus stalks and brown leaves; reeds spreading from the corners; the plank gate bridge rotten with gaps; a punt sunk at the landing; one ishigaki face bulging with a slump of fallen stones.

### (g) Shore landing (harbour, beach, lake shore)  [NEW TYPE]

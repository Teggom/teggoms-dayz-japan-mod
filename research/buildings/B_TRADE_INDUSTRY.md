# B: Trade, lodging, food and drink, crafts, workshops and industry (BL-B)

Brainstorm list for Stephen to collapse. It is deliberately **over-detailed**: every trade gets its own line, even where
the game will merge them. Text only. Anchor year 1730; 1680-1750 fully in.

**Lanes.** BL-A covers dwellings, including the living side of shop-houses and home stables. BL-C covers religious,
government, military, civic and infrastructure buildings, the honjin, waki-honjin and toiya-ba, and town layout. For a
shop-house, this file lists **only the shop or workshop side**: shop floor, work area, trade storage.

## 0. Conventions

**Setting tags** (after the description):
- `town shop`: shop-front in a machiya row, selling at the street
- `town workshop`: makes things in a machiya or back-alley nagaya, and may sell at the front
- `rural`: village or farm workshop
- `site`: a standalone works: kiln, mill, salt pan, mine, yard
- `stall`: yatai, booth or bench, no real building
- `itinerant`: a street trade with no premises (listed so Stephen can see it, then cut it or turn it into a prop)

**Floors.** `doma` = beaten-earth floor at street level, where wet, heavy and fire work happens. `raised` = a board
(itanoma) or tatami floor on posts, stepped up from the doma at the agari-kamachi. Most shops have both: a doma passage
(tōri-niwa) and a raised shop room (mise-no-ma) at the front.

**Era tags.** `[outside 1680-1750: date]` = real, but outside the core window. `[uncertain]` = I could not pin the
date or form; check before building.

**Source keys** are pointers for a follow-up check. They are not plate-by-plate citations verified in this session.
- JK = *Jinrin kinmōzui* (1690, illustrated encyclopedia of trades and people: the best in-era source)
- WS = *Wakan sansai zue* (1712 encyclopedia)
- HJ = *Hyakunin jorō shinasadame* (Nishikawa Sukenobu, 1723: women of every class and trade)
- NE = Saikaku, *Nippon eitaigura* (1688: merchant stories)
- KA = Kaempfer, travels on the Tōkaidō in 1691-92 (*History of Japan*)
- MS = Tanaka Kyūgu, *Minkan shōyō* (1721; the author was a Kawasaki post-town official)
- SB = *Shokunin burui* (Tachibana Minkō, 1770); KS = *Kinsei shokunin zukushi ekotoba* (Kuwagata Keisai, 1805)
- NS = *Nihon sankai meisan zue* (1799: regional products and industries); TM = *Tōkaidō meisho zue* (1797)
- EM = *Edo meisho zue* (1834-36); MM = *Morisada mankō* (1837-53: late, but the best street-trade detail)
- WP = the English or Japanese Wikipedia article on the topic; "web" = a site checked in this session, named inline

### 0.1 The standard shop-front kit (applies to every `town shop`; entries below list only what is trade-specific)

Noren curtain at the door, kanban signboard (hanging, standing, or shaped like the goods), lattice front (kōshi) or
removable shutters (shitomido / amado), a fold-down street bench (battari-shōgi), a raised mise-no-ma with a counter
area (chōba) behind a low lattice screen (chōba-gōshi), a low account desk, abacus (soroban), ledgers hung on a nail
(daifukuchō), inkstone and brush, a tobacco tray for customers (tabako-bon), cushions (zabuton), a brazier (hibachi),
balance scales (tenbin) or steelyard (sao-bakari), fire buckets (tenshu-oke) stacked outside, and a fireproof kura at the
back for stock. Big merchants add clerks' desks, a staircase chest (kaidan-dansu), and the shop's shop-mark on everything.

## 1. Metal

- **Village and farm-tool smith (kaji-ya, nō-kaji)**: makes and re-steels hoes, sickles, hatchets, nails and hinges.
  Village smithy or town workshop at the edge of a street; open front, doma, soot, a smoke vent in the roof.
  Items: forge hearth (hodo), box bellows (fuigo: a wooden box with a push-pull piston), anvil (kanatoko) set in a
  stump, quench tub (mizu-oke), tongs (hibashi / yattoko), sledges and hand hammers (tsuchi), charcoal bin and pine
  charcoal, grindstone and whetstones (toishi), finished hoes (kuwa) and sickles (kama) hung on the wall, scrap iron pile.
  Source: JK; KS.
- **Anchor and ship-iron smith (ikari-kaji / funa-kaji)**: forges big iron anchors (four-fluked ikari), nails and ship
  fittings. Harbour-side town workshop; doma, extra-large hearth. Items: a big hearth, several bellows, a heavy anvil,
  long tongs on chains or a crane pole, swage blocks, anchors lying finished, spikes and clench nails (funa-kugi).
  Source: SB (anchor smiths are a standard "shokunin" plate) [uncertain which plate].
- **Carpenter's-tool smith (daiku-dōgu kaji)**: specialist makers of plane irons (kanna), chisels (nomi), gimlets
  (kiri), laminating hard steel onto soft iron. Town workshop; doma. Items: small forge, fuigo, anvil, laminating steel
  bars, chisel blanks, files, whetstone trough, finished tool rack. Miki (Harima) became famous for this in the 18th c.
  [uncertain how early].
- **Saw smith (noko-kaji / metate-shi)**: forges saws, then cuts and sets the teeth (metate = tooth-setting; saw
  doctors also travelled). Town workshop; doma. Items: forge, long thin anvil, saw blanks, files (yasuri), tooth-setting
  hammer and anvil, saw vice (a wooden clamp), whetstones. The pull saws in use: kataba (single edge), maebiki (big rip).
  The double-edged ryōba is late [outside 1680-1750: late Edo / Meiji, uncertain].
- **Knife smith (hōchō-kaji)**: kitchen knives (deba, usuba, sashimi) and, in Sakai, **tobacco-cutting knives**, which
  carried a shogunate quality seal (Sakai kiwame) in the 18th c. Town workshop; doma. Items: forge, fuigo, anvil,
  quench trough with clay-coating pot, grinding wheel (a big wet stone turned by hand), whetstones, handle wood (hō),
  horn ferrules, finished knives in straw. Source: WP (Sakai knives).
- **File maker (yasuri-shi)**: cuts file teeth with a chisel (tagane) into soft steel, then hardens. Town workshop;
  doma. Items: small forge, a lead-bed anvil to hold the blank, cutting chisels, hammer, quench tub, finished files.
  Source: SB / KS [uncertain].
- **Swordsmith (katana-kaji)**: forges blades from tamahagane steel; sacred work. Town or castle-town workshop, often
  behind a fence; doma, darkened forge room so the colour of the steel can be read. Items: forge with shimenawa rope
  over it, a small shrine shelf (kamidana), box bellows, anvil, several strikers' sledges, tamahagane lumps, straw ash
  and clay slurry for folding, clay-coating trough (tsuchioki), long quench trough, tongs, file, a blade rack, white
  ceremonial clothing (eboshi). Source: WP; KS.
- **Spear and polearm maker (yari-kaji)**: usually the same smiths as swords; heads for yari and naginata. Merge with
  the swordsmith unless Stephen wants a "cheap weapons" smithy. Items: as swordsmith, plus spear shafts (e) of oak,
  ferrules (ishizuki).
- **Sword polisher (togishi)**: polishes blades through a sequence of stones. A quiet town workshop; raised board floor.
  Items: a sloping stone-holder (togi-dai) on a small stool, a set of whetstones from coarse to fine, water tubs, finger
  stones (hazuya, jizuya), paper and cloth, a blade stand. Source: WP.
- **Scabbard maker (sayashi)**: carves magnolia (hō) scabbards and plain storage mounts (shirasaya). Town workshop;
  raised floor. Items: hō-wood planks, rice-paste glue (sokui) and a spatula, small planes, chisels, carving knives,
  cramps and binding cord, a lacquer shelf. Source: WP.
- **Hilt wrapper (tsukamaki-shi)**: wraps sword hilts in ray skin (samegawa) and silk or leather braid. Town workshop;
  raised floor. Items: hilt cores, rolls of ray skin, silk braid spools, paper wedges (hishigami), glue pot, a hilt vice.
- **Sword-fittings maker (tsuba-kō, kinkō-shi, habaki-shi)**: guards, collars, pommels and small fittings in iron,
  copper, shakudō and gold. Town workshop; raised floor. Items: small charcoal forge, chasing hammers and punches
  (tagane), a pitch bowl (yani-dai), files, gravers, small crucibles, a patination pot (niage: plum vinegar and copper
  salts), finished tsuba on a board. The Gotō school did official work. Source: WP.
- **Armourer (katchū-shi / gusoku-shi)**: makes and repairs armour; by 1730 mostly ceremonial and "revival" pieces
  (the Myōchin school). Town workshop; doma for the metal, raised floor for lacing. Items: small forge, stake anvils,
  planishing hammers, iron scales and plates (sane, ita-zane), rawhide (nerikawa) plates, lacquer pots, rivets, silk
  lacing braid (odoshi-ito) on reels, a lacing frame, a finished armour on a box stand (yoroi-bitsu). Source: WP.
- **Caster of pots and kettles (imoji)**: casts iron cooking pots (nabe), rice kettles (kama), tea-ceremony kettles
  (chagama), and bronze small goods. Town workshop or edge-of-town site; doma, casting pit. Items: a cupola or crucible
  furnace (koshiki-ro), foot-operated bellows (fumi-fuigo), clay moulds (imono-gata) in parts, crucibles (rutsubo),
  ladles, a sand floor, scrap iron, cooling rack of new pots. Takaoka (from 1611) and Kyoto's kettle makers are
  period centres. The everyday side-spouted iron kettle (tetsubin) comes later [outside 1680-1750: mid-18th c. on,
  uncertain].
- **Bell caster, on site (bonshō imono)**: temple bells were usually cast **at a temporary casting site** near the
  temple, not in a shop. A site kit: a dug casting pit, a clay mould built up in the pit, a ring of furnaces and
  foot-bellows, a thatched shelter, fuel stacks. Cross-reference BL-C (temple).
- **Mirror maker (kagami-shi)**: casts and polishes bronze mirrors; itinerant polishers (kagami-togi) re-polished them
  with mercury-tin amalgam. Town workshop; raised floor for polishing. Items: small crucible furnace, moulds, a
  polishing board, whetstones, powdered amalgam jars, finished mirrors in boxes, mirror stands (kyōdai).
- **Coppersmith (dōki-shi, akagane-shi)**: raises sheet copper into kettles, water jars, hibachi liners, lantern tops;
  also copper roofing. Town workshop; doma. Items: sheet copper stack, stake anvils in a stump (tokodai), wooden and
  steel hammers, annealing hearth, shears, tin solder and soldering iron, finished kettles. Source: SB [uncertain].
- **Pewterer (suzu-shi)**: pewter and tin sake flasks, tea caddies, offering vessels. Town workshop; raised floor.
  Items: small melting pot, stone or clay moulds, a lathe for trimming, scrapers, burnishers, finished flasks
  (suzu-dokkuri). Osaka and Kyoto. Source: WP (suzu-ki) [uncertain on period].
- **Tinplate smith (buriki-ya)** `[outside 1680-1750: tinplate is Meiji]`. Listed only because it was in the brief;
  the 1730 equivalents are the coppersmith and the pewterer.
- **Ornamental metalworker (kazari-shi / kazari-shoku)**: small decorative metal: hairpins (kanzashi), door-pull
  plates (hikite), nail covers (kugi-kakushi), tansu fittings, pipe bowls. Town workshop; raised floor. Items: small
  charcoal hearth with a blowpipe, pitch bowl, chasing punches, files, saws, sheet brass and copper, silver wire,
  finished fittings on boards. Source: KS.
- **Gold and silver leaf beater (haku-uchi-shi)**: beats gold and silver to leaf between paper. Town workshop; raised
  floor, still air. Items: a stone or wood beating block, heavy hammers, packets of special beating paper (haku-uchi-gami),
  bamboo tweezers, cutting frame, leaf books. The shogunate regulated gold leaf (a gold-leaf guild, haku-za, c. 1696)
  [uncertain date]. Source: WP.
- **Needle maker (hari-shi)**: sewing and tatami needles. Kyoto's Misuya needles are the famous period brand. Town
  workshop; raised floor. Items: iron wire coils, a draw plate, wire cutters, a small anvil and eye punch, files, a
  hardening hearth, a tumbling tub for polishing, needles packed in paper. Source: WP.
- **Wire drawer (harigane-shi)**: draws iron and copper wire for needles, cages and fittings. Town workshop; doma.
  Items: draw plates, a long draw bench with a winch, annealing hearth, coils. Could merge with needle maker.
- **Nail maker (kugi-kaji)**: hand-forged square nails (wa-kugi) by the barrel. Town workshop; doma. Items: small
  forge, nail header (a heading block), anvil, nail rod bundles, barrels of nails by size.
- **Lock maker (jōmae-shi)**: box and door padlocks (wa-jō) with spring mechanisms. Town workshop; raised floor.
  Items: small forge, files, sheet iron, keys on strings, finished locks.
- **Scale maker (hakari-shi, the Hakari-za)**: steelyards and balance scales were a shogunate monopoly: the Moriya
  (Edo, for the east) and Shuzui (Kyoto, for the west) scale guilds. Town shop and workshop. Items: steelyard beams
  (sao), brass weights (fundō, the typical waisted shape), balance pans, measuring rules, official stamps, a register.
  Source: WP (Hakari-za).
- **Pipe maker (kiseru-shi)**: metal bowls and mouthpieces joined by a bamboo stem (rao). Town workshop; raised floor.
  Items: brass and copper sheet, small forge or blowpipe, mandrels, files, stock of bamboo stems, finished pipes on a
  rack. The street stem-replacer (rao-ya) is itinerant [its steam-whistle cart is late Edo].
- **Stirrup and bit maker (abumi-shi, kutsuwa-shi)**: iron stirrups (the open-sided, slipper-shaped abumi) and bits,
  often inlaid with silver. Town workshop; doma. Items: forge, anvil, iron sheet, inlay tools, stirrups hung in pairs.
  Kaga and Kyoto stirrups are known. Source: WP.
- **Clockmaker (tokei-shi)**: Japanese clocks (wadokei) with adjustable temporal hours, made for daimyo and temples.
  Rare; castle-town workshop; raised floor. Items: brass gears, files, a small lathe, verge-and-foliot parts, a clock
  on a tall stand (yagura-dokei). Niche; flag for cutting.
- **Gunsmith (teppō-kaji)**: matchlocks. Kunitomo (Ōmi) and Sakai. **Out of scope for the game**; listed only.
  Items: barrel forge, boring bench, lock parts, stocks.
- **Pot mender (ikake-ya)** `itinerant`: patched iron pots and kettles with solder at the roadside. Items: a small
  portable bellows and hearth, solder, a shoulder-pole kit.
- **Knife and blade sharpener (togi-ya)** `itinerant` or tiny booth: sharpened tools and razors. Items: whetstones in a
  wooden holder, a water tub.

## 2. Leather and hides

**Status note (neutral).** In this period, work with dead animals and hides was tied to hereditary outcaste
communities, called kawata (and, in official usage, eta). They held hereditary rights to the carcasses of dead cattle
and horses in a district, and many lived in separate hamlets, often by rivers. In the Kantō they came under the
authority of Danzaemon at Asakusa; Watanabe village in Osaka was a major leather and drum centre. Deer hides were also
imported in quantity through Nagasaki in the 17th c. Leather workshops therefore sit **outside or at the edge of towns**
in a period-true layout. Stephen decides how, or whether, the game shows this. Source: WP (burakumin, kawata,
Danzaemon).

- **Carcass processing and rawhide yard (kawa-hagi-ba)**: skinning dead stock, scraping, salting. Riverside `site` on
  the edge of a kawata hamlet; open-air yard and a shed; doma. Items: skinning knives, fleshing beam, fleshing knife
  (kawa-sen), salt, stretching stakes and pegs, drying frames, bone and horn piles, a water channel.
- **Tanner, white leather (shiro-nameshi / kawa-ya)**: Himeji "white leather", tanned with river water, salt and
  rapeseed oil, trodden by foot, then bleached in the sun. Riverside `site`: river soaking pens, a shed, a drying field.
  Items: soaking pens in the river, tubs, salt, oil jars, a treading tub, fleshing knives, drying frames and pegs,
  rolled finished hides. Source: WP (Himeji leather).
- **Smoked-leather maker (fusube-gawa)**: colours deerskin by smoking it with straw and pine resin. `rural`/edge of
  town; a small smoke shed. Items: a tall smoke drum or hut, a hide-wrapped rotating pole, straw and resin fuel,
  hides hanging. Source: WP (Inden / fusube).
- **Lacquered deerskin maker (inden-ya)**: Kōfu (Kai) speciality: lacquer patterns stencilled onto deerskin for
  pouches and armour. Town workshop; raised floor. Items: tanned deerskins, lacquer pots, paper stencils, spatulas,
  a drying cupboard, cut pouch parts. Source: WP (Inden-ya, from 1582).
- **Hide and deerskin dealer (kawa-don'ya)**: wholesale of hides; imported deerskins via Nagasaki. Town shop plus kura.
  Items: stacked bundles of hides, a hide-grading table, scales, tally sticks.
- **Drum maker (taiko-shi)**: hollows keyaki trunks (kuri-bachi drums) and stretches cowhide heads, nailed or roped.
  Town or kawata-village workshop; doma. Items: hollowed log bodies, adze and chisels, rawhide heads soaking in a tub,
  a stretching jack (ropes and a lever, or a jack under the drum), iron tacks (byō), drum stands, beaters (bachi),
  finished drums from hand drums to temple drums. Source: WP (taiko; Watanabe village).
- **Leather-soled sandal maker and repairer (setta-ya / setta-naoshi)**: setta are zōri with a leather sole and
  iron heel-plate; repairing them was a kawata trade in Edo. Town workshop or `itinerant`. Items: leather soles, woven
  bamboo-sheath uppers, awls, thread, heel-plates, a low bench.
- **Leather tabi and glove maker (kawa-tabi, yugake-shi)**: leather socks (largely replaced by cotton tabi after the
  1657 Meireki fire pushed leather prices up [uncertain; often repeated]) and deerskin archery gloves (yugake). Town
  workshop; raised floor. Items: deerskin, patterns, shears, awls, waxed thread, wooden hand forms, finished gloves.
- **Saddler and tack maker (kura-shi, bagu-shi)**: lacquered wooden saddle trees (kura) plus leather flaps, girths and
  cruppers. Town workshop; doma. Items: saddle trees on stands, lacquer shelf, leather straps, braided girths, flaps
  (aori), cushions, awls, finished saddles on a saddle horse. Stirrups come from the abumi-shi (Metal).
- **Pouch and tobacco-pouch maker (tabako-ire-shi, kinchaku-ya)**: leather and cloth pouches, tobacco cases, purses,
  the sagemono hung at the belt. Town workshop and shop; raised floor. Items: leather and cloth offcuts, patterns,
  shears, awls, clasps (kanagu) from the kazari-shi, netsuke toggles, finished pouches on a rack.
- **Hide-glue maker (nikawa-shi)**: boils hide and bone scraps for glue used by painters, ink makers, joiners.
  Kawata-village `site`; doma, smelly. Items: big boiling cauldron on a hearth, scrap bins, straining cloths,
  setting trays, a drying rack of glue sticks. Source: WP (nikawa).
- **Leather garment maker (kawa-baori)**: firemen's and hunters' leather coats. Town workshop. Items: large hides,
  patterns, lacquer or smoke for colour, awls. Probably merge with the pouch maker.
- **Shamisen maker (shamisen-shi)**: wooden neck and body (hard woods, often imported), with cat or dog skin heads.
  Town workshop; raised floor. Items: neck blanks, body frames, skins soaking, a stretching press with wedges and
  cords, pegs, silk strings, finished shamisen on the wall. Leather link noted; could sit under fine crafts instead.

## 3. Wood, bamboo and building timber

- **Carpenter's workshop and yard (daiku, sakuba)**: carpenters mostly worked on site; the master (tōryō) kept a yard
  for pre-cutting joints and storing timber. Town workshop with yard; doma. Items: ink line (sumitsubo) and marking
  pen (sumisashi), carpenter's square (sashigane), saws (kataba, maebiki), planes (dai-kanna), adze (chōna), axes
  (ono), chisels (nomi), mallet (genno), gimlets (kiri), wooden sawhorses (uma), a tool box (dōgu-bako), plan board
  (ita-zu), stacked squared timbers, shavings. Source: JK; WP.
- **Sawyer's shed (kobiki-ba)**: two-man or one-man rip sawing of logs into planks with the big maebiki-ōga saw.
  Timber-yard or `rural` shed; doma. Items: a log propped high on trestles at an angle, maebiki-ōga saws, wedges,
  ink line, finished plank stacks, sawdust. Source: WP (maebiki-ōga); KS.
- **Lumber dealer and timber yard (zaimoku-ya, kiba)**: timber wholesale; in Edo the Fukagawa Kiba yards (moved there
  1701) stored logs floating in ponds and canals. Waterside `site` and town office; open yard. Items: log pond,
  standing timber racks (tate-kake), stacked planks under roofs, log hooks (tobi-guchi), rafts, tally boards, the
  dealer's mark branded on log ends. Source: WP (Kiba).
- **Joiner, doors and screens (tategu-shi)**: sliding shōji, fusuma frames, wooden doors, lattice (kōshi). Town
  workshop; doma for planing, raised for assembly. Items: long planes, groove planes (mizo-kanna), fine saws, chisels,
  a kumiko lattice jig, frames leaning on the wall, paper rolls for shōji. Source: KS.
- **Cabinet maker (sashimono-shi)**: chests (tansu), boxes, shelves, trays, joined without nails. Town workshop;
  raised floor. Items: fine saws, small planes, chisels, marking gauge, clamps, paulownia and zelkova boards, a
  half-built tansu, iron fittings to fit. Tansu spread widely in this period. Source: KS; WP.
- **Paulownia box maker (kiri-bako-ya)**: fitted boxes for scrolls, tea bowls and dolls. Town workshop. Items: kiri
  boards, fine saws, planes, box string (sanada-himo), boxes stacked by size. Merge with sashimono.
- **Cooper, closed barrels (taru-ya)**: sake and soy casks (taru) of cedar, with bamboo hoops; the big trade feeding
  breweries. Town or brewery-side workshop; doma. Items: cedar staves drying in stacks, a giant upturned jointer
  plane (shōjiki), drawknife (sen), hoop driver (taga-hame), split bamboo for hoops, a bevelling knife, a compass for
  heads, finished casks with straw wrap (komo). Source: JK; KS.
- **Tub and bucket maker (oke-ya)**: open tubs, bath tubs, well buckets (tsurube), rice tubs, ladles. Town workshop;
  doma. Items: as cooper, plus tubs of all sizes stacked in the street, a hoop-coiling stand. Could merge with taru-ya.
- **Bentwood maker (magemono-shi)**: thin cypress bent into round boxes, steamers (seiro), sieves (furui), lunch boxes
  (wappa), stitched with cherry bark. Town or mountain `rural` workshop. Items: cypress strip soaking tub, a heated
  bending iron or hot-water trough, wooden forms, clothes-peg clamps, cherry bark strips, awl, stacked boxes and
  steamers. Source: WP (magemono).
- **Woodturner (kijishi / rokuro-shi)**: turns bowl and tray blanks on a rope lathe, often mountain people who moved
  with the timber and sold blanks to lacquerers. `rural` mountain workshop or town workshop; doma. Items: a two-person
  rope lathe (tebiki-rokuro: one pulls the strap, one turns), hooked turning chisels (rokuro-kanna), axe and adze for
  rough-outs, drying racks of rough bowls, shavings. Source: WP (kijishi).
- **Hakone woodcraft maker (Hakone zaiku)**: turned toys, boxes and souvenirs sold to Tōkaidō travellers at Hatajuku
  and Yumoto; the famous marquetry (yosegi) is later [outside 1680-1750: yosegi developed by Ishikawa Nihei, late Edo].
  Roadside town workshop and shop. Items: lathe, turning hooks, local woods by colour, finished toys and boxes on
  shelves at the front. Source: web (hakone-japan.com, Hatajuku); WP.
- **Comb maker (kushi-ya)**: boxwood (tsuge) combs; also tortoiseshell combs (bekkō, Fine crafts). Town workshop and
  shop; raised floor. Items: boxwood blanks drying, a fine comb saw, a tooth-spacing guide, files, rasps, polishing
  with scouring rush (tokusa), finished combs on a board. Source: KS; WP.
- **Clog maker (geta-ya)**: paulownia geta with separate or carved teeth; hanao thongs fitted. Town shop and workshop;
  doma plus raised. Items: paulownia blocks, saw, broad knife, drill for thong holes, hanao thongs in bundles, finished
  geta hung in pairs, a fitting stool. Repair (ha-ire, replacing teeth) was itinerant. Source: JK; MM.
- **Boat builder's shed (funa-daiku)**: river boats, fishing boats, ferries. Waterside `site`: an open shed at the
  water's edge. Items: hull on building stocks, planks bent over fire, clamping ropes and wedges, flat-headed boat
  nails (funa-kugi, the chisel-shaped type), a caulking iron and cypress-bark caulking (maki-hada), adzes, oars and
  sculls (ro), a steaming fire. Source: WP.
- **Shipyard for coasters (funaba / zōsen-ba)**: big coasting ships (bezaisen, higaki-kaisen) on stocks. Harbour
  `site`; open slipway with sheds. Items: a keel-plank on stocks, frames, huge planks, pulleys, a mast timber, a sail
  loft (see Textiles), a smith's shed. Larger version of the boat shed.
- **Cart maker and wheelwright (kuruma-ya)**: two-wheeled handcarts (daihachi-guruma in Edo from the 1650s; the smaller
  beka-guruma in Osaka) and ox-carts (ushi-guruma, Kyoto area). Town workshop; doma. Items: spoked wheel on a stand,
  hubs, felloes, iron tyres or straps, axle timbers, a finished cart. Source: WP (daihachi-guruma).
- **Palanquin maker (kago-shi, norimono-shi)**: cheap open kago (bamboo) and the enclosed, lacquered norimono of the
  rich. Town workshop. Items: bamboo frames, carrying poles, woven bamboo sides, lacquered panels and blinds, cushions.
  Source: WP.
- **Bowyer (yumi-shi)**: laminated bamboo-and-wood bows (the asymmetric yumi), glued and bound with wedges. Town
  workshop; raised floor. Items: bamboo and haze-wood strips, glue pot, a bundle of bamboo wedges and rope used for
  clamping, bow forms, finished bows, strings of hemp. Source: WP.
- **Fletcher (ya-shi)**: bamboo arrow shafts straightened over heat, fletched with feathers, iron heads fitted. Town
  workshop; raised floor. Items: bamboo stock, a charcoal pot and a straightening block (yatame-ki), feathers in boxes,
  glue, silk binding, arrowheads (yajiri), finished arrows in a quiver stand. Source: WP.
- **Shingle splitter and shingle roofer (kokera-shi, hiwada-shi)**: splits cedar or sawara into thin shingles; bark
  roofing with cypress bark (hiwada) for temples. Town or `rural` workshop. Items: froe (hegi-nata), splitting block,
  bundles of shingles, bamboo nails, cypress bark bundles. Source: WP.
- **Mortar and pestle maker (usu-ya)**: big wooden rice mortars (usu) and pounders (kine). `rural` or town workshop.
  Items: keyaki or pine log sections, adze, chisels, finished mortars.
- **Abacus maker (soroban-shi)**: frames and beads. Town workshop. Items: beads turned on a small lathe, bamboo rods,
  frames. Niche; merge with sashimono.
- **Measuring-box maker (masu-shi, the Masu-za)**: official rice and sake measures (masu); the Kyō-masu standard
  was a licensed guild. Town workshop. Items: boards, jointing planes, the official branding iron, stacked masu of
  each size. Source: WP.
- **Bamboo worker, baskets and sieves (take-zaiku, kago-ya, zaru-ya)**: baskets, sieves, trays, carrying baskets.
  Town workshop or `rural`; doma. Items: bamboo poles, a splitting knife (take-wari nata), sizing knives, soaking tub,
  half-woven baskets, finished baskets hung outside. Source: JK; KS.
- **Blind maker (sudare-ya)**: hanging reed and bamboo blinds, and misu (fine blinds with silk edges) for mansions.
  Town workshop. Items: a blind-weaving frame with weighted bobbins, split bamboo or reeds, silk edge cloth.
- **Tea-whisk and tea-ware bamboo maker (chasen-shi)**: tea whisks, scoops (chashaku). Takayama (Nara) monopoly.
  Town workshop; raised floor. Items: bamboo sections, small knives, a whisk-shaping stand, finished whisks in paper.
  Niche.
- **Ladle and spoon whittler (shakushi-zaiku)**: wooden rice paddles and ladles; Miyajima is famous. `rural` workshop.
  Items: blanks, knives, shaves, bundles of paddles.

## 4. Textiles, fibre, straw and rush

- **Cotton ginner (wata-kuri)**: removes seeds from raw cotton on a two-roller hand gin. `rural` or town workshop;
  raised floor. Items: roller gin (wata-kuri-ki), baskets of seed cotton, seed sacks, ginned cotton. Source: WP.
- **Cotton bower and wadding shop (wata-uchi, wata-ya)**: fluffs ginned cotton with a big bow struck by a mallet;
  sells wadding for futon and clothes. Town workshop and shop; raised floor, dusty. Items: a large cotton bow hung from
  a pole (wata-uchi yumi), wooden mallet, piles of fluffed cotton, a pressing board, cotton sheets (wata) folded.
  Source: KS; MM.
- **Cotton-cloth wholesaler (momen-don'ya)**: bulk cotton from the Kinai, Mikawa, Owari. Edo's Ōdenma-chō was the
  cotton street. Town shop plus kura. Items: bales of cloth bolts (tan) in straw wrap, a measuring table, whale-bone
  cloth rule (kujira-jaku), ledgers.
- **Spinner (ito-tsumugi)**: home work on a spinning wheel (ito-guruma). Cross-reference BL-A; as a trade building it
  is a nagaya or farmhouse room. Items: spinning wheel, rolled cotton slivers (yori-ko), skeins.
- **Silk reeler (ito-hiki / seishi)**: reels silk from cocoons in hot water by hand (te-biki / hand reeling). `rural`
  farmhouse or town workshop. Items: a small brazier with a pot of hot water, cocoons, a reeling frame or large
  wooden reel (waku), chopsticks to find ends, skeins. The seated, geared zaguri reeler is later [outside 1680-1750:
  spread late Edo, especially after 1859]. Source: web (Gunma world heritage, zaguri).
- **Floss-silk maker (mawata-ya)**: stretches boiled waste cocoons over frames into floss for padding. `rural`.
  Items: boiling pot, wooden or bamboo stretching frames (mawata-kake), drying racks of floss.
- **Silk thread dealer and twister (ito-ya, ito-don'ya, nenshi)**: twists and sells silk thread. Town shop or
  workshop. Items: skeins on poles, a twisting frame, bobbins, scales. Water-powered twisting mills at Kiryū are later
  [outside 1680-1750: c.1780s, uncertain].
- **Hemp and ramie processor (asa-hiki, o-umi)**: retting ponds, scraping the fibre, splitting and joining it into
  thread. `rural` `site`: a retting pond or stream pit, and a work shed. Items: retting pit, scraping board and blade
  (o-hiki), fibre hanks drying, a thread basket (o-oke). Source: WP.
- **Cloth bleacher (sarashi-ya)**: bleaches hemp, ramie and cotton by washing, fulling on a block (kinuta), and laying
  it out in the sun (Nara sarashi, Ōmi); in Echigo, ramie was bleached on snow (yuki-sarashi). `rural` `site`: a
  bleaching field by a river. Items: fulling block and mallets (kinuta), cloth lengths pegged out on the grass, lye
  tubs, washing boards. Source: WP; NS.
- **Silk drawloom weaver (Nishijin hata-ya)**: Kyoto's figured-silk brocade (nishiki, kinran), woven on the tall
  drawloom (sorabiki-bata, two people: one weaves, one sits above pulling the pattern cords). Looms stood on the doma for
  the humidity. Town workshop; doma with a tall ceiling. Items: drawloom, pattern cords, shuttles (hi), reed (osa),
  warp beam, bobbin winder, silk yarn on racks, a gold-thread box. Source: WP (Nishijin).
- **Town or village weaver (hata-ori)**: plain cotton, hemp or silk on a frame loom (taka-bata) or the older
  backstrap ground loom (izari-bata / ji-bata). `rural` or town workshop; raised floor or doma corner. Items: loom,
  shuttle, reed, warping frame (hebata), bobbin winder (ito-guruma), finished bolts. Source: JK; HJ.
- **Ikat weaver (kasuri)**: pre-tied, pre-dyed yarn woven into blurred patterns. Kurume and Iyo kasuri are later
  [outside 1680-1750: c.1800]; simpler kasuri earlier [uncertain]. Items: as weaver, plus a thread-binding frame.
- **Wild-fibre weaver (fuji-fu, shina-fu)**: cloth from wisteria and linden bark, for poor mountain people. `rural`.
  Items: bark bundles, boiling pot with ash, splitting knives, a ground loom.
- **Indigo dyer (kon'ya / ai-ya)**: blue-dyes thread and cloth; the most common dyer. Town workshop with a yard; doma
  with **vats sunk into the floor**, often four with a fire pit in the middle to keep them warm. Items: sunken indigo
  vats (ai-game), fire pits (hi-tsubo), fermented indigo (sukumo) in bales, lye tubs (akumizu), wheat bran and lime,
  stirring poles, dripping cloth over the vats, tall drying poles and frames in the yard or on the roof. Source: WP;
  Hiroshige's Kanda Kon'ya-chō is 1857, so check JK for an earlier plate.
- **Stencil (paste-resist) dyer (katazome-ya, komon-ya)**: small repeating stencil patterns for samurai kamishimo
  (komon), and larger ones for cotton (chūgata). Town workshop; long raised floor. Items: very long boards (nagaita,
  about 6 m) on trestles, paper stencils (katagami), rice-paste resist in a tub, spatulas (hera), bamboo stretchers
  (shinshi) in the yard, a steaming box, brushes. Source: WP (katazome).
- **Stencil cutter (katagami-shi)**: cuts stencils from persimmon-tannin-laminated paper; Ise katagami from Shiroko and
  Jike (Suzuka), sold by travelling agents. Town workshop; raised floor. Items: a cutting board, knives and punches
  (kiri-bori, dōgu-bori), stacks of shibu paper, silk-thread reinforcement. Source: WP (Ise katagami).
- **Yūzen dyer (yūzen-ya)**: freehand painted resist dyeing on silk, credited to Miyazaki Yūzensai in the Genroku era
  (in era). Kyoto town workshop; raised floor. Items: rice-paste cones (tsutsu), fine brushes, dye pots, silk stretched
  on shinshi, a steaming box, a rinsing stream (Kamo river). Source: WP (Yūzen).
- **Tie-dyer (shibori-ya)**: tie and stitch resist. **Arimatsu shibori** on the Tōkaidō (from 1608) sold to travellers
  at a roadside shop. Town workshop plus shop; raised floor. Items: binding stands and hooks, thread, cloth, indigo
  vats or a dyer contact, finished tenugui and yukata cloth hung at the shop front. Source: WP (Arimatsu).
- **Safflower-red dyer (beni-ya, momi-ya)**: red dye from safflower (Mogami, Yamagata), sold as beni cakes and used for
  red silk (momi) and lip colour. Town workshop; raised floor. Items: safflower cakes (beni-mochi), shallow tubs,
  sieves, rice vinegar or plum vinegar, dye cups. Source: WP.
- **General and black dyer, crest painter (someya, kurozome-ya, monkaki-shi)**: black dyeing for formal wear, and
  hand-painting of family crests on black garments. Town workshop. Items: dye tubs, drying poles, compass and crest
  pattern books, fine brushes, white pigment. Source: WP.
- **Tailor (shitate-ya, nui-mono-shi)**: sews kimono to order; much sewing was home work by women. Town workshop;
  raised tatami floor. Items: low sewing board, measuring rule (kujira-jaku), hand shears (nigiri-basami), pins and
  needles in a cushion (hari-yama), a small iron (hera and kote heated in a brazier), a flat iron pan (hinoshi), thread
  box, garments folded. Source: HJ.
- **Embroiderer (nuihaku-shi)**: silk and gold-thread embroidery for temple cloths, costumes and rich garments. Town
  workshop; raised floor. Items: embroidery frame on trestles, silk floss, gold thread, needles, pattern drawings.
- **Braid maker (kumihimo-ya)**: silk braid for armour lacing, sword hilts, obi cords, haori ties. Town workshop;
  raised floor. Items: the round braiding stand (marudai) with weighted bobbins (tama), the square stand (kakudai),
  the high stand (takadai) [uncertain date for takadai], silk skeins, a counterweight bag. Source: WP.
- **Futon and bedding maker (futon-ya)**: sews and stuffs quilts and quilted sleeved covers (yogi). Cotton bedding
  was spreading in the mid-18th c. and was still costly [uncertain on how common in 1730]. Town workshop; raised floor.
  Items: cotton wadding piles, covers, long needles, a stuffing board, a stack of finished futon.
- **Sock maker (tabi-ya)**: cotton tabi with clasps (kohaze; earlier ties). Town shop and workshop. Items: foot-shaped
  patterns, cut soles, clasps, sample tabi hung outside as a sign.
- **Mosquito-net maker and seller (kaya-ya)**: hemp nets, often green with a red edge, from Ōmi (Nishikawa house);
  street sellers cried them in summer. Town shop; raised floor. Items: bolts of net, frames for hanging samples,
  measuring rule. Source: WP (kaya); the Hakone "origin" story of the green colour is a legend, unverified.
- **Sedge-hat and rain-hat maker (kasa-ya, suge-gasa)**: sedge, bamboo and wood-strip hats for travellers and farmers.
  Town shop or `rural`. Items: sedge bundles, hat forms, bamboo frames, finished hats stacked and hung. Source: JK.
- **Rope and cordage maker (nawa-ya, tsuna-ya)**: hemp and straw rope, ship cordage. `rural` or harbour town; a long
  open yard for twisting. Items: a rope-twisting device (hand-cranked hooks), long rope walk, hemp hanks, coils of rope.
- **Net maker (ami-ya)**: fishing nets in hemp or cotton, dyed with persimmon tannin. Fishing village or town workshop;
  raised floor or yard. Items: netting needles (ami-bari), mesh gauges (me-ita), hemp twine, tannin tub, nets hung to
  dry on poles. Source: NS.
- **Sailcloth maker (ho-ya)**: straw or rush-mat sails earlier; woven cotton sailcloth after 1785 [outside 1680-1750:
  Kudō Matsuemon's thick "Matsuemon-ho", 1785]. In 1730, sails were narrow cotton strips sewn together [uncertain].
  Harbour workshop. Items: a sail-sewing floor, palms and needles, bolts, a finished sail.
- **Straw-goods maker (wara-zaiku)**: sandals (waraji, zōri), raincoats (mino), rice bales (tawara), bags (kamasu),
  mats (mushiro), rope. Mainly winter farm work, sold in towns and at every tea house. `rural` farmhouse doma or a
  nagaya room. Items: straw stacks, straw-beating stone and wooden mallet (wara-uchi ishi, yoko-zuchi), a straw-mat
  loom (mushiro-bata), a bale-making frame, sandal-knitting hooks, bundles of waraji hung from the eaves. Source: JK; NS.
- **Rush-mat weaver (goza-ya, omote-ya)**: rush tatami covers (the best from Bingo, "Bingo-omote") and goza mats.
  `rural` or town workshop. Items: rush (igusa) bundles, a mat loom with weights (goza-bata), hemp warps, cut mats.
- **Tatami maker (tatami-ya)**: builds straw-core tatami, covers them in rush, sews the cloth border. Town workshop;
  raised board floor, work done kneeling. Items: tatami needle (tatami-bari), elbow pad (hiji-ate), a tatami knife
  (tatami-bōchō), a work trestle (tatami-dai), straw cores (toko), rolls of rush covers, border cloth (heri), finished
  mats on edge. Source: KS; JK.
- **Palm-fibre worker (shuro-ya)**: hemp-palm fibre for ropes, brooms, brushes, raincoats. `rural` or town. Items:
  palm-fibre bundles, combing boards, brooms. The shuro scrubbing brush (tawashi) is later [outside 1680-1750: 1907].

## 5. Ceramics, stone, plaster and glass

- **Potter's works with climbing kiln (yakimono-ya, noborigama)**: glazed stoneware (Seto, Mino, Kyoto, Shigaraki,
  Bizen, Tokoname). Standalone `site` on a slope; a workshop shed with doma. Items: stepped multi-chamber kiln
  (noborigama) with a firebox, stacked red-pine firewood, kick wheel (keri-rokuro) and hand wheel (te-rokuro), wedging
  board, clay settling tanks (suihi), glaze tubs, long drying planks on racks, kiln shelves and props, finished wares
  in straw. A water-powered clay stamp (kara-usu) stood on the nearest stream in some pottery villages. Source: WP;
  NS; WORLD_CATALOGUE T37.
- **Old-style tunnel kiln works (anagama, ōgama)**: single-chamber kilns still used in places (Bizen, Tamba,
  Tokoname). `site`. Items: tunnel kiln up a slope, firewood, simpler shed. Merge with noborigama for the game.
- **Porcelain works (jiki; Arita / Imari)**: porcelain, with saggars (saya) and a separate enamelling trade. Kyushu
  (off-map, but porcelain was sold everywhere). `site`. Items: as noborigama plus porcelain stone crushing stamps on
  the stream, saggar stacks, blue-painting (sometsuke) desks with brushes and cobalt pots. Source: WP.
- **Overglaze enameller (aka-e-ya)**: paints enamels on finished porcelain and re-fires in a small muffle kiln. Arita
  had a separate enamellers' street (Akae-machi, 1672). Town workshop; raised floor. Items: small round muffle kiln,
  enamel pigment pots, fine brushes, painting rests, wares waiting on shelves. Source: WP.
- **Earthenware maker (doki-ya, kawarake-shi, hōroku-shi)**: unglazed wares: sacred dishes (kawarake), roasting pans
  (hōroku), braziers, portable stoves (kamado / hettsui, the small clay "shichirin" type is [uncertain date]), flower
  pots, clay bells. `rural` or edge-of-town workshop; doma and a small kiln or open firing pit. Items: clay heap,
  wheel, moulds, small updraught kiln, stacks of dishes. Source: JK; KS.
- **Roof-tile maker (kawara-yaki)**: fires roof tiles. The one-piece pantile (sangawara) was invented by Nishimura
  Hanbei of Ōmi in 1674 (in era), and in 1720 Edo began encouraging tile roofs against fire: tile demand boomed right
  around 1730. `site` on the edge of town, near clay; long drying sheds. Items: wooden tile moulds (kata), a wire
  clay cutter, slab-cutting frame, a burnishing spatula, drying sheds with racks of green tiles, a small updraught
  or "daruma" kiln [form uncertain for 1730], ridge-end tiles (onigawara) and a small carving table for them.
  Source: WP (kawara; Nishimura Hanbei).
- **Tile layer (kawara-buki)**: lays roofs; works on site. As a building it is a yard with tile stacks, mud (fuki-tsuchi)
  pit, ladders. Merge with the tile maker.
- **Stonemason's yard (ishiku, ishi-ya)**: lanterns (tōrō), grave stones (hakaishi, spreading among commoners),
  foundation stones, well curbs, stone mills, steps. Town-edge yard; open ground with a shed. Items: chisels (nomi),
  points, stone hammer (sekkō), splitting wedges (ya) and feathers, pry bar, a sledge (shura) and rollers, square and
  ink line, half-carved lanterns and grave stones, stone dust. Castle-wall specialists (Anō-shū) are a separate guild.
  Source: KS; WP.
- **Stone quarry (ishiba, chōba)**: Izu andesite quarries supplied Edo castle walls in the early 17th c.; Hakone and Izu
  are right on the map. `site`: a quarry face on a hillside with a shed. Items: quarry face with wedge-hole rows (ya-ana),
  split blocks, wedges, sledges, a slide (shura) track, a tool-sharpening forge, the lord's carved mark on blocks.
  Source: WP (Izu-ishi) [uncertain on how active in 1730].
- **Whetstone quarry and dealer (toishi-ya)**: whetstones from Kyoto (Narutaki), Amakusa and others; the dealer sells
  by grade. Town shop. Items: stones graded in boxes, a test trough, scales. Niche; merge with ironmonger.
- **Lime burner (ishibai-yaki, kaibai-yaki)**: burns limestone (Hachiōji / Ōme area for Edo) or seashells into lime for
  plaster. `site`. Items: a lime kiln (a stone or earth shaft), charcoal and wood, sacks of lime, a slaking pit,
  shells heaped at coastal sites. Source: WP (shikkui).
- **Plasterer's yard (sakan)**: mud walls and white lime plaster (shikkui) for kura. Works on site; the yard stores
  materials. Items: trowels (kote) of many shapes, hawk board (kote-ita), a mixing pit for mud and chopped straw
  (arakabe), seaweed-glue pot (funori), hemp fibre (susa), lime sacks, bamboo lath bundles (komai). Source: KS.
- **Glassmaker (biidoro-ya, garasu-ya)**: small glass: beads, hairpins, the popping toy (popin), cups, spectacle
  lenses; Nagasaki first, then Osaka and Edo. Rare; town workshop; doma. Items: small crucible furnace, blowpipes,
  marver stone, tongs, a cooling box, bead rods. [uncertain for Edo in 1730; Osaka glass c.1750s; Edo cut glass
  (kiriko) outside 1680-1750: 1834]. Source: WP.

## 6. Paper, lacquer, printing and the fine crafts

- **Paper mill (kami-suki-ba, washi)**: paper from kōzo bark (also gampi and mitsumata). Izu's **Shuzenji paper**
  (gampi) is right on the map. `rural` `site` by clean cold water; doma. Items: a bark-steaming vat (koshiki) over a
  cauldron, stripping knives, a stream soaking pit, ash-lye cooking cauldron, a picking tub (chiri-tori), beating
  block and wooden mallets, the paper vat (suki-bune) with mucilage (neri from tororo-aoi root), the mould and bamboo
  screen (suketa, su), a couching stack (shitoku) on a lever press with stones, drying boards leaned in the sun
  (hoshi-ita), brushes. Source: WP; NS.
- **Recycled paper maker (suki-kaeshi, Asakusa-gami)**: grey recycled paper from waste collected by paper buyers.
  Town-edge workshop by water. Items: as a paper mill but with waste-paper soaking tubs, grey sheets drying. Source:
  WP (Asakusa-gami).
- **Paper wholesaler (kami-don'ya)**: paper by the bundle; Ozu Washi in Nihonbashi from 1653. Town shop plus kura.
  Items: paper bales (maru) and reams (jō) in wrappers, a grading table, paper cutter's board and knife.
- **Persimmon-tannin maker (kakishibu-ya)**: presses unripe persimmons, ferments the juice for waterproofing paper,
  fans, umbrellas, nets and stencil paper; also makes shibu-gami (tannin paper). `rural` or town workshop; doma. Items:
  crushing tubs and mallets, big fermenting barrels, straining sacks, jars of kakishibu, drying boards of brown paper.
- **Paper-clothing maker (kamiko)**: robes of crumpled, oiled paper; cheap winter wear and a monk's garment. Town or
  `rural` workshop. Items: tannin-treated paper sheets, crumpling boards, sewing tools. Niche.
- **Scroll and screen mounter (hyōgu-shi / kyōji)**: mounts scrolls, pastes papers onto fusuma and folding screens.
  Town workshop; raised board floor. Items: a long lacquered table, paste bucket (nori), paste brushes (nade-bake,
  uchi-bake), a drying board (karibari) with sheets pasted to it, knives, rulers, scroll rollers (jiku), silk
  brocade offcuts. Source: KS; WP.
- **Lacquer tapper (urushi-kaki)**: scores lacquer trees in summer and collects the sap. `rural` itinerant,
  based in a hut. Items: curved scoring knife (kaki-kama), spatula (hera), small sap buckets, tree-climbing ladder.
- **Lacquer refiner and dealer (urushi-ya)**: strains raw sap, stirs it in the sun to dry and smooth it (kurome,
  nayashi). Town shop and workshop. Items: shallow wooden tubs, paddles, straining paper and a twisting press, lacquer
  kegs, a warm drying spot. Source: WP.
- **Lacquerer (nushi / nuri-shi)**: builds up lacquer on wood (trays, bowls, boxes, scabbards). Town workshop; raised
  floor, kept dust-free. Items: a humid drying cupboard (urushi-buro), spatulas, human-hair brushes (urushi-bake),
  lacquer pots, whetstones and charcoal for polishing, a turning stand, wares drying on racks. Centres: Wajima, Aizu,
  Kishū (Kuroe), Odawara (Odawara lacquer on Hakone wood, on the map). Source: WP.
- **Gold-lacquer artist (maki-e shi)**: sprinkled gold and silver designs on lacquer. Town workshop; raised floor.
  Items: fine brushes (rat-hair), powder tubes (funzutsu), gold powder boxes, a tiny spatula, burnishing stones,
  drawings (oshi-gata). Could merge with lacquerer.
- **Painter's studio (e-shi / machi-eshi)**: town painters for fans, screens, signboards, votive plaques and prints;
  the official Kanō studios are a cross-reference to BL-C. Town workshop; tatami floor. Items: brushes, ink stone
  (suzuri), shell-white (gofun), mineral pigments (iwa-enogu) in dishes, glue (nikawa) warmer, felt mat (mōsen), paper
  or silk on a stretcher, copybooks. Source: HJ; WP.
- **Signboard maker (kanban-shi)**: carves and paints shop signs, lettering in gold or black. Town workshop. Items:
  boards, carving chisels, lacquer and gold leaf, sample signs, three-dimensional signs (a giant pipe, sandal, brush).
- **Folding-fan maker (sensu-ya, ōgi-ya)**: bamboo ribs, pleated paper, painted leaves. Kyoto. Town shop and workshop;
  raised floor. Items: rib-splitting knives, bundles of ribs (hone), pleating moulds (two folded papers used as a press),
  glue, painted leaves, fans opened on display. Source: WP.
- **Flat-fan maker (uchiwa-ya)**: round bamboo-and-paper fans; Marugame's fans were sold to Konpira pilgrims. Town
  workshop. Items: split bamboo with spread ribs, paper, glue, tannin, fans in racks. Source: WP.
- **Umbrella maker (kasa-shi, wagasa)**: bamboo-ribbed paper umbrellas, oiled with perilla or linseed oil; cheap
  "bangasa" and the bull's-eye janome-gasa. Town workshop; raised floor plus a yard where open umbrellas dry in rows.
  Items: bamboo ribs, turned wooden head (rokuro), thread, paper, glue, oil pot, brushes, open umbrellas drying.
  Source: WP; JK.
- **Lantern maker (chōchin-ya)**: collapsible paper lanterns. The travellers' **Odawara chōchin** is on the map.
  Town workshop; raised floor. Items: a collapsible wooden form, spiral bamboo or split bamboo ribs, paper, glue,
  crest-painting brushes, lanterns hanging. Source: WP (Odawara chōchin) [uncertain founding date, early 18th c.].
- **Woodblock publisher (hanmoto / shomotsu-ya)**: publishes books and prints; the shop sells, the back rooms produce.
  Town shop plus workshop. Items: stock of books in stacks and bags, blocks stored in racks (hangi-gura), a
  publishing ledger. Colour prints: black (sumizuri-e), hand-coloured (tan-e, beni-e, urushi-e), and two- or
  three-colour prints (benizuri-e, from c.1744) are in era; full-colour nishiki-e are later [outside 1680-1750: 1765].
- **Block carver (hangi-shi / hori-shi)**: carves text and images into cherry blocks. Town workshop; raised floor.
  Items: cherry blocks, knives (hangi-tō), chisels and gouges, a mallet, a block stand, the pasted-down design.
- **Printer (suri-shi)**: prints from the blocks by hand. Town workshop; raised floor. Items: a baren (disc rubber),
  brushes, dishes of ink and colour, paper damp in a stack, registration marks (kentō) cut in the block, drying lines.
- **Bookbinder (seihon-shi)**: pouch-fold (fukuro-toji) stab-sewn books. Usually part of the publisher. Items: folded
  sheets, an awl and mallet, thread, a cutting board and big knife (tachi-bōchō), press boards, covers and title slips.
- **Bookseller (shomotsu-ya, jihon-ya, ezōshi-ya)**: learned books (Kyoto, Osaka) vs. Edo's own popular books and
  prints (jihon, ezōshi). Town shop. Items: books laid flat on the raised floor, prints hung on lines at the front,
  book bags. Source: WP.
- **Lending library (kashihon-ya)**: lent books from a pack carried door to door; in Edo there were hundreds by 1808
  [the heyday is outside 1680-1750, but it existed earlier]. `itinerant` with a base room. Items: a tall wrapped book
  pack, ledger.
- **Ink-stick maker (sumi-ya)**: Nara ink: pine or rapeseed-oil soot, hide glue, kneaded and pressed, dried in ash for
  weeks. Town workshop. Items: rows of oil lamps under clay covers for soot collection (a soot room), a kneading
  board, wooden moulds, ash trays for drying, sticks hung in straw. Source: WP (Kobaien, Nara).
- **Brush maker (fude-shi)**: animal-hair brushes (horse, deer, weasel, goat). Town workshop; raised floor. Items:
  hair bundles, combs for sorting, a small iron for singeing, bamboo shafts, glue, finished brushes. Leather link:
  animal hair.
- **Inkstone carver (suzuri-shi)**: carves inkstones. Town workshop. Items: stone blanks, chisels, polishing stones.
  Niche.
- **Seal carver (inban-shi, hanko-ya)**: seals in wood, stone, horn. Town shop. Items: seal stock, fine knives, a seal
  vice, seal paste (shuniku), impressions on paper as samples.
- **Doll maker (ningyō-shi)**: dolls for the Girls' Festival (the Kyōhō-bina style of 1716-36 is in era), gosho
  dolls, costumed dolls. Town workshop and shop; raised floor. Items: carved wooden or paulownia-paste heads, shell-white
  gofun coats, fine brushes, straw-bundle bodies, silk costume pieces, display tiers; seasonal doll markets (hina-ichi).
  Source: WP.
- **Clay-doll maker (tsuchi-ningyō)**: Fushimi dolls, moulded and painted, sold to pilgrims. Town workshop with small
  kiln. Items: plaster/clay moulds, a small kiln, paint pots, dolls in rows.
- **Mask carver (men-uchi)**: nō and kyōgen masks. Niche; town workshop. Items: cypress blocks, chisels, gofun, paint.
- **Buddhist sculptor and altar maker (busshi, butsudan-ya)**: statues and household altars; cross-reference BL-C
  religious. Town workshop. Items: carving blocks, chisels, lacquer, gold leaf, half-carved figures.
- **Rosary maker (juzu-ya)**: prayer beads. Town shop near temples. Items: bead lathe, drills, thread, tassels.
- **Incense maker (senkō-ya, kō-ya)**: incense sticks from powdered cedar leaves or tabu bark (Sakai is a centre) and
  fine aromatics. Town workshop and shop. Items: mill for leaf powder (a stone mill, or a water mill in the country),
  kneading tub, rolling boards, drying racks of stick incense, aromatic wood (jinkō) in boxes. The mechanical extruder
  is [uncertain date]. Source: WP.
- **Candle maker (rōsoku-ya)**: Japanese candles from sumac (haze) wax, hand-coated in layers around a paper-and-rush
  wick. Town workshop; raised floor. Items: a heated wax pan, wooden skewers (kushi) with wicks, a rolling board,
  candle racks, finished candles by size. Source: WP (warōsoku).
- **Tortoiseshell and horn carver (bekkō-shi, tsuno-zaiku)**: combs, hairpins and small goods of tortoiseshell and
  horn, heat-welded. Town workshop. Items: shell plates, a heated press, saws, files, polishing. Netsuke carvers
  (netsuke-shi, rising in this period) are similar.
- **Musical-instrument makers (koto-shi, shakuhachi, biwa, fue)**: each a small town workshop. Items: kiri koto
  bodies, bridges, strings; bamboo root sections for shakuhachi; flute bamboo and burning irons. Merge all.
- **Toy and kite maker (omocha-ya, tako-ya)**: kites, tops, paddles (hagoita), rattles, papier-mâché figures (hariko).
  Town shop. Items: paper, bamboo, paint, toys strung up at the front.
- **Playing-card maker (karuta-ya)**: printed and hand-coloured cards (unsun karuta); Kyoto's Gojō. Town workshop.
  Items: card stock, blocks, colour pots. Gambling links; the flower cards (hanafuda) are later [outside 1680-1750].
- **Cosmetics maker and shop (oshiroi-ya, beni-ya)**: white face powder, safflower lip colour, tooth blackening
  (ohaguro) materials, hair oil (bintsuke). Town shop. Items: powder jars, beni cups, oil pots, small mirrors.
  Source: HJ.
- **Toothpick and toothbrush shop (yōji-ya)**: willow-twig tooth brushes (fusa-yōji), tooth powder; Asakusa's temple
  approach was famous for them [its heyday outside 1680-1750: c.1760s]. Town shop. Items: bundles of fusa-yōji, tooth
  powder packets.
- **Spectacle maker (megane-ya)** [uncertain date]: lenses ground from crystal or glass. Rare. Items: grinding stones,
  frames of horn or tortoiseshell.
- **Cloisonné worker (shippō-shi)**: enamelled metal fittings; the Hirata family worked for the shogunate. Niche;
  merge with ornamental metal.
- **Firework maker (hanabi-ya)**: Kagiya (from 1659); the Ryōgoku river-opening fireworks began in 1733 (in era).
  Town-edge workshop with fire rules. Items: powder jars, paper tubes, a rolling board, fuses, finished rockets.
  Gunpowder link; may be cut.

## 7. Food and drink

### 7a. Brewing and fermenting

- **Sake brewery (saka-gura)**: winter-brewed sake (kan-zukuri); Itami, Ikeda, Nada, Fushimi. Complex of kura around a
  yard. `site`/town complex; doma inside, raised mezzanines. Items: rice-polishing mortars (foot-driven or water mill),
  big rice steamer (koshiki) on a cauldron, a koji room (kōji-muro, insulated, with koji trays: kōji-buta), starter tubs
  (moto-oke), huge fermentation tubs (ō-oke) with ladders and walkways, stirring poles (kai), a press box (fune) with
  cloth bags and a **lever press weighted with stones** (tenbin-shibori), a well, casks (taru), and the cedar ball
  (sugidama) under the eaves. Source: WP; WORLD_CATALOGUE T25; NS.
- **Sake shop (saka-ya)**: retail, sells by measure into customers' flasks. Standing and sitting to drink in the shop
  grew into the izakaya during the 18th c. Town shop; doma. Items: casks on a rack, a tap, wooden measures (masu), tin
  and ceramic flasks (tokkuri), a ladle, a crate or cask to sit on, the sugidama or a sake-brand kanban. Source: web
  (kikkoman.com, izakaya history); WP.
- **Shōchū still (shōchū-gura)**: distilling sake lees (kasutori) or, in Kyushu, sweet potato. `rural` or brewery-side.
  Items: a helmet-type pot still (kabuto-gama), cooling water tub, fuel, jars. Regional; may cut.
- **Mirin and sweet-sake maker (mirin-ya)** [uncertain how widespread in 1730]: fortified sweet rice liquor; also
  sold as a drink. Items: as brewery on a small scale, shōchū casks.
- **Koji maker (kōji-ya)**: grows koji mould on rice or barley and sells it to miso, soy and home brewers. Town workshop;
  doma plus an underground or thick-walled warm room. Items: kōji-muro (earth-walled cellar), wooden trays (kōji-buta)
  stacked, straw mat covers, a steamer, a spreading table. Source: WP.
- **Soy-sauce brewery (shōyu-gura)**: Noda, Chōshi and Yuasa; Tatsuno light soy from 1666. `site`/town complex; doma.
  Items: roasting pan for wheat, bean steamer, koji trays, huge cedar mash vats (moromi-oke) with walkways, mixing poles,
  a press box with cloth bags and a lever press, a heating cauldron (hi-ire), casks. Source: WP.
- **Miso maker (miso-ya)**: town workshop and shop; doma. Items: bean steamer, a mortar or foot-masher for beans, koji
  trays, tall miso vats with stones on the lids, a wooden spatula, tubs of miso at the counter scooped by weight.
- **Vinegar brewery (su-ya)**: rice vinegar and, later, sake-lees vinegar (the Mitsukan type is later [outside
  1680-1750: 1804]). Town workshop; doma. Items: fermenting vats, casks, ladles, jars.
- **Pickle maker and shop (tsukemono-ya)**: takuan (radish), salt pickles, miso pickles. Town shop; doma. Items:
  pickle vats with heavy stones on lids, bran beds, radishes drying on racks outside, tubs at the counter.

### 7b. Milling and staples

- **Water mill (suisha-goya)**: polishes and grinds rice, grain, buckwheat; later also oil seed and incense powder.
  `site` on a stream; wheel plus hut; doma. Items: undershot or overshot wheel, a shaft with cams lifting pestles
  (kine) into mortars (usu, the pounding-stamp type), a stone grinding mill (ishi-usu), sieves (furui), sacks and straw
  bags, a sluice gate. Source: WORLD_CATALOGUE T38; WP.
- **Rice polisher (tsuki-gome-ya)**: polishes brown rice by foot-pounding (a treadle mortar, fumi-usu / kara-usu) in
  town. Town workshop; doma. Items: several treadle mortars in a row, sieves, bran baskets, rice bales. Edo's
  polishers also went door to door. Source: JK; MM.
- **Flour shop (kona-ya)**: wheat, buckwheat and bean flours (kinako). Town shop; doma. Items: hand quern (ishi-usu),
  sieves, flour bins, scoop and measure.
- **Tofu shop (tōfu-ya)**: town shop, open early; doma, wet. Items: stone hand mill (ishi-usu) for soaked beans, big
  cauldron on a hearth (kamado), straining cloth bag and wooden press, coagulant (nigari), wooden forming boxes with
  holes, water tanks holding tofu blocks, a frying pot for fried tofu (abura-age, ganmodoki). The recipe book *Tōfu
  hyakuchin* is later (1782), but the shop is old. Source: WP; JK.
- **Konnyaku maker (konnyaku-ya)**: devil's-tongue jelly. Town or `rural`; doma. Items: grating board, cauldron, lime
  water, forming trays, water tanks.
- **Tofu-skin maker (yuba-ya)**: Kyoto and Nikkō. Items: shallow heated trays of soy milk, bamboo skewers for lifting
  sheets, drying rack. Niche; merge with tofu.
- **Wheat-gluten maker (fu-ya)**: raw and baked gluten (fu) for temple cooking. Town workshop. Items: kneading tub in
  water, steamer, baking plates. Niche.
- **Nattō maker (nattō-ya)**: fermented beans in straw wrappers, sold on Edo streets at dawn. Town workshop; warm room.
  Items: bean steamer, straw wrappers (wara-zuto), a warm cellar.
- **Hand-stretched noodle maker (sōmen-ya)**: dried thin noodles (Miwa, Banshū), made in winter. `rural` workshop with
  a yard. Items: kneading tubs, oiled coils in tubs, stretching poles in frames (hashi), long drying racks with
  curtains of noodles, cutting board, straw-bundled noodles. Source: NS.
- **Kudzu and starch maker (kuzu-ya)**: Yoshino kudzu starch from roots washed repeatedly. `rural` `site` by clean water.
  Items: root-crushing mortar, washing tubs in a row, settling tanks, drying boxes. Niche.
- **Agar maker (kanten-ya)**: freeze-dried seaweed jelly (invented in the 1650s at Fushimi, in era), made in winter
  fields. `rural` `site`. Items: seaweed boiling cauldron, straining bags, trays, drying racks in the frost. Niche.

### 7c. Sweets and snacks

- **Fine confectioner (jōgashi-ya / kashi-ya)**: refined sweets for the elite and gifts (e.g. Toraya, Kyoto). Town
  shop; raised floor, quiet and clean. Items: moulds (kashi-gata) of carved wood, bean-paste pans, sugar jars, sweets on
  lacquered trays, tiered boxes, a display case. Source: WP.
- **Cheap sweet shop (dagashi-ya, zatsugashi)**: sweets for commoners and children. Town shop. Items: jars and
  baskets of sweets, a small griddle, children's toys.
- **Rice-cake and dumpling shop (mochi-ya, dango-ya)**: pounded mochi, skewered dango, seasonal rice cakes (the cherry
  leaf sakura-mochi is from 1717, in era). Town shop or roadside; doma. Items: mortar and mallet (usu, kine) for
  pounding, steamer, charcoal grill with skewers, bean paste pots, sweet soy glaze. Source: WP (sakura-mochi).
- **Steamed-bun shop (manjū-ya)**: bean-paste buns. Town shop; doma. Items: steamers stacked on a cauldron, paste pots,
  buns on trays. Merge with mochi-ya.
- **Rice-cracker shop (senbei-ya)**: grilled crackers (period senbei were often wheat-based; the salty rice senbei
  type spread later [uncertain]). Items: griddle irons, charcoal, jars.
- **Candy maker (ame-ya)**: starch-syrup candy (mizu-ame), pulled candy, sold by candy peddlers. Town workshop or
  `itinerant`. Items: syrup cauldron, pulling hook, cutting board. The sugar-sculpture street craft is [uncertain date].
- **Amazake shop (amazake-ya)**: sweet fermented rice drink, served hot. Hakone's **Amazake-chaya** on the old
  Tōkaidō is one such shop, said to date to the early Edo period. Roadside tea house or `itinerant` with a pot.
  Items: a big pot on a hearth, cups, amazake tubs. Source: WP; Hakone Amazake-chaya.

### 7d. Eating places

- **Soba shop (soba-ya, soba-kiri)**: buckwheat noodles; common in Edo by the 1700s. In this period, soba was often
  **steamed in stacked trays (seiro)** rather than boiled. Town shop; doma for the kitchen, raised floor for eaters.
  Items: kneading bowl (kone-bachi, lacquered), rolling board (noshi-ita) and pins (menbō), wide noodle knife
  (soba-kiri bōchō) and cutting guide (koma-ita), seiro steamers, boiling cauldron, dipping-sauce pots, a buckwheat
  stone mill. Source: WP; MM.
- **Udon and cheap bowl shop (udon-ya, kendon-ya)**: noodles in bowls, served fast and cheap; the "kendon" shops date
  from the 1660s (in era). Town shop. Items: as soba, plus bowls on a counter. Merge with soba.
- **Cheap cook-shop (ichizen-meshiya, meshi-ya)**: rice, soup and a side for labourers. Town shop; doma with benches
  and a raised edge. Items: rice cauldron, soup pot, rice tub (ohitsu), bowls on trays, a board menu (wood strips).
- **Cooked-food and sake shop (nimeuri-ya / niuri-sakaya)**: simmered fish and vegetables with sake; the forerunner of
  the izakaya, emerging in the early 18th c. Town shop; doma, benches, barrels as seats. Items: simmering pots (nabe),
  skewers, sake casks, flasks, dishes. Source: web (kikkoman.com).
- **Tea-rice shop (Nara-chameshi-ya)**: rice cooked in tea with tofu soup; from Asakusa after 1657. Town shop. Items:
  as meshiya. Merge.
- **Restaurant (ryōri-jaya)**: sit-down restaurant in rooms, for parties; the grand ryōtei are later [outside
  1680-1750: late 18th c. on]. Town building with tatami rooms and a kitchen with doma. Items: kitchen: multiple kamado,
  cutting boards (manaita) on legs, knives, fish tubs, a live-fish tank (ikesu); rooms: individual tray tables (zen),
  lacquered bowls, sake flasks, braziers, lanterns.
- **Eel shop (unagi-ya, kabayaki)**: split and grilled eel with soy-based glaze; stalls in the Genroku era, shops
  multiplying later in the 18th c. [shops mostly c.1770s+, uncertain]. Town shop or stall. Items: long charcoal grill
  with a fan (uchiwa), skewers, eel tubs and a live-eel basket, an eel-splitting board with a spike, glaze pot.
  Source: web (wattention.com, gotokyo).
- **Sushi seller (sushi-ya)**: in 1730 sushi means pressed and quick-vinegared sushi (haya-zushi, oshi-zushi, box
  sushi); fermented nare-zushi still exists. Nigiri is later [outside 1680-1750: c.1820s]. Town shop or peddler with a
  box. Items: pressing boxes and heavy stones, rice tubs (hangiri), vinegar jar, bamboo leaves, cut fish, lacquered
  carrying boxes. Source: WP.
- **Tempura stall (tempura yatai)** [outside 1680-1750: stalls from c.1770s-80s]. Items: oil pot on a charcoal stove,
  skewers, batter bowl.
- **Dengaku stall or shop (dengaku-ya)**: skewered tofu or konnyaku grilled with miso. Stall or roadside shop.
  Items: charcoal grill, skewers, miso pot. Simmered oden in broth is later [uncertain].
- **Wild-meat shop (momonji-ya)** [uncertain; mostly later Edo]: boar and deer ("mountain whale") sold as medicine
  in winter. Town shop. Items: hanging carcass quarters, a pot, a chopping block.
- **Chinese-style banquet house (shippoku)** [outside 1680-1750: Nagasaki style spread to Kyoto and Edo later in the
  18th c., uncertain]. Items: round shared table, shared dishes. Probably cut.
- **Food stall (yatai) family**: portable roofed stall on two poles or a shoulder-pole stand, used for soba (the night
  "yotaka soba"), sushi, dengaku, amazake, cold water sold in summer (hiyamizu-uri), barley tea (mugiyu). `stall`.
  Items: a small roof, a charcoal stove, a pot, bowls on a shelf, a lantern with the dish name.

### 7e. Tea houses (one family, many forms)

- **Roadside tea house (kake-jaya / chamise)**: open-fronted thatch or board shed on the highway or a temple approach;
  tea, dumplings, rest. `stall`/small building; doma with benches (shōgi) covered in red felt or mats. Items: benches,
  a kettle on a hearth or portable stove, tea bowls on a tray, a tea caddy, dumpling grill, straw sandals for sale
  hung under the eaves, a reed screen (yoshizu). Source: KA; WORLD_CATALOGUE T18.
- **Rest-station tea house (tateba-jaya)**: larger tea houses at the official rest stops between post towns (Hatajuku
  on the Hakone road is one), where porters and horses changed. Roadside building; doma plus raised rooms. Items: as
  kakejaya, plus raised tatami rooms, meal trays, horse tie posts, palanquin rack, local specialty display. Source: web
  (hakone-japan.com).
- **Water tea house (mizu-jaya)**: tea houses at shrines, temples and parks in cities. As kakejaya. Merge.
- **Sencha stall (baisaō style)**: in 1735 the monk Baisaō began selling brewed sencha from a portable stall in Kyoto,
  and Nagatani Sōen's steamed sencha dates from 1738 (both in era). `stall`. Items: a portable stove, a small kettle,
  a teapot for leaf tea, cups, a bamboo carrying frame.
- **Theatre tea house (shibai-jaya)**: booked seats, meals and rests for kabuki audiences. Town building beside a theatre.
  Items: tatami rooms, meal boxes, lanterns with the theatre crests. Cross-reference the theatre in Services.
- **Sumō tea house (sumō-jaya)**: as shibai-jaya for sumō. Merge.
- **Guide tea house for the pleasure quarter (hiki-te-jaya)**: see Services, pleasure quarters.
- **Rendezvous tea house (deai-jaya)**: rooms rented by the hour. Neutral; cut candidate.
- **Tea-leaf dealer (cha-ya, cha-don'ya)**: sells leaf tea and powdered tea; Uji tea for the elite. Town shop. Items:
  big sealed tea jars (chatsubo), tea boxes, a stone tea mill (cha-usu) for matcha, scales, sample dishes, a roasting pan
  (hōji-nabe) [uncertain], paper packets.

### 7f. Food retail (see also Retail)

- **Rice dealer (kome-ya)**: retail rice by measure. Town shop; doma. Items: rice bales (tawara) stacked, rice bins,
  masu measures, a strike stick (tokaki), sieve, scoop. Source: JK.
- **Fishmonger (sakana-ya)**: town shop; doma, wet. Items: cutting boards (manaita), deba knives, fish tubs and baskets
  (bote), a live-fish tank (ikesu) or tub, salt fish, dried fish on a rack, a shoulder pole for the rounds. Most fish
  was sold by peddlers (bote-furi). Source: JK; MM.
- **Fish market (uogashi, uo-ichi)**: wholesale fish at dawn (Edo Nihonbashi; Osaka Zakoba). Waterside `site`: open
  sheds, stalls on a quay. Items: boards on trestles, tubs, baskets, big knives, tally boards, boats at the quay.
  Source: WP.
- **Kamaboko maker (kamaboko-ya)**: fish paste steamed or grilled; Odawara is famous [the Odawara fame is late 18th c.,
  uncertain]. Town workshop; doma. Items: stone mortar and pestle (suribachi) for paste, boards, steamers, charcoal
  grill, fish scraps bin.
- **Greengrocer (yaoya, aomono-ya)**: town shop; doma. Items: baskets and shelves of vegetables, radishes, eggplant,
  scales, a shoulder pole. Source: JK.
- **Vegetable market (yacchaba)**: wholesale greens (Kanda in Edo). `site`: sheds and stalls. Merge with market stalls.
- **Dried-goods shop (kanbutsu-ya)**: kelp (kombu, from Hokkaidō via the Kitamae ships), dried bonito (katsuobushi),
  dried fish, beans, dried mushrooms. Town shop plus kura. Items: bundled kombu, katsuobushi in boxes, dried fish on
  strings, jars, scales.
- **Salt shop (shio-ya)**: salt by the straw bag. Town shop. Items: salt bags (shio-dawara), bins, measures.
- **Water seller (mizu-ya)**: in low-lying Edo (Fukagawa) good water was sold by the bucket from boats. `itinerant`
  with a boat and barrels.

## 8. Lodging

- **Post-town inn (hatago)**: the standard inn with meals; two storeys, lattice front, rooms upstairs. Around 1730 the
  Tōkaidō's 53 stations held thousands of hatago (WORLD_CATALOGUE T30 cites about 3,000, an 1843 figure). Distinct
  building; doma entrance with a raised hall. Items: an entrance doma with a foot-washing tub (ashi-arai darai) and
  stool, the step (agari-kamachi), the front desk (chōba) with the guest register (yado-chō), a large kitchen with a
  row of kamado and a well, guest rooms with tatami, lamps (andon), tobacco trays, braziers, folded bedding (yogi, the
  inn's rental bedding) and a mosquito net (kaya), meal trays (zen), a bath room (a wooden tub: goemon-buro in the
  west, teppō-buro in the east), a privy, a sign-lantern outside and the confraternity plaques (see kō-yado).
  Touts (tome-onna) pulled travellers in. Source: KA; Hiroshige; WORLD_CATALOGUE T30.
- **Post-town inn with serving women (meshimori hatago)**: many hatago legally employed "rice-serving women"
  (meshimori-onna), who were also a form of licensed prostitution at post towns; the shogunate set limits per inn.
  Same building as a hatago; this is a staffing variant. Neutral; **cut candidate** (keep the building, drop the role).
  Source: WP.
- **Firewood-fee lodging (kichin-yado)**: the cheapest lodging: travellers brought their own rice and paid only for the
  firewood (kichin) to cook it. Shared rooms. Nagaya shell. Items: a shared hearth and cauldron, a firewood stack, straw
  mats, shared quilts, a cooking pot rental. Source: WORLD_CATALOGUE T18, T30.
- **Hot-spring inn (yu-yado / tōji-yado)**: at Hakone's seven springs (Hakone Nanayu: Yumoto, Tōnosawa, Miyanoshita,
  Dōgashima, Sokokura, Kiga, Ashinoyu). Ichinoyu (Tōnosawa) dates from 1630. Guests stayed for cure periods of
  weeks, often **cooking for themselves**; the inns rented pots and sold fuel. One-night stays by ordinary travellers
  were a legal fight with the post towns [outside 1680-1750: the Hakone ruling of 1811]. Building: inn rooms around a
  bath house fed by a spring pipe. Items: an indoor bath (uchiyu) with a wooden or stone tub fed by bamboo or wooden
  pipes, a changing area with shelves, communal self-catering kitchen (rental pots, charcoal), guest register, long-stay
  rooms with bedding. Source: web (hakone-japan.com, Tsutaya; mlit.go.jp, tōji); WP (Hakone onsen).
- **Communal hot-spring bath (soto-yu, kyōdō-yu)**: the village's public spring bath shared by inns and locals.
  Small bath house over a pool. Items: stone- or wood-lined pool, a roof, changing shelf, a bucket, a small shrine to
  the spring. Open-air rock pools as in modern resorts are not the typical period form.
- **Confraternity inn (kō-yado)**: inns affiliated with pilgrim or travel confraternities, marked with signboards under
  the eaves. The big Naniwa-kō network is later [outside 1680-1750: 1804]; Ise-kō affiliated inns existed earlier
  [uncertain]. A hatago variant. Items: as hatago plus a row of kō plaques (kō-kanban).
- **Charity lodging for pilgrims (zenkon-yado)**: farmers or temples lodging pilgrims for free as merit (common on the
  Shikoku route). Farmhouse room; cross-reference BL-A and BL-C.
- **Lawsuit inn (kuji-yado)**: inns in Edo, Osaka and Kyoto that lodged provincial litigants and helped them with
  paperwork and court; the kujiyado formed guilds in 1699 (in era); Edo had about 100 of them, many in Bakurō-chō.
  Town inn. Items: as hatago, plus a writing room with document boxes, legal forms and a scribe's desk. Source: web
  (NDL, Bakurō-chō; Wikipedia kujishi).
- **Boat inn and boat hire (funa-yado)**: waterside houses renting pleasure boats (yakata-bune), fishing boats and
  gear, with rooms to wait, eat and drink. Waterside building with a landing. Items: moored boats, oars and poles,
  fishing rods, lanterns, a raised waiting room, sake and trays. Source: MM; WP.
- **Horse-dealers' inn (bakurō-yado)**: inns near horse markets for dealers and drovers, with stabling. Town inn with
  a stable yard. Items: as hatago plus tie rails, mangers and a fodder store. Merge with commercial stables.
- **Servants' lodging and hiring house (hito-yado)**: lodging for people seeking service jobs, run by the employment
  broker (see kuchiire-ya). Nagaya/machiya. Items: shared rooms, a register of job seekers.
- **Honjin and waki-honjin**: cross-reference only (BL-C). Note: when no daimyo was staying, the waki-honjin often
  took ordinary paying guests like a hatago.

## 9. Services

### 9a. Bathing, grooming and health

- **Public bathhouse (sentō, yuya)**: by 1730 the usual type is a shallow hot bath entered through a low gabled
  "pomegranate door" (zakuro-guchi) that held in the steam; mixed bathing was common; the upstairs rest room for men
  had tea, snacks and board games. The women attendants (yuna) of the earlier bathhouses were banned in Edo in 1657.
  Town building; board floor sloping to drains. Items: street entrance with a noren, a raised pay stand (bandai) with a
  cashbox, changing area with shelves (tana) or baskets, a washing floor (nagashi) of boards, small wooden buckets,
  the zakuro-guchi and the dim bath beyond, a boiler room behind (kama-ba) with a big cauldron and firewood from
  demolition timber, an upstairs rest room with a go board, tea kettle, tobacco tray. Source: WP; WORLD_CATALOGUE T27.
- **Steam bath (mushi-buro, todana-buro)**: the older steam-cabinet bath; in some regions natural stone steam baths
  (ishi-buro) by the sea. [Largely replaced in cities by 1730, uncertain]. Items: a small closed steam room, a hot-stone
  or boiling-water box, sliding door.
- **Barber (kamiyui-doko)**: shaved the pate and dressed men's topknots; also a social hub. Three forms: a shop in town
  (uchi-doko), a booth at a bridge or crossroads (de-doko), and itinerant barbers (mawari). Town shop or booth; raised
  floor where customers wait. Items: razors (kamisori), combs, pomade (bintsuke-abura), paper cord (motoyui) for tying,
  a water basin and hot water, a small mirror, a whetstone and strop, the customer's low stool, a tobacco tray, a go or
  shōgi board for those waiting. Source: WP; MM; HJ.
- **Women's hairdresser (onna-kamiyui)** [outside 1680-1750: professional women's hairdressers from the 1780s, and
  repeatedly banned]. Before that, women did their own hair or had a maid do it.
- **Masseur and acupuncturist (anma, harii)**: largely a blind men's guild (tōdō-za); masseurs walked the streets at
  night blowing a whistle. Base in a nagaya room; `itinerant`. Items: acupuncture needles in a case, a guide tube
  (shinkan, a 17th-c. Sugiyama Waichi innovation), a staff, a whistle, a cushion.
- **Moxa shop (mogusa-ya)**: sells moxa (dried mugwort) for home treatment; Ibuki moxa (Kameya at Kashiwabara on the
  Nakasendō) was famous. Town shop. Items: moxa bales, paper packets, incense sticks for lighting, mortars.
- **Doctor's practice (isha)**: Chinese-style medicine (kanpō); the doctor took the pulse and compounded remedies.
  Home practice in a machiya; raised tatami rooms. Items: a drug chopper (yagen: boat-shaped iron trough with a wheel),
  mortars, a medicine cabinet with many small drawers (hyakumi-dansu), measuring scales, paper packets, a portable
  medicine chest (yakurō) for house calls, medical books, a patient's cushion. Dutch-learning (ranpō) doctors are mostly
  later [outside 1680-1750: from the 1770s]. Source: WP.
- **Eye doctor and dentist (me-isha, kuchi-isha, ireba-shi)**: specialists existed; dentists made carved **boxwood
  dentures**. Town practice. Items: fine tools, a lamp, a carving kit for dentures, sample teeth.
- **Midwife (sanba)**: home-based; cross-reference BL-A.
- **Medicine shop (kusuri-ya, kigusuri-ya)**: sells ready remedies by brand; shop signs were often huge and
  three-dimensional. Town shop. Items: drug chopper (yagen), drawers cabinet, jars, scales, paper packets, pill-rolling
  board, brand signboards, a stone mortar. Odawara's **Uirō** shop (a pill and a sweet of the same name, on the map) is
  a famous example with a distinctive castle-style front. Source: WP (Uirō).
- **Medicine wholesaler (kusuri-don'ya)**: Osaka's Dōshō-machi and Edo's Honchō 3-chōme. Town shop plus kura. Items:
  bales of raw herbs, imported drugs, sorting tables.
- **Travelling medicine seller (Toyama baiyaku)**: Toyama's "use first, pay later" medicine peddlers (from c.1690, in
  era) left a box of medicines in each house. `itinerant`; base in Toyama. Items: tall stacked pack of wooden boxes,
  paper balloons as gifts [later], ledgers. Source: WP.

### 9b. Money, brokerage and business

- **Moneychanger (ryōgae-ya)**: exchanged gold, silver and copper coin; the big ones (hon-ryōgae) also did loans and
  bills; small ones (zeni-ryōgae) sold copper coin. Town shop plus a kura; raised floor behind a stout lattice.
  Items: balance scales and graded weights (fundō), a strong money chest (senryō-bako), coin strings (zeni-sashi) of
  copper coins, silver lumps (chōgin, mameitagin) and gold coins (koban), a touchstone, abacus, ledgers, a coin tray,
  a heavy lattice screen at the counter. The typical sign is a coin-shaped kanban. Source: WP; NE.
- **Pawnshop (shichi-ya)**: loans against goods; the kura is essential. Town shop with a kura; raised floor, often a
  side entrance for discretion. Items: pawn tickets (shichi-fuda), a tag board, a ledger, the kura full of tagged goods
  (clothes, swords, tools), abacus, scales. Source: WP; MM.
- **Rice broker and rice exchange (kome-don'ya; Dōjima kome-ichiba)**: rice wholesale and trading; the **Dōjima rice
  exchange in Osaka was officially recognized in 1730**, the anchor year, and traded rice futures. Town building and
  an open trading floor. Items: rice samples in dishes, rice tickets (kome-kitte), tally boards, water towers or flags
  used to signal prices [uncertain], ledgers, abacus, big warehouse kura on the canal. Source: WP (Dōjima).
- **Rice-stipend agent (fudasashi)**: in Edo's Kuramae, collected shogunate rice stipends for retainers, sold it, and
  lent against it. Town shop plus kura. Items: stipend papers stuck on a straw bundle (the "fuda" on a stick),
  ledgers, rice samples, money chest.
- **Wholesaler (ton'ya / don'ya), generic**: every trade had wholesale houses by street. Big machiya plus row of kura.
  Items: goods in bales and boxes, a loading doma, carts, tally boards, the house mark on everything.
- **Shipping agent (kaisen-don'ya)**: organized coastal shipping; the sake barrel ships (taru-kaisen) split from the
  general cargo ships (higaki-kaisen) in 1730 (in era). Harbour town shop plus warehouses. Items: shipping registers,
  cargo tags, samples, a lookout, the warehouse row.
- **Warehouse for rent (kura-kashi)**: rented kura on canals. Kura shell. Items: bales, ladders, shutters, tallies.
- **Private courier (machi-bikyaku, hikyaku-don'ya)**: private express couriers for letters, money and goods between
  Edo, Osaka and Kyoto (organized from 1663, in era). Town office. Items: letter boxes (fubako), a courier's
  shoulder-pole box, a bell, schedules, ledgers, a waiting bench for runners.
- **Employment broker (kuchiire-ya / hito-yado)**: placed servants, labourers and samurai-house footmen on contracts.
  Town office. Items: registers of workers, contract papers, a waiting room. Source: WP.
- **Scribe and petition writer (daisho-ya)**: wrote letters, deeds and petitions for the illiterate, often attached to
  the lawsuit inns. Town booth or room. Items: writing desk, inkstone, paper, seals. [uncertain as a separate trade].

### 9c. Transport

- **Palanquin stand (kago-ya, tsuji-kago)**: hired palanquins with bearers; street stands in cities and relay points on
  highways. Town office and a rack. Items: kago on racks, carrying poles, straw sandals for bearers, a bench, a lantern.
- **Packhorse carrier, commercial (uma-kata; chūma)**: private packhorse transport; Shinano's chūma carriers ran a big
  business competing with the official post horses (lawsuits in the 1760s). Commercial stable and yard. Items: see the
  commercial stable below. The official post-station horses are BL-C.
- **Commercial stable and horse dealer (bakurō, uma-ichi)**: horse dealers and seasonal horse markets; Edo's
  Bakurō-chō is named for them. Stable building plus a yard. Items: stalls with a manger (kaiba-oke), tie rails
  (uma-tsunagi), a fodder cutter (a lever blade: magusa-kiri), straw and hay stacks, pack saddles (ni-gura) and riding
  saddles, **straw horseshoes (uma-no-kutsu / uma-waraji)** (Japanese horses were not iron-shod), water trough,
  harness hooks, a horse-dealer's ledger. Source: WP.
- **Cart haulage (kuruma-hiki)**: carters with daihachi carts. Yard. Items: carts, ropes, straw pads. Merge with cart
  maker or stable.
- **Ox-cart haulage (ushi-kata)**: Kyoto-Ōtsu ox carts on stone-slab cart tracks. Regional; `site` stable for oxen.
  Items: oxen stalls, carts, yokes.

### 9d. Entertainment and other services

- **Kabuki theatre (shibai-goya)**: licensed theatres in Edo, Kyoto, Osaka, plus touring stages. Big building with a
  drum tower (yagura) over the entrance; pit floor with box seating. Items: the stage and runway (hanamichi),
  box-seat partitions (masu-seki), a curtain, lanterns, a drum tower, play-boards (banzuke). May belong to BL-C
  civic; listed here as commercial. Source: WP.
- **Puppet theatre (ningyō jōruri)**: Takemoto-za in Osaka from 1684 (in era). As kabuki, smaller. Items: stage with
  a hand-rail screen, puppets, chanter's dais, shamisen.
- **Show booth (misemono-goya)**: temporary booths for animals, acrobats and curiosities at temple fairs. `stall`.
  Items: a painted signboard, a curtain, benches.
- **Storytelling hall (yose)** [outside 1680-1750: from the 1790s]. Earlier: street storytellers (tsuji-kōshaku) at a
  small table.
- **Archery gallery (yōkyū-ba)** [uncertain; mostly mid- to late Edo]: small-bow shooting galleries with women
  attendants. Items: small bows, arrows, target, a drum.
- **Go and shōgi parlour (go-kaisho)**: rooms for playing board games for a fee. Town room. Items: go boards (goban),
  shōgi boards, bowls of stones, cushions, a tea kettle.
- **Gambling den (bakuchi-ba)**: illegal, but widespread in samurai mansions' servants' rooms and temples. Items: dice
  cups, a cloth mat, coins. Cut candidate.
- **Fortune-teller (eki-sha, uranai-ya)**: a street booth or table with a lantern, divination sticks (zeichiku) in a
  cylinder, counting rods (sangi), a book. `stall`.
- **Pleasure quarters (yūkaku)**: the licensed quarters (Edo's Yoshiwara, Kyoto's Shimabara, Osaka's Shinmachi) were
  walled districts with a single gate. Building types inside: brothel houses with a latticed front room where the
  women sat (jorō-ya / mise), the houses where top courtesans met clients for banquets (age-ya), the guide tea houses
  that booked them (hikite-jaya), and staff houses. Unlicensed districts (okabasho) existed in many towns, and the
  post-town meshimori inns are the Tōkaidō version. Items: lattice fronts (harimise), lanterns, tatami banquet rooms,
  shamisen, sake trays, bedding rooms. **Neutral listing; a strong cut candidate.** Source: WP.
- **Rental shop (sonryō-ya)** [uncertain date]: rented bedding, pots, clothes and furniture to the poor and to
  travellers. Town shop. Items: stacks of futon, pots, a ledger.
- **Recycling trades (kuzu-hiroi, furugane-kai, hai-kai, shimogoe)**: Edo recycled almost everything: waste-paper
  buyers, old-metal buyers, ash buyers (ash went to dyers and farmers), candle-drip buyers, old-umbrella buyers, and the
  night-soil trade (human waste sold to farmers as fertilizer). `itinerant`, with a dealer's yard or shed. Items:
  baskets, sacks, a shoulder pole, a sorting yard, a manure boat or buckets. Source: WP.
- **Street repairers (itinerant)**: clog-tooth replacer (geta no ha-ire), pot mender (ikake-ya), knife grinder, mirror
  polisher, pipe-stem replacer (rao-ya), china mender (yakitsugi [outside 1680-1750: late 18th c.]). All `itinerant`;
  props, not buildings.

## 10. Retail

(Each is a `town shop` with the standard kit in §0.1; items listed are what makes it read as that shop.)

- **General household goods (ara-mono-ya, yorozu-ya)**: brooms, buckets, pots, sieves, mats, straw goods. Items:
  goods hung from the eaves and piled on the doma, brooms in bundles, stacked tubs.
- **Ironmonger (kanamono-ya)**: tools, nails, pots, locks, knives. Items: tools on the wall, nails in boxes by size,
  iron pots stacked, scales.
- **Ceramics shop (setomono-ya)**: "Seto-mono" was the general word for ceramics in the east. Items: bowls and dishes in
  straw-packed stacks, shelves, a packing corner with straw.
- **Lacquerware shop (nurimono-ya)**: bowls, trays, boxes. Items: lacquered stacks on shelves, display trays, boxes.
- **Silk draper (gofuku-dana)**: Echigoya (Mitsui) in Edo sold at fixed cash prices and cut cloth to any length from
  1683 (in era). Big shop; long raised tatami sales floor with clerks. Items: bolts of silk (tan-mono) in rolls,
  sample books, cloth rule (kujira-jaku), hand shears, a room for high-ranking customers, rows of clerks' desks,
  a shop-boy corps (detchi) at the front, a clothes-rack display (ikō). Source: WP (Mitsukoshi); NE.
- **Cotton and hemp cloth shop (futomono-dana)**: plain "thick goods" for commoners. Items: as draper, cheaper.
- **Old-clothes shop (furugi-ya)**: most commoners bought used clothing; Edo's Tomizawa-chō and the Yanagihara
  embankment stalls. Items: garments hung on poles and bamboo racks, folded piles, a patching corner. Source: WP.
- **Haberdasher (komamono-ya)**: small goods: combs, hairpins, pouches, purses, netsuke, towels (tenugui), thread,
  needles. Items: small goods in shallow display boxes and on boards, hairpins in a straw stand.
- **Paper and stationery shop (kami-ya, fude-sumi-ya)**: paper, brushes, ink, inkstones, envelopes. Items: paper
  reams, brush racks, ink sticks in boxes.
- **Lamp-oil shop (abura-ya)**: rapeseed and fish oil for lamps; sold by measure into customers' jugs. Items: oil
  casks, ladles and funnels, measures, oil jugs, lamp wicks (tōshin) of rush pith. Source: JK; HJ.
- **Wick seller (tōshin-ya)**: rush-pith wicks. Merge with oil shop.
- **Candle shop (rōsoku-ya)**: see Fine crafts; retail side: candles by size in boxes, candle stands.
- **Charcoal and firewood dealer (sumi-ya, takigi-ya)**: charcoal in straw bales (sumi-dawara), firewood bundles.
  Town shop with a yard. Items: charcoal bales stacked, firewood bundles, scales, a charcoal saw, a sifter.
- **Tobacco shop (tabako-ya)**: cut tobacco (kizami), cut by hand with the Sakai knife from pressed leaf. Items: leaf
  bales, a cutting board with a clamp and the big knife, paper packets, pipes on display.
- **Pipe shop (kiseru-ya)**: see Metal. Retail side: pipes on racks, pouches.
- **Tea, sake, soy, miso, fish, vegetables, rice, dried goods, salt**: see §7.
- **Umbrella, clog, sandal and hat shop (kasa-ya, geta-ya, zōri-ya)**: see makers. Retail side: goods hung and stacked
  at the front.
- **Travel-goods shop (tabi-dōgu-ya)**: at post towns: sandals, straw raincoats, hats, pouches, pack frames, maps and
  guidebooks, lanterns. Items: straw sandals hung in bunches, mino and kasa, travel pillows, a guidebook stack.
- **Souvenir and local-specialty shop (meibutsu-ya)**: each post town's famous product (e.g. Odawara uirō, Hakone
  woodcraft, Arimatsu shibori, Kuwana clams). Items: the product displayed on a stand at the front, a sign naming the
  meibutsu.
- **Toy and doll shops**: see Fine crafts. Seasonal doll markets (hina-ichi) are stalls.
- **Plant nursery (ueki-ya)**: Edo's Somei village nurseries; Itō Ihei's azalea book of 1695 (in era). `rural`/edge of
  town yard. Items: potted plants on stepped stands, bonsai, trees in straw root-balls, watering buckets, a shed.
  Source: WP (Somei).
- **Goldfish seller (kingyo-ya)**: goldfish breeding in Kōriyama dates from 1724 (in era). Shop or `itinerant`. Items:
  tubs and ceramic basins, shallow ponds, dip nets.
- **Bird shop (kotori-ya)**: songbirds (bush warbler) in cages. Town shop. Items: bamboo cages stacked, feed boxes.
- **Insect seller (mushi-uri)** [uncertain; mostly mid- to late Edo]: singing insects in tiny cages. `itinerant`.
- **Flower seller (hana-ya)**: flowers for altars and graves, mostly by peddlers; shops near temples. `stall`.
- **Sword and arms dealer (tōken-ya, katana-ya)**: blades, fittings, armour. Town shop. Items: swords on racks, fittings
  in boxes, appraisal papers.
- **Second-hand goods dealer (furu-dōgu-ya)**: used furniture, tools, household goods. Town shop. Items: piles of
  assorted goods.
- **Old-metal dealer (furugane-ya)**: scrap iron and copper bought from peddlers. Town yard. Items: scrap heaps, scales.
- **Fertilizer dealer (hoshika-don'ya, shime-kasu)**: dried sardine fertilizer (hoshika) and oil-cake (abura-kasu,
  shime-kasu) for cotton and rice fields. Town shop plus kura. Items: straw bags of dried sardines, pressed oil-cake
  discs, scales.
- **Market stalls (ichi, rokusai-ichi, toshi-no-ichi)**: periodic markets (six a month in many towns) and year-end
  markets. `stall` kit: straw mats on the ground, trestle boards, small roofs, baskets, scales.
- **Street peddlers (furi-uri, bote-furi)**: much of daily retail came by shoulder pole: fish, vegetables, tofu, nattō,
  water, mosquito nets, goldfish, brooms. Not buildings; a prop kit (shoulder pole, baskets or tubs, a trade call).

## 11. Rural industry and resource sites

- **Charcoal kiln, black charcoal (kuro-zumi-gama)**: earth dome kiln on a hillside, smothered to cool. `site` in the
  forest. Items: earth or clay dome kiln with a flue, a stack of cut wood, charcoal bales (sumi-dawara) of straw or
  kaya, rakes (eburi) and long hoes, a thatched shelter hut. Source: WP; WORLD_CATALOGUE.
- **Charcoal kiln, white charcoal (shiro-zumi-gama / binchō)**: stone kiln; the charcoal is pulled out red-hot and
  smothered in sand and ash (keshi-bai). Kishū's binchō-tan dates from the Genroku era (in era). `site`. Items: stone and
  clay kiln, long iron rake, a sand-and-ash quench pit, oak (ubame-gashi) stacks.
- **Charcoal burner's hut (sumiyaki-goya)**: the shelter by the kiln. Items: a small hearth, straw mats, a water jar,
  axe and saw.
- **Salt field, spread type (agehama)**: labourers carry sea water to a levelled sand field, spread it, rake the dried
  salty sand into a filter box. Coastal `site`. Items: levelled sand field, sea-water buckets on a shoulder pole
  (shio-oke), wide rakes (manga), a filter box (numai / tare-bune) with a tap, brine vats. Source: WP; NS.
- **Salt field, tidal type (irihama)**: Seto Inland Sea (Akō); embanked fields filled by the tide through channels.
  `site`. Items: embankment, sluice gate, ditches, sand beds, as agehama. Regional; merge.
- **Salt-boiling hut (kama-ya)**: boils the brine in a big flat pan. Coastal `site`. Items: a wide shallow pan (iron,
  or the Inland Sea "stone pan" of stones and clay), a firebox under it, huge pine and pine-needle fuel stacks, brine
  vats, draining baskets for wet salt, a salt store (shio-naya), straw salt bags. Source: WP; NS.
- **Logging camp (soma-goya)**: fellers and log-skidders in state or domain forests. `site`. Items: axes (ono), felling
  saws [the felling saw spread in the Edo period; axes were primary], wedges, log chutes (shura) down a slope, a camp hut.
- **Timber rafting station (ikada-ba)**: logs bound into rafts on rivers. Riverbank `site`. Items: log rafts, rope and
  vine bindings, poles, a shed.
- **Gold or silver mine (kinzan, ginzan)**: Sado, Iwami, Ikuno; Izu's gold mines (Toi, Yugashima) peaked in the early
  17th c. [largely outside 1680-1750, uncertain how active]. `site`. Items: adit mouth (mabu) with timber supports,
  oil-lamp holders (kantera: shell lamps), picks and hammers (tsuchi, tagane), ore baskets, a drainage pump (the
  Archimedes-screw "suishō-rin" or bamboo pumps), ore-crushing stones and grinding mills, women's sorting and washing
  sheds with sluices, a smelting hut (fuki-ya) with small furnaces and bellows. Source: WP (Sado, Iwami).
- **Copper mine and smelter (dōzan)**: Besshi opened in 1691 (in era); Ashio. `site`. Items: as gold mine, plus
  roasting beds and a larger smelter, copper ingots.
- **Sulphur workings (iō-yama)**: sulphur was dug from volcanic vents for match-tinder and gunpowder. Hakone's Ōwakudani
  is a volcanic valley [uncertain whether worked in 1730]. `site`. Items: sulphur crust, baskets, a melting pot, a
  shelter.
- **Iron smelter (tatara, takadono)**: iron-sand smelting in a clay furnace rebuilt every firing; Izumo and the Chūgoku
  mountains (off-map). The **balance bellows (tenbin-fuigo)** is dated to 1691 (in era). `site`: a tall barn over the
  furnace, with iron-sand washing channels (kanna-nagashi) on the hillside. Items: clay furnace, two balance bellows,
  charcoal stores, iron-sand heaps, the bloom (kera), a sacred tree and shrine (Kanaya-go). Source: web (unnan-tatara.jp);
  WP.
- **Quarry**: see §5.
- **Lime kiln**: see §5.
- **Silkworm house (yōsan-ya / kaiko-beya)**: farmhouse upper floor or a special room with racks of trays; the tall
  multi-storey sericulture farmhouses are later [outside 1680-1750: 19th c.]. `rural`. Items: bamboo tray racks
  (kaiko-dana), flat woven trays (ebira / kaiko-kago), mulberry leaves in baskets, a leaf-chopping board and knife,
  straw cocooning frames (mabushi), a charcoal brazier for warmth. Cross-reference BL-A. Source: WP.
- **Indigo-processing barn (sukumo-ya / ai-nedoko)**: Awa (Tokushima) indigo: leaves composted for months on a clay
  floor, turned and watered, into sukumo. `rural` `site`: a tall barn with a thick clay floor. Items: clay composting
  bed (nedoko), wooden shovels and rakes, water buckets, straw mat covers, sukumo in straw bags, drying yard for leaves.
  Source: WP (Awa indigo).
- **Safflower processing (benibana)**: petals washed, fermented, pressed into cakes. `rural`. Items: tubs, straw mats,
  a press for beni-mochi, drying boards. Regional (Yamagata). Merge.
- **Wax works (rō-shibori, haze)**: crushes and steams sumac berries, presses out wax, then bleaches it in the sun.
  `rural` `site`. Items: stamp mill or mortar, steamer, a wedge press (shime-gi) with hemp bags, wax cakes, a
  bleaching field of shallow trays in the sun. Kyushu and Shikoku. Source: WP.
- **Oil press (abura-shime-ya)**: rapeseed and sesame for lamp and cooking oil; the oil-cake goes as fertilizer.
  Town workshop or `rural`; doma. Items: roasting pan, stone mortar or water stamp, steamer, hemp or straw bags, a
  **wedge press (shime-gi)** driven by a big hammer, oil casks, oil-cake stack. The water-driven oil mills of Hyōgo and
  Nishinomiya are later [outside 1680-1750: c.1790s+, uncertain]. Source: WORLD_CATALOGUE; WP.
- **Tea processing shed (seicha-goya)**: steaming, then hand-rolling and drying on a heated board. `rural` (Uji,
  Shizuoka). Items: steamer on a cauldron, cooling fan, a paper-lined drying board (hoiro) over a charcoal box, woven
  trays, tea jars. Source: WP (Uji tea; Nagatani 1738).
- **Tobacco drying shed**: leaves hung on ropes under the eaves or in a barn. `rural`. Items: strung leaves, poles,
  bales. Merge with farm barn.
- **Sugar press (satō-shime)** [outside 1680-1750 for the main islands: domestic sugar spread after Yoshimune's
  promotion and the Sanuki wasanbon of the 1790s; Satsuma and Ryūkyū earlier]. Items: ox-driven roller press, boiling
  pans. Probably cut.
- **Umeboshi works (ume-boshi)**: plums salted in vats and dried on mats in summer; Odawara's Soga plums are nearby.
  `rural`. Items: salting vats with stones, big bamboo drying mats (su) on trestles, jars.
- **Fishing-village net shed (ami-goya / naya)**: net storage and repair. Coastal `site`. Items: nets hung on poles,
  floats (uki) of wood or gourd, sinkers (clay or stone), oars, baskets, a tannin tub.
- **Fish-drying racks (himono-ba)**: split fish dried on racks; also sardine-drying on the beach. Coastal `site`.
  Items: bamboo racks, salt tubs, split fish, baskets.
- **Dried-sardine fertilizer works (hoshika)**: huge sardine catches (Kujūkuri beach) dried on the sand or boiled and
  pressed. Coastal `site`. Items: seine nets, drying sand, boiling cauldrons, presses, straw bags.
- **Dried-bonito works (katsuobushi-goya)**: boiling, deboning, smoking over hardwood fires for days, then curing with
  mould (the mould method is credited to Tosa, c.1674, in era). Izu's Tago and Nishi-Izu are on the map. Coastal
  `site`. Items: boiling cauldron, filleting boards and knives, bamboo trays, a smoke room with a hearth, shelves of
  smoked fillets. Source: WP (katsuobushi).
- **Nori farm and drying yard (nori-hibi, nori-hoshi)**: seaweed grown on bamboo stakes (hibi) in the shallows at
  Shinagawa and Ōmori on the Tōkaidō (from about the Genroku to Kyōhō eras, in era); chopped and dried in sheets on reed
  screens on wooden frames. Coastal `site`. Items: bamboo hibi stakes in the sea, small boats, chopping boards and
  knives, tubs, wooden frames (waku), reed mats (su), drying racks leaning to the sun. Source: WP (Asakusa nori).
- **Whaling station (kujira-gumi)**: organized net whaling (Taiji, Kyushu, Tosa); flensing on the beach. Regional,
  off-map; `site`. Items: flensing sheds, cauldrons for oil, huge knives, whale-bone racks. Probably cut.
- **Oyster beds (kaki)**: Hiroshima oyster farming; oyster boats that sold oysters in Osaka. Regional; cut candidate.
- **Fish weir (yana)**: river weir of bamboo slats to catch sweetfish (ayu) and eels. River `site`. Items: bamboo slat
  ramp, stakes, baskets, a watch hut.
- **Cormorant-fishing house (u-shō)**: cormorant masters on the Nagara and other rivers. Riverside house with cages.
  Items: cormorant baskets, torches (kagaribi) and iron fire baskets, narrow boats, leashes. Regional; cut candidate.
- **Hunter's hut (ryōshi-goya / matagi-goya)**: hunters for boar, deer and bear; guns required a domain licence
  (teppō-aratame), traps and spears were common; the Tōhoku bear hunters (matagi) are regional. `rural` forest hut.
  Items: spears, snares and deadfall traps, a skinning board, drying hides, meat hanging to smoke, a small hearth, straw
  boots. Source: WP.
- **Thatch meadow and store (kaya-ba)**: common land where thatch grass was cut; grass stored in stooks. Cross-reference
  BL-A/BL-C (village commons). Items: stooks, sickles, a grass barn.
- **Mushroom logs (shiitake)** [outside 1680-1750: Izu Amagi shiitake cultivation from the 1790s, uncertain]. Items:
  oak logs leaning in a shaded frame.

## 12. Tools and fittings that recur across many trades (the shared core)

These appear in so many workshops that a single "workshop dressing kit" could carry most of them, with a few
trade-specific hero items on top.

- **Heat:** hearth (kamado) with a cauldron (kama); charcoal brazier (hibachi) and portable stove; a charcoal bin and
  straw charcoal bales; the box bellows (fuigo) in every metal trade.
- **Water:** tubs and buckets (oke, taru) in every size; a well or a water jar (mizu-game) with a ladle (hishaku);
  soaking tubs (paper, bentwood, bamboo, leather, tatami rush).
- **Cutting and shaping:** saw (nokogiri), plane (kanna), chisel (nomi), knife (kogatana), adze (chōna), hatchet
  (nata), awl (kiri), files (yasuri), shears (hasami), whetstones (toishi) with a water trough.
- **Striking:** hammers and mallets (tsuchi, genno, kizuchi); a low work block or stump.
- **Measuring:** carpenter's square (sashigane), ink line (sumitsubo), cloth rule (kujira-jaku), balance scales and
  weights (tenbin, fundō), steelyard (sao-bakari), rice and liquid measures (masu).
- **Pressing:** the **lever press weighted with stones** (sake, soy, paper, oil variants) and the **wedge press
  (shime-gi)** (oil, wax): one mechanism, several trades.
- **Pounding and grinding:** mortar and pestle (usu and kine; suribachi), hand stone mill (ishi-usu), treadle mortar
  (fumi-usu), and the water-mill stamp: rice, flour, clay, ore, incense, oil seed, porcelain stone.
- **Drying:** bamboo poles and racks, drying boards leaning in the sun, rope lines, shinshi stretchers, straw mats on
  trestles: dyers, paper, noodles, fish, nori, umbrellas, tiles, pottery, hides, persimmons.
- **Steaming:** stacked steamers (seiro, koshiki) on a cauldron: sake, soy, miso, koji, paper bark, noodles, sweets.
- **Containers and packing:** straw bales (tawara), straw bags (kamasu), straw mats (mushiro, komo), baskets (kago,
  zaru), wooden boxes, casks, ceramic jars.
- **Surfaces:** a low work board or bench used while kneeling or cross-legged on a raised floor; trestles and long
  boards; the raised shop room and earth floor combination.
- **Glue and finish:** rice-paste glue (nori, sokui), hide glue (nikawa), persimmon tannin (kakishibu), lacquer
  (urushi), oil.
- **Shop-front kit:** noren, kanban, lattice, counter screen, abacus, ledgers, scales, tobacco tray, fire buckets (§0.1).
- **Religious corner:** almost every workshop and shop had a god-shelf (kamidana) with a small shrine, and many had a
  shimenawa, a lucky rake (kumade) or a beckoning cat [the beckoning cat (maneki-neko) is later: outside 1680-1750].

## 13. Obvious collapse candidates (advice only; Stephen decides)

1. **One "smithy" shell, three dressings.** Village smith, tool smith, knife smith, saw smith, file maker, nail smith,
   anchor smith → **Smithy** (forge, bellows, anvil, quench tub + a wall of the product). Swordsmith is the same shell
   with a shimenawa and a clean, darker forge room. Polisher, scabbard, hilt and fittings makers → one **"sword
   finishing"** raised-floor room.
2. **Casting.** Pot caster, mirror maker, pewterer → one **foundry** (crucible furnace, moulds, sand floor). Bell
   casting stays a site kit.
3. **Fine metal bench.** Ornamental metal, gold leaf, needle, wire, lock, pipe, stirrup, cloisonné, clock → one
   **metal bench workshop** on a raised floor with a small hearth; swap the product boards.
4. **Leather.** Carcass yard, white tanner, smoked leather, glue maker → one **riverside tannery site** (pits, frames,
   smoke hut, cauldron). Drum, saddle, pouch, inden, setta, glove, garment, shamisen → one **leather workshop** with a
   drum or saddle as the hero prop.
5. **Carpentry.** Carpenter's yard, sawyer, lumber yard, shingle splitter → one **timber yard** kit. Joiner, cabinet
   maker, box maker, abacus, masu, mortar maker, instrument makers → one **joinery workshop**.
6. **Round wood.** Cooper, tub maker, bentwood maker → one **cooperage**. Woodturner and Hakone woodcraft → one
   **lathe workshop** (and a souvenir shop front on the Hakone road).
7. **Bamboo.** Baskets, sieves, blinds, fans, umbrellas, tea whisks, lanterns, bows and arrows share split bamboo:
   a **bamboo workshop** with umbrella/lantern/fan as dressing variants, plus the umbrella drying yard.
8. **Straw and rush.** Straw goods, rope, rush mats, tatami, sedge hats, palm fibre → one **straw and rush workshop**
   (tatami maker could stay separate because it is a common town shop).
9. **Cotton and silk prep.** Ginner, bower, wadding, futon maker → one **cotton shop**. Silk reeling, floss, thread,
   sericulture → a **silk farmhouse** variant (BL-A shell).
10. **Weaving.** Village weaver, ikat, wild-fibre → one **loom room**; Nishijin drawloom is the "rich" variant.
11. **Dyeing.** Indigo, stencil, yūzen, shibori, safflower, black and crest dyers → one **dyer's shell** with sunken
    vats + yard frames; stencil cutter merges in. Indigo sukumo barn stays rural.
12. **Sewing.** Tailor, embroiderer, tabi maker, braid maker, mosquito nets → one **sewing room** on tatami.
13. **Kilns.** Noborigama, anagama, porcelain, enameller → one **pottery site**. Earthenware maker and tile maker
    could share a small-kiln **tile works**. Lime kiln and charcoal kiln are look-alike earth domes.
14. **Stone.** Mason's yard, quarry, whetstone dealer, inkstone carver → **mason's yard** plus a quarry site.
15. **Paper and print.** Paper mill, recycled paper, kakishibu → **paper mill** site. Publisher, carver, printer,
    binder, bookseller, lending library → one **publisher-bookshop** with a back print room. Mounter and painter could
    share a **painting studio**.
16. **Lacquer.** Lacquer refiner, lacquerer, maki-e → one **lacquer workshop** (the dust-free cupboard is the tell).
17. **Small sacred crafts.** Buddhist sculptor, rosary, incense, candle, doll, mask → a **"temple-street" workshop**
    with swapped goods.
18. **Brewing.** Sake, soy, miso, vinegar, mirin, koji, pickles → one **brewery complex** (tubs, press, steamer, koji
    room); the sake version is the P2 hero (sugidama). Small miso, pickle and koji shops are one **ferment shop**.
19. **Wet food shops.** Tofu, konnyaku, yuba, fu, nattō → one **tofu shop**.
20. **Mills.** Water mill, rice polisher, flour shop, and the milling steps of oil, incense and clay → **water mill**
    site + a **treadle-mortar shop** in town.
21. **Eating.** Soba, udon, kendon, meshiya, nimeuri, chameshi → one **eatery** (kitchen doma + raised eating edge);
    restaurant is the upscale version. Sweets: mochi, manjū, senbei, candy, cheap sweets → one **sweet shop**; the
    fine confectioner is a dressing upgrade.
22. **Tea houses.** Kakejaya, tateba-jaya, mizujaya, amazake shop, sencha stall → one **roadside tea house** in three
    sizes (bench shed, open shop, tateba with rooms). Theatre, sumō and rendezvous tea houses → one town tea house.
23. **Stalls.** Every yatai, market stall, fortune-teller, barber booth, flower seller → a **stall kit** with swapped
    props.
24. **Lodging.** Hatago, meshimori hatago, kō-yado, kuji-yado, horse-dealers' inn → one **hatago** with sign variants;
    kichin-yado and hito-yado → a nagaya **cheap lodging**; hot-spring inn and communal bath → an **onsen inn** plus
    a bath house (Hakone-specific; worth keeping distinct).
25. **Money.** Moneychanger, pawnshop, rice broker, stipend agent, lender → one **merchant-with-kura** shop (strong
    lattice, money chest, scales, ledgers).
26. **Health.** Doctor, medicine shop, moxa, acupuncture, dentist → one **apothecary** (drug chopper, drawer cabinet).
27. **Retail.** Most retail differs only by goods. A few shells (small shop, big draper-style shop, wholesaler with
    kura) with swapped goods cover general goods, ironmonger, ceramics, lacquerware, draper, old clothes,
    haberdasher, paper, oil, charcoal, tobacco, travel goods, souvenirs, arms, second-hand.
28. **Transport.** Commercial stable, horse dealer, packhorse carrier, palanquin stand, cart haulage → one
    **stable yard** (straw horseshoes and pack saddles as hero props).
29. **Rural coast.** Net shed, fish-drying racks, sardine works, katsuobushi smokehouse, nori drying yard → one
    **fishing-village kit** with rack variants; salt pans plus boiling hut stay distinct.
30. **Mines.** Gold, silver, copper, sulphur → one **mine site** (adit, sorting shed, smelter hut); tatara is off-map
    and could be cut.
31. **Likely cuts outright** (off-map, niche, or out of era): gunsmith, tinplate, clockmaker, spectacles, whaling,
    oysters, cormorants, sugar press, shippoku, tempura stalls, women's hairdresser, yose, insect sellers, shiitake,
    hanafuda, the pleasure quarters (keep or cut is Stephen's call), gambling den.

# A: Dwellings, every class, country and city (brainstorm list for collapsing)

Agent BL-A, 2026-09-29. Text only. Deliberately over-detailed: Stephen collapses it.

**Lane:** homes and their outbuildings. Shops, inns, workshops and breweries are BL-B. Offices, guardhouses, temples,
shrines and village granaries are BL-C. Where a home has a shop or office, only the living rooms are here.

**Tags used:**
- `[outside 1680-1750: date]` = real, but from outside the window. Listed so Stephen can decide.
- `[off-map: region]` = in the window but far from the Kyoto–Osaka–Edo / Tōkaidō / Nakasendō map in WORLD_CATALOGUE.
- `[survivors late]` = what you can visit today is a late-Edo or Meiji rebuild; the type is older.
- `(assumed)` / `(verify)` = my best reading, not pinned to a source.

**Room words used below:**
- *doma*: earth floor at the entrance / kitchen end. *niwa* is the same in western Japan.
- *itanoma*: board-floored room. *hiroma*: the big board room with the irori in a Kantō three-room house.
- *doza*: living on packed earth covered with straw and mushiro mats instead of boards.
- *nando / heya / nema*: the closed sleeping room, often with a raised sill to hold in the straw bedding.
- *dei / zashiki / oku*: front best room / formal room / back room.
- *daidokoro*: the family room next to the kitchen doma.
- *tōri-niwa*: the earth passage running the depth of a machiya. *hashiri-niwa*: its kitchen stretch.
- *zushi-nikai*: the low, attic-like upper storey of a Kamigata townhouse.
- *hibukuro*: the tall open smoke void above a machiya kitchen.

**Two cross-cutting rules that shape every interior (from PLAYBOOK §2):** no tatami in poor rural homes; commoners,
however rich, stay plain inside (no nageshi, no tsuke-shoin, no patterned paper, no lacquer or gold on the building).
Bedding is also a class marker in 1730: towns have cotton futon, and the rural poor mostly sleep on straw under mats
or a sleeved quilt (yogi / kaimaki). Wadded cotton futon spread into the countryside later. [Hanley, *Everyday Things
in Premodern Japan*]

## Poor

### Rural: rice villages

- **Tenant rice farmer's hut (kosakunin no ie / mizunomi-byakushō no ie)**: a landless or near-landless family renting
  paddy from a landholder. One room about 3 × 4 ken, half of it earth. Rooms: doma + board or doza platform, no
  tatami, no ceiling (open to the soot-black thatch).
  Items: irori, jizai-kagi pot hook, iron pot (nabe), one-hole clay kamado, water jar (mizugame), wooden sink board,
  mushiro, straw bedding, hoe (kuwa), sickle (kama), mino raincoat, kasa hat, waraji on a peg, straw-beating stone and
  mallet (yokozuchi), a few straw rice bales (tawara), zaru baskets, box trays (hakozen), a paper charm (ofuda)
  pasted on a post. [Kawashima, *Minka*; tools per *Nōgyō Zensho* 1697]
- **Doza house (doza-zumai)**: the poorest east-Japan form: no board floor at all, people live on straw heaped on the
  packed earth and covered with mats. Rooms: one earth room with a sunken irori, a straw-heaped sleeping corner.
  Items: irori pit ringed with stones, deep straw layer, mushiro, straw bedding pile, pot, jar, rope coils, sandals
  drying by the fire. [Kawashima; persisted into Meiji in Tōhoku, so period-safe but also long-lived]
- **Dependent household's hut (nago / hikan no ie)**: a hereditary dependent family living on a big farmer's land and
  working for him in return for a plot. Sits in the master's yard or next to it. Rooms: doma + one board room.
  Items: as the tenant hut, fewer tools (borrowed from the master), straw work in progress (rope, sandals, bags),
  a hemp spinning basket. `[outside 1680-1750: common 1600-1680; by 1730 fading in Kinai, still present in the east
  and mountains]`
- **Day labourer's hut on the village edge (hiyatoi / kado-ya)**: someone with no land who hires out by the day for
  planting, harvest or road work. Rooms: doma + tiny platform. Items: carrying pole (tenbinbō), rope-net earth
  carrier (mokko), backpack frame (shoiko), sickle, straw bedding, one pot, one bowl set, sandal-making stool.
- **Widow's or elder's hut (poor inkyo-ya)**: a one-room cottage for an old parent after the heir takes over, or a
  widow living alone. Items: small irori, spinning wheel (itoguruma), hemp or cotton thread basket, a small Buddhist
  shelf with a tablet (ihai), prayer beads, one pot, a teapot-less kettle, straw bedding.
- **Kinai tenant house with a bamboo floor (take-yuka / sunoko-yuka)**: western Japan's cheap alternative to boards:
  split-bamboo slats laid over joists. Rooms: niwa + bamboo-slat room. Items: as tenant hut, plus cotton-ginning
  roller (watakuri), cotton-bow (watauchi-yumi), spinning wheel, raw cotton in baskets (Kawachi cotton boom is in era).
  (verify how common take-yuka still was by 1730)
- **Paddy / crop watch hut (ta-goya / shishi-goya)**: lived in for weeks in late summer and autumn to scare boar and
  deer off ripening fields. Rooms: one raised floor or earth, a fire. Items: bamboo clappers on long ropes (naruko),
  wooden drum or clapper board, straw bedding, small pot, torch (taimatsu), mino, a gun only for licensed pest-control
  hunters (see hunter's hut).
- **Famine refugee shelter (kiga goya / sukui-goya)**: rough shelters for the starving. **In era:** the Kyōhō famine of
  1732–33 hit western Japan hard. Rooms: none, a lean-to. Items: reed mats, gruel pot, straw, begging bowl, a bundle
  of bedding. (Official relief huts themselves are BL-C.)

### Rural: dry-field, upland, mountain and forest

- **Slash-and-burn upland farmer's hut (yakihata no ie)**: hill families growing millet, buckwheat and beans on burnt
  forest patches. Rooms: doma + board room. Items: rotary stone quern (hikiusu) for buckwheat, wooden mortar and
  pounder (usu, kine), millet heads hanging, beans in straw bags, digging stick / mattock, fire rake, irori, pot.
- **Seasonal slash-and-burn field hut (yakihata-goya)**: a hut at the burnt plot, used during clearing and harvest.
  Items: fire, straw bedding, mattock, seed bag, water gourd.
- **Charcoal-burner's kiln hut (sumiyaki-goya)**: a lean-to beside the kiln for the nights the kiln burns; the kiln
  is BL-B. Items: charcoal bales (sumi-dawara), bale-weaving frame, charcoal rake, billhook (nata), axe (ono), saw,
  backpack frame (shoiko), straw bedding, pot on three stones, water bucket.
- **Charcoal-burner's village house (sumiyaki no ie)**: the family home down in the hamlet. Rooms: doma + board room.
  Items: stacked charcoal bales in the doma, bale straw, axes, billhooks, saws, shoiko, irori, kamado, straw snow boots.
- **Logging-crew camp hut (yamagoya / soma-goya)**: long rough hut for a crew of fellers and sawyers (hiyō) far up the
  valley, e.g. the Kiso forests (tightly restricted by Owari domain in our era). Rooms: one long board sleeping
  platform, big central fire. Items: long irori, big pot, felling axes (yoki), two-man ripsaw (ōga), crosscut saws,
  log hooks (tobi), wedges, rope coils, millet and dried radish sacks, mino, kasa, straw boots.
- **Woodcutter's family house (kikori no ie)**: as the charcoal-burner's house but with axes, wedges and firewood
  bundles (maki) in place of charcoal bales.
- **Wood-turner's forest hut (kiji-shi no koya)**: itinerant bowl-turners who claimed forest rights from Kimigahata in
  Ōmi and moved from valley to valley. Rooms: hut + open work area. Items: strap-driven lathe (rokuro), bowl blanks,
  rough-turned bowls in stacks, adzes, turning hooks, sharpening stone, irori. (The workshop side overlaps BL-B.)
- **Raftsman's riverside hut (ikada-shi no koya)**: crews floating timber down the Kiso, Tenryū, Ōi or Hozu (to
  Kyoto) rivers. Items: long poles (sao), rope coils, iron raft staples, straw cape, fire, rice and salt sacks.
- **Mountain hunter's hut (ryōshi-goya / kari-goya)**: seasonal shelter for village hunters. **In era:** Tsunayoshi's
  1687 gun registration made village guns licensed "scare-guns" for pests. Items: matchlock (licensed), spear, snares
  and traps, pelts drying, meat drying racks (momonji), bear-gall pouch, fire, straw bedding. The Tōhoku *matagi*
  hunting camps are the famous version `[off-map: Tōhoku]`.

### Rural: fishing, coast and salt

- **Poor fisherman's hut (ryōshi no koya)**: a hired net hand or small line fisher. Board walls, boards weighted with
  stones or thatch. Rooms: doma (where nets live) + board room. Items: nets on pegs, net needles (amibari), wooden or
  gourd floats, stone or clay sinkers, oar (ro), fishing lines on frames, fish baskets (biku), salted fish tub, drying
  rack by the door, irori, mushiro, a small Ebisu shelf.
- **Diver's beach fire hut (ama-goya)**: a round or square hut on the beach where women divers warm up between dives;
  some families lived in similar huts. Items: central fire, straw mats, wooden tub float (iso-oke), abalone chisel
  (isonomi), rope, head cloth, shellfish baskets. `[off-map: Shima / Ise coast; also Bōsō in Kantō]`
- **Salt-field labourer's hut (hama-ko no ie)**: workers raking and hauling brine on salt fields; near Edo the Gyōtoku
  fields supplied the city. Rooms: doma + board room. Items: sand rakes, shoulder buckets (ninai-oke) and yoke,
  straw salt bags (kamasu), wooden salt tubs, sand-levelling board, irori, straw bedding. (The boiling shed is BL-B.)
- **Nori farmer's cottage (nori-shi no ie)**: seaweed growers on Edo Bay (Shinagawa, Ōmori). Items: reed drying
  screens (nori-su), drying frames outside, chopping board and knife, tubs, bundles of brush poles (hibi), board
  shelves of dried sheets. (verify start date: Shinagawa nori farming c. Genroku–Kyōhō)
- **Seasonal fishing-camp shed (bangoya)**: shed where a crew sleeps during a run (sardine, bonito). Items: long sleeping
  board, fire, net piles, crew's straw bedding, pots, sake barrel.
- **Boat dwelling (ebune / ie-bune)**: families living aboard small boats in the Seto Inland Sea. Items: rush-mat cabin
  roof, clay stove on board, bedding, nets, water jar. `[off-map: Seto Inland Sea]`

### Rural: highway and post-town poor

- **Hired horse-leader's room (mago no ie)**: a driver who leads other people's horses; rents a small doma room.
  Items: straw horse-shoes (umagutsu) in bundles, halter rope, whip, driver's lantern, mino, kasa, straw bedding.
- **Porters' bunkroom (ninsoku-beya / kumosuke-beya)**: shared sleeping hall for road porters and palanquin bearers
  (kumosuke) at a post town. Overlaps the BL-C toiyaba; listed here as housing. Rooms: earth doma + long board
  platform. Items: long sleeping platform, shared quilts, carrying poles, a parked palanquin (kago), heaps of sandals,
  dice and bowl (kumosuke gambled famously), sake jug, pipes.
- **River porters' row (kawagoshi ninsoku nagaya)**: men who carried travellers across the unbridged Ōi River
  (Shimada / Kanaya on the Tōkaidō). Items: carrying platforms (rendai) stacked outside, loincloths drying, ropes,
  crossing tickets board (kawa-fuda), sake, straw bedding.

### Urban: Edo, Kyoto, Osaka, castle towns

- **Edo back-alley single unit (ura-nagaya, kushaku-niken)**: the classic 9-shaku by 2-ken tenement: about 3 m wide
  including the doma. Tenants: peddlers, craftsmen, day labourers. Rooms: 1 tsubo doma at the door with kamado and
  sink, one 4.5- or 6-mat room, no back door. Tatami in 1730 is plausible but not certain for the cheapest units
  (verify; boards + goza is the fallback).
  Items: one-hole kamado, water jar, sink board, one shelf, folded bedding behind a low folding screen
  (makura-byōbu), wicker trunk (kōri), andon, small hibachi, cheap bowls, tenant's trade tools (see variants).
  [Fukagawa Edo Museum, c.1840 reconstruction `[survivors late]`; *Morisada Mankō* 1837–53]
- **Edo back-alley two-room unit (ura-nagaya, niken)**: bigger unit for a craftsman family: doma + 4.5 mat + 6 mat, a
  small back yard. Items: as above plus a small tansu, a Buddhist shelf, a clothes rack (ikō), more bedding.
- **Back-to-back ridge-split tenement (mune-wari nagaya)**: a row split down the ridge line so units face both ways and
  have no through-draught or back window. The cheapest urban housing. Items: as single unit.
- **Ura-nagaya occupant variants (dressing sets for the same shell)**: the museum shows the same unit dressed by trade.
  - Peddler (bote-furi): shoulder pole, two baskets, steelyard scale (sao-bakari), measure box (masu).
  - Carpenter: toolbox (dōgubako), saws, planes, ink line (sumitsubo), square (sashigane).
  - Plasterer: trowels (kote), mortar board, straw fibre bundle.
  - Boatman: long pole, straw cape, oilcloth, rope.
  - Shamisen teacher: shamisen, plectrum, practice books, one good kimono on a rack.
  - Seamstress / washerwoman: sewing box (haribako), pan iron (hinoshi), washing tubs, drying pole.
  - Fortune-teller: divination sticks (zeichiku), paper lantern sign, low desk.
  - Palanquin bearer / porter: shoulder pads, carrying pole, sandals.
  - Town fireman (machi-bikeshi, set up 1720 in Edo, in era): fire hook (tobiguchi), padded fire coat (hikeshi-banten).
  - Paper-waste collector: back basket, bamboo tongs, sorted paper bundles.
  - A vacant unit: bare.
- **Shared alley facilities of an ura-nagaya court** (site, not a building, but it defines the home): shared well,
  shared toilet with half-height doors, rubbish pit (gomi-tame), a small Inari shrine, washing and drying poles,
  potted plants on the drain board. [Fukagawa Edo Museum]
- **Kyoto back-alley row (roji / zushi nagaya)**: rows reached by a narrow passage through a street house. Rooms:
  earth kitchen strip with Kyoto kamado (kudo), one or two tatami rooms. Items: kudo, water jar, Atago fire-charm
  paper ("hi-no-yōjin") by the stove, shared well, sink, bedding, small Buddhist shelf. Nishijin home-weavers lived in
  such rows; their looms are BL-B.
- **Osaka back-alley row, rented bare (ura-nagaya, hadaka-gashi)**: in Osaka the tenant brought his own tatami, sliding
  doors and fittings; an empty unit is a bare frame. Items: as Edo, with a multi-hole Osaka stove (hettsui), tenant's
  own tatami, own fusuma. [Osaka Museum of Housing and Living, c.1830s reconstruction; (verify the hadaka-gashi detail)]
- **Castle-town backstreet row (jōkamachi no ura-nagaya)**: the same tenements in a provincial castle town. Items: as
  Edo ura-nagaya. (Obvious collapse.)
- **Fire-ruin shack (yake-ato no kari-goya)**: a temporary board-and-mat hut on the ashes after one of the big city
  fires, lived in for weeks or months. Items: charred posts reused, reed screens (yoshizu), salvaged pot and kettle,
  a scorched chest, straw bags of relief rice, bedding bundle.
- **Rōnin's rented room (rōnin no nagaya)**: a masterless samurai living in a tenement, often earning by teaching or
  piecework. Items: one or two swords on a simple rack, umbrella frames and oiled paper (paper-umbrella making was an
  attested poor-samurai side job; the rōnin cliché is later fiction), books, go board, writing box, sparse bedding.

### Marginalised communities (neutral, historical)

These were hereditary or assigned status groups outside the four-class order. Their housing mostly looked like other
poor or middling homes of the same region; the difference is location, duties and tools.

- **Kawata (eta) village house (kawata-mura no ie)**: status-bound communities with hereditary duties such as leather
  and hide work, drum-making, some policing and execution-ground work. Many also farmed. Villages often sat on
  riverbanks or marginal land. Rooms: as a poor or middle farmhouse of the region.
  Items: farm tools, leather-soled sandal (setta) making and repair kit, leather offcuts, drum hoops or drum heads,
  straw sandals, rope, bamboo; hide drying frames sit outside (the tannery itself is BL-B). [Botsman, *Punishment and
  Power in the Making of Modern Japan*; Amos, *Embodying Difference*]
- **Hinin hut (hinin-goya)**: groups under a hut-boss (koya-gashira), some born into the status, some fallen into it.
  Duties included sweeping, prison and execution assistance, and licensed street performing (torioi). Rooms: one mat
  hut. Items: straw mats, straw cape, broom, alms basket, begging bowl, woven hat (amigasa), a shamisen for torioi.
  [Botsman; Edo's hinin bosses were at Asakusa and Shinagawa]
- **Unregistered squatters' shelter under a bridge or on a riverbank (kawara-goya / hashi-shita)**: people dropped off
  the population registers (nobinin). Items: reed mats, driftwood fire, one pot, bundle, begging bowl.
- **Cremation-ground attendant's hut (onbō no ie)**: families who ran village or town cremation grounds. Items: firewood
  stacks, straw, rakes, ash jars, a small Buddhist image. (verify; mention neutrally or drop)

### Hermits, ascetics and huts

- **Grass hermitage (sōan / iori)**: a poet or recluse's thatched hut. **In era:** Bashō lived in his Fukagawa hut from
  1680 (rebuilt 1682 after a fire). Rooms: one or two small mat rooms, a tiny earth kitchen. Items: low desk, inkstone
  and brushes, poem slips (tanzaku), a gourd for rice, water jar, one pot, tea kettle, a hanging scroll, a Buddha
  image, travel hat and staff by the door. [Bashō-an site, Kōtō]
- **Ten-foot-square hut (hōjō-an)**: the classic portable recluse hut of Kamo no Chōmei. Archetype only.
  `[outside 1680-1750: 1212]` Items: as sōan, plus a sutra desk and a small Amida image.
- **Mountain ascetic's hut (yamabushi no gyōja-goya)**: a shugendō practitioner's retreat by a falls or peak. Items:
  conch trumpet (horagai), ringed staff (shakujō), prayer beads, portable altar backpack (oi), fire-ritual hearth
  (goma), sutras, small cap (tokin).
- **Wandering carver-monk's hut**: e.g. Enkū (d. 1695), who carved thousands of rough Buddhas in Mino and Hida.
  Items: chisels, adze, logs and half-carved figures, sutra box, rice pot. Stephen's call whether this is a unique
  landmark.

### Servants' and dependents' quarters inside other homes

- **Farm servants' loft (genin-beya / hōkōnin no nedoko)**: seasonal or yearly farm servants sleeping in the loft over
  the doma or next to the stable. Items: straw bedding, wicker trunk, sandals, a lantern, their own bowl box.
- **Merchant apprentices' loft (detchi-beya)**: boys and young clerks sleeping in the zushi-nikai over the shop.
  Items: shared futon rolled up, wooden box pillows (hako-makura), wicker trunks, a lantern, practice copybooks.
- **Maids' room (jochū-beya)**: a small room off the kitchen. Items: sewing box, small mirror stand, trunk, futon,
  one kimono on a peg.
- **Samurai-house lower servants' rooms (chūgen-beya / komono-beya)**: in the gatehouse (nagaya-mon). Items:
  crest-marked coats (happi), lanterns with the lord's crest, procession luggage boxes (hasami-bako), spear-sheath
  and umbrella cases, sandals in rows, dice, sake. (Chūgen rooms had a reputation as gambling dens.)

## Lower-middle

### Rural: farmhouses by plan

- **Small landholder's three-room farmhouse, Kantō (honbyakushō no ie, hiroma-gata)**: a registered tax-paying farmer.
  Thatch, hipped roof. Rooms: big doma (kitchen + work + sometimes a stable corner), hiroma (board room with irori),
  closed sleeping room (nando), a front zashiki with mats or a couple of tatami at most.
  Items: irori with fish-shaped hook bar (yokogi), jizai-kagi, iron kettle (tetsubin), kamado with 2–3 holes, sink,
  water jar, rice chest, tawara, pickle tubs (tsukemono-oke), miso jar, hakozen stack, kamidana, a small butsudan,
  hand loom (izaribata), spinning wheel, andon, mino and kasa on pegs, threshing comb (senba-koki, new in the
  Genroku era), round straw cushions (enza), straw bedding or yogi in the nando.
  [Kitamura house, 1687, Hadano, at Nihon Minka-en: an in-window survivor]
- **Four-room grid farmhouse (yotsuma-dori / ta-no-ji-gata)**: the standard middle-farmer plan by the 18th century in
  Kinai and spreading east. Rooms: doma + four rooms in a square: dei (front best room), zashiki (back, formal),
  daidokoro (family room, irori), nando (sleeping).
  Items: as above, plus a small tokonoma-like shelf or hanging scroll in the zashiki (only if permitted locally),
  zabuton for guests (rare, verify), lacquered festival trays in a box.
- **Farmhouse with inside stable (uchi-umaya no ie)**: Kantō, Kai, Shinano. The horse lives in a corner of the doma so
  it stays warm and watched. Rooms: doma with stable pen + three-room plan.
  Items: manger (kaiba-oke), fodder cutter (kaiba-giri), straw, harness, pack saddle, straw horseshoes, a Batō Kannon
  charm or monkey charm on the stable post, manure fork, plus the three-room set.
- **Kinai farmhouse with ox (ushi-ya tsuki)**: western Japan ploughed with oxen more than horses. Items: ox stall in
  the niwa, plough (karasuki), yoke, nose-rope, manger, plus the four-room set.
- **Cotton-growing farmhouse, Kawachi / Settsu (momen-zukuri no ie)**: family spinning and weaving for the Osaka
  market, in era. Items: cotton gin roller (watakuri), cotton bow, spinning wheel, loom, indigo cloth bundles, dried
  sardine fertilizer bags (hoshika, shipped from the Kantō coast), plus the four-room set.
- **Tea-grower's farmhouse (chanōka)**: Uji near Kyoto, and Suruga on the Tōkaidō. Tea is steamed and dried at home.
  Items: paper-covered drying table over charcoal (hoiro), steaming basket (seiro), tea jars (chatsubo), tea boxes,
  winnowing trays, plus the farm set. In era: Nagatani Sōen's sencha method is 1738.
- **Flood-country farmhouse with a raised storehouse (wajū no ie + mizuya)**: Mino / Owari lowlands inside ring levees.
  The main house sits on a mound; a storehouse (mizuya) sits on a higher stone mound with food, papers, bedding and the
  family's escape boat (agebune) hung under the eaves. Items: agebune, emergency rice jars, bedding chests, rope, plus
  the farm set. `[survivors late]`
- **Lakeside farmhouse with a spring-water washing room (kabata no ie)**: Ōmi (Harie, Shiga), on the map near the
  Nakasendō / Tōkaidō junction. Spring water runs through a small tank room beside the kitchen for washing rice, pots
  and vegetables. Items: stone tanks, washing tubs, pots soaking, carp in the outer tank eating scraps, plus farm set.
  `[survivors late; practice older]`
- **Separate-kitchen farmhouse (bunto / kamaya-bunri)**: main house plus a separate cooking building joined by a gutter
  roof. Found in Chiba / Ibaraki in Kantō and in southern Kyushu. Items in the kamaya: kamado bank, water jars,
  firewood, pickle tubs; main house keeps the irori. [Kawashima]
- **Archaic low-eaved farmhouse, Settsu / Tanba (hakogi-type)**: very low eaves, thick earth walls, dark interior;
  the Hakogi house near Kobe is Japan's oldest minka. Items: standard farm set, sparse. `[outside 1680-1750: the
  survivor is c.1400s; the old form was still lived in]`
- **Dry-field farmhouse on a new-field settlement (shinden no ie)**: Musashino upland west of Edo, settled in long
  strip plots (Santome-shinden, 1694; Kyōhō new fields, 1720s): all in era. Rooms: three-room plan. Items: very long
  well rope and bucket (deep wells), stone quern, wheat and barley, udon board and rolling pin, flail (karasao), millet,
  windbreak trees outside; sweet potato arrives in Kantō 1735 (Aoki Kon'yō).
- **Sericulture farmhouse with rearing loft (yōsan nōka)**: silkworm trays on racks in the loft. Items: rearing trays
  (kaiko-kago), rack posts, mulberry leaf baskets, cocoon frames (mabushi), hand-reel (zaguri).
  `[outside 1680-1750: big rearing-loft houses are late 18th–19th c.]`

### Rural: mountain, highland and snow

- **Kiso mountain-village house (itabuki ishi-oki no ie)**: boards weighted with stones, a low upper floor used for
  storage. Rooms: doma + irori room + back room + loft. Items: irori, tetsubin, shoiko, snowshoes (kanjiki), straw
  snow boots, tochi nuts and chestnuts drying, stone quern, bentwood boxes (magemono), bags of millet.
  [Kiso villages; `survivors late`]
- **Hida farmhouse, board roof (Hida no itabuki minka)**: similar to Kiso with lower-pitched board roofs, big smoke-black
  beams. Items: as Kiso plus a big irori frame with drying rack above (hidana), sake jug. [Hida no Sato, Takayama]
- **Gasshō-zukuri house (Shirakawa-gō / Gokayama)**: steep thatch, three or four attic floors. Large extended families
  (up to 30–40 people) under one head. The attic was for storage and paper-making; saltpetre (enshō) for Kaga domain
  was made in pits under the floor in era. Rooms: doma, big irori room (dei), butsuma, sleeping rooms, attic floors.
  Items: several irori, big Jōdo Shinshū gilded altar (this region is strongly Shin), attic storage bales, washi
  paper-making vats and screens (Gokayama), saltpetre pit, straw snow gear, mulberry (sericulture is later).
  `[survivors mostly 18th–19th c.; the silk-attic use is late]` Tier: large ones are upper.
- **Honmune-zukuri (Shinano)**: huge almost-square board-roofed farmhouse with a sparrow-dance gable ornament
  (suzume-odori). Upper farmers on the Nakasendō side of Shinano. Items: as a headman's house.
  `[mostly after 1750; the form begins in the 18th c.; check]`
- **Snow-country L-house (chūmon-zukuri)**: a front wing holds the entrance and stable so snow never blocks the door.
  Items: snow tools, snowshoes, stable set. `[off-map: Niigata, Yamagata, Akita; mostly late Edo]`
- **Nanbu L-house with the horse wing (magariya)**: family and horses under one L roof. Items: stable set, big irori,
  horse altar. `[off-map: Iwate; 18th c.; WORLD_CATALOGUE already says don't build]`
- **Helmet-roof silk house (kabuto-zukuri)**: roof ends cut open to light silkworm lofts. `[off-map: Yamagata,
  Gunma, Fukushima; late Edo–Meiji]`
- **Raised-ridge Kai silk house (Kōshū takahe-zukuri)**: a raised central roof section for silk lofts.
  `[outside 1680-1750: late Edo–Meiji]`
- **Other far regional forms** (one line for completeness): kudo-zukuri U-roof (Saga), futamune twin-roof (Satsuma),
  Ryūkyū stone-walled houses, Ainu chise. `[off-map]`

### Rural: coast

- **Independent fisherman's house (ryōshi no ie)**: owns a boat and some gear. Board or thatch, a big doma for nets.
  Rooms: doma + two board rooms. Items: nets, floats, sinkers, net-mending needles, octopus pots (takotsubo), eel traps,
  oars, fishing rods, fish-drying racks, salted-fish tubs, almanac (koyomi) for tides, Ebisu figure on the kamidana,
  irori, kamado, bedding.
- **Small salt-maker's house (shio-yaki no ie)**: a family working its own small salt field (agehama type) near Edo
  Bay or on the Tōkaidō coast. Items: brine buckets and yoke, sand rakes, salt bags, wooden salt tubs, firewood
  stacks. (Boiling shed = BL-B.)
- **Coastal sailor's home in a port town (sendō no ie)**: crewmen and skippers of coastal freighters. Items: rope
  coils, oilcloth coat, iron-bound sea chest (funa-dansu `[mostly later 18th–19th c.]`), charms from Konpira shrine,
  bedding. Rooms: as a small machiya or nagaya.

### Post towns

- **Kiso post-town house, home side (Kiso no shukuba-machiya)**: Tsumago / Narai / Magome type: boards weighted with
  stones, overhanging low upper floor (dashibari). Rooms: doma through to the back, irori room (daidokoro), back
  room(s), low upper storey for storage and sleeping; kura, stable and toilet in the back yard. Items: irori,
  tetsubin, kamado, hakozen, kamidana, butsudan, lanterns (chōchin) on a rack, mino and kasa, sleeping futon upstairs,
  bentwood boxes, combs (Yabuhara on the Nakasendō made combs in era) as local colour. `[survivors late]`
- **Tōkaidō post-town house (Tōkaidō no shukuba-machiya)**: deeper, tiled or board, plastered fronts, e.g. Seki-juku,
  Yui, Futagawa. Items: as Kiso without the snow gear, plus fire buckets, a well in the yard. `[survivors late]`
- **Packhorse owner's house (umakata no ie)**: post-town families owed horses to the station quota and kept them at
  the rear. Rooms: town house + back stable. Items: pack saddles (nigura), straw horseshoes, halters, fodder tubs,
  fodder cutter, horse bells, driver's lantern, horse charm, plus the home set.
- **Support-village farmer (sukegō-mura no ie)**: a farm household liable to send men and horses to the post station.
  Same shell as the farmhouse with inside stable; flagged only because the road duty justifies a horse. (Collapse.)

### Urban townsmen (living side only)

- **Street-front row unit, home side (omote-nagaya no sumai)**: a small shop at the front (BL-B), the family behind it.
  Rooms: one 4.5–6 mat back room, a kitchen doma along one side, a sleeping loft. Items: futon, small tansu, hibachi,
  butsudan, kamidana with Ebisu and Daikoku, andon, kitchen set, clothes rack.
- **Kyoto townhouse, home side (Kyō-machiya)**: the long narrow "eel's bed" plan. Rooms: tōri-niwa with a hashiri-niwa
  kitchen under a tall smoke void (hibukuro), daidokoro (boards or tatami), naka-no-ma, oku-no-ma facing the tsubo-niwa
  garden, zushi-nikai above the front, kura at the back.
  Items: a row of kudo stoves, well in the passage, stone sink, Atago fire charm, Kōjin kitchen-god shelf with a row
  of small Fushimi-doll Hotei figures (verify date of that custom), tsubo-niwa lantern and basin, butsudan in the
  oku-no-ma, tansu, hanging scroll, byōbu in summer/winter swap. [Kyoto machiya: most survivors are after the 1864 fire,
  `survivors late`]
- **Osaka townhouse, home side (Ōsaka machiya)**: like Kyoto's, slightly different stove (hettsui) and fittings; Osaka
  rental custom rented many houses bare. Items: as Kyoto. (Collapse candidate with Kyoto.)
- **Edo townhouse, home side (Edo machiya)**: shallower than Kamigata, cantilevered eaves (dashigeta), kitchen doma at
  the side; after 1720 fire rules pushed tile roofs and plastered walls. Rooms: shop front (BL-B), living room, kitchen,
  loft, often a small plastered fireproof room inside (nurigome) for valuables. Items: Edo-style one-hole kamado plus a
  big rice kamado, nurigome with chests, fire buckets, kamidana, butsudan, tansu, hibachi.
- **Castle-town townhouse (jōkamachi no machiya)**: as the Edo or Kyoto house by region, one storey, simpler. (Collapse.)
- **Alley landlord-agent's house (ōya / iemori no ie)**: the man who managed a tenement court for its owner: collected
  rent, kept the residents' register, sold the court's night soil. Sits at the alley mouth. Rooms: doma + two rooms.
  Items: account books, abacus (soroban), residents' register (ninbetsu-chō), writing box, low desk, lantern, keys.
- **Town doctor's house (machi-isha no ie)**: home plus a consulting room. Items: medicine cabinet with many drawers
  (kusuri-dansu), boat-shaped herb grinder (yagen), small scales, paper medicine packets, Chinese medical books, pulse
  cushion, mortar and pestle.
- **Writing-school teacher's house (terakoya no sumai)**: the school was the teacher's own main room. BL-C may claim it
  as civic. Items: many small desks (tsukue), inkstones, brushes, practice copybooks (ōraimono), blackened practice
  sheets, bookshelves, abacus.
- **Arts teacher's house (o-shishō no ie)**: shamisen, koto, dance or flower teacher. Items: instruments, music books,
  a best kimono on a rack, hibachi, tea things. (Collapse with nagaya variant or small machiya.)
- **Retirement cottage (inkyo-ya / hanare)**: detached small house for the retired head of a townsman or farm family.
  Items: tea kettle, books, go board, pipe tray (tabako-bon), a small garden view.

### Low-rank samurai and part-samurai

- **Foot-soldier row house (ashigaru nagaya / kumi-yashiki)**: long rows of units for a domain's foot soldiers, each
  with a tiny front garden and vegetable plot. Rooms: doma with kamado, one 6-mat and one 4.5-mat room.
  Items: lacquered issue hat (jingasa), crest-marked coat, wooden practice sword, a spear only if individually issued
  (mostly the lord's armoury held them), side-job gear (umbrella or lantern frames, insect cages, kite paper),
  vegetable tools, kamidana, butsudan, andon. [Hikone ashigaru houses survive, Edo period, on the Nakasendō]
- **Edo low shogunal retainer's house (gokenin no ie, kumi-yashiki)**: small detached houses in a block granted to a
  unit, e.g. Okachimachi for foot guards. Items: swords on a rack, formal wide trousers (hakama) and crested coat on a
  rack, writing desk, side-job gear (potted plants, umbrellas), kamidana, butsudan.
- **Town-police officer's house, Edo (dōshin no ie, Hatchōbori)**: small plot of about 100 tsubo; dōshin often rented
  part of it to doctors or teachers. Items: truncheon (jitte) and cord kept at home, crested short coat, sword rack,
  writing box, lantern. (Their office is BL-C.)
- **Lower samurai's house in a castle town (kachi / heishi no ie)**: small detached house with a board fence and a
  simple gate. Rooms: small genkan (no shikidai), 3–4 tatami rooms, kitchen doma, garden.
  Items: sword rack (katana-kake), armour chest (yoroi-bitsu), spear on hooks over the entrance, bow, writing desk,
  books, butsudan, kamidana, hibachi, andon, a crested screen (tsuitate) in the entrance.
- **Farmer-samurai house (gōshi / Hachiōji sennin-dōshin no ie)**: part-time warriors who farmed. The Hachiōji
  "Thousand Men" guarded Nikkō in shifts. Farmhouse shell with a formal genkan step (shikidai) showing rank. Items:
  farm set plus armour chest, spear, matchlock, formal kamishimo on a rack. [Edo-Tokyo Open-Air Architectural Museum,
  kumigashira house, `survivors late`]

### Religious families living apart

- **Shrine-priest family house (shake)**: hereditary priest families lived in their own houses near big shrines. The
  Kamigamo shake-machi in north Kyoto is the model: earth walls, gates, a stream led through each garden for
  purification. Items: court-style cap (eboshi) and white robe on a rack, paper streamers (gohei) and cutting knife,
  offering stands (sanbō), sakaki branches, prayer texts (norito), household shrine, ritual calendar.
  [Kamigamo shake-machi, `survivors mostly late Edo`] Tier: senior houses are upper.
- **Village farmer-priest's house (kannushi-nōka)**: a village shrine was often kept by a farmer family. Farmhouse set
  plus ritual robe, gohei, offering stands, drum, shrine keys.
- **Mountain-cult guide's house (oshi / sendōshi no ie)**: guides and hosts for pilgrims to Fuji (Fujiyoshida) or
  Ōyama (Sagami, near the Tōkaidō, hugely popular with Edo people). Home, pilgrim lodging and prayer room in one;
  lodging side is BL-B. Items: altar with the mountain deity scroll, charm stacks and printing blocks, pilgrim
  registers, white pilgrim clothes, bells, staffs.
- **Village mountain-ascetic household (sato-yamabushi no ie)**: shugen practitioners who lived in villages doing
  prayers and exorcisms. Items: conch, portable altar, goma hearth in a prayer room, charms, prayer beads, staff.

## Upper

### Rural elite

- **Village headman's house, Kantō (nanushi no ie)**: big thatch, board gatehouse (nagaya-mon), a formal entrance with
  a shikidai step and a raised guest room (jōdan-no-ma), by permission, for receiving officials. Rooms: huge doma with
  a stove bank, hiroma, daidokoro, several tatami rooms, formal zashiki with tokonoma, village-business room, kura
  behind. Items: large irori, 3–5-hole kamado bank, stacks of hakozen and lacquered guest trays, nagamochi chests,
  tansu, big butsudan, kamidana, byōbu, hanging scroll, vase, hibachi, crested lanterns, fire buckets, rice measures
  (masu), steelyard, village registers and tax ledgers in chests, seal box, writing desk.
  [Yoshino house, Edo-Tokyo Open-Air Museum, `survivors late`; WORLD_CATALOGUE T29]
- **Kinai village headman's house (shōya no ie)**: thatch main roof with tiled lower roofs, white plaster, huge
  earth-floored kitchen with a row of stoves. Items: as the Kantō headman, stoves called kudo / hettsui.
  [Yoshimura house, Habikino, Osaka: early Edo, in the window]
- **Great headman over many villages (ōjōya no ie)**: bigger again, with an office wing. Items: as headman, plus
  document chests and a visiting-officials' suite. (Collapse with headman.)
- **Hereditary intendant's family house (daikan-ke)**: e.g. the Egawa family at Nirayama, Izu. The office side is a
  jinya (BL-C); the family rooms are here. Items: as headman plus weapons (spears, armour), Chinese books.
  [Egawa house, Nirayama: early Edo core]
- **Wealthy farmer-entrepreneur's compound (gōnō no ie)**: a landlord who also brewed sake or soy, lent money, and let
  land to tenants. Main house, several kura, the brewery (BL-B), tenant cottages nearby. Items: account ledgers
  (daifukuchō), money chests (senryōbako), abacus, scales, fine lacquer, tea-ceremony things, haikai poetry books
  (village poetry circles are in era), go board, tansu rows.
- **Net-boss house (amimoto no ie)**: owner of big sardine seines on the Kujūkuri coast; employed dozens of hands.
  Rooms: big thatch house, huge doma, rooms for hired men. Items: net stores, dried sardine fertilizer (hoshika) bags,
  ledgers, big stove bank, many trays and bowls for crews, Ebisu shrine. [Sakuta house, late 17th c., Nihon Minka-en:
  in window]
- **Salt-field owner's house (hamadanushi no ie)**: owner of salt fields and boiling sheds. Items: ledgers, salt
  samples, the headman set. `[off-map for the big ones: Akō and Seto; small version possible at Gyōtoku]`
- **Horse-dealer's house (bakurō no ie)**: dealer with several stables and a yard; some doubled as horse inns.
  Items: stable set in quantity, halters and bridles on racks, horse charms, ledgers. [Suzuki house at Nihon Minka-en
  is a later horse-inn example, `survivors late`]
- **Honjin / toiya family private rooms (honjin-ke no oku)**: the family's own rooms behind a post-station inn or
  office. The inn is BL-B/C; the family set is the headman set.
- **Large gasshō and honmune houses**: see the mountain section; the biggest belong here.

### Urban merchant elite

- **Great merchant's residence (ōdana no oku)**: the family quarters behind or beside a big shop (Mitsui, Kōnoike,
  Sumitomo type). Plain outside, rich in materials inside (the "hidden luxury" of sumptuary rules). Rooms: many tatami
  rooms, oku-zashiki on a garden, family altar room, tea room, several kura, servants' wing, kitchen with a big stove
  bank. Items: rows of tansu, byōbu, hanging scrolls, flower vases, tea utensils (kettle, water jar, tea caddy,
  bowls), incense set, koto and shamisen, bookshelves, go and shōgi boards, lacquer trays and nested boxes (jūbako),
  hibachi, andon and candle stands, tabako-bon. [*Morisada Mankō*; merchant houses of this rank mostly lost]
- **Wealthy townsman's landlord house (jinushi / iemochi no ie)**: owns the plot and the tenements behind. Items: as
  merchant but smaller; ledgers, rent books. (Collapse.)
- **Merchant's suburban villa (bessō)**: Mukōjima (Edo), Higashiyama (Kyoto), Osaka outskirts. Sukiya-style rooms, big
  garden, tea hut. Items: tea set, garden-view rooms, sake sets, poetry things, fishing rods.
- **Tea hut (chashitsu / sōan-chashitsu)**: an outbuilding of upper houses, samurai and merchant. Rooms: a small mat room
  with a sunken hearth (ro), crawl-in door (nijiriguchi), prep room (mizuya). Items: kettle (kama), water jar,
  bowls, whisk, tea scoop, caddy, one scroll, one flower; stone basin (tsukubai) and waiting bench outside.

### Samurai

- **Mid-rank samurai house in a castle town (chūkyū bushi no yashiki)**: a plot with a roofed gate (yakui-mon or
  kabuki-mon), front garden, main house, kura, maybe a stable. Rooms: genkan with shikidai, formal zashiki with
  tokonoma and built-in desk (tsuke-shoin), family rooms, kitchen doma, maids' room, servants' room.
  Items: sword racks, armour chest and armour, spears on hooks over the entrance (yari-kake), bow and quiver, writing
  desk, bookcase, crested entrance screen, byōbu, scrolls, hibachi, pipe tray, crested lacquer trays, kamishimo on a
  rack, butsudan, kamidana, horse gear if mounted rank. [Hikone, Hagi and Kakunodate survivors; `survivors mixed`]
- **Shogun's bannerman's house, Edo (hatamoto yashiki)**: 300 to several thousand tsubo, a gatehouse (nagaya-mon)
  housing retainers, main house with an office room, stable, kura. Items: as mid-rank samurai plus horse gear, gate
  lanterns with crest, retainers' rooms (see servants).
- **Senior retainer's mansion (karō yashiki)**: the big houses near the castle, with their own nagaya-mon, retainers,
  kura and garden. Items: as bannerman, larger; family armour display, many byōbu, several scrolls.
- **Daimyo's main Edo residence (kami-yashiki)**: hosted the lord during his Edo years and his wife and heir always.
  Rooms: omote (formal halls: BL-C), oku (lord's private rooms) and ōoku-like women's quarters, kitchens, and a
  perimeter of barrack nagaya. Items (home side): lacquered bridal furniture sets, shell-game boxes (kaiōke),
  painted fusuma, byōbu, koto, incense sets, hibachi, silk bedding, mirror stands, many attendants' rooms.
  [Akamon of the Kaga mansion is 1827, `survivors late`]
- **Daimyo's secondary Edo residences (naka-yashiki / shimo-yashiki)**: for the heir or retired lord, and a suburban
  villa-warehouse-refuge. Items: as kami-yashiki, lighter; stroll garden, tea hut. (Collapse with kami-yashiki.)
- **Retainers' barracks inside a daimyo mansion (edo-zume nagaya)**: long two-storey rows around the mansion edge
  where men on Edo duty lived, often two or three to a room, many cooking for themselves. Items: futon, trunks, sword
  rack, writing box (letters and diaries home), cheap sake, go and shōgi, a small stove, kettle, rice bag.
  [Diary of Sakai Banshirō, `outside 1680-1750: 1860`, for the texture]
- **Domain warehouse-residence in Osaka (kura-yashiki)**: rice warehouses are BL-B/C; the resident officer's family
  rooms are the mid-rank samurai set.
- **Daimyo's castle palace (honmaru goten)**: mostly BL-C (government). Only the lord's private oku rooms are homes.

### Nobility

- **Court noble's residence, Kyoto (kuge yashiki)**: around the imperial palace. Most kuge had tiny incomes and modest
  houses; a few (Konoe, Kujō) had large ones. Rooms: gate, genkan, shoin rooms, garden. Items: court robes on racks,
  lacquered cap boxes, koto and biwa, incense-game sets, waka poetry papers and boxes, calligraphy set, go board,
  kemari ball for the families that taught it. [Reizei house, rebuilt 1790 after the Tenmei fire:
  `outside 1680-1750: 1790`, closest survivor]

### Marginalised communities: their elite

- **Kawata leader's compound (Danzaemon yashiki, Asakusa)**: the hereditary head of Kantō kawata communities ran a
  large compound with offices and a big residence. Items: as a headman's house plus registers and leather goods.
  Osaka's Watanabe-mura had rich leather dealers with big townhouses. [Botsman; Ooms]

## Outbuildings of homes (all tiers)

- **Earth-walled plastered storehouse (dozō / kura)**: fireproof. Items: nagamochi, lidded chests, rice bales, jars,
  boxed lacquer tray sets, bagged byōbu, boxed dolls, armour boxes, seed rice, documents. Tier 2–3.
- **Board storehouse (itagura)**: rural, cheaper. Items: grain bales, straw bags, tools, seed. Tier 1–2.
- **Log storehouse (azekura)**: old style, rare in homes by 1730. (verify, probably drop)
- **Rice storehouse (kome-gura)**: a kura for tax and seed rice at headman houses. Items: bales, measuring masu,
  strickle (tokaki), ledgers.
- **Fireproof inner room (nurigome)**: Edo; a plastered room inside the house. Items: chests, ledgers, money box.
- **Dug cellar (ana-gura / tsuchi-gura)**: pit for root vegetables, sometimes under the floor. Items: sweet
  potatoes (post-1735 in Kantō), radishes, jars.
- **Barn / work shed (naya / sagyō-goya)**: threshing and straw work. Items: threshing comb (senba-koki), winnowing
  machine (tōmi, late 17th c. Kinai), grain sieve (mangoku-dōshi, 18th c.), winnowing basket (mi), treadle mortar
  (karausu), rope and mat looms, straw piles.
- **Pounding shed (tsukiya / usu-ya)**: a shed for the treadle mortar. Items: karausu, mortar, pestles, grain.
- **Detached stable (umaya)**: Items: manger, straw, harness, pack saddle, fodder cutter, straw horseshoes, horse charm,
  manure fork.
- **Ox shed (ushi-goya)**: western version. Items: manger, plough, yoke, nose-rope.
- **Woodshed (maki-goya)**: split firewood stacks, kindling, axes, chopping block. Firewood also stacks under eaves.
- **Ash shed (haiya)**: a small fire-safe shed for hearth ash kept as fertilizer. (Kinki and Chūbu, verify.)
- **Manure shed and pits (koe-goya / koetsubo)**: night-soil vats, compost, shoulder buckets (koe-oke), dipper.
- **Toilet (setchin / kawaya)**: rural outhouse over a jar or pit, often by the gate or near the manure pit; urban
  shared toilets with half doors; upper houses have indoor toilets for guests. Items: wooden lid, wiping sticks or
  recycled paper (Asakusa-gami in Edo), water bucket.
- **Bath hut (furoba / yudono)**: poor families mostly washed in a tub in the yard (gyōzui) or used town bathhouses;
  middle and upper rural homes had a tub bath. Kamigata tub with iron bottom (goemon-buro) vs Edo tub with an iron
  firepipe (teppō-buro) per *Morisada Mankō* `[late Edo source, verify for 1730]`; steam baths (mushi-buro) are older.
  Items: wooden tub, firebox, buckets, dipper, stool, washcloths, rice-bran bag (nuka-bukuro).
- **Drying shed or eaves (hoshi-ba)**: persimmons (hoshigaki), tobacco leaves, radishes, noodles. Items: strings of
  fruit, bamboo poles, racks.
- **Net shed (ami-goya)**: nets, floats, sinkers, tar or persimmon-tannin tubs for net dyeing.
- **Boat shed (funa-goya)**: a family's small boat and oars. (BL-B may hold the commercial version.)
- **Silkworm shed (sanshitsu)**: `[outside 1680-1750: late]`.
- **Hen coop (tori-goya)**: a few chickens (mostly for eggs to sell and for time-telling). (verify, egg-eating rises later)
- **Roofed well (ido-ya)**: pulley well with bucket or lever well (hanetsurube).
- **Separate kitchen (kamaya)**: see bunto house.
- **Flood storehouse (mizuya)**: see wajū house.
- **Spring-water washing room (kabata)**: see Ōmi farmhouse.
- **Gatehouse with rooms (nagaya-mon)**: headman (boards) and samurai (plaster) houses; servants' rooms and storage.
- **Retirement cottage (inkyo-ya)**: see lower-middle urban; rural version the same.
- **Household shrine in the yard (yashiki-gami / Inari hokora)**: a tiny shrine in a corner of the plot (site object;
  shrines proper are BL-C).
- **Field hut (nora-goya)**: tool and rest hut at distant fields. Items: hoe, bucket, straw mat, water jar.

## Items that show up almost everywhere

**The shared core (nearly every home):** irori or kamado (or both), iron pot (nabe), rice pot (kama), water jar
(mizugame), wooden sink board (nagashi), wooden buckets (oke) and tubs (tarai), ladle (hishaku), bamboo baskets
(zaru), box trays (hakozen), bowls and chopsticks, mushiro mats, straw bedding or futon, straw sandals (waraji,
zōri), straw raincoat (mino), hat (kasa), fire-striker and tinder (hiuchi-ishi, hiuchi-gane, hokuchi), paper charm
(ofuda) on a post, small kamidana or Buddhist shelf, tobacco pipe (kiseru), a light (pine-resin torch, fish-oil or
rape-oil andon), firewood.

**Rural-only core:** jizai-kagi, straw rope and straw work, hoe, sickle, tawara bales, winnowing basket, mortar and
pestle.

**Town-only core:** tatami, cotton futon, hibachi, tansu, andon, chōchin, kōri trunk, clothes rack (ikō), folding
screen for bedding.

**Tier markers:** a tokonoma scroll and vase (upper, or headman with permission), shikidai entrance step (samurai,
headman, honjin), crested lacquer (samurai), sword rack and armour chest (any samurai), tea utensils (upper), many
kura (upper).

**Date traps for 1730:** tetsubin and tansu are spreading (fine for tier 2–3), zabuton for commoners is uncertain,
candles are expensive, cotton futon is scarce in poor rural homes, no glass, no side-handled teapot (verify),
sweet potato only in Kantō from 1735.

## Obvious collapse candidates (advice only, Stephen decides)

1. **All poor one-room rural huts** (tenant, doza, nago, day labourer, widow, Kinai bamboo-floor, woodcutter's,
   charcoal-burner's house, slash-and-burn house): one shell in 2 sizes, doza vs board floor as a floor swap, and the
   occupant shown by a prop set (farm / charcoal / woodcutter / cotton / fisher).
2. **Seasonal huts** (paddy watch, kiln hut, yakihata field hut, hunter's hut, raftsman's hut, field hut, famine
   shelter, fire-ruin shack, riverbank squatter): one lean-to and one small hut, dressed per role.
3. **Logging camp and porters' bunkroom and river porters' row**: one long "bunk hall" shell.
4. **Middle farmhouses**: keep the Kantō three-room (with and without inside stable) and the Kinai four-room (with
   ox); treat cotton, tea, dry-field, flood, kabata and separate-kitchen as prop sets or one-off add-ons (mizuya,
   kabata room, kamaya) rather than new houses.
5. **Kiso and Hida mountain houses** merge; post-town Kiso house can share the same kit.
6. **Headman houses** (Kantō nanushi, Kinai shōya, ōjōya, daikan family, gōnō, net boss, salt owner, horse dealer):
   two shells (east thatch, Kinai thatch-and-tile), dressed by source of wealth.
7. **All ura-nagaya forms** (Edo single, Edo two-room, mune-wari, Kyoto roji, Osaka bare, castle town): one kit with
   1- and 2-room units, a Kyoto stove swap and an "empty unit"; occupations are dressing sets.
8. **Kyoto and Osaka machiya** merge; **Edo machiya** stays separate (different front); castle-town machiya reuses
   whichever region it sits in.
9. **Small professional homes** (doctor, terakoya, arts teacher, landlord agent, rōnin) are all dressing sets on the
   small machiya or nagaya unit.
10. **Low samurai**: ashigaru row, gokenin and dōshin can share one row/small-house kit; lower castle-town samurai and
    gōshi share one small detached house with gate.
11. **Upper samurai**: mid-rank, hatamoto and karō are one kit at three plot sizes; daimyo kami/naka/shimo-yashiki are
    one hero complex; the retainers' barracks reuse the ashigaru row kit.
12. **Religious homes**: shake, farmer-priest, oshi and village yamabushi could all be the headman or small-farm shell
    with a ritual prop set; Kamigamo shake is the one worth a distinct look if a Kyoto district is built.
13. **Hermitages**: sōan and hōjō-an are one hut; the yamabushi and carver huts are prop sets on it.
14. **Drop or defer** everything tagged `[off-map]` and `[outside 1680-1750]` except as possible late-game colour:
    chūmon, magariya, kabuto, takahe, Kyushu forms, ebune, sericulture lofts.
15. **Outbuildings**: kura (dozō + itagura), one shed (barn / pounding / wood / net / drying by props), stable (horse or
    ox), toilet, bath hut, roofed well, nagaya-mon. That is about 7 kits covering all 25 listed.

# BL-C: Religious, government, military, civic and infrastructure buildings, and settlement layout

**Agent:** BL-C (text-only brainstorm, 2026-09-29). **Anchor year 1730**, window 1680–1750.
**Lanes:** homes are BL-A (including samurai homes and the headman's house as a home). Shops, inns, tea houses, crafts
and industry are BL-B. Everything here is deliberately wider than the game will be; Stephen collapses it.

**Conventions**
- `[outside 1680-1750: date]` = real in wider Edo but not standing / not yet in use in the window. Listed, not dropped.
- `(assumed)` = sensible, not sourced in this pass. `(verify)` = I have a source memory but did not re-check it.
- **Items** are dressing that makes the room read as that building. Loot lies on floors and surfaces; nothing here is a
  container.
- Sources are short names. Recurring ones: **JAANUS** (Japanese Architecture and Art Net Users System, term
  dictionary); **Taigaichō** (the 1843 Tōkaidō / Nakasendō *shukuson taigaichō* station surveys); **Coaldrake**
  (*Architecture and Authority in Japan*); **Vaporis** (*Breaking Barriers*, *Tour of Duty*); **Botsman** (*Punishment
  and Power in the Making of Modern Japan*); **Dore** (*Education in Tokugawa Japan*); **Hur** (*Death and Social Order
  in Tokugawa Japan*); **Jinnai** (*Tokyo: A Spatial Anthropology*); **Hanley** (*Everyday Things in Premodern Japan*);
  **McClain** (*Kanazawa*); **Kelly** (*Water Control in Tokugawa Japan*); ja.wikipedia entries named by topic.

**Counts in this file:** settlement layouts 15 · Shinto 38 · Buddhist 62 · Government 46 · Military 44 · Civic and
infrastructure 63 (entries, including sub-variants that are listed as their own line).

**Three 1730 facts that change what a map should show** (details in the sections):
1. **Shrines and temples are fused (shinbutsu shūgō).** A 1730 shrine of any size often has Buddhist buildings (a
   pagoda, a goma hall, a bettō-ji temple run by monks), and many temples have a small tutelary shrine. Separation is
   1868. A "pure" shrine precinct is anachronistic except at Ise and Izumo.
2. **Edo Castle and Osaka Castle have no keep in 1730.** Edo's burned in 1657 and only the stone base was rebuilt
   (1659); Osaka's burned by lightning in 1665. Kyoto's Nijō keep still stands until 1750.
3. **Every household is registered at a Buddhist temple (terauke / danka system, from the 1630s–60s).** So every
   village has, or belongs to, a temple, and temple graveyards are everywhere.

## 1. Settlement layouts

General rule of thumb for all settlements (assumed, from period maps): **sacred sites sit at the edges and on
slopes**, the **working core sits on the flat or along the road**, and **government shows as one walled or gated
compound**, not a district, except in castle towns and the three great cities.

### 1.1 Rice-farming village (hirachi no mura / heiya no nōson)

- **Scale:** the Genroku village registers (1700–02) list roughly 63,000 villages for ~26 million koku, so the average
  village assessed ~400 koku (assumed arithmetic; verify). Typical: **40–100 households, 250–500 people.** Villages
  sit 1–2 km apart.
- **Forms:** nucleated cluster (shūson) on a terrace edge or levee above the paddies; dispersed farmsteads each in
  its own windbreak grove (sanson: Tonami plain, Izumo, Kantō *yashikirin*); street village along a road (rosō).
- **Layout:** houses on the dry ground, paddies below, dry fields (hata) and woodlot (satoyama) behind. Irrigation
  channels thread the houses; a water gate at the intake. The **chinju shrine** in a grove on rising ground at the
  edge; the **village temple** at the other edge or on a slope with its graveyard. **Dōsojin, Jizō and kōshin stones**
  at the village entrances and crossroads. The **kōsatsuba** at the entrance or in front of the headman's gate.
- **Buildings (rough proportion for a 60-house village):**
  - 1 headman's house (nanushi/shōya) with gate and 1–2 kura (BL-A), doubling as the village office
  - 5–10 larger "full farmer" (honbyakushō) houses; 40–50 small farmer / tenant (mizunomi) houses (BL-A)
  - 1 chinju shrine (+ haiden if the village is rich), 2–6 hokora and field shrines
  - 1 village temple (sometimes 2 of different sects), or 1 small dō hall served from a temple elsewhere
  - 1 gōkura (village storehouse) in many villages; 1 kōsatsuba
  - 5–15 wells (lever or pulley), shared or per house
  - 1–3 water-gate / sluice points, sometimes a watch hut
  - 0–1 smithy, 0–1 general store (BL-B), threshing yards and drying racks everywhere
  - 1 graveyard at the temple plus scattered family graves at field edges; 1 cremation or burial ground (sanmaiba)
    outside the village in some regions
  - a fire-watch ladder with an alarm bell in larger villages (assumed)
- **Src:** ja.wikipedia *Genroku gōchō*; Hanley; PLAYBOOK §2.1.

### 1.2 New-field village (shinden-mura)

- **What:** planned villages on reclaimed land. A boom under Yoshimune's Kyōhō reforms (1720s), so very 1730.
- **Layout:** a straight road with **long strip plots** running back from it: house and windbreak at the front, then
  field, then woodlot. Musashino's Santome (1694, Kawagoe domain) is the model: each plot ~40 × 675 m (verify).
- **Buildings:** identical farmhouses at regular spacing, a new small shrine, often **no temple yet** (families stay
  registered at their old temple), a newly dug well per few houses (deep wells on the dry Musashino plateau).
- **Src:** Santome shinden (Saitama local histories).

### 1.3 Mountain village (sanson)

- **Scale:** 10–40 households; hamlets (kumi / buraku) of 3–10 houses strung along a valley.
- **Layout:** houses stepped along the contour on terraces with stone walls; small terraced paddies at the valley
  floor, dry fields and slash-and-burn (yakihata) on slopes; forest above.
- **Buildings:**
  - farmhouses, charcoal-burner huts, watermill (BL-B)
  - **yama-no-kami** (mountain deity) shrine, often a stone or tiny hokora under a big tree at the forest edge
  - a small dō hall or hermitage rather than a full temple
  - timber-control posts where the forest belongs to the lord. In the Kiso valley, Owari domain's **timber office
    at Agematsu** and forest guards enforce the "five trees" felling ban (c. 1708; verify)
  - log bridges, vine or cantilever bridges, suspended water pipes (kakehi)
  - summit or pass shrine (tōge no jinja), pass tea house (BL-B)
- **Src:** Totman *The Green Archipelago* (forest control); Kiso local histories.

### 1.4 Fishing village (gyoson / ura)

- **Scale:** 30–150 households, much denser than farm villages.
- **Layout:** houses packed in narrow lanes parallel to the shore, gable ends to the sea; boats hauled onto the beach
  in front; net sheds and drying racks along the beach line; a steep path up to the shrine and temple on the headland
  or hill behind.
- **Buildings:**
  - 1–3 net-owner (amimoto) big houses with store sheds; many small fisher houses (BL-A)
  - net sheds (ami-goya), boat sheds, fish and seaweed drying racks, salt huts (BL-B)
  - **fish-spotting tower (uomi-yagura)** on the headland for net fisheries (assumed common for sardine and tuna nets)
  - **Ebisu / Konpira / Sumiyoshi / Funadama** shrine; a kōshin or Jizō at the landing
  - **young men's lodge (wakamono-yado)**, typical in fishing villages
  - a coastal lookout (tōmi-bansho) and beacon post in domains with sea frontiers
  - graves on the slope facing the sea
- **Src:** ja.wikipedia *Wakamono-yado*; Kotobank *Tōmi-bansho*.

### 1.5 Post town (shukuba-machi) on the Tōkaidō / Nakasendō

- **Numbers (Taigaichō 1843, so a century later than 1730; scale down slightly):**
  - **Tōkaidō, 53 stations: 111 honjin, 68 waki-honjin, 2,988 hatago, ~48,000 houses, ~196,000 people.** Average
    station: ~2 honjin, ~1.3 waki-honjin, ~56 hatago, ~900 houses.
  - Small examples: **Mariko** 211 houses, 1 honjin, 2 waki-honjin, 24 hatago. **Tsumago** (Nakasendō) 83 houses,
    1 honjin, 1 waki-honjin, 31 hatago, 418 people. **Kusatsu** (the junction) 586 houses, 2 honjin, 2 waki-honjin,
    72 hatago. **Kuwana** 2,544 houses, 120 hatago.
  - Kiso stations had to keep 50 porters and 50 horses, Tōkaidō stations 100 and 100 (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.2).
- **Layout:** strictly linear along the highway for 0.4–2 km, one house deep (sometimes two), with long narrow plots
  running back to stables, kura, kitchen gardens and fields.
  - **Each end:** a mitsuke (bank or masugata crank), a kōsatsuba near the entrance, sometimes a bansho.
  - **Centre:** toiya-ba (with its yard), honjin and waki-honjin, the dense block of hatago, the headman.
  - **Ends:** cheaper lodging, tea houses, smithy and farrier, then farms. Between stations: tateba rest stops and
    "ai-no-shuku" half-way villages.
  - **Behind the street, on the hill side:** 1–5 temples and 1–3 shrines, reached by lanes off the main street.
  - **Nearby:** ichirizuka pairs outside town, pine or cedar avenues, a river crossing or a sekisho in some cases.
- **Proportion for a mid Nakasendō station (~150 houses; assumed from the averages):** 1 honjin, 1 waki-honjin,
  1 toiya-ba, 25–40 hatago, 5–10 cheap lodgings, 10–20 tea houses and food stalls, 60–90 ordinary town houses and
  shops, 2–3 temples, 1–2 shrines, 1 kōsatsuba, 1 fire ladder, many rear stables.
- **Src:** Taigaichō (via Monumento, Kusatsu city, Kuwana city, Tsumago pages); Vaporis.

### 1.6 Intermediate stops (ai-no-shuku, tateba)

- **What:** unofficial rest hamlets between stations where lodging was banned but food and horse changes happened.
- **Buildings:** 5–30 houses, tea houses and a rest yard (BL-B), a horse trough, a Jizō or Batō Kannon, maybe a
  small dō. No honjin, no toiya-ba. (assumed from Vaporis)

### 1.7 Castle town (jōkamachi)

- **Numbers:** ~170 castles stand after the 1615 one-castle-per-province rule (Kotobank *Ikkoku ichijō rei*: from
  3,000+ to ~170). By 1730 about **170–190 castles** are daimyo seats; roughly **100+ smaller daimyo** govern from a
  **jinya** (walled office-residence) and their "castle town" is a jinya town (assumed count; verify).
- **Zoning, from the centre out** (the planned ideal; real towns bend to rivers and hills):
  1. **Castle core:** honmaru / ninomaru / sannomaru enclosures inside moats.
  2. **Upper samurai (jōshi) quarter:** big yashiki inside the outer moat or right outside the ōte-mon.
  3. **Middle samurai:** grid blocks of walled yashiki, quiet streets of nagaya-mon and plastered walls.
  4. **Townsmen's quarter (machi-ya / chōnin-chi):** along the main road through town. **Trades cluster in named
     streets:** Kaji-machi (smiths), Konya-machi (dyers), Daiku-machi (carpenters), Uo-machi (fish), Tera-machi,
     Kōya-machi, and so on. Market and merchant houses on the road; craftsmen on the side streets.
  5. **Ashigaru (foot-soldier) rows:** long plain row houses in belts on the approaches, as a defensive screen.
  6. **Temple district (tera-machi):** a line of 10–30 temples with big walled grounds and graveyards along the
     edge that faces the likely attack, doubling as a defensive line.
  7. **Edge:** the outcast community (kawata / eta village, hinin huts), usually across a river or at the far edge
     near the execution ground, which itself sits on a highway approach (assumed pattern; Botsman).
- **Streets:** T-junctions, cranks (kagi-no-te) and dead ends to slow attackers; masugata gates where roads enter.
  Townsmen's blocks often square (Edo-style 60 ken = ~109 m), samurai blocks longer.
- **Proportions:** samurai land commonly **60–70 % of the town area**; townsmen 15–25 %; temples and shrines
  10–15 % (Edo's late-period split was 68 / 16 / 16, see 1.9). Samurai and their dependents are often 40–50 % of the
  population (assumed; McClain gives Kanazawa's samurai as roughly half).
- **Buildings per mid castle town (~20,000 people; assumed):** 1 castle (with or without a keep); 1 domain school
  in a few domains only (mostly after 1750); 1 town magistrate's office (machi-bugyō) + 1 kōri-bugyō (rural
  magistrate) office; 1 jail; 5–20 kido gates in the townsmen's quarter; 3–10 fire ladders; 20–40 temples; 5–15
  shrines including a **Tōshōgū** in many domains; 1–3 dōjō; 1 horse ground; 1 archery range; domain storehouses
  and a rice market.
- **Src:** Kotobank; ja.wikipedia *Jōkamachi*; McClain; WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.1.

### 1.8 Jinya town (jinya-machi)

- **What:** the seat of a castle-less small daimyo or a shogunal daikan. One walled compound (§4) on a slight rise,
  a short street of samurai houses, a townsmen's street, a temple or two. Looks like a big post town with a
  government compound. Takayama (shogunal from 1692) is the surviving example.
- **Src:** Takayama Jinya site.

### 1.9 The great cities (Edo, Kyoto, Osaka)

**Common structure: the chō (ward) is the unit.** A chō is one block-length of street and its two frontages, run by
its own house-owners (iemochi), with a ward office, a ward elder (machi-doshiyori / chō-doshiyori), **a kido gate at
each end**, a guard hut and a notice spot. Tenants in back-alley nagaya have no vote.

- **Edo (~1 million by the 1720s; the 1721 townsmen count ~500,000, samurai and clergy uncounted):**
  - **1,678 chō by 1745** (Tokyo Metropolitan Archives, "Edo no han'i"). 933 in 1713, so the city is growing fast in
    our window.
  - **Land use (late Edo): samurai 68 %, temples and shrines 16 %, townsmen 16 %** (Tokyo planning bureau, citing
    Okamoto). Daimyo yashiki alone ~36 %.
  - **The Yamanote** (hills to the west) is samurai estates and temples; **the Shitamachi** (reclaimed lowland to the
    east) is townsmen, canals, markets and warehouses.
  - **Townsmen's block:** a 60-ken (≈109 m) square; street-front shops (omote-dana) around the edge; the centre
    (kaisho-chi) filled with back-alley tenements (ura-nagaya) on narrow roji lanes, each alley with **a shared well,
    a shared toilet, a rubbish point and a tiny Inari shrine**. The Edo saying "Ise-ya, Inari ni, inu no kuso"
    (Ise-ya shops, Inari shrines and dog dung) names the three most common sights.
  - **Firebreaks after the 1657 Meireki fire:** broad plazas (hirokōji: Ueno, Ryōgoku) and cleared strips and
    embankments (hiyoke-chi, hiyoke-dote). Temples moved out to the edges (Asakusa, Komagome, Fukagawa).
  - **Per chō:** 2 kido, 1–2 kidoban huts, 1 jishinban (often with a fire ladder), sometimes a well, often an Inari.
    Samurai districts have **tsuji-ban** guard posts at crossroads instead. One **fire tower per ~10 chō** in the
    Kyōhō period (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.2).
  - Edo castle's outer moat has the 36 mitsuke gates; the castle keep is **absent** (1657).
- **Kyoto (~350,000 in the 1730s, assumed):**
  - Grid of the old capital; **the chō is the two sides of one street between cross-streets (ryōgawa-machi)**, not a
    block. Groups of chō form a chō-gumi. Each chō has its own **chō-ie / kaisho** (ward meeting house), kido gates
    and ward rules (chō-shikimoku).
  - Temple districts (tera-machi on Teramachi-dōri and the eastern hills), the imperial palace and court nobles'
    enclosure, Nijō castle with the shoshidai and the two machi-bugyōsho nearby.
  - Remains of the Odoi earthwork (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.1).
- **Osaka (~400,000 in the 1730s, assumed):**
  - **Osaka sangō: 620 chō** in three groups (Kita 250, Minami 261, Tenma 109; late-18th-c. count, ja.wikipedia).
  - A merchant city with few samurai (the castle garrison and the Osaka jōdai / machi-bugyō officials).
  - Canals (horikawa) everywhere; bridges ("Naniwa happyaku-ya-bashi"; in fact about 200, with only a dozen or so
    built by the shogunate, the rest by townsmen, assumed).
  - **Domain warehouse-residences (kura-yashiki) on Nakanoshima and Dōjima**, about 100 (assumed).
  - **Dōjima rice exchange officially licensed in 1730** (anchor year).
  - Castle keep **absent** (1665).
- **Src:** Tokyo Metropolitan Archives; Tokyo planning bureau PDF (Okamoto); ja.wikipedia *Ōsaka sangō*; Jinnai.

### 1.10 Temple town (monzen-machi) and shrine-gate town (toriimae-machi)

- **Examples:** Zenkōji (Nagano, main hall rebuilt 1707, in window), Narita (boosted by Edo exhibitions from 1703),
  Kotohira (Konpira), Nikkō, Kōyasan (a mountain town of hundreds of sub-temples), Ise's Uji-Yamada.
- **Layout:** one approach street (sandō) climbing to the temple gate, lined with souvenir, food, rosary, incense,
  talisman and medicine shops, tea houses and pilgrim lodgings (all BL-B). Pilgrim-guide houses (shukubō at temples,
  *oshi* houses at Ise) have altar rooms and big kitchens. Side streets have the temple's sub-temples (tatchū), their
  graveyards, the magistrate's office if shogunal land, and outcast/entertainment edges.
- **Proportion (assumed):** 50–70 % of frontage is pilgrim trade, 20–40 % sub-temples, the rest ordinary housing.
- **Ise special:** the shrines were rebuilt in **1729** (the 20-year sengū cycle: 1709, 1729, 1749), so in 1730 the
  buildings are brand-new and the empty alternate site beside each sits bare with a small hut over the central-post
  spot. Pilgrim waves (okage-mairi) of millions in 1705.
- **Jinai-machi:** fortified temple towns of the Jōdo Shinshū (16th-c. origin), still ordinary towns in 1730 with a
  moat line and a central temple: Imai-chō, Tondabayashi.
- **Src:** ja.wikipedia *Zenkōji*, *Jingū shikinen sengū*, *Tondabayashi jinaimachi*.

### 1.11 Port town (minato-machi)

- **Examples:** Sakata, Mikuni, Hyōgo, Shimonoseki, Tomo-no-ura, Uraga, Nagasaki, Miya (Atsuta).
- **Layout:** streets parallel to the shore, lanes at right angles down to the water; quays with stone steps (gangi)
  and mooring stones; rows of warehouses on the waterfront; shipping agents (funa-toiya, kaisen-donya: BL-B) on the
  first street back; temples and a **weather-watching hill (hiyori-yama)** behind; lighthouse lantern (tōmyōdō) on
  the point; shipyards at one end, fish market at another.
- **Government:** a ship-inspection office (funa-bansho) at controlled ports (Uraga from 1720), port magistrate in
  shogunal ports.
- **Period markers:** the western coastal route (Kitamae) opened in 1672; the **taru-kaisen split from the
  higaki-kaisen in 1730**, the anchor year.
- **Nagasaki:** Dejima (Dutch, 1636) and the **Chinese quarter (Tōjin-yashiki, 1689)**, both walled compounds with a
  single guarded gate.
- **Src:** ja.wikipedia *Taru kaisen*, *Tōjin yashiki*; Kotobank *Hiyori-yama*.

### 1.12 Mining town (kōzan-machi)

- **Examples:** Sado Aikawa (shogunal gold and silver, magistrate's office), Ikuno, Iwami Ōmori (silver; a daikansho
  town). By 1730 production is past its peak.
- **Layout:** a narrow valley town: the magistrate's office compound, ore-processing sheds, workers' row houses,
  temples up the slopes, adit mouths above. (BL-B owns the processing sheds.)
- **Src:** ja.wikipedia *Sado kinzan*, *Iwami ginzan* (assumed detail).

### 1.13 River-crossing town

- **What:** stations where the shogunate forbade bridges (Ōi River: Shimada and Kanaya). Travellers were stranded
  by high water (kawa-dome) for days, so these towns have **extra inns and a river-crossing office** (§4.14).
- **Src:** Shimada city (kawa-goshi system from 1696).

### 1.14 Hot-spring town (onsen-machi)

- **What:** Hakone's seven springs, Arima, Kusatsu (Jōshū). A cluster of bath inns around a public bath (sotoyu) and
  the spring's shrine / Yakushi hall (the Buddha of healing). Lodging and baths are BL-B; the Yakushi-dō and the
  shrine are here (§3).

### 1.15 Outcast and marginal settlements (listed neutrally)

- **Kawata (eta) communities:** leatherworking, drum-making, prison and execution labour, some policing. Outside the
  townsmen's area, often on river flats. Buildings: ordinary houses, tanning yards, a temple (often Jōdo Shinshū),
  their own headman's compound (in Edo, Danzaemon's at Asakusa). **Hinin huts (hinin-goya)** near bridges and river
  beds. Botsman.
- **Sick-prisoner infirmaries (tame)** run by the hinin headmen at Asakusa (1687) and Shinagawa (c. 1698; verify).

## 2. Shinto

**Scale ladder (small to big):** stone kami marker → hokora → house-plot shrine → field shrine → village chinju →
town shrine → provincial first shrine (ichinomiya) / great shrine.

### 2.1 Shrine types by scale

1. **Roadside or field micro-shrine (hokora / hokura).**
   - Function: a single kami's dwelling at a field corner, tree root, spring or pass.
   - Space: a miniature wooden or stone shrine house 0.3–1 m high on a stone base; no interior to enter.
   - Items: small doors, a gohei wand, a shimenawa, a sake cup, a rice or salt dish, a small vase of sakaki, fox
     figures (Inari).
   - Src: WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.5.
2. **House-plot shrine (yashiki-gami).**
   - Function: the family's tutelary kami in the corner of a farm or samurai yard (often Inari).
   - Space: hokora under a tree, sometimes a small roofed shed around it (saya).
   - Items: as the hokora, plus mini-torii, fox pair.
3. **Alley Inari (roji Inari).** Edo back-alley shrine for the tenement. Tiny red hokora, a mini torii, fox pair,
   offering shelf, a lantern hook. Jinnai.
4. **Village tutelary shrine (chinju / ujigami-sha).**
   - Function: the village kami; centre of the festival year and the shrine guild (miyaza). Most have **no resident
     priest**; a villager serves in rotation or a travelling priest comes.
   - Space: torii + short sandō + a **small honden inside a sheltering hall (saya-dō / ōi-ya)** + sometimes a haiden;
     board floors; bare earth precinct; a big tree (goshinboku); a sumo ring for offering-sumo (hōnō-zumō) in some.
   - Items: honden as in 2.2; haiden with a bell rope, offering box, ema, straw mats (goza), a drum; stone lanterns;
     a stone basin.
   - Src: ja.wikipedia *Miyaza*; *Chinju no mori*.
5. **Town shrine (machi no jinja).** A chinju for several chō with a resident priest's house, a kagura stage, a
   mikoshi store, a sub-shrine row, a festival ground. In Kyoto/Osaka, the festival float store (#29) sits in the
   ward itself, away from the shrine. (assumed)
6. **Major shrine complex (taisha, ichinomiya, sōja).** Full precinct (2.2) behind a tower gate; cloisters; multiple
   sub-shrines; Buddhist buildings (2.3); priests' residences; a Noh or kagura stage; festival grounds. Examples in
   our map: Atsuta (Miya), Mishima Taisha, Suwa Taisha (Nakasendō region), Kasuga, Kitano, Yasaka (Gion-sha), Hie,
   Sengen at Fuji.
7. **Tōshōgū (Ieyasu shrine).** Many domains built one; gongen-zukuri (honden and haiden joined by a stone-floored
   ishi-no-ma), rich carving and colour, a karamon. Nikkō (1636 rebuild) is the extreme. Coaldrake.
8. **Ise (Jingū).** Unpainted shinmei-zukuri, thatch, raw hinoki, multiple fences; rebuilt 1729 (see 1.10). Unique;
   no Buddhist buildings allowed near it.
9. **Mountain summit / pass shrine (okumiya, tōge no yashiro).** A stone or small board shrine on a peak or pass,
   often with a stone wall against wind and a pilgrim hut (§3.4). Items: iron swords and votive plaques left by
   climbers, straw sandals, coins, a small bell.

### 2.2 Buildings inside a shrine precinct

10. **Main sanctuary (honden / shinden).**
    - Function: houses the kami's object (shintai); closed to worshippers.
    - Space: raised board floor on posts, a veranda (en) with rail, steps with a small roof (kōhai) in nagare style;
      styles shinmei, taisha, kasuga, nagare, hachiman, hie, gongen.
    - Items: closed inner doors with metal fittings, bamboo blinds (misu), shintai box (mishōtai) in a zushi, a bronze
      mirror on a stand, gohei on stands, sakaki vases, eight-legged offering tables (hassoku-an), white-wood trays
      (sanbō), sake flasks (heishi), clay plates (kawarake), hanging crest curtains (tobari), inner guardian lion-dogs
      (wooden komainu), hanging lanterns.
    - Src: JAANUS *honden*, *hassokuan*, *sanbou*.
11. **Worship hall (haiden).**
    - Function: where worshippers and priests pray in front of the honden.
    - Space: open-fronted board-floored hall, often wider than deep; sometimes an earth-floored passage through it
      (wari-haiden).
    - Items: large offering box (saisen-bako, with slatted top), a bell with a thick cloth rope (suzu, or a flat gong
      waniguchi on older halls), a big drum on a stand, votive plaques (ema) on the walls and beams, dedicatory
      boards listing donors, rolled mats and round straw cushions (enza), hanging lanterns with the crest, a
      sakaki-offering stand, a sword or arrow offering, calligraphy name board (gaku) over the entrance.
12. **Offering hall (heiden).** Between haiden and honden; a narrow hall where offerings (heihaku) are placed. Items:
    offering tables, sanbō stacks, cloth offerings on stands, gohei.
13. **Stone-floored link hall (ishi-no-ma).** Gongen-style connector; stone or plastered floor lower than the halls.
    Items: none beyond hanging lanterns and blinds.
14. **Kagura stage (kagura-den / maidono).**
    - Function: sacred dance and music for the kami.
    - Space: raised square stage, open on three or four sides, board floor, a dressing room (gakuya) at the back.
    - Items: large drum (ōdaiko) and small drums, flutes (fue) on a rack, kagura bells (suzu) on a stand, masks
      hanging on pegs, gohei, folded fans, lanterns.
15. **Noh stage (nōbutai) at great shrines.** Square roofed stage, bridgeway (hashigakari), painted pine backboard,
    three small pines along the bridge. Itsukushima's stage dates to 1680 (in window). Items: the painted pine,
    drums, masks box (dressing only), a curtain (agemaku). JAANUS *noubutai*.
16. **Ema hall (ema-dō).** Open pavilion hung with large votive paintings from the Momoyama era on. Items: big framed
    ema (horses, ships, battles, festivals), small ema, benches for resting pilgrims.
17. **Purification pavilion (temizuya / chōzuya).** Four-post roofed shelter over a stone basin fed by a bamboo pipe.
    Items: stone basin, wooden ladles on a rack (hishaku), a small towel bar (assumed), a donor inscription.
18. **Offering kitchen (shinsen-sho / shinsen-den).** Where food offerings are cooked. Items: kamado, water jars,
    white-wood cutting boards, sanbō and kawarake stacks, rice steamer, sake casks.
19. **Portable-shrine storehouse (mikoshi-gura / mikoshi-den).** A kura or roofed shed with wide doors. Items: the
    mikoshi on its carrying poles on trestles, lion-dance heads (shishigashira), festival lanterns, banners on poles,
    drums, spare poles.
20. **Treasure store (hōko / shinpō-gura).** Log or plastered kura on stilts (azekura in old shrines). Items: sword
    boxes, mirror boxes, costume chests, scrolls, dedicated armour.
21. **Priests' residence and office (shake / kannushi-yashiki; "shamusho" is the modern name).** A house (BL-A shell)
    with an office room. Items: talisman-making desk, printing blocks and brushes, stacks of paper amulets (ofuda),
    registers, the shrine seal, ritual robes on a rack.
22. **Talisman / amulet window (juyo-sho; mostly modern as a separate building, assumed).** In 1730 amulets are sold
    from the priest's house or a small booth at the haiden. Items: ofuda stacks, omamori, a counter.
23. **Sub-shrines (sessha, massha).** Small shrines along the precinct, one hokora each or a row under one roof
    (narabi-sha). Items: as hokora.
24. **Distant-worship point (yōhai-jo).** A stone or small fence facing a far shrine or mountain. No interior.
25. **Pilgrims' overnight hall (sanrō-den / tsuya-dō).** Board-floored hall where worshippers keep vigil. Items:
    straw mats, a hearth, lanterns, pegs for hats and straw raincoats.
26. **Tower gate (rōmon) / guardian gate (zuishin-mon).** Two-storey or roofed gate with **seated archer guardians
    (zuishin)** in side bays. Items: two zuishin statues behind grilles, hanging lanterns, a name board.
27. **Cloister (kairō) and sacred fence (tamagaki, mizugaki).** Roofed corridor or lattice/board fence round the
    inner sanctum.
28. **Sacred bridge (shinkyō).** Arched bridge reserved for the kami or the lord at big shrines. Vermilion.
29. **Festival float store (yama-hoko-gura) (Kyoto/Osaka wards).** Tall kura holding a dismantled float. Items:
    wheels, pole timbers, rolled tapestries in boxes, lanterns.
30. **Sumo ring (dohyō) at a village shrine.** Earth ring of straw bales; sometimes a four-post roof. (assumed common
    for offering sumo)

### 2.3 Buddhist buildings inside shrines (1730 syncretism)

31. **Attached administering temple (bettō-ji / jingū-ji).** Monks who run the shrine, living beside it; ordinary
    temple buildings (§3). Gion-sha was run by the Tendai Kanshin-in; Tsurugaoka Hachiman had a pagoda and many
    monk houses (ja.wikipedia *Shinbutsu shūgō*, *Bettō*).
32. **Pagoda at a shrine** (e.g. Itsukushima 1407, Hachiman shrines). See §3.2.
33. **Original-Buddha hall (honji-dō) / goma hall at a shrine.** The Buddha of whom the kami is a manifestation;
    esoteric fire-ritual hall. Items as §3.2 goma-dō.

### 2.4 Non-building sacred structures (names only)

34. Torii (shinmei, myōjin, ryōbu, sannō, Kashima, hachiman; stone, wood, vermilion; tunnels of donated torii at
    Inari shrines), shimenawa (straw ropes, the Izumo-thick type), shide paper strips, komainu (stone lion-dogs, a/un
    pair), kitsune pair (Inari), ox (Tenjin), monkey (Hie/Sannō), deer (Kasuga), stone lanterns (kasuga-dōrō),
    hanging bronze lanterns, onbashira pillars (4 per Suwa shrine, Shinano), sacred tree with shimenawa, sacred rock
    (iwakura), stone-pile boundary, offering stones, stone steps, donor stones (hōnō-hi), sacred horse stable
    (shinme-sha, with a live or wooden white horse), sacred pond with a Benten island.

### 2.5 Cult tells (a few props make a generic shrine read as a specific one)

35. **Inari:** vermilion, fox pairs with a key or jewel, rows of small torii, fried-tofu offering dishes.
36. **Konpira / Sumiyoshi / Ebisu (sea):** ship ema, anchors, net floats, sea-bream offerings.
37. **Akiba / Atago (fire):** fire-prevention ofuda at every door, tengu images. Popular in fire-prone Edo.
38. **Yama-no-kami / Suijin (mountain / water):** stone markers, axes or sickles offered, bamboo water pipes, snake
    motifs.

## 3. Buddhist

**Scale ladder:** roadside Jizō → hall (dō) with no priest → hermitage (an) → village temple → town temple →
sub-temple (tatchū) → major temple complex / head temple (honzan).

### 3.1 Temples by scale

1. **Hermitage (an / iori / an'shitsu).**
   - Function: a priest's, nun's or lay recluse's hut, often attached to a temple or alone in the hills. Bashō's
     Bashō-an (1680s, Fukagawa) is the period icon.
   - Space: 1–3 rooms, thatch, a small board or tatami room with a butsudan niche, earth kitchen corner.
   - Items: small Buddha statue in a zushi, incense burner, candle stand, sutra desk, a bowl gong, prayer beads, a
     brazier or hearth, writing box, a few books.
2. **Unstaffed hall (dō / dōshō).**
   - Function: a Jizō-dō, Kannon-dō, Yakushi-dō, Amida-dō, Kōshin-dō, Fudō-dō in a village or on a road, kept by
     villagers; also the village meeting place.
   - Space: 2 × 2 or 3 × 3 ken, one room, board floor, a raised altar at the back, a veranda.
   - Items: the statue on an altar shelf, bowl gong, incense burner, candle stands, vases, votive plaques, hanging
     lanterns, straw cushions, a donation box, rows of small votive statues.
3. **Village temple (mura-dera; often a Sōtō Zen, Jōdo, Shingon or Shinshū temple).**
   - Function: registers families (terauke), holds funerals and memorial rites, keeps the graveyard, often teaches
     children.
   - Space: **main hall and priest's quarters joined or under one roof (hondō-kuri)**, a bell tower, a small gate,
     the graveyard behind.
   - Items: see main hall and kuri below; plus the death register (kakochō) and the **temple registers (shūmon
     aratame-chō) copies**.
   - Src: Hur.
4. **Town temple.** Separate gate, main hall, kuri, bell tower, sometimes a founder's hall, a graveyard hemmed in by
   walls; tera-machi rows (§1.7).
5. **Sub-temple (tatchū / shiin).** A small walled temple inside a big temple's grounds, founded as a memorial to a
   past abbot or a patron family: gate, small hall, abbot's quarters with a garden, a kitchen. Kyoto Zen head temples
   have 10–40 each (assumed range).
6. **Major complex / head temple (honzan, daijiin).** Full precinct (3.2), walls, several gates, dozens of
   buildings, sub-temples, schools for monks (danrin), gardens.
7. **Pilgrim-lodging temple (shukubō).** A sub-temple that also lodges pilgrims (Kōyasan, Zenkōji). Lodging side is
   BL-B; the altar room is here. Items: altar room with statue, big tatami halls, sutra books.
8. **Nunnery (ama-dera) and divorce temples (kakekomi-dera).** Tōkeiji (Kamakura) and Mantokuji (Kōzuke) took in
   wives fleeing marriages. Walled, one gate, main hall, nuns' quarters. ja.wikipedia *Enkiri-dera*.
9. **Mausoleum temples (bodaiji, reibyō).** A daimyo family's temple with a mausoleum enclosure (tamaya). Shogunal:
   Zōjōji (Hidetada, Ienobu 1712, Ietsugu 1716) and Kan'eiji (Ietsuna 1680, Tsunayoshi 1709), all in window.
10. **Memorial temple for the unclaimed dead (muen-dera).** Ekōin (1657, Ryōgoku) for the Meireki fire dead; mounds
    and memorial stones.

### 3.2 Buildings in a temple precinct

11. **Main gate, two-storey (sanmon / san'mon).** Zen and Pure Land head temples. Upper floor is a shrine room with
    the Shaka triad and 16 arhats, reached by a steep stair in a side hut. Items: statues in a row, ceiling
    paintings, altar table, incense burner.
12. **Guardian-king gate (niōmon).** Two muscular guardian statues behind wire or lattice screens. Items: statues,
    huge straw sandals (ō-waraji) offered by pilgrims, hanging lanterns, paper slips on the grille.
13. **Outer / middle / Chinese / imperial gates (sōmon, chūmon, karamon, chokushimon).** Simple gate, inner gate,
    cusped-gable gate, imperial-messenger gate kept shut.
14. **Main hall (hondō; butsuden in Zen; kondō in old temples; Amida-dō, Kannon-dō, etc.).**
    - Function: enshrines the principal image (honzon); rites and congregational prayer.
    - Space: inner sanctum (naijin) with a raised board floor and the altar dais; outer worship space (gejin) with
      tatami (Pure Land, Shinshū) or boards; Zen butsuden has a **stone-tiled floor** and people stand.
    - Items: principal statue on a **Sumeru dais (shumidan)**, a shrine cabinet (zushi) with doors, a canopy
      (tengai), hanging openwork ornaments (keman), banners (ban), the **three altar pieces (mitsugusoku: incense
      burner, candle stand pair, flower vase pair)** or the five-piece set, a lotus-flower vase, offering stands
      (kuge-dai), a front table (maezukue), a **sutra desk (kyōzukue)**, the **priest's platform (raiban)**, a
      **bowl gong (keisu / kin) on a cushion**, a **wooden-fish drum (mokugyo; spread via Ōbaku from the 1650s)**,
      hand bells, cymbals, a large drum, memorial tablets (ihai) rows, altar cloths (uchishiki), hanging lanterns,
      a donation box, votive plaques, prayer-bead rack, zabuton rows.
    - Src: JAANUS *shumidan*, *mitsugusoku*, *keman*, *raiban*.
15. **Lecture / Dharma hall (kōdō; hattō in Zen).** Large hall for sermons; Zen hattō has a raised abbot's seat
    platform, a dragon painted on the ceiling, a stone floor. Items: preaching seat (kōza) with steps, a lectern,
    drums, bells, rows of cushions.
16. **Founder's hall (kaisandō / mieidō / soshidō / daishidō).** Houses the founder's portrait statue; in Pure Land
    and Shinshū head temples it is the **largest building** (Chion-in mieidō 1639; Higashi Honganji). Items: the
    founder's seated statue in a zushi, portraits, altar set, tablets.
17. **Priests' quarters and kitchen (kuri / kuin).**
    - Function: kitchen, office and living quarters; the working heart of the temple.
    - Space: huge earth-floored kitchen with exposed beams and a smoke gable; board-floored office; tatami living
      rooms; guest room.
    - Items: rows of kamado with big iron cauldrons, water jars, rice-washing tubs, huge rice-steaming baskets,
      cutting boards, stacked lacquer meal trays (zen), the **kitchen guardian statues (Idaten in Zen; Daikokuten)**,
      a big wooden **cloud-shaped gong (unpan) and a hanging wooden board struck with a mallet (han)** in Zen,
      account books, a writing desk, a temple seal box.
18. **Abbot's quarters (hōjō).** Zen: a six-room tatami building facing a raked-gravel garden; the central room has
    an altar. Items: fusuma paintings (allowed here), an altar, a tokonoma with a scroll, a desk, a fly whisk
    (hossu), a teaching stick (keisaku / kyōsaku), a folding chair (kyokuroku).
19. **Guest hall (kyakuden) and study hall (shoin).** Tatami reception rooms with alcove and staggered shelves. Items:
    tokonoma scroll, flower vase, armrest, tea utensils, screens.
20. **Monks' hall / meditation hall (sōdō / zendō).** Zen: long raised platforms (tan) along both walls, one tatami
    per monk, bedding in cupboards above, a central image of Monju. Items: sitting cushions (zafu), clappers
    (taku), a small bell, incense sticks as timers, the jikijitsu's (monitor's) stick.
21. **Refectory (jikidō / saidō).** Long hall with low tables. Items: nested bowls (ōryōki), meal boards, the
    hanging wooden fish (gyoban / kaipan) struck to call meals (Ōbaku/Zen), a statue of Binzuru or Kasyapa.
22. **Monks' dormitory (sōbō / ryō).** Rows of small rooms (BL-A-like), at big teaching temples.
23. **Sutra repository (kyōzō / kyōko).**
    - Function: stores the canon.
    - Space: a small square hall or plastered kura; many contain a **revolving octagonal sutra case (rinzō)** on a
      pivot, with statues of Fu Daishi and sons at the entrance.
    - Items: rinzō, shelves of numbered wooden sutra boxes, folded (orihon) sutras. The **Ōbaku Tetsugen edition of
      the full canon (completed 1681)** spread to many temples in our window.
    - Src: JAANUS *rinzou*; ja.wikipedia *Tetsugen-ban*.
24. **Bell tower (shōrō / kanetsuki-dō).** Open four-post pavilion (or with a flared skirt wall, hakama-goshi) over a
    stone platform. Items: **great bronze bell (bonshō)**, **suspended log striker (shumoku) on ropes**, the rope,
    a stone step. Also serves as the **time bell** (§6).
25. **Drum tower (korō / taikorō).** Two-storey pavilion with a big drum; common in Shinshū and Zen. Items: large
    drum on a stand, drumsticks, a stair.
26. **Pagoda (tō: three-storey sanjūnotō, five-storey gojūnotō).**
    - Function: a relic monument; not entered by worshippers.
    - Space: a square core around the **central pillar (shinbashira)**; ground floor has an altar with four Buddhas
      facing out; upper floors are only a timber lattice with ladders.
    - Items: four statues, painted pillars (sometimes), a railing, bronze finial (sōrin) visible outside, wind bells
      at the eave corners.
27. **Two-storey treasure pagoda (tahōtō).** Shingon/Tendai; square lower storey, round collar, square roof. Items:
    Dainichi statue on an altar.
28. **Esoteric fire-ritual hall (goma-dō).** Shingon/Tendai and shugendō. Items: **goma hearth altar (goma-dan) with
    a copper hearth**, stacked goma sticks, a Fudō statue with flame halo, ritual implements (vajra, bells) on a
    table, soot-blackened ceiling, a donors' board.
29. **Kannon / Yakushi / Jizō / Fudō / Enma halls (dō).** Single-deity halls. Enma-dō: the judge of hell Enma and
    the hag Datsueba; items: statues, a pair of judgement scrolls (hell paintings), a mirror. Yakushi: medicine jar
    statue, votive eyes or limbs.
30. **Memorial-tablet hall (ihai-dō / reiden).** Rows of stepped shelves of family memorial tablets. Items: tablets,
    small lanterns, altar.
31. **Mausoleum (reibyō / tamaya / otamaya).** Walled enclosure with a gate, a small richly decorated hall or a stone
    stupa (hōkyōintō / gorintō) on a platform. Items: stone lanterns in rows (donated by retainers), a worship hall
    table, a karamon.
32. **Bathhouse (yokushitsu / yudono).** Zen seven-hall set: a **steam bath (mushiburo)** with a slatted floor over a
    boiler and a tub room; Tōfukuji's survives from 1459. Items: slatted floor, iron cauldron and fire pit, wooden
    buckets, a small statue of Batsudabara (the bath bodhisattva), towel pegs.
33. **Latrine (tōsu / sesshin).** Zen: rows of floor pits (Tōfukuji's 14th-c. latrine survives). Items: pit slots,
    water jars, a statue of Ususama Myōō, ladles. (Scatological in reality; in game, a board building with slots.)
34. **Ordination platform (kaidan-in).** Only at a few major temples. A walled stone terrace inside a hall.
35. **Worship-stage hall (kake-zukuri / butai-zukuri).** A hall built out over a cliff on a timber lattice
    (Kiyomizu-dera rebuilt 1633). Special structure; interior as a main hall.
36. **Tea room at a temple (chashitsu).** A 2–4.5-mat room with a small crawl-in door. Items: sunken hearth, kettle,
    tea caddy, scroll, flower (tea-house commerce is BL-B; the temple tea room is here).
37. **Temple storehouse (hōzō / kura).** Plastered kura. Items: statue crates, festival goods, lacquered chests.
38. **Kōshin hall (kōshin-dō).** For the 60-day Kōshin vigil groups; Shōmen Kongō statue, three monkeys. Items:
    statue, vigil mats, a brazier.
39. **Benten hall on a pond island.** Small hall on an island reached by a bridge.
40. **Tutelary shrine inside a temple (chinju-sha).** A small shrine in the temple grounds (syncretism).
41. **Graveyard (bochi) and its hut (bōchi-goya / sotoba shed).** Graves (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.6), six Jizō (roku
    Jizō) at the entrance, a water point with buckets and ladles, a **rack of tall wooden memorial slats (sotoba)**,
    a hut for tools and a coffin bier (kan-dai) (assumed), a pile of unclaimed-grave stones (muen-botoke) in a
    pyramid.
42. **Temple school room (tenarai-dokoro).** Many terakoya ran in the temple's guest room or kuri. See §6.

### 3.3 Sect differences that change the buildings

43. **Zen (Rinzai, Sōtō).** The **seven-hall set (shichidō garan)**: sanmon, butsuden, hattō, hōjō, kuri, sōdō,
    yokushitsu, tōsu, plus kyōzō and bell tower, on one axis. Stone floors in the butsuden and hattō; raked-gravel
    gardens at the hōjō. Zen gongs: unpan, han, gyoban. Sōtō is the most common village temple sect in the east.
44. **Ōbaku Zen (Manpukuji, from 1661).** Ming Chinese style: chairs and tables, round windows, the hanging wooden
    fish (kaipan), Chinese lattice railings, peach-shaped openings, Chinese inscription boards. Very current in 1730.
45. **Pure Land (Jōdo).** A huge mieidō, a big sanmon, a tatami gejin for chanting crowds, large bells (Chion-in's
    1636 great bell). Amida triad; hanging lotus ornaments.
46. **True Pure Land (Jōdo Shinshū, Honganji branches).** **No pagoda, no goma hall, often no bell tower but a drum
    tower; the founder's hall is bigger than the Amida hall at head temples; a vast tatami gejin** for lay
    congregations; a huge kuri for communal meals. Few graves and no memorial rites around them in principle (Shinshū
    regions cremated more). Village Shinshū **"dōjō"**: a house-like meeting chapel run by lay members.
47. **Nichiren (Hokke).** Founder's hall (soshidō) central; **daimoku stone pillar** carved "Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō";
    Kishimojin (child-protecting goddess) hall; **fan drums (uchiwa-daiko)** instead of mokugyo; a gohonzon
    calligraphy mandala scroll as the main object in small temples.
48. **Shingon and Tendai (esoteric).** Mountain sites; tahōtō; goma-dō; an inner sanctuary (okunoin) up the mountain;
    Kōbō Daishi halls; two mandala scrolls hung facing each other (Taizōkai / Kongōkai) in the main hall; esoteric
    altar (dan) with vajras, bells and five vases.
49. **Shugendō (mountain ascetics, yamabushi).** Practice halls (gyōja-dō, En-no-Gyōja statue), conch shells, ringed
    staffs (shakujō), practice sites at waterfalls and cliffs with chains (kusari-ba), summit huts.
50. **Ji-shū and others.** Minor; odori-nenbutsu stages (assumed). Skip unless needed.

### 3.4 Roadside, pilgrim and folk Buddhist structures

51. **Roadside Jizō (michi-no-Jizō).** Stone figure, often with a red bib and cap, a small offering stone, sometimes
    under a tiny roof (Jizō-dō). Items: bib, cap, flowers, coins, water cup, a pinwheel (assumed Edo).
52. **Six Jizō row (roku Jizō).** At cemetery entrances and village edges.
53. **Horse-headed Kannon (Batō Kannon) stone.** On pack-horse roads, for dead horses. Nakasendō has many.
54. **Kōshin stones (kōshin-tō), 23rd-night stones (nijūsan-ya tō), memorial stupas (kuyō-tō), nenbutsu stones,
    myōgō stones.** Village-group monuments, mostly Edo-period.
55. **Pilgrim overnight hall (tsuya-dō / henro-goya / zenkon-yado).** Free shelter for pilgrims on the Shikoku and
    Saigoku routes: a roofed board platform or a hut. Items: straw mats, a fire pit, a hook for hats, a donation box,
    a Kōbō Daishi statue.
56. **Mountain pilgrim hut (murodō).** Stone-and-timber hut high on a sacred mountain; Tateyama's murodō (rebuilt
    1617/1726) is Japan's oldest mountain hut. Items: sleeping platform, hearth, straw bedding, pilgrim staffs, a
    small altar.
57. **Waterfall practice site (taki-gyōba).** A Fudō statue at the fall, a small changing hut, a shimenawa across
    the fall.
58. **Stone Buddhas carved in cliffs (magaibutsu) and cave halls (iwaya-dō).** A hall built against or into a cliff
    (assumed common in Shugendō areas).
59. **Stone boat-shaped grave Buddhas, gorintō and hōkyōintō.** In graveyards (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.6).

### 3.5 Buddhist items used outdoors (names only)

60. Stone and bronze lanterns, water basin (chōzubachi), incense burner pavilion (large bronze jōkōro under a roof,
    at busy temples; smoke is wafted over the body), offering-coin box, flagpole banners (nobori), prayer-slip
    stands, rows of small donated Jizō.
61. **Memorial stones for the Kyōhō famine (1732)** would appear right after the anchor year (kiga kuyō-tō); a very
    local, very dark detail (neutral).
62. **Temple exhibitions (kaichō)** set up temporary stands and banners (Narita in Edo, 1703 onward). Event dressing.

## 4. Government

**What reads as "official" (PLAYBOOK §2.2):** black boards, a heavy gate, a white-gravel court, a genkan with a
shikidai step, the three capture tools on a rack, crest curtains, a notice board out front.

### 4.1 Shogunal and city administration

1. **Town magistrate's office (machi-bugyōsho).**
   - Function: Edo's North and South offices (Ōoka Tadasuke is South Magistrate 1717–1736, so in office in 1730);
     also Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, Sakai, Fushimi, Nagasaki, Sado, Uraga. Police, courts, city administration.
   - Space: a black nagaya-mon gate with guard rooms; front court; genkan; office wings for yoriki and dōshin
     (inspectors and constables); **the trial court: a white-gravel yard (oshirasu) below stepped floor levels**
     (commoners kneel on a straw mat on the gravel; samurai on the veranda; the magistrate on the tatami room above);
     **the magistrate lives in the rear of the same compound**.
   - Items: magistrate's raised seat and armrest, low writing desks, inkstones, document boxes (bunko) in stacks,
     record ledgers, the **sodegarami, sasumata and tsukubō (capture tools) on a rack at the gate**, crest curtain
     (maku) at the gate, torches and lanterns with the office name, straw mats on the gravel, rope bundles for tying
     suspects (hojō), a small holding room, a clerk's counter, a messenger bench, a hanshō bell or drum.
   - Src: ja.wikipedia *Machi-bugyō*, *Oshirasu*; Botsman.
2. **Shogunal council chamber (hyōjōsho).** Highest court (Tatsunokuchi, Edo). Same pattern as the bugyōsho, grander.
   The **petition box (meyasubako, 1721)** was set at its gate on the 2nd, 11th and 21st of each month. Items: the
   locked petition box (a small lidded box on a stand), bench, guard. ja.wikipedia *Meyasubako*.
3. **Finance magistrate's office (kanjōsho).** Offices for tax, roads, rivers, daikan oversight. Items: abacus rows,
   ledgers, scales, maps rolled in boxes, seal box.
4. **Shrine and temple magistrate (jisha-bugyō).** Served from the daimyo's own residence; no separate building.
   (Note only.)
5. **Kyoto deputy (shoshidai) and Osaka castellan (jōdai) residences-offices.** Big walled yashiki with offices; near
   Nijō and Osaka castles.
6. **Holding and interrogation stations (ōbanya / sayadō).** Several in Edo; a board building where constables held
   and questioned suspects before sending them on. Items: lattice holding cell, rope, capture tools, a desk, a
   brazier, a lamp. ja.wikipedia *Ōbanya*.
7. **Mints (kinza, ginza, zeniza).** Gold, silver and copper-coin mints (the Ginza in Edo and Kyoto). Walled works
   with offices, furnaces and guards. Items: coin moulds, scales, ingot trays, strings of coins, account books. (Mint
   industry could be BL-B; the guarded office is here.)
8. **Foreign compounds at Nagasaki: Dejima (1636) and Tōjin-yashiki (1689).** Walled; a single guarded gate with an
   inspection office. Items: gate guard room with ledgers and seals, capture tools.

### 4.2 Domain and district administration

9. **Shogunal district office / jinya (daikansho, gundai).**
   - Function: a shogunal intendant's (daikan) seat collecting tax and judging cases for a region (~50,000–100,000
     koku); also the seat of a castle-less daimyo.
   - Space: nagaya-mon gate; genkan; **ōhiroma** hall; officials' rooms (yakusho); clerks' office; a **white-gravel
     court**; **rice storehouses**; the intendant's private residence behind; kitchen; guard room; sometimes a small
     jail.
   - Items: tax registers (kenchi-chō, nengu warituke-jō), village detail books (meisai-chō), **rice sampling spikes
     (sashi)**, standard measuring boxes (kyōmasu), scales and weights, survey poles (kenzao) and ropes, document
     chests, seal box, abacus, sword rack at the entrance, hibachi, zabuton, a tobacco tray, a notice board outside.
   - Src: Takayama Jinya site (shogunal from 1692; its rice store came from the castle in 1695, verify).
10. **Domain government office (han-chō, in the castle's ninomaru or a separate yakusho).** Offices for elders
    (karō), finance (kanjō-kata), rural magistrates (kōri-bugyō). Items as the jinya, more of them.
11. **Rural magistrate's office (kōri-bugyōsho / gundai-sho).** Domain equivalent of a daikansho in outlying
    districts. Smaller jinya.
12. **Village headman's office function.** No separate building in most villages: **the headman's house (BL-A) is
    the office**. Its office room holds: village registers (shūmon aratame-chō, gonin-gumi-chō), tax allocation
    papers, the village seal, a writing desk, abacus, a document chest, a kura with the village records. Some
    villages had a **village office (mura-kaisho / gōya)** or used the shrine haiden or a dō for meetings (§6).
    Src: Hanley; ja.wikipedia *Nanushi*.
13. **Ward meeting house (chō-ie / chō-kaisho).** Kyoto and Osaka wards owned a small house for meetings and the ward
    records; a caretaker lived in it. Items: ward rulebook, member registers, a meeting room with zabuton, a ward
    lantern, fire buckets. Edo's city-wide machi-kaisho (relief fund office) is `[outside 1680-1750: 1791]`.
14. **Notice board (kōsatsuba / seisatsuba).**
    - Function: posts shogunal and domain law boards (Christian ban, arson, poison and counterfeit bans, fugitive
      reward notices, porter and horse fare boards at stations). The 1711 Shōtoku boards stay up 150 years.
    - Space: a roofed frame on a stone plinth behind a low fence, at bridges, crossroads, post-town entrances,
      village entrances.
    - Items: 5–7 black-lettered wooden boards (kōsatsu), a fence (saku), a tile or board roof, a stone base; plus
      temporary paper notices pinned to a side board. At stations add the **fare board (dachin-fuda)**.
    - Src: WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.2 [T23].
15. **Border / domain checkpoint (kuchi-dome bansho, sakai bansho).** Domain-level control posts at domain borders
    (not shogunal sekisho): a gate, a guard hut, a bar. Items: capture tools, ledgers of passes, a bar gate, a
    lantern, a bench.

### 4.3 Highway control

16. **Highway checkpoint (sekisho).**
    - Function: shogunal barrier checking passes; "guns in, women out". About 50 across Japan (assumed); the big
      four for our map are **Hakone and Arai (Imagiri) on the Tōkaidō, Usui and Kiso-Fukushima on the Nakasendō**.
    - Space: **two gates (Edo-side and Kyoto-side) ~18 m apart (Hakone)** with the road between; the **upper
      guardhouse (ōbansho)** for the officials with raised tatami rooms facing the road; the **foot-soldiers'
      guardhouse (ashigaru-bansho)**; a **lookout (tōmi-bansho)** on the slope; **palisades (saku)** from the
      mountain to the lake; stables; a small **jail (rōya)** at Hakone; a **women's inspection room** where
      **female inspectors (hitomi-onna)** checked women travellers; kitchen; stores.
    - Items: **display of weapons at the guardhouse: rows of spears, matchlocks, bows and the three capture tools**
      on racks under the eave; a **board where passes are presented**; officials' desks, ledgers of passes (tegata)
      and the pass seals to compare; inkstones; a bench outside; lanterns; the gate bar and wicket; a mounting
      block; a drum or bell to close the gate.
    - Src: Hakone Sekisho reconstruction (2007); Arai Sekisho (surviving 1855 building); Vaporis *Breaking
      Barriers*.
17. **Arai sekisho variant: the lake-shore sekisho with a boat landing.** Travellers arrived by the Imagiri ferry;
    the checkpoint has a landing stage inside its fence.
18. **Post-station office (toiya-ba).**
    - Function: organises the relay of official porters and horses and cargo from station to station; staffed by
      the toiya (head), toshiyori (elders), chōtsuke (clerks), umazashi and hitozashi (horse and porter
      dispatchers).
    - Space: an open-fronted office on the main street with a raised board floor facing an earth yard; a yard for
      horses and porters with tie posts; baggage stands; a back office.
    - Items: counters and desks, the relay ledgers (tsugitate-chō), fare boards, **large steelyard scales for cargo**,
      stacked pack saddles (ni-gura), porter's carrying poles and frames, rope bundles, lanterns with the station
      name, tie rings, mounting block, baggage benches, a timetable board, the station seal.
    - Src: WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.2 [T21, T20]; Vaporis.
19. **Cargo weight-check station (kanme-aratame-sho).** At five stations (Shinagawa, Itabashi, Fuchū, Kusatsu,
    Oiwake; set up c. 1712, verify) officials weighed official cargo against the limit. Items: a very large
    beam scale on a frame, weights, a register, benches.
20. **Official courier relay point (tsugi-hikyaku).** A room in the toiya-ba where official letter boxes were handed
    on. Items: lacquered letter box (fubako) on a pole, bells, a register.
21. **Daimyo inn (honjin).**
    - Function: the official lodging of daimyo, court nobles and shogunal officials; the house of the station's
      leading family (often also toiya or headman). Ordinary travellers never lodge here.
    - Space: a formal **gate (yakui-mon or kabuki-mon)** in a walled front; a **genkan with a shikidai step**; a
      suite of **tatami rooms ending in the jōdan-no-ma (raised room)** for the lord, with tokonoma, staggered
      shelves and a built-in desk; attendants' rooms; a special bath and toilet for the lord; a garden; a large kitchen;
      a separate side entrance for the family's own use.
    - Items: the lord's raised seat with an armrest (kyōsoku), sword stand (katana-kake), folding screens, a hanging
      scroll and flowers in the tokonoma, lacquered trays and bowls, the lord's bath tub, mosquito-net frames,
      **name placards (sekifuda) announcing which lord is staying, hung at the station entrance and the gate**,
      **crest curtains (maku) across the gate**, crest lanterns, bedding stacks, ledgers of stays.
    - Src: WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.2 [T19, T57]; Kusatsu honjin (surviving); Mishima city.
22. **Deputy daimyo inn (waki-honjin).** A smaller honjin, used when the honjin was full or for lesser officials; in
    between times it took ordinary travellers of standing. Same items, smaller jōdan room, no or smaller gate. [T18]
23. **Post-town end marker (mitsuke).** See WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.1; a stone-faced earth bank, sometimes a bansho.
24. **River-crossing office (kawa-kaisho) and porters' station.**
    - Function: at unbridged rivers (Ōi: Shimada and Kanaya, from 1696), sets fares by water depth, sells crossing
      tickets (kawa-fuda), dispatches river porters (kawa-goshi ninsoku) and litters.
    - Space: an office on the bank road with a raised floor; the porters' **waiting sheds (bango-ya)**, about 10 per
      bank (one per porter group); a gauge post in the river.
    - Items: ticket counter, ticket boards, the **depth-gauge post (marked at knee, thigh, waist, chest)**, a fare
      board by depth, **carrying platforms (rendai)** of several sizes stacked outside, ropes, ledgers, lanterns.
    - Src: Shimada city; ja.wikipedia *Kawa-goshi*.
25. **Ferry control point (watashi-ba bansho).** On ferried rivers (Rokugō from 1688 after its bridge washed away;
    Tenryū) and the **Shichiri-no-watashi sea ferry (Miya–Kuwana)**; a guard hut and fare office by the landing.
    Items: fare board, ledgers, a bell, a lantern post (Miya's **jōyatō** constant lantern, 1625).
26. **River traffic inspection station (funa-bansho, kawa-bansho).** Edo's Nakagawa funa-bansho (1661) checked boats
    entering Edo. A guard building on the bank with a boat slip. Items: capture tools, ledgers, a signal bell, a
    chain or boom (assumed).
27. **Port ship inspection (Uraga bugyōsho, from 1720).** Inspected every ship entering Edo Bay. Office with a
    waterfront inspection shed. Items: ledgers, cargo lists, measuring rods, a flag or lantern signal post.

### 4.4 Tax and stores

28. **Village storehouse (gōkura).**
    - Function: holds the village's tax rice before shipment and, in many villages, seed and relief reserves.
    - Space: a board or plastered kura on a raised stone base, sometimes on short posts with rat guards; a broad
      door with a heavy lock; slatted floor; vents.
    - Items: stacks of rice bales (tawara) on duckboards, measuring boxes (masu) and strickle, rice sampler spike,
      scales, a tally board on the wall, a heavy lock and key, brooms.
    - Src: WORLD_CATALOGUE 2.7; ja.wikipedia *Gōkura*.
29. **Shogunal rice storehouses (o-kura, Asakusa o-kura).** A long row of kura along the Sumida with **comb-tooth
    boat inlets (hori)** so boats unload straight into them. Guard houses, a magistrate's office (kura-bugyō). Items:
    bales in stacks to the roof, carrying hooks, masu, scales, ledgers, lamps with fire covers. (Asakusa: about 50
    storehouse buildings and 8 inlets, assumed; verify.)
30. **Domain warehouse-residence (kura-yashiki).** In Osaka (and Edo), each big domain's walled compound with a canal
    gate (funa-iri), kura and an office for sales of tax rice. Items: rice bales, bills of sale, a counting room,
    the domain crest on lanterns and gate curtains.
31. **Treasury storehouse (kinzō / okane-gura).** Plastered kura with iron-clad doors in castles and bugyōsho.
    Items: iron-banded money chests on the floor (dressing), coin strings, ingot boxes, a guard's stool.

### 4.5 Law and punishment (listed neutrally)

32. **Jail (rōya / rōya-shiki).**
    - Function: holds suspects and prisoners awaiting judgement or punishment (prison terms as punishment were rare).
    - Space (Edo Kodenmachō, ~2,600 tsubo; ~2,677 tsubo from 1775): walled compound with a moat-like ditch; offices
      and guard rooms; cell blocks split by status: **ōrō** (large commoner cell), **nikenrō** (second commoner
      cell), **agari-ya** (lower samurai, priests, doctors), **agari-zashiki** (from 1683, for bannermen and high
      clergy, tatami and better fittings); **hyakushō-rō** for peasants `[outside 1680-1750: 1775]`; an
      interrogation room (sensaku-sho); a torture store (gōmon-gura); an execution yard inside the walls for
      beheadings; a women's cell.
    - Items: **double timber lattice cells (inner lattice + outer corridor + outer lattice)**, a small food hatch, a
      toilet tub in the cell, stacked tatami on which the cell boss sat (in the ōrō), straw mats, wooden bowls,
      guards' lanterns and clappers, keys on a board, rope, capture tools, ledgers, a brazier in the guard room.
    - Src: Chūō ward site; ja.wikipedia *Denmachō rōya-shiki*; Botsman.
33. **Provincial jail / lock-up (at a jinya, castle town or sekisho).** A small lattice-fronted board building with
    2–4 cells. Items: as above, fewer.
34. **Temporary house confinement (oshikome / azukari).** Suspects held in a headman's house or a ward office. No
    building; a lattice room (BL-A).
35. **Execution grounds (keijō / shiokiba).** Edo's **Kozukappara** (north, on the Nikkō/Ōshū road) and
    **Suzugamori** (south, on the Tōkaidō), both 1651, deliberately on highway approaches; Kyoto's Awataguchi;
    Osaka's Sennichi. Space: a fenced open ground beside the road. Items (neutral): bamboo fence, stone bases for
    posts, a covered exposure platform (sarashi-ba) or a head-display stand (gokumon-dai), a well, a memorial
    Jizō (Kozukappara's great Jizō, 1741, in window) and a memorial temple nearby.
    - Src: ja.wikipedia *Kozukappara keijō*, *Suzugamori keijō*; Botsman.
36. **Public exposure platform in town (sarashi-ba).** At Nihonbashi's south end next to the notice board. A roofed
    board platform with a low fence.
37. **Sick-prisoner infirmary (tame).** See §1.15.

### 4.6 Fire and ward watch

38. **Fire watchtower (hi-no-mi yagura).**
    - Function: lookout and alarm. Shogunal fire-brigade towers from 1658, 3 jō (~9 m), plain wood; daimyo and town
      towers black and lower; in the Kyōhō period one per ~10 chō, otherwise a ladder on the ward office.
    - Space: a four-leg tapering timber tower with a ladder, a small roofed platform on top.
    - Items: **alarm bell (hanshō)** and hammer, a drum (shogunal towers used drums; town towers bells, assumed),
      railing, a lantern, a flag or wind vane (assumed).
    - Src: WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.2 [T12, T13].
39. **Fire ladder with bell (hanshō-dai / hi-no-mi hashigo).** A tall ladder on a ward office roof or a post,
    with a small bell. The cheap everywhere version.
40. **Shogunal fire brigade compound (jōbikeshi yashiki).** From 1658; about 10 in 1730 (assumed). A walled yashiki
    with a tall tower, quarters for firemen (gaen), equipment sheds. Items: tower with drum, **matoi standards** on
    racks, fire hooks (tobi-guchi), long ladders, water buckets, padded fire coats hung up, lanterns, the
    commander's office.
41. **Townsmen's fire brigade station (machi-bikeshi; the Iroha 47 groups, 1720).** Ōoka's reform, perfect for 1730.
    A shed or room in a jishinban. Items: the group's **matoi**, ladders, fire hooks, big rakes, buckets, water
    barrels, a banner with the group letter, padded jackets, a bell. **No hand pump yet:** the ryūdosui pump is
    `[outside 1680-1750: 1754+ in Edo, mostly later]`.
42. **Ward gate (kido).** Posts, beam, two leaves plus a wicket; closed at about 10 pm (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.1).
43. **Gatekeeper's hut (kido-ban / bantaya).** 6 × 9 shaku with a 10-shaku eave, two old watchmen (WORLD_CATALOGUE
    [T11]). In Edo the gatekeepers **sold cheap goods on the side** (straw sandals, candles, sweets, paper, charcoal:
    "bantarō" shops). Items: a lantern with the ward name, **wooden clappers (hyōshigi)** for the night rounds, a
    small counter with sundries, a brazier and kettle, a sleeping mat, a fire hook, a staff.
    Src: ja.wikipedia *Kido-ban*.
44. **Townsmen's self-watch post (jishin-ban).** Run by the ward house-owners in rotation, often at the corner
    opposite the kido; doubles as the ward office. Items: **the three capture tools on a rack**, a fire ladder on the
    roof, fire buckets and hooks, a ward ledger, a lamp, a brazier, a register of residents, a bench, a notice board.
45. **Samurai-district crossroads guard post (tsuji-ban).** Staffed by samurai households or hired men; several
    hundred in Edo (assumed). Items: capture tools, a six-foot staff (rokushaku-bō), a lantern, a bench, a brazier.
46. **Castle / gate guardhouse (bansho).** See §5.

## 5. Military

**The 1615 rule and 1730:** the *ikkoku ichijō* order cut 3,000+ castles to ~170 (Kotobank; some sources ~400
destroyed in days). New castle building needed shogunal permission and repairs needed a filing (Buke shohatto,
1615/1635). So in 1730:
- ~170–190 castles stand, almost all **daimyo residences-and-offices**, not fighting forts. They are maintained, not
  new.
- **Many keeps are already gone** and not rebuilt: **Edo (1657), Osaka (1665)**; others burned or were never built.
  A castle without a keep, holding its office functions in a turret and the palace, is a normal 1730 sight. Nijō
  keeps its tenshu until 1750.
- **Abandoned castle sites (haijō) from 1615** survive everywhere as earthworks: overgrown baileys, dry moats,
  stone-wall stumps. Good game content (listed at 5.1 #1).
- There are no barracks in the modern sense: retainers live in their own yashiki in the castle town; foot soldiers in
  row houses (BL-A). Castle guard is by rotating watches in gate bansho.

### 5.1 Castle parts

1. **Abandoned castle site (shiro-ato / haijō).** Earthworks and stone-wall ruins of a pre-1615 mountain or hill
   castle, overgrown, sometimes with a small shrine on the old honmaru. Items: tumbled stone, a hokora, a memorial
   stone (assumed).
2. **Castle keep (tenshu).**
   - Function: symbol and last redoubt; in peacetime mostly a store and lookout, rarely lived in.
   - Space: stone base (tenshudai) with a stone-lined basement store; 3–5 storeys of plain board floors, heavy posts,
     very steep stairs, an outer walkway strip (musha-bashiri) along the walls; the top floor has a view railing and
     sometimes a small shrine. Types: stand-alone, linked (renketsu), connected (fukugō), ring-linked (renritsu).
   - Items: **weapon racks (spears, guns, bows)**, armour chests (gusoku-bitsu), arrow boxes, **stone-drop hatches
     (ishi-otoshi) in the floor edge**, **gun and arrow loopholes (sama: round, triangular, square)** with sliding
     covers, powder and bullet boxes, a well in the basement at some castles, a small shrine on the top floor, water
     barrels, lanterns. Gold-leaf roof fish (shachihoko) outside.
   - Src: JAANUS *tenshu*; ja.wikipedia *Tenshu*.
3. **Keep base without a keep (tenshudai).** Edo and Osaka in 1730: a bare stone platform. A strong landmark.
4. **Corner turret (sumi-yagura).** 2–3 storeys at enclosure corners. Items: weapon racks, stored goods (turrets
   were **storehouses in peacetime**: armour, bows, salt, rice, archives), loopholes, stone-drops.
5. **Long wall turret (tamon-yagura).** A long single-storey turret along the rampart, used as storage and barracks
   for watches. Items: stacked chests, racks, straw mats, lanterns.
6. **Drum turret (taiko-yagura).** Houses the drum that signalled times and musters. Items: great drum, stand, a bell.
7. **Named storage turrets.** Salt turret (shio-yagura), gun turret (teppō-yagura), bow turret (yumi-yagura), armour
   turret (gusoku-yagura), moon-viewing turret (tsukimi-yagura, with open verandas). Items match the name.
8. **Well turret / well house (ido-yagura).** Roofed well inside an enclosure. Items: well curb, pulley, buckets.
9. **Outer gate of a box gate (kōrai-mon).** Low-roofed gate with small roofs over the leaves. Items: iron-plated
   doors, bar, wicket.
10. **Inner turret gate of a box gate (yagura-mon).** A gate under a turret; the box (masugata) between the two gates
    is a square killing yard. Items: iron-plated leaves, the upper room with loopholes and stone-drop slots, weapon
    racks.
11. **Main gate (ōte-mon) and rear gate (karamete-mon).** Usually box gates; the ōte has a big guardhouse.
12. **Buried gate (uzumi-mon).** A tunnel-like gate through a stone wall. Items: heavy door, bar.
13. **Other gates: yakui-mon, kabuki-mon, niwa-mon (garden gate), hidden gate (kakushi-mon), water gate (mizu-mon)**
    for boats in moat-fronted castles.
14. **Barbican (umadashi).** An earth or stone outwork in front of a gate. No interior.
15. **Earth-and-plaster wall (dobei) with loopholes.** Tile-capped; loopholes every 1–2 ken; sometimes a stone-drop
    bay. (WORLD_CATALOGUE 2.4)
16. **Stone walls (ishigaki), moats (wet mizu-bori, dry kara-bori), earth ramparts (dorui).** Names only.
17. **Bridges: earth causeway (dobashi), wooden bridge, removable / drawbridge (hiki-bashi, hane-bashi), roofed
    corridor bridge (rōka-bashi).** Names only.
18. **The lord's palace (goten / honmaru goten / ninomaru goten).**
    - Function: residence and government seat; where the daimyo lives when in his domain, receives vassals and runs
      the domain.
    - Space: a chain of single-storey buildings connected by corridors: **front (omote)**: entrance hall (genkan with
      tōzamurai waiting room), **great audience hall (ōhiroma)** with a jōdan-no-ma, formal studies (shiro-shoin,
      kuro-shoin), office rooms for elders; **middle (naka-oku)**: the lord's private rooms; **inner (oku)**: wife
      and women's quarters; **kitchen (daidokoro) wing**; a Noh stage in the courtyard at bigger castles; gardens.
    - Items: painted fusuma (gold ground in the formal rooms, ink in private ones), coffered ceilings, tokonoma with
      staggered shelves and built-in desk, a raised dais with armrest, sword stands, lacquered boxes, folding
      screens, braziers (hibachi), andon lamps, tobacco sets, bells to summon attendants (assumed), a Noh stage's
      pine board.
    - Src: Nijō Ninomaru goten (surviving); Nagoya honmaru goten (reconstructed); Coaldrake.
19. **Rice storehouses (kome-gura / hyōrō-gura).** Kura rows inside an enclosure. Items: bales, masu, scales.
20. **Powder magazine (enshō-gura / enshōgura).** A stone or thick-plastered building apart from others; Osaka
    Castle's **stone powder magazine (1685)** survives. Items: powder barrels and boxes on slatted shelves, copper
    scoops, no fire; bamboo and wooden tools only; lead ingot stacks nearby.
    - Src: Osaka Castle site.
21. **Armoury (bugu-gura / buki-gura / gusoku-gura).** Plastered kura or a turret. Items: armour on stands and in
    chests, helmets on stands, spears in long racks, naginata, **matchlocks in racks**, matchcord coils, bows and
    quivers, arrow bundles, bullet moulds, powder flasks, cartridge boxes (hayago), campaign curtains (jinmaku)
    rolled, banners (nobori, sashimono), war drums, **conch trumpets (horagai)**, war fans (gunbai), saddles and
    horse armour, pack-horse loads.
22. **Treasury (kinzō).** Osaka's survives from 1751 (edge of window). §4.4 #31.
23. **Gate guardhouse (bansho).** A board hut or long room at each important gate; Edo's **hyakunin-bansho**
    ("hundred-man guardhouse", surviving) at the Ōte-san-no-mon. Items: **weapon display under the eave (spears,
    guns, bows in racks)**, capture tools, a raised tatami guard room, a register of passers, lanterns, a brazier,
    a bell, a watch timetable board.
24. **Castle stable (umaya).** Hikone's (late 17th c., the only surviving castle stable) is a long L-shaped building
    with **21 stalls**. Items: stalls with boards and posts, mangers, straw, tack on pegs, saddles on stands,
    buckets, a grooms' room with a board floor. Src: Hikone Castle site.
25. **Kitchen building and workshops within the castle.** Big earth-floored kitchen, a carpenters' shed, a smithy for
    repairs (assumed).
26. **Castle shrine (shiro no jinja).** A small shrine to the castle's protector inside an enclosure (e.g. the keep
    top-floor shrine or a hokora in a bailey).
27. **Moat-side willow / pine rows and bamboo thickets.** Planting as defence (bamboo on earthworks). Names only.

### 5.2 Training grounds

28. **Swordsmanship hall (kenjutsu dōjō).**
    - Function: sword training; domain halls in the castle town and private town schools (machi-dōjō) in Edo.
    - Space: a single board-floored hall with an entrance and changing area; a raised seat or shelf for the teacher
      at the **kamiza (upper end)** with a **kamidana to the war deities (Katori, Kashima)**.
    - Items: **wooden practice swords (bokutō) and leather-covered bamboo swords (fukuro-shinai; true shinai with
      protective gear begin with Naganuma's school in 1711–16)**, sword racks, **name-plate board of students
      (nafuda-kake)**, scroll of school rules, a drum to mark sessions, water bucket and ladle, towels on pegs.
      Protective armour (men, kote, dō) sets are early and rare in 1730 `[common from c. 1750s]`.
    - Src: ja.wikipedia *Naganuma Kunisato*, *Kendō no rekishi*.
29. **Spear and naginata hall (sōjutsu-jō).** As the dōjō; long rack of padded-tip training spears (tanpo-yari),
    naginata. (assumed separate at larger domains)
30. **Grappling hall (jūjutsu-jō).** Tatami floor, kamidana, rules scroll.
31. **Gunnery hall and range (hōjutsu-jō / teppō-ba).** A shed with a firing line and a target mound at 20–50 m.
    Items: matchlocks on racks, target boards, powder boxes, cleaning rods, matchcord, a sand-filled target mound
    with a small roof. (assumed layout)
32. **Archery range (yumi-ba / kyūdō-jō / shajō).**
    - Space: a roofed **shooting hall (shajō)** with a board floor, a strip of open ground (yamichi) usually ~28 m,
      and a **roofed earth target mound (azuchi)**; an arrow path (yatori-michi) for fetching arrows.
    - Items: bows on racks (upright), arrow cases (yazutsu), gloves (yugake), targets (mato) on stakes in the mound,
      a **straw practice target (makiwara)**, a score board, a drum or flag for the target keeper.
33. **Long-range through-shooting hall (tōshiya).** Sanjūsangendō's 120-m veranda at Kyoto; **Wasa Daihachirō's
    record 8,133 hits in 1686** (in window). Edo had its own Sanjūsangendō (Fukagawa, rebuilt 1701). Items: long
    veranda, counting boards, arrows by the thousand, a judges' seat.
34. **Horse-training ground (baba / uma-ba).** A long straight earth track with banks or rails (rachi) on both sides,
    a viewing stand (sajiki); Edo's Takadanobaba (1636). Items: railings, tie posts, a mounting block, water trough,
    saddle rack, a bench, pine trees along the track.
35. **Mounted archery course (yabusame-ba) and dog-shooting ground (inuoumono-ba).** Yabusame revived by Yoshimune
    (1720s, in window) at shrines; three targets on posts along a track. Inuoumono is a Kamakura/Muromachi sport,
    rare by 1730 `[mostly outside: revived only briefly]`.
36. **Swimming / water-training (suijutsu) site.** Domain river stretches with a changing shed (assumed).
37. **Falconry grounds and lodges (takaba, onari-goten).** **Yoshimune revived shogunal falconry in 1716.** Hunting
    grounds around Edo with guard posts (takaba-bansho) and rest lodges. Items: falcon perches, leather gloves, hoods,
    cages (mews, taka-beya), feed boxes.

### 5.3 Coast and frontier

38. **Coastal lookout (tōmi-bansho).** From 1638 (Nomo, Nagasaki) at ports and headlands to spot foreign ships. A tiny
    hut ~1 ken × 1 jō, board walls and floor, two watchmen, often seasonal (spring to summer). Items: a spyglass or
    none (Dutch telescopes existed; rare), a log book, a lantern, a signal fire pit or flag pole, a bench, a
    brazier. Src: Kotobank *Tōmi-bansho*; Shingū town, Noheji town pages.
39. **Beacon post (noroshi-ba / noroshi-dai).** A cleared hilltop with a stone or earth hearth, wood stacks, a hut.
    Chains of them relayed news to Nagasaki (17th c.); many later chains are coastal-defence builds
    `[many outside 1680-1750: 1800s]`. Items: firewood stacks, green pine for smoke, straw, a hut, buckets.
40. **Harbour guard stations and batteries at Nagasaki (bansho, from the 1640s–50s).** Guard posts run by the
    Fukuoka and Saga domains. Items: cannons on wooden carriages (few), gun racks, guard rooms.
41. **Coastal artillery batteries (daiba)** `[outside 1680-1750: 1800s, Shinagawa 1853]`.
42. **Ship sheds for official vessels (o-funa-gura).** Long boat sheds with water gates; the shogunal warship
    Atakemaru was **scrapped in 1682**; its shed and the official boats remained at Fukagawa (verify). Hagi domain's
    ship shed survives. Items: a hull on stocks or afloat, oars on racks, masts, sails in rolls, anchors, ropes.
43. **Domain boundary markers and guard huts (sakai-bansho).** §4.2 #15.
44. **Sekisho palisade and lookout.** §4.3 #16.

## 6. Civic and infrastructure

### 6.1 Schools and learning

1. **Writing school (terakoya / tenarai-juku).**
   - Function: reading, writing and abacus for commoner children, run by priests, masterless samurai, doctors or
     townsmen. Common in cities by 1730; the rural boom is later (late 18th–19th c.).
   - Space: a tatami room in a temple, a house or a rented shop; 10–50 children.
   - Items: **rows of small low desks (tsukue)**, inkstones and water droppers, brushes, **practice books blackened
     with ink (tenarai-sōshi)**, copybooks (ōrai-mono), an abacus rack, the teacher's desk facing the room, **a
     hanging scroll of Tenjin (Sugawara Michizane)** with an offering, a switch or pointer, finished calligraphy
     hung on the walls, shoe racks. Src: Dore; Rubinger.
2. **Domain school (hankō).**
   - Function: Confucian learning and martial arts for samurai sons. **Few exist in 1730**: most of the ~270 are
     founded after 1750 (ja.wikipedia *Hankō*). In window: **Hagi Meirinkan (1719)**, Okayama's, Yonezawa's roots
     (1697), Sendai's Yōkendō (1736). Owari had one from the Kan'ei era.
   - Space: a compound with a **Confucian shrine hall (seibyō / taiseiden)**, a lecture hall (kōdō), reading rooms,
     dormitories (ryō), a library, martial-arts halls, an archery range, a horse ground.
   - Items: lecture platform and lectern, low desks, book boxes and stacked Chinese classics, a Confucius statue or
     tablet on an altar with **ritual vessels for the sekiten ceremony (bronze food vessels, wine cups)**, calligraphy
     boards, a drum to mark classes.
3. **Shogunal Confucian academy (Yushima Seidō, moved 1690).** Taiseiden hall on a stone platform, black-lacquered,
   with gates (Nyūtoku-mon, Kyō-mon) and a lecture hall. Items as above, grander. Became the official Shōheikō
   `[outside 1680-1750: 1797]`.
4. **Local school for commoners (gōkō).** **Shizutani School (Okayama; founded 1670, lecture hall 1701, Bizen tile
   roof, lacquered floor)** is the model. Items: lecture hall with lacquered board floor, desks, lecture platform,
   Confucius hall, library, a stone wall.
5. **Merchant academy (Kaitokudō, Osaka, 1724; semi-official 1726).** A townsmen's academy in a large machiya with a
   lecture room. Items: lecture desk, rows of desks, bookcases, a notice of school rules.
6. **Private academy (shijuku).** A scholar's house with a lecture room: Itō Jinsai's Kogidō (Kyoto), Ogyū Sorai's
   school (Edo). Items: lecture desk, book stacks, students' desks, a portrait of the master (assumed).
7. **Library / book storehouse (bunko).** Domain and temple libraries in fireproof kura. Items: book boxes on
   shelves, labelled chests, ladders.

### 6.2 Health and relief

8. **Charity clinic (Koishikawa Yōjōsho, 1722).** Set up from a meyasubako petition; free care for the poor in the
   shogunal herb garden. Board-floored wards. Items: rows of bedding on straw mats, a doctor's room with a
   **medicine chest of many small drawers**, a **boat-shaped medicine grinder (yagen)**, mortar and pestle, a
   decoction stove with pots, scales, paper packets, a register. Src: ja.wikipedia *Koishikawa yōjōsho*.
9. **Medicinal herb garden (o-yakuen).** Koishikawa (1684), Komaba. Plots, a drying shed, a keeper's house. Items:
   drying racks and baskets, labelled beds.
10. **Famine relief huts (sukui-goya / o-sukui-goya).** Temporary board shelters and gruel kitchens set up in famines
    (Kyōhō famine 1732, two years after the anchor). Items: huge cauldrons, ladles, bowls, straw mats, rice
    bales. Seasonal/event dressing.
11. **Relief granary (gisō, shasō).** Community or domain famine reserves. Aizu's shasō dates from 1655 (in use in
    1730); most others are `[outside 1680-1750: Kansei era, 1790s]`. Space and items as the gōkura (§4.4), plus a
    lending ledger.
12. **Isolation / birth / menstrual huts (ubuya, taya).** Separate huts in some coastal and mountain villages where
    women stayed during birth and menstruation (listed neutrally; ja.wikipedia *Ubuya*). Items: a hearth, straw
    bedding, water jar.

### 6.3 Water supply

13. **Pulley well (tsurube-ido) with roof.** Stone or wooden curb, a pulley on a crossbeam, a small roof, a drain
    stone. Items: two buckets on a rope, a washing tub, a ladle, a small water-kami stone or offering.
14. **Lever well (hanetsurube).** Counterweighted pole on a post. Rural. Items: bucket on a pole, stone weight.
15. **Box well on the city aqueduct (jōsui-ido).** Edo's water came through **wooden pipes (tōi) and bamboo pipes
    (kakehi)** from the Kanda (1590s) and Tamagawa (1653) systems into square wooden well boxes. **Four of Edo's six
    systems (Aoyama, Mita, Senkawa, Kameari) were shut in 1722**, so in 1730 only Kanda and Tamagawa run. Items:
    square wooden curb, pulley, buckets, a wooden cover.
    - Src: ja.wikipedia *Edo no jōsui*.
16. **Aqueduct water gate and inspection station (mizu-ban-ya).** At Yotsuya Ōkido the Tamagawa aqueduct entered the
    city through an inspection station that guarded its water. Items: sluice boards, a gauge, guards' capture tools,
    a lantern.
17. **Aqueduct bridge (kakehi / suidō-bashi).** A wooden trough carried across a river on a bridge (Suidōbashi over
    the Kanda river). Special structure, no interior.
18. **Water-sellers' landing (mizu-ya).** Edo boat water sellers filled tubs at pipe ends (assumed detail).
19. **Public spring / wash place (arai-ba, kawabata).** Stone steps into a stream or a stone-lined pool where
    villagers washed. Items: washing stone, tubs, a ladle, a small Suijin stone.

### 6.4 Roads, bridges, ferries

20. **Mile mound (ichirizuka).** Paired mounds every ri (~3.9 km), ~9 m square and ~3 m high, with enoki (55 %),
    pine or cedar (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.6 [T22]). Items: the tree, a stone marker (some), a bench-stone.
21. **Signpost stone (michi-shirube / oiwake-ishi).** At forks: carved directions ("right to Edo, left to ...").
22. **Road avenue (namiki).** Pines or cedars both sides (the Nikkō cedar avenue, 1625–51). Names only.
23. **Stone-paved pass road (ishidatami).** Hakone 1680 (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.6).
24. **Plank bridge (ita-bashi).** Simple beams and boards on posts.
25. **Earth-covered bridge (dobashi).** Logs covered with earth and turf.
26. **Log bridge (maruki-bashi).** Rural; one or two logs.
27. **Large wooden trestle bridge (Nihonbashi type).** Edo's great bridges were being built in window: Ryōgoku
    (1661), **Shin-Ōhashi (1693), Eitai (1698)**; the Tōkaidō's **Yahagi bridge** (Okazaki, the longest on the road,
    ~370 m). Railings with **bronze finials (giboshi)** only on the most important bridges (Nihonbashi, Kyoto's
    Sanjō and Gojō). Items: giboshi, railings, a bridge-name post, lanterns (assumed), a notice board at the end.
28. **Arched wooden bridge (sori-hashi / taiko-bashi).** Shrines, gardens, Kintaikyō (Iwakuni, rebuilt 1674, five
    arches) as a landmark.
29. **Stone arch bridge (ishi-bashi / megane-bashi).** Nagasaki's Megane-bashi (1634); Kyushu mostly. Rare
    elsewhere.
30. **Cantilever bridge (hane-bashi).** Saruhashi (Kōshū road): stacked cantilevered beams over a gorge.
31. **Vine bridge (kazura-bashi).** Iya valley (Shikoku); remote mountain crossing.
32. **Boat bridge (funa-bashi).** Boats chained side by side with planks on top (Toyama's Jinzū river, 60+ boats).
    Rivers where fixed bridges were forbidden or impractical.
33. **Bridge-keeper's hut (hashi-ban-goya).** At big bridges and toll bridges (Eitai became a townsmen-run toll bridge
    in 1719, in window). Items: toll box, lantern, broom and rake, a staff, a register, a bench, fire buckets.
34. **Ferry landing (watashi-ba / tosen-ba).** Stone or earth ramp, mooring posts, a waiting shed, the ferry hut.
    Items: fare board, ferry boats (flat-bottom), poles, ropes, a bell or drum to call the boat, benches, a lantern.
35. **Ferry-keeper's hut (watashi-mori goya).** Small board hut. Items: poles, a brazier, straw raincoats, a sleeping
    mat.
36. **Rope-guided ferry (hiki-bune).** A rope strung across the river for the ferry (assumed on narrow rivers).
37. **Relay stables for official horses (tenma).** No single big station stable: post-town houses kept horses
    at the rear (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.4); the toiya-ba yard has tie posts. Supporting villages (sukegō) sent extra
    horses and men. Items: stalls, mangers, pack saddles, straw sandals for horses (uma-waraji), bells, rope.
38. **Roadside horse trough and tie posts.** Stone or wooden trough, iron rings.
39. **Rest stones and benches at passes (koshikake-ishi).** Names only.

### 6.5 Harbours and shipping

40. **River quay with steps (gangi / kashi).** Stone steps into a canal or river; mooring posts; the **kashi** (quay
    with a trade's name: fish quay, rice quay). Items: mooring stones and posts, bales, barrels, handcarts, planks.
41. **Warehouse row (kura-nami) on a canal.** Plastered kura side by side, doors to the water. (BL-B owns the
    merchant ones; the domain/shogunal ones are §4.4.)
42. **Lighthouse lantern (tōmyōdō / jōyatō).** A stone or wooden lantern tower on a quay or headland, lit by oil;
    Miya's jōyatō (1625). Items: oil lamp, oil jar, a ladder, a keeper's hut.
43. **Weather-watching hill (hiyori-yama).** A hill above a port with a **stone direction marker (hōi-ishi)** carved
    with compass points, where sailors read the weather. Items: the stone, a shelter bench, a small shrine.
44. **Harbour breakwater and boat-hauling slip.** Stone jetty (hatoba), a capstan (manrikisha) for hauling boats
    (assumed).
45. **Shipwright's slip.** BL-B (boat-builder); listed here only as a waterfront site.

### 6.6 Agriculture and water control

46. **Water gate / sluice (hi, hi-guchi, suimon).** Wooden sluice boards in a stone or timber frame at the head of a
    channel. Items: sluice boards, a windlass or lever, a gauge stake, sandbags.
47. **Water guard hut (mizu-ban goya).** In droughts villages stood guard at sluices day and night because water
    disputes were fierce (Kelly). Items: a straw mat, a lantern, a staff, a brazier, a record board.
48. **Weir (seki / iseki).** A stone-and-timber weir across a river diverting water into a channel. Structure only.
49. **Reservoir pond (tame-ike).** Kinai and Sanuki; earth dam with a sluice tower (assumed) and a small Suijin
    shrine.
50. **River embankments (tsutsumi) and flood-control works.** Bamboo-planted levees, stone groynes (seigyū) and
    gabions of stones in bamboo baskets (jakago). Names only.
51. **Treadwheel for irrigation (fumi-guruma).** From the 1660s (Osaka), so in. Small wooden paddle wheel trodden by
    a person. (WORLD_CATALOGUE 2.7)
52. **Dragon-spine chain pump (ryūkotsusha).** Older; wooden chain pump. Names only.
53. **Field hut (nora-goya).** Tool and rest hut at distant fields. Items: hoes, straw raincoat, a pot, a mat.

### 6.7 Community buildings

54. **Village assembly place (yoriai-dokoro).** Usually **not a separate building**: the headman's house, the
    shrine haiden or a village dō (§3.1 #2). Some villages had a **village hall (mura-kaisho / gōdō)**. Items: rows
    of straw mats, a hearth or brazier, the village rules board (mura-okite), a record chest, tea kettle.
55. **Young men's lodge (wakamono-yado / wakashu-yado).** Where unmarried young men slept and organised festival and
    fire duties; strongest in fishing and coastal villages. A plain house or room. Items: sleeping mats, straw
    bedding, a hearth, festival drums and lion head (assumed), a rules board, sake barrel.
56. **Young women's lodge (musume-yado).** Less common, same idea (assumed).
57. **Time-bell tower (toki no kane).** Edo had about nine official time bells (Hon-ishichō from the 1620s,
    Asakusa, Ueno, Shiba and others; verify count); castle towns rang a bell or drum. A bell tower (§3.2 #24) plus
    the keeper's hut. Items: bonshō, striker log, **incense clock (kō-dokei) or water clock**, a time table board,
    a lantern. Bell-keepers collected a fee from each household in range.
58. **Firebreak plaza and embankment (hirokōji, hiyoke-chi, hiyoke-dote).** Open spaces kept clear after 1657;
    in practice filled with temporary stalls, shows and tea stands (BL-B). A site, not a building.
59. **Public toilet at street corners (tsuji-setchin / kōshū benjo).** In Kyoto and Osaka, night-soil dealers set up
    roadside toilets to collect fertiliser (ja.wikipedia *Kōshū benjo*). Items: a half-wall booth, a buried pot,
    a lid.
60. **Rubbish collection point (gomi-tame) and landfill (Eitai-jima, 1655 rule).** In each Edo alley a wooden
    rubbish box; collected by boat to reclaimed land. Items: a board bin, a broom, baskets.

### 6.8 Death

61. **Graveyard (bochi / hakaba) outside a temple.** Village communal graves, family graves at field edges, and in the
    **Kinki "two-grave system" (ryōbosei)**: a **burial grave (ume-baka)** in a field away from the village and a
    **visiting grave (mairi-baka)** with the stone at the temple. Items: grave stones (WORLD_CATALOGUE §1.6), wooden
    grave posts (bohyō) for fresh graves, sotoba slats, flower tubes of bamboo, water cups, a small roofed frame
    over a fresh grave (assumed), bamboo fences around fresh graves against animals, buckets and ladles at a
    water point.
    - Src: ja.wikipedia *Ryōbosei*; Hur.
62. **Cremation ground / crematory hut (sanmai-ba, hiya, kasō-ba).** Outside the village or town; in Edo several
    (Senju, Kirigaya and others, assumed list); run by cremation attendants (onbō). A roofed shed over a stone or
    clay pit, or an open pit. Cremation was common in Jōdo Shinshū regions and cities; **burial was the norm in most
    rural areas** (Hur). Items (neutral): stone-lined pit, firewood stacks, straw bundles, iron poles and tongs,
    ash urns, water buckets, a small Jizō, a hut for the attendant.
63. **Coffin-bier shed and funeral goods store.** Kept at a temple or village: the bier (kan-dai / gan), a palanquin-
    shaped coffin carrier (kan-goshi), white banners, lanterns, the funeral canopy (tengai) (assumed).

## Obvious collapse candidates (advice only; Stephen decides)

1. **One "guard post" kit for all small control buildings:** kidoban, jishinban, tsuji-ban, bridge-keeper hut, ferry
   hut, water-guard hut, tōmi-bansho, domain border post, holding station. They are all a 1–2-room board hut with a
   bench, lantern, brazier and a capture-tool or tool rack. Differences are one prop (sundries counter, fire ladder,
   toll box, spyglass).
2. **One "official compound" shell:** bugyōsho, jinya/daikansho, kōri-bugyōsho, domain office, shoshidai office, Uraga
   bugyōsho, kanjōsho. Black nagaya-mon + genkan + office rooms + white-gravel court + kura. Vary by size and the
   presence of a court and cells.
3. **One "kura" shell** for gōkura, okura, kura-yashiki, treasury, powder magazine (stone variant), armoury,
   shrine treasure store, mikoshi store, temple store, library. Only the interior fill changes.
4. **One small sacred hall shell (2 × 2 or 3 × 3 ken, board floor, veranda):** Jizō/Kannon/Yakushi/Kōshin/Enma dō,
   village haiden, ema hall, pilgrim overnight hall, village assembly place. Fill decides which it is.
5. **Hokora as a family:** roadside, field, house-plot, alley Inari, sub-shrine, summit shrine = one micro-shrine in
   wood and one in stone, plus 4–5 prop swaps (fox pair, sakaki, shimenawa, torii).
6. **Temple scale ladder collapses to three sets:** (a) village temple: hondō-kuri + bell tower + graveyard;
   (b) town temple: gate + hondō + kuri + bell + graveyard; (c) one hero complex (Zen seven-hall or a Pure Land
   mieidō complex). Sect differences become prop swaps (unpan and han for Zen; fan drum and daimoku pillar for
   Nichiren; drum tower instead of bell tower and big tatami gejin for Shinshū).
7. **Shrine scale ladder collapses to three:** village chinju (saya-dō + small haiden), town shrine (haiden + honden
   + kagura stage + mikoshi store), one hero shrine (add rōmon, kairō, sub-shrine row, a pagoda for syncretism).
8. **Castle:** one keep (or, cheaper and period-correct for Edo/Osaka, a bare keep base), one generic turret in two
   heights, one box-gate pair, one tamon-yagura module, one palace wing module repeated, plus the ruin set for
   abandoned 1615 castles. Drop named storage turrets (salt, moon-viewing) into the one turret with prop fills.
9. **Training grounds:** one dōjō hall (fill swaps for sword / spear / jūjutsu), one archery range, one horse track.
   Drop the gunnery range, tōshiya, yabusame and swimming site unless a landmark is wanted.
10. **Schools:** one terakoya room fill (fits any temple guest room or machiya upstairs) and at most one Confucian
    hall (Shizutani as the hero). Drop domain schools as a type for 1730 because most are post-1750.
11. **Bridges:** plank, trestle (hero), arched, earth-covered and boat bridge cover everything; drop vine,
    cantilever and stone arch unless the terrain wants a landmark.
12. **Water:** three wells (pulley, lever, aqueduct box) + one sluice + one weir is enough; drop the aqueduct bridge,
    water sellers and reservoir unless mapped.
13. **Death:** one graveyard kit (with the sotoba rack and the six Jizō), one cremation hut; the execution ground is a
    fenced site built from existing fence, stone base and Jizō parts.
14. **Drop or defer as out-of-window:** machi-kaisho (1791), gisō/shasō (mostly 1790s), daiba batteries (1850s),
    ryūdosui pump (1750s+), Shōheikō (1797), hyakushō-rō (1775), full kendō armour (1750s+), most beacon chains
    (1800s).
15. **Honjin / waki-honjin** can share one shell (waki-honjin = honjin without the gate and with a smaller jōdan
    room). **Toiya-ba, kawa-kaisho and kanme-aratame-sho** can share one open-fronted office shell with a yard; the
    fill (fare board, depth gauge, big scale) decides which.

# KEEP list: trades, workshops, lodging and services (review sitting 2, Stephen + lead, 2026-09-29)

**Status:** APPROVED by Stephen, 2026-09-29, including 3 extras per group ("these are cheap, let's add some more;
worst case we cut those").

This collapses `research/buildings/BUILDING_LIST.md`: shops and retail, food and drink, lodging, services, crafts by
family, rural industry (about 300 entries), plus its §6.2–6.4 suggestions. The GAP_AUDIT meibutsu index was used to
pick the trades our corridor actually needs.

**Layers:** as `KEEP_DWELLINGS.md` (shell / variant / dressing). Loot tiers are ignored for now.

**The main rule:** in this period, most trades worked in the FRONT ROOM (mise) of an ordinary townhouse. The shutters
come off, the room opens to the street, and goods show on the raised floor. Outside, under the eaves: noren,
signboards, a fold-down bench (battari-shōgi), display tubs. So most trades are **dressing on dwelling shells 11/12/13**
(townhouses and the street-front row), not new shells.

**The cost rule (Stephen, 2026-09-29):** a shell assembled from existing kit parts is cheap. What costs is NEW parts
(kiln, forge, stage, vats) and NEW props. **Stretch** items are marked ☆. They were picked to reuse existing parts and
props, and they're the first to cut if shells turn out slow.

## A. Shop dressing sets on townhouse shells (no new shells): 22 + 3 ☆

- **Everyday shops:** general goods · draper · old clothes · ironmonger · ceramics and lacquerware · paper and brushes
  · oil and candles · charcoal and firewood · tobacco · travel goods and souvenirs (Ōtsu-e, Odawara lanterns) · rice
  dealer · fishmonger and greengrocer
- **Food:** tofu and wet food · soba and rice eatery · sweets and rice cakes (the single most common meibutsu on both
  roads)
- **Drink (Stephen: "definitely sake"):**
  - **sake shop (saka-ya)**: casks on a rack, masu measures, flasks, customers drinking on casks. The **sugidama**
    (cedar ball) over the door is the universal "sake here" sign.
  - **cooked-food and sake house (nimeuri-ya)**: the early-1700s izakaya forerunner. Simmering pots, benches, barrels
    as seats.
  
  Sake is also served in the tea house and inn sets. Doburoku (cloudy home-brew) is a farmhouse prop. Shōchū is cut
  (Kyushu).
- **Services:** apothecary and doctor · pawnbroker and moneychanger (with kura behind) · publisher and bookshop
- **Crafts that fit a townhouse:** leather goods · weaving looms · sewing · painting · lacquer · combs · dolls ·
  temple-street crafts (Buddhist images, rosaries, incense, candles)
- ☆ **tatami maker** · ☆ **lantern and umbrella shop** · ☆ **toy and clay-doll shop** (Fushimi dolls)

## B. New shells

### Roadside, food and drink: 4 + 3 ☆
1. **Roadside tea house**, 3 sizes: bench shed / open shop / rest-stop with rooms (tateba). It also serves sake. The
   pass tea house (tōge-jaya) is a size.
2. **Stall kit**: food stall (yatai), market stall, barber booth, fortune-teller, show booth (misemono-goya)
3. **Brewery complex**: sake as the hero (sugidama, vats, press, steamer, koji room); soy and Hatchō miso as dressing.
   **This is the big prop lift.**
4. **Water mill**: wheel, cam-driven stamps, stone mill
- ☆ **Restaurant with garden (ryōri-jaya)**: the upscale detached restaurant
- ☆ **Market hall**: an open shed with stalls; the generic town market (Nihonbashi's fish market stays a landmark)
- ☆ **Country sake brewer**: the small village brewery; the brewery complex at small size, reusing its props

### Lodging and services: 6 + 3 ☆
5. **Inn (hatago)**, 2 sizes: the most common building in a post town. The serving-girl inns (meshimori) and the
   pilgrim-group inns are sign and dressing variants.
6. **Honjin**; the waki-honjin is a smaller variant, without the gate
7. **Hot-spring inn + bath house**: at EVERY hot-spring town (Hakone, Atami, Shuzenji, Arima, Kusatsu), not
   Hakone-only
8. **Public bathhouse (sentō)**: every city and big town (Edo had hundreds)
9. **Stable yard**: post horses, packhorse carriers, horse dealers (Chiryū horse fair, Kiso horses)
10. **Hot-spring bath hut**: the wild or village communal spring (also in the rural list; one shell)
- Cheap lodging (kichin-yado) = the tenement shell, dressed. No new shell.
- ☆ **Pilgrim lodge (shukubō / oshi house)**: Fuji north side and Ōyama pilgrims. Headman-house parts.
- ☆ **Barber and hairdresser shop (kamiyui-doko)**: a small street-corner shop, a town gossip spot
- ☆ **Palanquin station (kago-ya)**: bearers' room and stored palanquins; the stable yard's small cousin

### Workshops: 6 + 3 ☆
11. **Smithy**, with a swordsmith variant (shimenawa, dark forge room): Seki and Sakai blades
12. **Foundry**: Kuwana cast iron; pots, kettles; bells as a site kit
13. **Earth-floor workshop**, dressed as joinery, abacus maker (Ōtsu), woodturner (Hakone), bamboo and basket (Arima,
    Minakuchi), straw and rush
14. **Raised-floor bench workshop**, dressed as sword polisher and fittings, metal bench, lacquer (Kiso; the dust-free
    drying cupboard is its tell), sewing, comb maker (Oroku combs), print room, painting studio
15. **Timber yard**: sawpit, log stacks, shingle splitting
16. **Tannery**: one riverside site (pits, frames, smoke hut). Stephen's "3 leather buildings → 1" case; leather goods
    are the townhouse dressing in A.
- ☆ **Loom house**: weaving shed for the silk and cloth towns (Hachiōji, Gunnai, Nishijin as the rich variant)
- ☆ **Oil and candle works**: wedge press, oil casks, wax (the water-driven oil mills are 1790s+, so hand press only)
- ☆ **Cooperage**: the big-tub and bucket maker. Its round-wood products are props the brewery needs anyway.

### Yards and sites: 6 + 3 ☆
17. **Dyer's yard**: sunken indigo vats, tall cloth-drying poles (Arimatsu / Narumi tie-dye)
18. **Pottery kiln site**: climbing kiln + work shed (Seto and Tokoname are near Nagoya)
19. **Tile works**: small kilns, drying racks (booming after Edo's 1720 tile push)
20. **Stonemason's yard**: Okazaki stone lanterns
21. **Paper mill**: vats, drying boards in the sun (Mino, Shuzenji)
22. **Salt works**: salt pans + boiling hut (Gyōtoku, Kira, Yui–Kanbara)
- ☆ **Boatbuilder's yard**: a slip on the shore, hull on trestles
- ☆ **Umbrella and paper drying yard**: rows of open umbrellas drying (Kanō umbrellas); mostly props
- ☆ **Bleaching field (sarashi-ba)**: cloth laid out by a river (Takamiya ramie)

### Rural and wilderness: 8 + 3 ☆
Stephen: "things people come across in the wild". Most reuse the field hut (dwelling 2), bunk hall (4) or shed (24),
plus one special structure.
23. **Charcoal kiln site**: earth-dome kiln + field hut. Very common in the mountains.
24. **Logging camp**: bunk hall + sawpit + timber slide
25. **Quarry**: cut rock face + shed (Izu stone, Okazaki granite)
26. **Lime kiln**: stone kiln + shed (Akasaka, Ōme)
27. **Mine site**: adit, sorting shed, miners' bunk hall. Dressing: mine shrine (yama-no-kami), ore baskets, windlass,
    drainage troughs, spoil heaps. Period mining on the map: **gold in Izu and Kai, sulphur at Hakone**. Several can go
    in the wilderness as extra content (Stephen).
28. **Bonito smokehouse**: Yaizu, west Izu
29. Salt works (= 22) and the hot-spring bath hut (= 10) also count here
- ☆ **Horse pasture (maki)**: fences, herder's hut, drover camp (Mochizuki, Kiso horse country)
- ☆ **Tea processing shed**: steaming and drying (Abe tea); shed + props
- ☆ **River fish weir house (yana / ajiro)**: sweetfish trap and watch hut

### Pleasure quarters and entertainment: 3 + 3 ☆
Stephen wants "a pleasure district or two". The licensed quarters were Edo's Yoshiwara, Kyoto's Shimabara and Osaka's
Shinmachi. Unlicensed districts and the Tōkaidō meshimori inns use the inn shell with dressing. Handled neutrally.
30. **Brothel house with the latticed front room (harimise)**: the signature look
31. **Banquet house (ageya)**: Kyoto's Sumiya (1641) survives, so a real plan exists. Guide tea houses (hikite-jaya)
    are townhouse dressing. The walled quarter's single great gate + moat wall is a civic/outdoor kit.
32. **Kabuki theatre**: a LANDMARK (Stephen). Drum tower (yagura) over the entrance, stage, runway (hanamichi), box
    seating. Edo's three licensed theatres use the same shell with different crests and banners. Theatre tea houses =
    townhouse dressing.
- ☆ **Puppet theatre (ningyō jōruri)**: the smaller theatre (Osaka Takemoto-za, from 1684)
- ☆ **Sumō arena**: a temporary fund-raising ring at a temple (kanjin-zumō); fences, stands, ring
- ☆ **Archery gallery (yōkyūba)**: a small amusement archery booth (verify its 1730 date)

## Totals

- **Core:** 32 new shells + 22 dressing sets
- **Stretch ☆:** 18 shells + 3 dressing sets
- **The biggest new prop lifts:** the brewery, the smithy/foundry, the kilns, the theatre stage, the dyer's vats and

## Cut

- **The agents' outright cuts:** gunsmith, tinplate, clockmaker, spectacles, whaling, oysters, cormorants, sugar press,
  shippoku cuisine, tempura stalls, women's hairdresser, yose storytelling halls, insect sellers, shiitake, hanafuda,
  gambling den, shōchū still, firework maker
- **Later than 1730:** Banko ware (c.1736–41), daruma (c.1780s), kanten (1840s), safflower trade (c.1790s), eel
  kabayaki shops (1830s), Hakone yosegi (c.1800s), water-driven oil mills (1790s+)
- **Stays a landmark, not a type:** Dōjima rice market, Nihonbashi fish market, Mariko's Chōjiya, Odawara's Uirō
- **Moved:** tatara ironworks (off-map, cut); Osaka domain warehouses (kura-yashiki) → government sitting

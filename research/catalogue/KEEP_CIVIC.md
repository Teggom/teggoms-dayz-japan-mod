# KEEP list: religious, government, military, civic (review sitting 3, Stephen + lead, 2026-09-29)

**Status:** APPROVED by Stephen, 2026-09-29 ("everything you've shown me here is good").

This collapses `research/buildings/BUILDING_LIST.md`: Shinto, Buddhist, government, military, civic and infrastructure
(about 220 entries), plus §6.5–6.6. The layers are as in `KEEP_DWELLINGS.md`. ☆ = stretch: a cheap extra, cut first.

**Landmarks are kept separate (Stephen):** this list holds only REUSABLE shells. The one-off hero builds made from them
go to the landmark sitting: the hero shrine's tower gate + cloister, the hero temple complex, the 5-storey pagoda, the
named checkpoints (Hakone, Arai, Kiso-Fukushima, Usui), Kodenmachō jail, the Great Buddha Hall and the big trestle
bridges.

## Shinto (6 + 3 ☆)
1. **Micro-shrine: 4 stone variants + 4 wood variants** (Stephen). Roadside, yard (yashiki-gami), alley Inari and
   summit shrines. Prop swaps: fox pair, sakaki, shimenawa, small torii. Model the variants from real examples (many
   photos exist).
2. **Worship hall (haiden)**, 2 sizes
3. **Main sanctuary (honden)**, 2 sizes
4. **Kagura dance stage**
5. **Purification pavilion (temizuya)**
6. **Priests' office and amulet window**
- **The shrine ladder:** village shrine (small haiden + honden) → town shrine (+ kagura stage, temizuya, mikoshi store
  = kura) → hero shrine (landmark sitting). Pagodas at shrines reuse the temple pagoda: correct for 1730, when shrines
  and temples were still mixed.
- ☆ noh stage · ☆ votive picture hall (ema-den) · ☆ red arched sacred bridge

## Government (8 + 3 ☆)
1. **Guard hut**: ward-gate keeper, self-watch post, crossroads guard, bridge keeper, ferry hut, border post, water
   guard. One prop decides which (sundries counter, fire ladder, toll box, spyglass).
2. **Official compound**, sizes: town magistrate, intendant (daikansho / jinya), domain office, rural magistrate.
   Black nagaya-mon gate, genkan, offices, white-gravel court, kura; with or without a court and cells.
3. **Open-front office with yard**: post-station office (toiya-ba), river-crossing office, cargo weight station
4. **Highway checkpoint kit**: gate, guardhouse, palisade, inspection room. The four named checkpoints are landmarks
   built from it.
5. **Jail compound**: cell blocks, guard office, single gate. Kodenmachō is the landmark instance.
6. **Notice board (kōsatsuba)**
7. **Ward gate (kido)** + gatekeeper hut (= 1)
8. **Fire watchtower**, 2 heights; the fire brigade station = compound (2) + tower
- **Execution grounds:** IN (Stephen), built from existing fence, stone base and Jizō parts; the public exposure
  platform the same way. Handled neutrally.
- ☆ rice storehouse row (shogunal / domain) · ☆ courier relay post · ☆ ward meeting house

   - **roof:** grey tile / **green copper** (Nagoya and Edo used copper)
   - **roof-end fish (shachi):** gold / plain
   - **walls:** white plaster / **black boards** (Matsumoto style)
   
   **Bare keep base** variant for Edo and Osaka (keeps gone by 1730).

7. **Castle ruin set**: overgrown stone bases, earthworks, moat traces (Yamanaka, Azuchi)
8. **Training hall (dōjō)**: sword / spear / grappling fills
9. **Archery range**
- The powder magazine and armoury = stone kura variants (dwelling 22).
- ☆ horse-training track · ☆ coastal lookout + signal-fire post · ☆ mounted-archery course (yabusame, revived 1728)

## Walls (the reusable wall kit)
| Wall | Where | Build type |
|---|---|---|
| **Stone rampart (ishigaki)** | Castle bases, big embankments | Terrain + stone; technique variants in OUTDOOR_LIST |
| **Castle plaster wall (dobei)** | On the ramparts | White, tile-capped, triangle and square firing ports (sama) |
| **Wooden palisade** | Checkpoints, military posts | Sharpened timber fence |
| **Ornate earthen wall (tsuiji-bei)** | Temples, noble and rich compounds | Tile-capped. Up to 5 horizontal lines (sujibei) mark temples tied to the imperial house |
| **Board fence (itabei)** | Samurai and merchant compounds | Plain boards |

Garden bamboo fences belong to the outdoor kit.

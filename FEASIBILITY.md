## 0. The answer on one screen

**Is it feasible? Yes, as a long project.** It has three parts with very different risk:

| Part | Risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The map** (terrain, roads, water, forests) | **Moderate, and the most automatable part** | The elevation data for Japan is excellent. Almost every input to a DayZ terrain is a file a script can write. The open question is how many GUI clicks each rebuild costs you (spike T, §8). |
| **The art** (buildings, clothing, armour, weapons, trees, props) | **The real cost.** Large volume, known methods | Nothing Japanese exists in vanilla, and there is no usable prior art (§7). | **The systems** (katana, bow, matchlock, bamboo, base building, riding) | **Mostly moderate. Riding is hard** | Katana and spear reuse vanilla animation sets **[V]**. Vanilla contains a half-finished bow **[V]**. Bamboo cutting and base building are vanilla mechanisms. Riding needs our mod to own the player animation graph. |

**The rough size** (±50%, calibrated on the Pokemon work and on WORLD_BUILDINGS.md §6.5):

| | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Agent sessions | **~150-270**, several times the Pokemon mod so far |
| Your in-game checks | **~40-70**, batched |

**What changed since WORLD_BUILDINGS.md §3.4 called a new terrain "not realistic":**
- That verdict was for the Pokemon overhaul, where a new terrain was optional and the assumption was that you would drive Terrain Builder for weeks.
- Here the terrain is the point of the project. So the question becomes how small the GUI part can be made.
- Two findings shrink it:
  1. Every Terrain Builder input is plain text or an image, and the object import is one text line per object **[W S1, S3]**.
  2. The source world format (8WVR) is documented, with an MIT-licensed writer **[W S6, S7]**. Skipping Terrain Builder entirely is plausible, but unproven for DayZ.

**Where it starts:**
1. You answer the decision list (§9).

3. **Spike wave 1:** five Opus agents in parallel, each proving one pipeline, all tested together in one small test world (§8, step 2).

## 1. How a DayZ map is made, in plain terms

A DayZ terrain is six layers. Each one is a file or a list we can generate.

| Layer | What it is | Who makes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Heightmap** | A grid of elevations (ESRI `.asc`). Practical maximum 4096² **[W S10]** | Python: real elevation data plus design (§2) |
| **Satellite image + surface mask** | The colour seen from a distance (~1 m/px, ~16 k px for 15 km **[V]**). The mask says which ground texture and grass goes where. DayZ allows **6 surfaces per 512 px tile** **[V: Livonia uses 6]** | Python, from slope, height, the road and town masks, and CC0 textures |
| **Objects** | Every tree, rock, building, fence and road piece. **Chernarus has 2.97 M, of which 2.52 M are plants [V]** | Scripted placement. Nobody places 2.5 M trees by hand; tools do it from masks |
| **Roads** | DayZ uses fixed road *pieces* (straights, corners, ends), not Arma's shapefiles. Vanilla types include `grav` (6 m gravel) and `mud` (5 m) **[W S15]**. Those two suit feudal roads from day one | Python: route by slope, then snap to pieces, then write the import text |
| **Water** | Only the sea flows by itself. Lakes and rivers above sea level are "pond" models. Livonia ships 805 bespoke river pieces **[V]** | Our own `mlod.py` can write pond models |
| **Economy + AI data** | Navmesh (infected and animal pathing), loot points, loot tiers, animal territories, spawns | Loot and positions are generated (WORLD_BUILDINGS.md §4.2-4.3 already worked this out). **The navmesh is a GUI step** (§6) |

Then come the world config and the mission folder:
- **World config:** sun latitude ~35°N, an April date, spring temperatures, surfaces and grass definitions.
- **Mission folder:** types, events, spawn gear and weather.

These are routine config work for us.

**Tools on this machine [V]:** `TerrainBuilder`, `TerrainProcessor`, `NavMeshGenerator`, `ImageToPAA`, `CeEditor`, `Binarize`, `AddonBuilder` (all in `DayZ Tools\Bin`), plus Blender 5.2 and our MLOD writer. D: has 757 GB free.

## 2. Terrain data, and what "condensing" really means

### 2.1 The data (free)

| Source | Resolution | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **GSI (国土地理院) DEM** | 5 m in parts (lidar), 10 m nationwide, bare earth | Best quality. Licence PDL1.0: credit plus a note of edits **[W S19]**. **Open question:** Survey Act approval for derived products. Approvals are free, but whether a heavily reshaped game heightmap counts as a derived product is unclear **[W S20]** |
| **Copernicus GLO-30** | 30 m, surface model | Free, including commercial use, with a fixed credit notice **[W S22]**. No paperwork. The cleanest licence |
| **JAXA AW3D30** | 30 m, surface model | Free with ©JAXA credit. The newest version skipped Japan **[W S21]** |

**Recommendation:** prototype with GSI 10 m (nothing is published during the spikes). Settle the licence before release: ask GSI, or rebuild the detail from Copernicus.

### 2.2 You can't just shrink Japan

- Edo to Osaka is ~500 km by the old roads. Squeezing that into ~13 km is **1:40**.
- Squeeze only horizontally and every slope becomes 40× steeper: cliffs everywhere.
- Squeeze in all three directions and Mt Fuji becomes a 95 m hill.

**So "condensed" has to mean *composed*:**
1. **Design the big shapes** (coast, bays, basins, ridges, one volcano) as a hand-made layout you approve (§3).
2. **Transplant real 1:1 patches** of GSI terrain for local character. For example: real Hakone ridges for the pass, a real Kanto paddy plain, a real rocky Izu coast, the real hills around Kyoto. They are blended into the design at their true scale.
3. **Run an erosion pass** over the whole map so the seams disappear and valleys drain naturally.
4. **Flatten the pads:** castle bases, town grids, road beds and stepped paddy terraces.

All of this is numpy/scipy work I can do and check by rendering previews.

**The volcano:**
- The real Fuji can't fit (its base is ~40 km wide).
- A ~800-1000 m cone near a map edge works as a skyline landmark visible from most of the map.
- Vanilla Sakhal ships volcano assets **[V: `structures_sakhal/specific/volcano`]**. They are worth a look for crater rocks and steam vents.

### 2.3 Water is a design constraint, and a gift

- Only sea-level water is free.
- **Edo and Osaka were canal cities.** Put both on bays and cut the canals just below sea level: the ocean fills them for free. That is historically right and costs nothing.
- **Inland rivers and the lake** (Kamo river, a Lake Biwa stand-in) need generated pond models. One test pond is in spike T. Whether stepped river segments look acceptable is unverified.

## 3. A first-draft layout (for you to react to, not a decision)

The map is Livonia-sized, **12.8 km**. North is up.

```
 N ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  high mountains - cedar forest - NAKASENDO (inland road) │
   │  mountain villages, onsen, temple on a peak, bamboo      │
   │   [lake]                                ▲ volcano        │
   │  KYOTO basin      central range        (skyline)         │
   │  temples, pagoda  + pass w/ checkpoint     HAKONE-style  │
   │  palace, grid     (sekisho)                pass +        │
   │                                            checkpoint    │
   │ OSAKA                 mid castle town          EDO       │
   │ castle, canals ────── TOKAIDO (coast road) ─── castle,   │
   │ (SW bay)     post towns every ~1.5 km          canals    │
   │                       paddies, fishing villages (SE bay) │
   └──────────────────── Pacific coast ───────────────────────┘
```

Why this shape works in play:
- **Two routes** between the capitals: the fast coast road (Tokaido) and the slow mountain road (Nakasendo).
- **Passes with checkpoints** make natural PvP chokepoints.
- Loot tiers fall out naturally from the geography:
  - castles and the capitals: high
  - post towns: middle
  - farms and fishing villages: low
- The Tokaido was lined with pines and had milestone mounds. Those are cheap details with a strong period feel.

**City scale:** "Edo" is a castle on stone walls with a moat, a samurai district, a dense merchant district, temples and canals. That is roughly 1.5-2 km across and 200-400 buildings, not a million people.

## 4. The art: what has to be modelled

### 4.1 Buildings: code-generated from a kit

Japanese architecture is ideal for generating from code:
- It is built on a fixed grid (the ken, ~1.82 m) from posts and beams.
- It has a few wall types (plaster, board, lattice, shoji) and four roof families (gable, hip, hip-and-gable, and thatch variants).

**A parametric Blender kit** takes these inputs and outputs a full p3d with every LOD:
- footprint in ken
- storeys
- roof type and material
- wall mix
- veranda
- shop front

One kit gives dozens of distinct houses. The same approach covers these, because each is a few parameters:
- stone castle walls (their curved fan profile)
- torii gates
- stacked castle keeps
- pagodas
- plaster walls with tile caps
- gates

**What an enterable building needs** is already worked out (WORLD_BUILDINGS.md §4.1):
- LODs: Resolution, Geometry, View and Fire Geometry, Roadway, Memory
- a `model.cfg`
- a `class Doors` config

**Sliding doors (shoji and fusuma) are supported** through `type="translation"`. The vanilla boxcar and barn doors slide **[W S23]**. Loot points are generated from our own floor faces, so no in-game loot editor is needed.

**The rough catalogue:**

| Group | Count | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Enterable building types | ~40-60 | Town houses, farmhouses, inns, storehouses, temples, shrines, guard posts, the checkpoint, castle keeps, tea houses, a sake brewery |
| Props | ~100-150 | Lanterns, wells, torii, fences, graves, jizo statues, bridges, boats, water wheels, rice racks, shop curtains, carts |

Interiors are tatami rooms with a sunken hearth (irori). The hearth could double as a working vanilla fireplace **[I]**.

### 4.2 Trees, bamboo, ground cover

- **Day one:** vanilla beech, oak, spruce, Scots pine and birch fill the generic forest. Japanese forests really are beech, oak, pine and conifer, and placing vanilla models on a new map is standard practice.
- **New hero species:**
  - sakura: several ages, used in towns, temples and along rivers
  - sugi cedar: tall shrine and mountain groves
  - bamboo groves
  - Japanese black pine: coast and Tokaido rows
  - maple: temples
- **How trees are built:** vanilla trees use the `TreeAdv` and `TreeAdvTrunk` shaders with a wind parameter, and the rvmats are plain text we can copy **[V]**.
- **Cuttable bamboo:** a `CfgNonAIVehicles` class named `TreeSoft_<p3dname>` sets whether it can be cut, the drops, the tool damage and the output item **[W S24]**. It only works on objects placed in the terrain, which is why the bamboo test rides on spike T's test world.
- **Ground:** paddies as a surface with young-rice grass, spring flowers, and falling petal particles near sakura.

### 4.3 Clothing and armour

**Vanilla slots map cleanly onto the theme:**

| Slot | Items |
|---|---|
| Head | kabuto helmet, straw hat (kasa), jingasa, headband |
| Mask | face armour (mempo), ninja wrap, the komuso basket hat |
| Body | kimono, haori, ninja jacket |
| Vest | the do chest armour, with the shoulder plates attached |
| Gloves | kote arm guards |
| Legs | hakama, leggings |
| Feet | waraji sandals, tabi, geta |
| Back | pilgrim's pack (oizuru), a bundle cloth |

- **Rain protection comes free:** the straw raincoat (mino) can use vanilla wetness protection.
- **Every worn piece needs male and female versions.** Our local research already verified the `DayzTemporarySkeleton` route (item_overhaul/FEASIBILITY.md §2c.3). Clothing needs no Workbench step.
- **Rigid pieces are moderate, then easy.** That covers helmets, hats, masks and packs.
- **Tops and trousers are hard.**
- **Floor-length robes and wide hakama are unproven.** Vanilla long coats and the nurse dress show that length works on the stock skeleton **[W]**. Nobody documents how very wide or very long garments deform. Samurai armour is lamellar (rows of laced small plates), which is actually well suited to generating from code.

**The Pokemon overhaul needs the same Wearable spike** (FEASIBILITY.md §2c.6). One spike answers the question for both projects.

### 4.4 My honest limits on art

**Strong:**
- anything geometric or repetitive done in code: buildings, walls, armour plates, weapons, props
- looking at my own Blender renders and fixing what's wrong
- CC0 PBR textures (ambientCG and Poly Haven are CC0; Poly Haven even has tatami and bamboo **[W S25, S26]**)

**Weak:**
- organic sculpting: demon masks, statues, faces
- hand-painted textures

Those pieces will look simpler. **If you want hero pieces to look better,** the options are CC-BY or paid store models with game-use licences, or a commission (decision 8).

Two things are missing: the arrow ammo classes they name aren't defined anywhere **[V]**, and the player graph may lack the bow states **[W, unverified]**. It needs the player animation graph replaced (it depends on `@Survivor-Animations` **[V SERVER_NOTES.md]**). **Only one mod per server can own that graph [W]**, so ours must be it. The Pokemon riding work (queued) is the same R&D | **Hard.** Shared with Pokemon |
| **Ox carts** | No precedent anywhere **[W]** | **R&D, later** |
| **No cars** | Just don't spawn any | Free |
| **Spring** | Latitude, date and temperatures in the world config; spring showers in the weather config; petal particles | Easy |
| **Infected** | Vanilla infected wear Western clothes. Retexturing them is cheap but they stay Western in cut. New infected meshes have precedent **[W]**, and infected have their own animation graph, so they don't take the player-graph slot **[V]**. They reuse the clothing pipeline | Decision 4 |

## 6. The GUI steps that stay yours (unless a spike removes them)

- **Terrain Builder** (only if the Terrain-Builder-free route fails): import the generated inputs, generate layers, export WRP. Spike T times this.
- **Navmesh:**
  1. Boot a local DayZDiag server with `-startNavmeshDataServer`.
  2. Click Connect → Generate → Save in NavMeshGenerator **[W S13]**.

  It's needed after every terrain rebuild, or infected and animals stand frozen. **Plan:** test grey-box builds without AI, and regenerate the navmesh only at milestones.
- **Starting test servers.** Per the standing rule, you start them, never me. The Japan test world gets its own launcher, profile and port so it never touches your live server.
- **Workbench** only if we ever make new player animations (riding, a custom bow draw).
- **Workshop publishing** stays yours, as always.

## 7. Prior art (there's nothing to lean on)

- **There is no feudal-Japan terrain for DayZ or Arma [W, absence].**
- The Japan-themed DayZ maps are modern and closed:
  - **Japan – Oshima Island** forbids unpacking **[W S11]**.
  - **Hashima Islands** uses retextured vanilla assets **[W S11]**.
- **Kerama Islands (Arma 3, Japan)** took one author 18 months **[W S11]**. It's a useful yardstick for a solo human.
- **Reforger's Nihon Asset Project** has torii and lanterns, but it's a different engine **[W S17]**.
- **SLC Swords** (katanas) is built from CGTrader and Sketchfab models under their own licences **[W]**.
- Arma 3 data can't be used in DayZ **[W]**.
- **Conclusion:** everything is built from scratch, with CC0 textures.

## 8. How it begins: the plan

**Step 0 (now):** you read this and answer §9.

**Step 1: a 10-minute look on your current server** (no new mod; add it to your next play session). With VPP, spawn these and hold each one:
- `Sword`: this is how a katana would swing
- `Spear`: yari and naginata
- `RecurveBow`: the dormant vanilla bow; does it even raise and draw?

This tells us whether the bow's player animations work before any agent touches it.

**Step 2: spike wave 1.** Five Opus agents in parallel, each owning its own folder, **then one bundled test session**:

| Spike | Proves | Kill / pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| **T: Terrain round trip** | A 2 × 2 km test world from a real Hakone patch: heightmap, 3-6 surfaces, satellite, ~5 k vanilla trees, one gravel road, one pond, one vanilla house. It loads in game on a separate launcher. It tries **both** routes: Terrain-Builder-free (8WVR writer + our own layer tiles + binarize) and the Terrain Builder fallback (inputs ready to import, with the clicks listed) | **Pass** = the world loads and the list of clicks per rebuild is known. **If both routes fail,** the project stops here, cheaply |
| **B: One building** | A two-room town house (machiya) from the parametric kit: sliding shoji, tiled roof, hearth, roadway floors, generated loot points. Placed into the T world | Doors slide, you can walk upstairs, loot spawns, no falling through floors |
| **W: Wearables** | Straw hat (rigid), kimono top (hard), waraji (feet), male and female. Shared with the Pokemon Wearable spike | Moves with the body, seams meet. Same kill rule as FEASIBILITY.md §2c.6 |
| **A: Arms** | Katana on `Sword`, yari on `Spear`, and a finished `RecurveBow` + arrow ammo modelled on the crossbow bolt | Katana and yari are near-certain. The bow either draws, fires and drops a recoverable arrow, or we learn exactly which graph states are missing |
| **F: Flora** | One sakura (wind, LODs) and one cuttable bamboo clump that yields a bamboo pole. Placed into the T world | It sways, looks right at distance, and chopping gives poles |

These agents need about a day or two of runtime. The test for you is **one session of ~30-45 min** on the test world.

**Step 3: the design document.** Map layout, town and point-of-interest list, loot tiers, the asset catalogue with counts, era details. For GO/NOGO.

**Step 4: the grey-box map.** The full heightmap, roads, water, forests of vanilla trees, and placeholder boxes where towns go. You run around it to judge scale and feel before any serious modelling is spent. This is the milestone where it starts to feel real.

**Step 5 onward:** asset waves (the building kit and then town by town, flora, clothing and armour, weapons), then systems (bamboo, base building, bow, matchlock, riding), then economy, loot, performance and polish. Each wave ends in a batched walkthrough.

**Organisation:** a new private git repo `japan_dev` like `pokemon_dev`, per-agent file ownership, a commit and push per phase, and only Opus agents.

## 9. Decisions only you can make

A note: A lot of what I read above is not "for sure" yet. I would say doing a research session and seeing the weapons that were in japan. What did they use for starting fire. What did they use for splitting trees or bamboo, that sort of thing. That would be critical. 

1. **Era.**
   - **Recommended:** c. 1600 (Sekigahara), late Sengoku into early Edo. Armour is still in use, matchlocks are common, castles are new, and the highways and post towns are being set up.
   - **Alternative:** mid-Edo (Hiroshige's Tokaido prints): peaceful, fewer armoured samurai, more travellers and ronin.
   I would say recommended here. Adds a nice blend, allows for a variety of things. 
2. **Map size.**
   - **Recommended:** 12.8 km (Livonia-size). It fills faster, and asset density is the bottleneck.
   - **Alternative:** 15.36 km (Chernarus-size).
   Do recommended here as well. I'm indifferent, as long as we have a decent map. 
3. **Cities.** **Recommended:** Edo + Osaka + Kyoto in between, plus one mid-map castle town and the post towns. Or only the two ends.
    My conern with this is, most maps have a "path" you follow. IE one direction you can walk where it always gets slightly harder the more you go. IE better loot and more weapons as you head NW on Chernarus. Or NW on bitterroot. That sort of thing. My concern with saying Edo + Osaka + Kyoto is, where becomes the "path" the player moves in? Maybe it's just one city, like Osaka right? Maybe it's near Nara, maybe it gets tougher the further north you go and you start south of osaka or something. I think trying to find the right "Map spot" and right "Spawn spot" is important. I don't really care too much to have it stretch from osaka to tokyo, that' pretty far. Yeah you'd get Fuji, but honestly it would be so many mountains in between, I feel like unless there's a good justification, maybe around Kyoto somewhere makes more sense. 
4. **The threat.**
   - (a) new Japanese-dressed infected (the clothing pipeline, more art)
   - (b) retextured vanilla infected (cheap, Western cut)
   - (c) no infected: wildlife + players, with bandit NPCs later'
   threat would be A, japanese dressed infected, but having an AI mod that spawns wandering ronin or bands of bandits would be great. 
5. **Riding.** **Recommended:** a horse (historically right, same tech as the Pokemon riding). The ox as the mount, or ox carts, is R&D for later.
  Yeah for later. This would be the "car", not too pressed. 
6. **Dependencies.** **Recommended:** pure vanilla, no CF. Note that riding makes our mod *the* player-animation mod on that server. If this ever runs next to @Pokemon, the two must share one animation owner.
    Pure vanila would be the best. This wouldn't run with Pokemon. This is a pure overhaul standalone mod. 
7. **Relation to the Pokemon overhaul.**
   - Both are "full scope" projects that share three spikes (wearables, custom buildings, riding).
   - Which comes first, or should the two be interleaved so each spike serves both?
   - A thought, not a push: Pokemon Legends: Arceus *is* feudal-era Japan (Hisui). This map could one day host the Pokemon family too.
    Pokemon is not on the table here. 
8. **Asset sources.** **Recommended:** 100% made by us + CC0 textures. Or allow CC-BY and paid store models for hero pieces (masks, statues).
  Yes that's fine. I don't wnat to pay for anything, so made by us or free models we can find online. 
9. **Terrain data licence.** **Recommended:** prototype on GSI, decide before any release: ask GSI or switch the detail to Copernicus.
    I don't care, I'm not that pressed. 
10. **GO / NOGO on steps 1 and 2** (the 10-minute look and spike wave 1).
    For now, lets do a 2km x 2km map, it will be our test island map. Everything we build will go on this map for now for testing. Once we settle on the region and map bounds, etc, then we can build the proper map. I don't care too much befor then. I really want to focus on getting the feudal japan aesthetic correct. The lightning, the buildings, etc. I want to leverage paintings and historial artifacts as much as possible, to really capture the essence of that time period. 
    I don't want to do the go/nogo on step 1 and 2, I'll be telling you to do the 5 agents and test island to start.
